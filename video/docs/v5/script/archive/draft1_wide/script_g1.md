## The story in 3 sentences

A computer in space keeps overheating, because with no air up there the heat has nowhere to go, and it's only
running a little chatbot, while one full cabinet of the AI chips behind yours throws off as much heat as forty
boiling kettles. So people put data centers in strange places, inside a mountain in Italy, under the sea off
Shanghai, and in a nuclear bunker under a park in Stockholm, and that last one keeps its neighbors warm. Now
Helsinki wants to do that a hundred times bigger, for seventy thousand homes, so from 2027 your chatbot could heat a
flat in Helsinki.

- **Throughline question:** "Where do you put a machine this hot?" (the topic's own question). Lines 1-5 raise it
  (even freezing space can't take the heat of four chips, and a full cabinet is forty kettles), lines 6-8 answer it
  the usual way (somewhere cold and strange), line 9 turns it (put it where people need the heat), and lines 10-12
  land it on the core line.
- **The turn (line 9):** "It keeps the neighbors warm." The oddest place on the list is the one that doesn't fight
  its heat: since 2014 the bunker has sent it into Stockholm's heating pipes. Everything before line 9 is about
  getting rid of heat; everything after it is about keeping it.
- **The one joke (line 4):** "It's just Google's little chatbot." The letdown: a computer in orbit, the most high-tech
  picture there is, and all it runs is a little chatbot (a small Gemma model on four chips), and even that
  overheats. It also does story work: it ties the satellite to "your chatbot" and sets up the full cabinet's forty
  kettles.
- **Cold retell (the writer's own, for the critic to beat):** a computer in space keeps overheating because there's
  no air to carry the heat away, and it's only a little chatbot; a full AI cabinet is forty kettles. So data centers
  go inside a mountain, under the sea, even into a nuclear bunker under a Stockholm park, and that one heats its
  neighbors. Helsinki wants to go a hundred times bigger, seventy thousand homes, so your chatbot could heat a flat in
  Helsinki.
- **Ad or news?** News: what happened (a computer in orbit overheats), then why it matters (AI's heat, your chatbot,
  the homes it could warm). No product and no sell; Google enters at ~12 s as the test's owner, teased, not pitched.

1. **Lines 2-3, the space paradox** (a "wait, seriously?" fact that experts love to correct): "Space is freezing!"

core line ("finally, a use for my chatbot"). Open loop: line 1's "why would it overheat?" closes at line 3, and the
bigger "so where do you put them?" closes at line 12. The loop: the last frame's radiator glow becomes frame 0's
