## 2. Diagnosis (v3 G5b, then ref 1 / v1)
1. **No through-line.** 13 plain cuts across 6 places keep 0.085 of the pixels across a cut (0.53 / 0.19); the price tag is
   off screen 17.9 s straight while the cause is told; "you" is drawn five ways and strangers' backs read as you. The board
   planned it: "cut (on 'X')" at every join, "change size and angle at every cut", three continuous script lines cut up.
2. **No hierarchy.** The subject was as strong as in the references; the world was louder: environment contrast 11.3 (6.6 /
   6.1), its line coverage 11.6 % (6 / 6), 10x their saturated pixels, 25 movers per 0.5 s (17 / 11), the story 22 % of the
   motion (60 / 34). Pip beat the subject per pixel in 12 of 13 panels; line weight followed depth, not story role.
3. **A rough pen.** 3.4-6.2x the strokes and 10-27x the near-miss ends of the G4 frame: the sketch pen (jitter, overshoot,
   broken lines, open loops, offset fills, one near-black ink) became the film's line; G4 passed with it on 1/4 of the strokes.
4. **Light without logic.** Pools darker than the snow they light; the night room brightest at the top (already in the G4
   frame); halos as opaque discs; no kit file draws a cast or contact shadow; no key light outdoors.

## 3. Verdict on sketch-first: it depends on what the sketch decides; the way v3 did it, it hurt
The sketch did its one job (it locked the story cheaply) but locked the wrong things: plain cuts by rule (G3 §4's default), no
thread object, no tiers, no light rig; and its pen became the film's. Blank paper flattered every check (carry 0.41 on the
board); G5b then invented after the lock (a new world per chart, 25 movers per 0.5 s) and carry fell to 0.085. Sketch-first
is right only if the sketch decides flow, focus and light, and only its pen is thrown away.
