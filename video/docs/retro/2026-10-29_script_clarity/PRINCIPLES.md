## The principles (draft 1)
1. Tell it like news you'd pass to a friend (line 1, the owner's model).
2. Every thing gets its everyday name, the one regular people use, and keeps it all film.
3. Introduce a thing before you point at it; every "it", "they", "this", "that" has one obvious target, or say the noun.
4. Say the plain thought itself, as the typical case: who does what, where.
5. One new thing per sentence, hooked onto what the last sentence ended on.
6. Literal words for a worldwide ear: no idioms, slang or wordplay to decode.
7. The one joke is the plain situation said kindly: one step, no outrage.
8. Numbers and comparisons by ear: one per sentence, round, tied to an everyday thing, saying what is alike.
9. The cold ear decides: regular people hear it once and say each line back; a snag is a finding, a score is not.
