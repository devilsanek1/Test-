# Video 3, G0.5: the core line

Written 2026-10-25 by the editor-in-chief. Inputs: 52 lines (6 levers, 3 draft lines and the owner's line), 4 judges,
a 3-person viewer panel and two sceptics per finalist. Facts: `research/factlock.md` (FL) and `research/widen.md` (W).
Nothing is committed.

## 1. Pick one

**A (recommended). "Don't use AI? It's still raising laptop prices."** Lever: unfairness. 8 words.
- Frame 0 / cover, ALL CAPS, 8 words: **DON'T USE AI? IT'S STILL RAISING LAPTOP PRICES**. Keep STILL: without it, the caps line can read as "Don't use AI, it's raising laptop prices", i.e. your own AI use causes it, which we never say.
- Hook seed, 0-2 s: "Don't use AI? It's STILL raising laptop prices." [dry, indignant on their behalf, quick; beat after the question; lean on STILL]. By ~5 s: "AI data centers are buying up the memory chips."
- Emotion: "that's not fair, how?" You pay more because of something you never chose. It pulls in the big crowd of AI sceptics.
- Why: 70.0/90 with the top gut (8) and share (8.3) scores. #1 for 3 of 4 judges, top 5 for all 4. It's the only line in all 3 viewers' top 5. Maria and Dev would stop and share, and nobody called it bait.
- Sceptics: minor x2, couldn't refute it. Fix applied: the old frame 0 "YOUR LAPTOP STILL PAYS" (it read as the laptop you own, with no price) is gone. "Laptop prices" also keeps it off the power-bill story.
- Risk: "AI" read as AI features in your laptop that you pay for and never use. The frame-0 drawing and the ~5 s line must show data centers.
- Truth status: TRUE (fact-checked 2026-10-25). Demand from AI data centers is the main reason memory chips cost about 5x a year earlier (FL 4.1, 4.5), and that is pushing laptop prices up (FL 1.1, 3.1, 5.2) whether or not you use AI. With no number, it holds worldwide. It doesn't claim "only" (tariffs and CPU price rises play a part) or that AI users cause it, as long as STILL stays on frame 0.

**B. "AI is making your next laptop pricier."** Lever: personal loss (the owner's line, made true). 7 words.
- Frame 0 / cover, ALL CAPS, 7 words: **AI IS MAKING YOUR NEXT LAPTOP PRICIER** (the voice says the same words).
- Hook seed: "AI is making your NEXT laptop pricier." [plain, a touch incredulous, news about them; lean on NEXT]. Then: "AI data centers are buying up the memory chips it needs."
- Emotion: dread about a bill you can't avoid, plus "what does AI have to do with my laptop?"
- Why: the top score, 70.8, tied with L5 (the same line), with truth 8.5 and clarity 9. Top 5 for all 4 judges and #1 for the story judge. But viewers found it flat: only Maria would stop. Jayden: "swipe". Dev: "kind of flat".
- Sceptics: minor x4, couldn't refute it. It has no number, so it holds worldwide. I considered "AI DATA CENTERS ARE MAKING..." but it's 8 words and slower, so the drawing does that job instead.
- Risk: a plain headline with no twist. The frame-0 image has to bring the jolt.
- Truth status: TRUE (fact-checked 2026-10-25). Laptops cost more because AI demand drove up memory chip prices (FL 1.1, 4.1, 4.5, 5.2), and "next" holds: memory prices are still climbing (FL 4.3), the cheap models go first (FL 5.1, 5.3), and experts don't expect relief before 2027-28 (FL 6.2). It compares with the same laptop a year ago, so never add "than your last one".

**C. "You're losing a bidding war to AI."** Lever: the mechanism (the video's own twist). 7 words.
- Frame 0 / cover, ALL CAPS, 7 words: **YOU'RE LOSING A BIDDING WAR TO AI**, drawn over a laptop on the auction block with its price tag climbing.
- Hook seed: "You're LOSING a bidding war to AI, for the memory chips your next laptop needs." [dry, half-amused, mildly bad news to a friend]
- Emotion: the sting of losing a contest you didn't know you were in, plus "wait, what war?"
- Why: 66.5 (#7) and top 5 for 3 of 4 judges. Payoff is 8: the auction is the video's "oh", so the payoff is built in.
- Sceptics: minor x2. Fix applied: the voice names the prize in the same breath.
- Risk: all 3 viewers called it clickbait ("for what?"). On its own, "losing to AI" reads as a story about jobs. Clarity 6.8.
- Truth status: TRUE as a metaphor, but only with the hook's second half (fact-checked 2026-10-25). AI data centers do outbid laptop makers for memory chips and get served first (FL 4.5). "Losing" must mean paying more (FL 4.1) and the cheap models dropping out (FL 5.1, 5.3), never that your laptop gets no memory or less of it (W 1). You don't bid yourself; your laptop brand does and passes the cost on (FL 4.1). The cover alone, with no prize named, misleads (jobs), so the drawing must name the prize.

**The owner's line,** "AI is making your PC more expensive" (L1), came 9th (65.3). All 4 judges' fixes turned it into B. "Your PC" means the one you already own, whose price didn't change. "PC" also lost Maria ("we have a laptop") and Dev ("for gamers"), though it was Jayden's #1. The owner's instinct is the right core: B is their line with two words changed, and A is their message with the hook built in.

**Recommendation: A.** It keeps the owner's message and adds the unfairness that makes people stop and share. It's the judges' #1 and the only line all 3 viewers put in their top 5. B is the safe fallback.

## 2. Per pick: callback, what the script must prove, best draft

**Rules for all three.**
- Frame 0 shows a data center (a server tower scooping up memory chips) next to the laptop, and the voice says "AI data centers" by about 5 s. That rules out the "AI features" misreading.
- Any number gets "in the US" if it's US-only (FL 1.1).
- Never put "5x" next to "laptop": the chips went up about 5x, while US computer prices are up about 10% (FL 1.1; the average new US PC +12%, FL 5.2).
- Never say "only" or "all because of AI", "every", "double", "record", "ever", or "AI is raising prices on everything" (FL 1.2, 1.5).
- No villain: makers sell to whoever pays most (FL 4.5).

**A. "Don't use AI? It's still raising laptop prices."**
- Callback (last line): "You don't have to use AI. You just have to need memory." This only works if "memory chips" has been said twice by then. A plainer alternative: "...You just have to need a laptop."
- Must prove, in this order:
  1. The link, right away: AI data centers are buying up the memory chips laptops need (FL 4.5). This answers "how, if I don't use it?" and kills the AI-features reading.
  2. Laptops really cost more: in the US, computers cost almost 10% more than a year ago, the biggest jump in at least 15 years, after 20 years of getting cheaper apart from one pandemic bump (FL 1.1, 1.3, 1.6).
  3. Why those chips: AI data centers need huge amounts (one big AI rack holds as much memory as about 1,300 laptops; W 9, our arithmetic) and pay the most, so makers sell to them first (FL 4.5). The chips cost about 5x a year earlier (FL 4.1).
  4. How it reaches someone who never uses AI: memory and storage are almost 40% of a PC's parts cost, against about 15% normally (FL 3.1). Your laptop brand bids for you and passes the cost on (FL 4.1). It's less a shortage than an auction (FL 4.8).
  5. Payoff: the cheap ones go first (FL 5.1, 5.3), and experts don't expect it to ease before 2027-28 (FL 6.2). Then the callback.
  - Never say "AI users are causing it", "you're paying for AI" or anything about the power bill.
- Best draft: `script/draft_retention.md`. Its body is the cleanest cause chain (price, then 5x chips, then data centers, then "laptop makers bid for you... deeper pockets"), and that chain is exactly the answer to "how am I paying?". Swap H1 for the core line, move "AI data centers" up to about 4 s, and replace "Maybe be nice to your old laptop" with the callback.

**B. "AI is making your next laptop pricier."**
- Callback: "That's how a data center ends up on your laptop's price tag." It closes the "what does AI have to do with my laptop?" gap that the line opens.
- Must prove, in this order:
  1. It's real: in the US, computers cost almost 10% more than a year ago, after 20 years of getting cheaper apart from one pandemic bump (FL 1.1, 1.6). Optional: the average new US PC passed $1,000 for the first time (FL 5.2).
  2. What AI has to do with it: it's mostly the memory chips, which are almost 40% of a PC's parts cost against about 15% normally (FL 3.1), and cost about 5x a year earlier (FL 4.1).
  3. Why: AI data centers need mountains of memory (W 9) and makers sell to them first because they pay more (FL 4.5). It's less a shortage than an auction (FL 4.8).
  4. Why "next": memory prices are still climbing (FL 4.3), the cheap ones go first (FL 5.1, 5.3), and experts expect supply to stay tight into 2027-28 (FL 6.1, 6.2).
  - Never say "than your last one" (on a quality-adjusted basis, 2026 prices are about back at their 2021 level).
- Best draft: `script/draft_personal.md`. It's second person and built on the viewer's own replacement laptop, so "your next laptop" is already its spine. Put the core line in its frame 0 (replacing "AI outbid you for your laptop's memory") and in H1. B3-B6 already cover steps 2-3.

**C. "You're losing a bidding war to AI."**
- Callback: "Next time you hit 'add to cart,' you're kind of bidding against a data center." The cart pop then loops back to frame 0.
- Must prove, in this order:
  1. The prize, in the hook breath: the memory chips your next laptop needs.
  2. You're losing: in the US, computers cost almost 10% more than a year ago after 20 years of getting cheaper, apart from one pandemic bump (FL 1.1, 1.6, 1.7), and memory chips cost about 5x a year earlier (FL 4.1).
  3. Who's bidding: AI data centers. They pay the most, so they get served first (FL 4.5), and the scale is huge (W 9).
  4. How you're in it: your laptop brand does the bidding and you pay at checkout (FL 4.1). It's less a shortage than an auction (FL 4.8).
  5. What losing means: the cheap ones drop out first (FL 5.1, 5.3), and experts expect the squeeze to run into 2027-28 (FL 6.1, 6.2).
  - Never say "or you get less" in the sense of spec cuts (W 1).
- Best draft: `script/draft_auction.md`. Its H1 ("You're losing an auction you didn't know you were in") is this line's twin, and its cart-pop loop is the callback. Swap the frame 0 and H1, and keep the body.
