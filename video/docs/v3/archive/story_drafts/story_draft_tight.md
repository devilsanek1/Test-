# Video 3, G1: story draft (tight editor)

Draft, 2026-10-26. Not committed. Written from scratch on the "then / now" spine (the laptop deal that flipped),
with the judges' grafts. Core line (locked): **AI is making your next laptop pricier.**

## The story in one breath (the retell test)

Laptops used to keep getting better for the same money, but this past year in the US computers went up almost 10%,
because your laptop runs on memory chips and AI data centers need mountains of them. They pay more, so they get first
dibs, so those chips cost about five times what they did and laptop makers pass that on. And since chip prices are still
climbing, waiting a year probably won't help. That's how an AI data center ends up on your next laptop's price tag.

- **Throughline (one question):** how are AI data centers making my next laptop pricier, when laptops always got better for the money?
- **Situation:** the viewer is about to buy a laptop and still counts on the old deal: wait a bit and you get more for the same money.
- **Turn (line 5):** your laptop and an AI data center want the same thing, memory chips, and the data center wants mountains of them.
- **Payoff (line 9):** the price-tag callback. "This time, waiting a year probably won't help" (line 8) flips the old deal from line 3, so NEXT is earned without saying "next laptop" twice.

**3. but** (that's backwards from how laptops always went)

**4. but** (the kink)

**6. so** (same sellers, so the bigger payer goes first)
[easy shrug, plain business talk, no bad guy in your voice]
They pay more, so they get FIRST DIBS.
- Picture: three chip factories load the data center's trucks first while the laptop-factory trucks wait their turn.
- Fact: FL 4.5 VERIFIED (makers put higher-priced AI and server memory first). Normal business, no villain. "First dibs" is the owner's phrase.

**7. which means**
[back to normal pace, matter-of-fact, number clear, don't oversell it]
Which means those chips cost about 5 TIMES last year's price, and laptop makers pass that on.
- Picture: a "5x" tag swings off the memory chip (never on the laptop), and an arrow carries a slice of it onto the laptop's tag.
- Fact: FL 4.1 VERIFIED (DRAM costs ~5x y/y, Q2 2026, Gartner, passed on to buyers). Optional on screen: FL 3.1 (memory and storage almost 40% of a PC's parts cost, normally ~15%; never "RAM is 40%").

**8. but** (unlike the old deal)
[warmer and honest, a little rueful, talking to one person]
And THIS time, waiting a year probably won't help, because chip prices are still climbing.
- Picture: a calendar page flips, and the wall line keeps climbing past today as a dotted line toward a "2027-28" marker.
- Fact: FL 4.3 VERIFIED forecasts (still rising, more slowly). On screen with the dotted line, FL 6.2 REPORTED in soft wording: "Experts don't expect it to ease before 2027-28" (FL 6.1 VERIFIED: Micron says tighter first). "Probably" because of the FL 6.3 dissent.

**9. so** (payoff)
[soft and low-key, like a friend wrapping up, no mic drop]
That's how an AI data center ends up on your NEXT laptop's price tag.
- Picture: back at the desk, the new laptop's tag turns over on its string to show a tiny inked data center on its back. Hard cut to frame 0 for the loop.
- Fact: the CORE_LINE s1 callback; it sums up FL 4.1 + 4.5. No advice, no "than your last one".

## Notes for the merge and the fact check

- **The owner's locked line 2** ("AI data centers get first dibs on the memory chips your laptop needs") no longer sits
  in slot 2. In slot 2 it would answer the hook's question at 4 s and leave nothing to pull the viewer forward. Its words
  live on in lines 5-6 ("your laptop runs on memory chips", "first dibs"). The owner said "start from scratch", but they
  should confirm this move.
- **The one joke** is line 3 (buyer's remorse). Every other line is said straight.
- **Line 3 has no "usually".** It describes the annoying feeling, not a yearly statistic, and the chart on screen shows
  the 2021 bump. If the fact check wants the hedge, use "next year's was usually better" (+1 word).
- **Line 8 edges toward advice** ("waiting a year probably won't help"). It is hedged, and it rests on VERIFIED
  forecasts plus the 2027-28 range on screen. It is the line that flips the old deal from line 3, so I kept it. Softer fallback:
  "And this time, it probably won't sort itself out by next year, because chip prices are still climbing."
- **Fixes from the judges' notes:**
  - no "new customer showed up" (it squashed the timeline);
  - "memory chips", never "the exact same chips";
  - no "less a shortage than an auction";
  - one payoff line with "next laptop" said once;
  - the price line drawn on the cozy wall, not as a slide chart;
  - the 5x said as "last year's price" and kept on the chip.
- **Sound:** a soft positive phone-style ding as the price tag pops on frame 0 (playbook: one in the first second).
