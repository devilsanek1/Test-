# Video 3, G1: story draft (wit writer)

Draft, 2026-10-26. Not committed. Written from scratch on the "then / now" spine (the laptop deal that flipped), with
the judges' grafts. Core line (locked): **AI is making your next laptop pricier.**

## The story in one breath (the retell test)

Laptops used to get better for the same money, usually right after you bought one. But this past year, in the US,
computers went up almost 10%. That's because your laptop runs on memory chips, and so do AI data centers, just
mountains of them. They pay more, so they get first dibs, and the chips left over cost about five times what they did.
Normally you'd wait for the better one, but experts expect this to last at least another year. That's how an AI data
center ends up on your next laptop's price tag.

- **Throughline (one question):** what do AI data centers have to do with the laptop I'm about to buy? Line 2 opens it.
  Lines 3-4 sharpen it, because laptops weren't just getting a bit pricier, they reversed twenty years of getting
  better for the money. Lines 5-7 answer it, and line 9 closes it.
- **Situation:** the viewer's old laptop is on its last legs and they're about to buy the next one. They still count
  on the old deal: hang on a bit and you get more laptop for the same money.
- **The one joke (line 3):** buyer's remorse. A better one for the same money usually turned up right after you bought
  yours. Everyone has felt it, it comes out of the situation, and it sets up the flip.
- **Turn (line 5):** your laptop runs on memory chips, and so do AI data centers, just mountains of them. The viewer sees
  that the two are after the same thing, so "what does AI have to do with my laptop?" answers itself.
- **Flip (line 8):** the joke comes back. Waiting for the better one used to be the smart move, but experts expect the
  squeeze to last at least another year. That is what earns NEXT.
- **Payoff (line 9):** one closer, the price-tag callback, with "next laptop" said only once.

## The script, line by line

**1. (first)**
[bright and casual, a real question to a friend, then plain news with a tiny wince]
Buying a laptop soon? AI's making it pricier.
- Picture: frame 0, text AI IS MAKING YOUR NEXT LAPTOP PRICIER. A cozy desk at dusk, the old laptop with a taped hinge
  and a roaring fan, and the new one's paper price tag popping up on its string (soft ding, our own sound). Behind the
  desk, an AI data center fills the window, and a server tower scoops up memory chips.
- Fact: the core line is TRUE (CORE_LINE s1: FL 1.1, 4.1, 4.5, 5.2). "Next" rests on FL 4.3, 6.1 and 6.2, and "soon" makes
  the laptop in question the viewer's next one. There's no number, so it holds worldwide. Never "than your last one".

**2. but** (a self-correction: which AI)
[quick self-correction, like you're being precise, tiny pause first]
Well… AI DATA CENTERS are.
- Picture: the camera drifts toward the window as the data center's lights come on, row by row.
- Fact: framing only. "AI data centers" is heard by about 4 s, which rules out the "AI features in my laptop" misreading.

**3. but** (that's backwards from how laptops always went) **THE ONE JOKE**
[dry and knowing, a touch nostalgic, then deadpan on the tail, don't sell it]
Which is weird, because laptops used to get better for the same money… usually right after you bought one.
- Picture: on the wallpaper above the desk, a pencil line of little price tags runs downhill from 2005 to 2026, with one
  small 2021 bump, labelled "US computer prices, for the same computing power". At one tag, a tiny ink version of the
  viewer unboxes a laptop, and a shinier one slides onto the shelf beside it with the same price. The viewer stares at it.
- Fact: FL 1.6 VERIFIED ("kept getting cheaper", one pandemic bump, so the chart keeps the 2021 bump). FL 1.8/1.9: the index
  is quality-adjusted, so "better for the same money" is exactly what it measures. The tail is the joke, a feeling,
  not a claim. Never "every year" (FL 1.7). "Twenty years" lives on the chart and is not spoken.

**4. but** (the kink)
[straight and level, a flicker of disbelief, let the number land]
But this past year, in the US, computers went UP… almost 10%.
- Picture: at "today" the wall line kinks sharply up, and the new laptop's tag on the desk ticks up a notch. Small label:
  "+9.7%, US, Sept 2026 vs a year earlier".
- Fact: FL 1.1 VERIFIED (+9.7% y/y, Sept 2026, BLS), with "in the US" said aloud and "computers", not "laptops", since the
  series is computers. Optional on screen: FL 1.3 "biggest jump in at least 15 years". Never "record" or "ever".

**5. because** (THE TURN: what changed at the kink)
[leaning in, a notch slower, letting them in on it, a little awed at the end]
Because your laptop runs on memory chips, and so do AI data centers… just MOUNTAINS of them.
- Picture: push into the laptop's open back to one tiny memory chip, then pull all the way out through the window. Scale
  shot from low down, the desk lamp in the foreground: one AI server rack towers over a heap of about 1,300 laptops.
  Footnote: "1 AI rack ≈ the memory of ~1,300 laptops (our math: 20.7 TB ÷ 16 GB)".
- Fact: W 9 VERIFIED (GB300 NVL72: ~20.7 TB HBM plus ~17 TB LPDDR5X, the same type as many thin laptops). "So do AI data
  centers" claims only that they run on memory chips too, never "the exact same chips". The 1,300 is our own arithmetic,
  shown on screen only.

**6. so** (both want the same chips, so the bigger payer goes first)
[easy shrug, plain business, no bad guy in your voice]
They pay more, so they get FIRST DIBS.
- Picture: three chip factories along a road load the data center's big trucks first. The laptop maker's little van
  idles at the back of the line. On screen: "3 companies make almost 9 in 10 of the world's memory chips".
- Fact: FL 4.5 VERIFIED (makers put higher-priced AI and server memory first). FL 4.7 VERIFIED for the screen line.
  Normal business, no villain. "First dibs" is the owner's G2 phrase.

**7. which means** (the kink, explained in one short sentence)
[back to normal pace, matter-of-fact, the number clear, don't oversell it]
Which means the chips left over cost about 5 TIMES last year's price.
- Picture: the van finally gets one small crate with a "5x" tag swinging off it (the tag stays on the crate, never on the
  laptop). The crate goes into the laptop, and the laptop's price tag ticks up again.
- Fact: FL 4.1 VERIFIED (DRAM costs ~5x y/y in Q2 2026, Gartner, passed on to buyers). "Left over" follows from FL 4.5
  (output increasingly going to AI servers) and FL 4.8 (it's a shortage), and gives no quantity. Optional on screen:
  FL 3.1 (memory and storage almost 40% of a PC's parts cost, normally ~15%; never "RAM is 40%").

**8. but** (the flip: the old move doesn't work right now)
[warmer, a little rueful, like breaking mild bad news to a friend]
NORMALLY you'd wait for the better one, but experts expect this to last at least another year.
- Picture: back at the wall, the shelf spot where the shinier laptop popped up in line 3 stays empty. The line keeps
  climbing past "today" as a dotted line toward a "2027-28" marker. On screen: "Experts don't expect it to ease before
  2027-28".
- Fact: FL 6.2 REPORTED, so it's soft ("expect", never "will"; FL 6.3 dissent). FL 6.1 VERIFIED (Micron: 2027 and 2028
  tighter than 2026). "At least another year" is the low end of the range (Gartner: late 2027 at the earliest). FL 4.3
  VERIFIED (prices still climbing) supports the dotted line. No advice: it describes the old habit and the forecast.

**9. so** (payoff)
[soft and low-key, like a friend wrapping up, no mic drop]
That's how an AI data center ends up on your NEXT laptop's price tag.
- Picture: back at the desk, the new laptop's tag turns over on its string to show a tiny inked data center on its
  back. Hard cut to frame 0 for the loop.
- Fact: the CORE_LINE s1 callback, summing up FL 4.1 + 4.5. No new claim, no advice, no "than your last one".

## Notes for the merge and the fact check

- **Why it's a story, not a list.** Every line is the answer to the viewer's next thought:
  - "AI?" leads to "which AI" (2);
  - "that's weird" leads to "how it used to go" (3);
  - "but now" brings the kink (4);
  - "why?" gets the shared chips (5);
  - "who gets them?" gets first dibs (6);
  - "so what?" gets the 5x (7);
  - "can't I just wait?" gets the forecast (8);
  - and it closes (9).

  Facts appear only when the story needs them, so the cheap-models-go-first and 40% facts are left off the voice on
  purpose.
- **The owner's locked line 2** ("AI data centers get first dibs on the memory chips your laptop needs") is no longer in
  slot 2. In slot 2 it answers the hook's question at 4 s, so nothing pulls the viewer forward. Its pieces live on in
  lines 5-6. If the owner wants it word for word, this swap for lines 5-6 keeps the chain intact (24 words, one fewer):
  "Because AI data centers get FIRST DIBS on the memory chips your laptop needs. They pay more… and they need MOUNTAINS
  of them." It's weaker, because it states the answer before the click. The owner said "start from scratch", but they
  should confirm.
- **The one joke** is the tail of line 3. Everything else is said straight, including "first dibs" (a shrug, not a gag).
- **Line 8 brushes against advice.** It describes the old habit and quotes a soft forecast, and never says to buy or
  wait. If the fact check wants it softer, here's a fallback (17 words): "And normally you'd wait for the better one, but
  experts don't expect this to ease for at least a year."
- **"Which is weird"** (line 3) briefly attaches to "AI data centers are", and the "because" clause settles it within a
  second. Swap in "Which is backwards" if the listen-through trips on it (same length).
- **Judges' fixes:**
  - no "new customer showed up" (it squashed the timeline);
  - "so do AI data centers" rather than "the exact same chips";
  - no "less a shortage than an auction";
  - the throughline cut to one question;
  - one closer with "next laptop" said once;
  - the joke and the history in one breath;
  - the price line drawn on the cozy wall with its 2021 bump and a US label;
  - the 5x said as "last year's price" and kept on the crate;
  - two spoken numbers, with "twenty years" and "2027-28" on screen only.
- **Sound:** our own soft notification-style ding as the price tag pops on frame 0 (playbook: one in the first second).
