# Video 3 script: "The auction you didn't know you were in" (core line B)

Draft, 2026-10-25. Not committed. Built on the locked core line **"AI is making your next laptop pricier."**
(`../CORE_LINE.md` section 1, pick B, and section 2 "B."). Angle: metaphor first. The core line opens the gap "what does
AI have to do with my laptop?", and one image closes it: an auction you didn't know you were in. The cart tap is your
bid, the memory chip is the lot, an AI data center is the giant bidder and your laptop brand bids for you. Narrator: a
dry, warm smart friend. Facts: `../research/factlock.md` (FL) and `../research/widen.md` (W) only. The voice is American
English, so it says "data centers". Grafted from `draft_auction.md` (paddles, shortage-shelf reveal, "give or take a
pandemic") and `draft_personal.md` (the cozy desk nook).

## At a glance

- **Frame-0 text (all hooks):** **AI IS MAKING YOUR NEXT LAPTOP PRICIER**, in bold ink caps in the upper-middle third,
  well clear of the top edge (no paper edge at the top).
- **Frame-0 image (all hooks, mid-action):** desk height, slightly low. In the middle there's a shiny new laptop on a
  small wooden auction block, with a paper price tag hanging from its hinge. Right beside it, almost touching, stands an
  AI data center: a tall server tower with rows of blinking lights and a small brass plate reading "AI DATA CENTER".
  The right edge crops it, so it reads as huge, and its paddle is already halfway up. In the bottom-left foreground, the
  viewer's thumb is pressing a green "Add to cart" under the same laptop on their phone. The data center is drawn as a
  calm giant, not a monster.
- **The jolt** (the core line is plain, so it has to come from three places):
  1. the data center standing right next to the laptop on frame 0;
  2. the happy cart pop, followed 0.4 s later by the laptop's tag flipping up;
  3. line 2, "You're bidding against AI data centers, and losing", which says "AI data centers" at about 4 s.
- **Length:**

  | Hook | Spoken words | Estimated length at 160-170 wpm plus planned beats |

  At 175 wpm, each comes in about 2 s shorter.
- **Recommended hook: H1.** The voice and frame 0 say the same seven words, which makes it the clearest. H3 is the A/B

## The first second: sound

- **What and where:** `phone_cart_add` (our own, 0.41 s) at **0.07 s** (frame 2 at 30 fps), exactly as the thumb
  presses "Add to cart".
- **What follows:** at 0.45 s the data center's paddle reaches the top, and the laptop's tag flips up with a small tick
  (`card_flip`). The tag shows an up arrow and blurred, climbing digits, never an invented price. The voice comes in at
  0.45 s, on the pop's tail. The music is almost silent until line 2.
- **Why it fits:**
  - It's the happiest sound in online shopping, and in this story that tap *is* your bid, so the central metaphor
    starts on frame 0 before a word is said.
  - The good sound followed by the tag going up is the jolt that the plain line lacks.
  - The image is the same under all three hooks, so the sound works with each of them.
  - The last shot goes back to the thumb over the same button, so the loop closes on the same pop.

## Rules check

- **Length:** 111 / 112 / 114 spoken words (H1 / H2 / H3), about 43-44.5 s at 160-170 wpm. The body is the same after
  every hook.
- **Frame 0:** the core line as text, mid-action, with the data center next to the laptop. No greeting, no intro, no "I
  started reading".
- **"AI data centers":**
  - Spoken at about 4 s with H1, about 4.5 s with H2 and about 5.2 s with H3.
  - The "AI DATA CENTER" plaque is on screen from frame 0, so "AI" can't be read as AI features.
- **Proof order:**
  1. It's real (L3-L4).
  2. What AI has to do with it: mostly the memory chips, about 5x a year earlier, and almost 40% of a PC's parts cost
     on screen (L5).
  3. Why: data centers need mountains and pay more, so they get first dibs, less of a shortage than an auction (L6-L7).
  4. Why "next": still climbing, the cheap ones go first, tight into 2027-28 (L8).

  Then the callback (L9).
- **Banned wording:**
  - The voice never says "every", "ever", "only", "all because of AI", "double", "record", "than your last one" or
    "AI is raising prices on everything".
  - "5x" never sits next to "laptop", in the voice or on screen.
- **US flags:** "In the US" is in the voice and on the tag in L3, and "US" is on the sub-$500 chip in L8.
- **No villains:**
  - The data center is a calm giant and the auctioneer is neutral.
  - "They pay more, so they get first dibs" is read as a shrug.
  - The laptop brand is on your side.
- **AI-tell pass:**
  - There's one contrast line (L7), and no other "X, not Y" template.
  - No "here's the thing", "turns out", triplets, colon reveals, self-answered questions or em-dashes.
  - The connectives are so, and, which.
- **Humour:** one named joke ("give or take a pandemic"). The rest is dry wit from the situation: "and losing",
  "first dibs", "your laptop brand bids for you".
- **Ending:** it ends on the payoff plus the callback, with no "follow for more", and the last tap loops into frame 0.
