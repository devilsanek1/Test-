# Video 3 script draft: "The auction you didn't know you were in"

Draft, 2026-10-25. Angle: metaphor first, with one image carrying the whole video: an auction where your "add to cart" tap
is a bid, the memory chip is the lot, an AI data centre is the giant bidder, and your laptop brand bids for you.
Narrator: dry, warm, a smart friend. Not committed.

- **Frame 0 text (7 words):** **AI is outbidding you for memory chips.**
- **Sound in the first second:** our own "added to cart" pop at **0.25 s** (frame 8 at 30 fps). Frame 0 opens with the
  thumb already pressing "Add to cart", and the pop lands as the thumb lifts and the button turns green. The VO starts
  about 150 ms after the pop's tail, so the pop is heard clean. **Why it fits:** it's the small happy shopping sound
  everyone knows, and the whole story turns that exact tap into a bid. The last line goes back to the same tap and the
  same pop, so the loop closes on frame 0. It's our own sound, never a store's real one.
- **Length:** 106 spoken words with hook 1 (108 with hook 2, 107 with hook 3). At 165-170 wpm plus about 2.2 s of
  planned pauses, that's **about 40 s**.

## Cued script (ElevenLabs paste, with hook 1)

```
[dry, half-amused, quick, slight smile, like breaking mildly bad news to a friend; start right after the cart pop]
You're LOSING an auction you didn't KNOW you were in.

[easy storyteller, normal pace; the aside quicker, lower and drier; the one joke, don't sell it]
For twenty years, computers kept getting CHEAPER… give or take a pandemic.

[straight and level, slower on the number, every word clean, no smile]
Now, in the US, they're up almost TEN percent in a year.

[matter-of-fact, explaining to a friend, a touch quicker, a small shrug, no blame]
A lot of that's the MEMORY chips, which go to whoever pays the MOST.

[lower and slower, understated, eyebrows up as the giant rises]
Right now… that's AI data centers.

[leaning in, letting them in on it, a bit quieter; knowing half-smile on "auction"]
Everyone calls it a shortage… but it's more of an AUCTION.

[deadpan, even, dry, no wink in the voice]
Your laptop brand does the bidding… and YOU pay at checkout.

[honest and straight, a little more serious, unhurried; don't go gloomy or newsy]
So the CHEAP laptops drop out first, and the squeeze could last into twenty twenty-eight.

[back to easy and warm, small shrug, light smile, trail off softly]
Next time you hit "add to cart," you're kind of BIDDING against a data center.
```

## Plain version (captions and timing)

You're losing an auction you didn't know you were in. For twenty years, computers kept getting cheaper, give or take a
pandemic. Now, in the US, they're up almost ten percent in a year. A lot of that's the memory chips, which go to whoever
pays the most. Right now, that's AI data centers. Everyone calls it a shortage, but it's more of an auction. Your laptop
brand does the bidding, and you pay at checkout. So the cheap laptops drop out first, and the squeeze could last into
2028. Next time you hit "add to cart," you're kind of bidding against a data center.

## Sound notes

- 0.25 s: the "added to cart" pop (our own: a soft, rounded two-step pop with a tiny bright tick).
- L3: a dry chalk squeak as the line snaps up. L5: a low, soft swell under the tilt, felt more than heard. L6: one gavel
  knock on "auction". L7: a little receipt-printer chatter. L9: the same cart pop as frame 0.
- Music: cinematic but subtle, almost nothing under the hook; the voice is always on top.

## Rules check

- Frame 0 is the payoff in words (7 words, true per 4.5), starting mid-action. No greeting, no intro.
- Every number is on a VERIFIED line. The only US figure (+9.7%) says "in the US" in the VO and on the tag.
- No "every year for 20 years", no "record", no "AI is raising prices on everything", no "not a shortage".
- No villain: the auctioneer is neutral and selling to the top bid is treated as normal ("just how selling works" lives in the cue, not the VO).
- One contrast line only (L6). No "here's the thing", no triplets, no colon reveals, no em-dashes, no "follow for more".
- Wit comes from the situation: "give or take a pandemic" (the one joke), "you pay at checkout", the cheap laptops
  "drop out" of the bidding, and the closing "kind of".
- It ends on the payoff (cheap laptops go first, it could last into 2028, your cart is a bid), and the last tap loops to frame 0.
