# Video 3 script draft: "The wrong-way price alert" (retention cut)

Topic B, "AI is outbidding you for the memory in your laptop". Written 2026-10-25 from the retention editor's angle: a
number going the wrong way on frame 0, a new hook every 5-8 s, one long open loop ("AI outbid you", closed at the twist),
and a last line built for comments and rewatches. Draft only, not committed. Facts: `v3/research/factlock.md` (F) and
`v3/research/widen.md` (W).

## At a glance

- **Frame 0 text (7 words):** **US computer prices: +9.7%. AI outbid you.**
  Small chip under it: "vs a year ago · US CPI, Sept 2026 (BLS)". The claim sits in the upper-middle third, well clear of the top edge.
- **Sound in the first second:** our own two-note **price-alert ding** at **0.10 s** (frame 3 at 30 fps). It's a soft,
  rising glass/marimba ding, about 350 ms long. It lands as a notification card pops in place on the drawn phone (a scale
  bounce from 0.9 to 1.0, with no slide-in from the top).
  - Why it fits: that ding is the little shopping win everyone knows ("the thing you're watching just got cheaper"). Here
    the card shows an up arrow, so the good-news sound delivers the wrong-way number. That mismatch is the hook.
  - The voice starts at 0.35 s, on the ding's tail.
  - It's also the loop. The last frame's heart pop leads straight back into this ding.
  - It's our own sound, never Apple's, Amazon's or a price-tracker app's real one.
- **Length:** 109 spoken words with H1 (112 with H2, 114 with H3). At 165-170 wpm, plus about 2.5 s of planned beats,
  that's about 41-42 s with H1 and about 44 s with H3.
- **Recommended hook: H1.** It's the shortest, it has the most concrete number, and it says "in the US" out loud.

| H2 | Your next laptop will probably cost more, because AI's outbidding you for the memory inside it. | 16 | You vs. AI, a curiosity gap: how does AI bid on *my* laptop? (F4.5) | "Which is weird" = getting pricier; the body then shows how the bidding works. |

With H2 and H3 the US figure stays on screen from frame 0 (the frame-0 chip).

```
[the scale moment; start easy, dry little smile on RIDICULOUS; 250 ms beat after "amount"; then slower, lower, every word clear, lean on THOUSAND; 250 ms after; DON'T use an awe voice or whisper]

[the penny drops; back to normal pace, brighter; lean on AUCTION, 200 ms beat after; "laptop makers bid for you" matter-of-fact, 150 ms; then the one joke, deadpan, faint smile, don't sell it; 200 ms after; DON'T blame anyone, it's normal business]

[soft, affectionate, dry, a smile you can hear; easy pace; lean lightly on NICE; let "laptop" fall gently, no big finish; 300 ms tail, then the heart pop]
Maybe be NICE to your old laptop.
```

The longest gap is about 7.8 s.

**Open loops:**
1. Frame 0 says "AI outbid you". How do you bid on anything? It stays open about 24 s and closes at L5: "Laptop makers bid for you".
2. A chain of short loops, each answered within about 6 s:
   - L2 "weird": why? L3 answers.
   - L3 "five times": why? L4 answers.
   - With H3, "a chip in your laptop": which one? L3 answers.
3. The ding at 0.1 s is a price alert that went the wrong way. On a rewatch it lands as the joke it is.

**Comment and rewatch triggers** (tagged by mechanism):
- L7 + "est. 2017" sticker: comment bait. People post their laptop's age ("mine's from 2014 and held together by tape").
  It's warm, never a "comment below".
- L5 "deeper pockets": a quotable "wait, seriously?" line, and the one joke.
- L4 "over a thousand laptops' worth": a fact people want to send someone.
- L2 chart: PC builders and RAM shoppers pile in with their own prices. The narrator is right (F4.1, W7), so the comment
  fight works for us.
- Loop: L7's heart pop runs into frame 0's ding. Played back, "Maybe be nice to your old laptop. … In the US, computers
  cost almost ten percent more" reads as cause and effect, without a dangling sentence.

## Plain script (captions and timing, H1)

In the US, computers cost almost ten percent more than a year ago. Which is weird, because for twenty years computers
kept getting cheaper, apart from a little pandemic bump. But the memory chips inside cost about five times what they did
a year earlier. Because AI data centers want a ridiculous amount, and one rack can hold over a thousand laptops' worth.
So it's less a shortage than an auction. Laptop makers bid for you, and data centers just have deeper pockets. So if
your laptop dies before Christmas, you'll pay more or get less, and the cheap ones go first. Maybe be nice to your old
laptop.

## Rule check

- **Length and hooks:** 109 / 112 / 114 words, about 41-44 s. The body runs unchanged after all three hooks.
- **Frame 0:** a 7-word true claim, mid-action (the line is already moving and the card pops). No greeting, no intro.
- **First-second sound:** the ding at 0.10 s, positive, our own.
- **Structure:** hook, stakes (the wrong-way contrast and the "why?"), explanation, twist, then the payoff for the viewer.
  It ends about 3 s after the payoff, with no "follow for more".
- **AI-tell pass:**
  - "Less X than Y" is used once; no other contrast template.
  - No "here's the thing", "turns out", self-answered questions, triplets, em-dashes or one-word punches.
  - The connectives are which / because / but / so / and.
- **Facts:**
  - Every line maps to a VERIFIED row (above).
  - The only number in the voice-over that isn't in the factlock is the rack comparison (W9, our arithmetic, stated conservatively).
  - US figures are flagged: H1 says it out loud, and the frame-0 chip, the L2 label and the L6 chip all say "US".
- **No villains.** The data centre is polite and just richer; the makers sell to the highest bidder, which is normal.
  The video never says "every year for 20 years", "record", "ever", "RAM is 40%" or "AI raises prices on everything".
