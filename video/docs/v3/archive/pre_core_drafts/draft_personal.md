# Video 3 script draft: "Bidding against a building" (second-person, personal)

Draft, 2026-10-25. Not committed. This is one of the competing G1 drafts. The angle is second person: it starts with the
viewer's own dying laptop and the Christmas cart, pulls back once to the size of a data center, then comes back to the
same desk. Facts come only from `../research/factlock.md` (FL) and `../research/widen.md` (W). The voice is American
English, so the voiceover says "data centers".

## Frame 0 and the first second

- **Frame-0 text (7 words):** **AI outbid you for your laptop's memory**
  Alt if the owner wants the price in the claim itself: "Laptops cost more because AI outbid you" (7).
- **Frame-0 image (the same for all 3 hooks, mid-action):** the cozy desk nook at night. It's boxed in by a bookshelf on
  the left and a frosty window with Christmas string lights on the right, inside a warm lamp pool. The old laptop has
  tape on its hinge and a flickering "1%" battery. The viewer's thumb is already landing on **Add to cart** for a shiny
  new laptop on their phone.
- **Positive phone sound:** our own soft two-note rising **"added to cart" pop** (about 180 ms) with a tiny tick as the
  cart badge bounces to "1". It lands at **0.10 s (frame 3 at 30 fps)**, exactly when the thumb hits the button. The
  voice comes in at 0.30 s, after the pop's tail. Music stays almost silent under it.
  **Why it fits:** it's the exact moment the viewer is in (buying the replacement), and it's the happiest sound in online
  shopping. At about 1.2 s the price chip ticks up ("US · computers +9.7% vs a year ago"), so the good sound sets up the
  bad news without a word. Since the image is the same under every hook, the pop works with all three.
- **Second positive sound (end):** a soft "like" heart pop at about 41.5 s, when a heart sticker lands on the old
  laptop's lid. It closes the loop back to frame 0.

## Three hooks (the body below works after any of them)

| # | Hook line (spoken) | Mechanism | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 (default) | "Your laptop's dying, and replacing it costs almost ten percent more than a year ago." | Personal, mid-action; the ending "go easy on the old one" loops straight back into it | 15 |
| H2 | "In the US, you're paying almost ten percent more for a computer than a year ago." | Number first, US said out loud; closest to FL 1.1's safe wording | 16 |
| H3 | "There's a chip in your laptop that AI wants more than you do, and you're paying for it." | Curiosity gap; B3 answers it ("it's mostly memory") | 18 |

Every hook claims that computers or laptops cost more. That's what makes the body's first line ("And computers had been
getting cheaper...") follow from all three. The US flag sits on screen under every hook (the hook chip, plus the chart
label in B2).

## Cued script (H1 route)

Times assume 165 wpm plus the pauses written in the cues. At 170 wpm it ends about a second earlier.

**H1 · Hook + stakes · 0.3-5.8 s**
[quick and warm, mid-story, like catching a friend up on their own problem; sympathetic half-smile on "dying"; 120 ms after "dying"; lean on "more"; no news-anchor drop on "a year ago"; 250 ms beat after]
> Your laptop's dying, and replacing it costs almost ten percent MORE than a year ago.

Image (cozy enclosure, frame 0): the desk nook described above. The cart pop, then the price chip ticks up: "US · computers +9.7% vs a year ago (BLS, Sept)".
Fact: FL 1.1 VERIFIED (+9.7% y/y, US CPI). The index is quality-adjusted (FL 1.8-1.9), so "replacing it" means getting the same computing power.

**B2 · Stakes: why it's weird · 6.0-11.5 s**
[half-amused disbelief, a notch slower, like "can you believe this"; lean on "cheaper"; "apart from a little COVID bump" quick and tossed off, no smile-break; 200 ms after]
> And computers had been getting CHEAPER for twenty years, apart from a little COVID bump.

Image: a finger-drawn price line on the fogged window glides downhill from 2005 to 2025, with one small bump at 2021, then hooks sharply up at the end. Label: "US prices, same computing power".
Fact: FL 1.6 VERIFIED. It never says "every year" (FL 1.7 is FALSE).

**B3 · Explanation · 11.7-16.4 s**
[back to normal pace, level and plain, explaining to a friend who doesn't do tech; small lift on "memory"; tiny wide-eyed smile on "tons of it"; 150 ms after]
> This time it's mostly MEMORY, and AI data centers need tons of it.

Image: the camera pushes in through the laptop's cracked hinge to a memory stick glowing inside, tiny as a sugar cube, then pulls out through the window into the night.
Fact: FL 4.1 VERIFIED (DRAM about 5x, passed on to buyers), 3.1 VERIFIED (memory and storage are nearly 40% of a PC's parts cost, against about 15% normally), 4.5 VERIFIED. "Mostly" is there because the Intel CPU hike (FL S.1) and measurement effects (FL 1.9) play a small part too.

**B4 · Explanation, scale · 16.5-21.3 s**
[the scale moment: quieter, a notch slower, a little awed, like looking up at something huge; 100 ms before "thirteen hundred", say it clean, don't boom it; no trailer voice; no smile; 350 ms after so the picture lands]
> One of their server racks has as much memory as thirteen hundred laptops.

Image (**scale moment**): the camera pulls back over the town to a huge data center on the horizon, shot from a low, Arcane-style angle. One lit server rack stands tower-tall beside a tiny laptop, and 1,300 little laptops stack up next to it to match. Footnote: "Nvidia GB300 NVL72: ~20.7 TB GPU memory ÷ 16 GB laptop".
Fact: W §9 VERIFIED (the spec). The arithmetic is ours: about 1,300 laptops for GPU memory alone, about 2,400 with all of it, so the line is conservative.

**B5 · Explanation, why · 21.6-26.0 s**
[normal pace, fair-minded shrug, no blame, like "that's just business"; lean on "first"; 80 ms before "because"; no smile; 150 ms after]
> So the memory makers sell to them FIRST, because they pay more.

Image: a memory factory's loading dock. Crates of glowing chips roll on a conveyor into the data center's giant door, while a small van marked "laptops" at the side gate gets one crate. Workers with clipboards, plain business, no villain faces.
Fact: FL 4.5 VERIFIED (close to its safe wording). No villain.

**B6 · Twist + THE one joke · 26.1-31.2 s**
[THE one joke. "more like an auction" a touch brighter, like it just clicked; 250 ms beat before "and"; "bidding against a building" flat and deadpan with a half-smile you can hear, don't sell it; 350 ms after]
> It's more like an AUCTION than a shortage… and you're bidding against a building.

Image: an auction hall. On one side the data center, drawn as a calm giant (not a monster), raises a huge paddle. On the other, the viewer's hand from the desk nook raises a tiny one. The price board flips "$2.80 → $12 per GB" (Morgan Stanley, quoted by Google, 2025 → 2026). One soft gavel tap on "building".
Fact: FL 4.8. The line keeps the "shortage" (every source uses the word) and adds the auction. The board figure is W §1/§9 VERIFIED.

**B8 · Payoff · 31.8-36.9 s**
[warm and honest, a little closer to the mic, talking to one person; normal pace; no doom, no smile; lean on "cheap"; "going first" a hair slower; 200 ms after]
> So you'll probably pay more this Christmas, and the CHEAP ones are going first.

Image: back home in the nook. On the phone's shop page, the cheapest laptop listings grey out one by one ("no longer available"). Small card: "Worldwide, Jul-Sep 2026: about 1 in 5 fewer PCs shipped".
Fact: FL 1.1 (US prices), 5.1 VERIFIED (US PCs under $500: -18.7% vs -7.0% overall), 5.3 VERIFIED (makers shift to pricier models), 4.3 VERIFIED forecast (prices still climbing in Q4), card 2.1/2.4-2.6 VERIFIED. "Probably" keeps the forecast soft.

**B9 · Land back home · 37.1-41.8 s (+0.5 s tail to about 42.3 s)**
[easy and gentle; quick and level through "next year looks even tighter", then slower and fond on "go easy on the old one", soft smile; trail off, no punchline sell, no title-card landing; 500 ms of room tone to the loop]
> And next year looks even tighter, so go easy on the old one.

Image: the viewer turns the phone face down, puts a fresh strip of tape on the old laptop's hinge and pats the lid. A heart sticker pops onto it (the like-pop SFX), the string lights twinkle, hold 0.5 s, and it loops to frame 0. Small on-screen source: "Micron, SK hynix: 2027 tighter than 2026".
Fact: FL 6.1 VERIFIED (Micron: "much tighter in 2027 and 2028"). W §9 has the SK hynix CEO (VERIFIED there, REPORTED in FL 6.2), so the line says "looks", not "will be". "Go easy on the old one" isn't a fact claim; W §10 VERIFIED that people are keeping their devices longer.

## Plain script (captions and timing, H1 route)

Your laptop's dying, and replacing it costs almost ten percent more than a year ago. And computers had been getting
cheaper for twenty years, apart from a little COVID bump. This time it's mostly memory, and AI data centers need tons of
it. One of their server racks has as much memory as thirteen hundred laptops. So the memory makers sell to them first,
because they pay more. It's more like an auction than a shortage, and you're bidding against a building. So you'll
probably pay more this Christmas, and the cheap ones are going first. And next year looks even tighter, so go easy on
the old one.

## Self-check against the rules

- Frame 0 states the payoff in 7 words, mid-action. No greeting, no "I started reading".
- A positive phone sound lands at 0.10 s, with its place and reason given above.
- No "delve", "here's the thing", triplets, colon reveals or em-dashes in the voiceover. There's one comparison line ("more like an auction than a shortage"), and no "it's not X, it's Y". There's one joke, and it's named in its cue.
- US-only figures are flagged: the hook chip and the B2 chart say "US", and H2 says it out loud.
- The script never says "every year for 20 years", "record" or "AI is raising prices on everything". Every figure is about computers only.
- No villain: the memory makers "sell to them first, because they pay more", read as a shrug, and the building is drawn as a calm giant.
- It ends on the payoff plus one warm line, with no "follow for more", and the last line loops into H1.
