## 1. A robot calls every pitch, but nobody can say what a "swing" is (World Series Game 2, Sat 24 Oct 2026)

- **News:** Game 2, Rays at Brewers, Milwaukee, Sat 24 Oct. Brewers won 5-4 (lead series 2-0). Top of the 9th, two
  out, tying run on third, full count: Yandy Díaz tried to check his swing; home-plate umpire Jim Wolf didn't call
  a swing; on appeal, first-base umpire James Hoye ruled he went around. Game over. Replays didn't settle it; fans split.
- **Twist:** 2026 is MLB's first season with the robot (ABS / Hawk-Eye) challenge system for balls and strikes. But
  check swings aren't reviewable, and the Official Baseball Rules **contain no definition of a swing at all**. It's
  pure umpire judgment. Meanwhile MLB has *already* defined one for testing in the minors (bat head passes 45° from
  the handle; Hawk-Eye measures it) and runs check-swing challenges in Triple-A in 2026. Fox's Joe Davis on air: "We
  need to get a system that can get [check swings] right." Manfred: no decision yet on bringing it to MLB.
- **Big question:** When we hand judgment calls to machines, what happens to the calls nobody ever wrote a rule for?
- **So what chain:** robot can measure a pitch to the millimetre -> but a machine can only check a rule you can
  write down -> the most dramatic call in the World Series had no written rule -> a championship can turn on a
  judgment the cameras could have measured, and baseball already has the fix in its minor leagues.
- **Thesis:** Everyone is talking about whether Yandy Díaz swung, but this call reveals baseball's rulebook never
  defined a swing, which matters because a robot can only enforce a rule someone actually wrote down.
- **Frame 0:** "Baseball never defined a swing."
- **Hooks:** "The World Series just ended on a rule that doesn't exist." / "Baseball has a robot that sees a pitch
  to the millimetre. It wasn't allowed to look." / "Did he swing? Nobody knows, because nobody ever wrote down what
  a swing is."
- **Key image:** scale: a giant stadium at night, thousands of camera beams converging on one bat frozen mid-air,
  a protractor ghosting in at 45°. Cozy: the umpire alone in a tiny pool of light, rulebook open to a blank page.
- **Evidence:** Strong for the game facts (NBC News, CNN live, Yahoo/USA Today, Bleacher Report). "No definition in
  the rulebook" is stated by ESPN and others; minor-league 45° rule from FanGraphs, Baseball America, ESPN. Disputed:
  pitcher name (Ashby vs Megill) varies by source; whether he actually swung is genuinely unknowable, don't claim either way.
- **Risks:** sports-only audience; US-centric; must not imply the call was wrong. Series continues (story evolves).
  Consequence is real but low-stakes for non-fans; lean on the "machines can only enforce written rules" bigger idea.

## 3. Why a building you'll never see is on your electricity bill (data centres, 9-23 Oct 2026)

- **News:** Wed 21 Oct, Trump posted that new large data centres are "mandated" to make their own electricity and
  should pay a "dividend" to communities (The Hill, Spectrum News, 24/7 Wall St). No amounts, no enforcement
  mechanism. Twelve days earlier (9 Oct) FERC had **rejected** PJM's plan to make big data centres bring their own
  generation (docket ER26-3515), saying it intrudes on state jurisdiction; it only approved a registry of 50 MW+ loads.
  Data centres are a top midterm issue in both parties (the House passed a Ratepayer Protection Act 417-3).
- **Twist / mechanism:** you aren't billed for electricity data centres *use*; you're billed for the grid *reserving*
  power for them. PJM (13 states + DC, ~65M people) runs a capacity auction: everyone pays to guarantee enough plants
  for future peak demand. Forecast data-centre demand bid that price up roughly 10x; PJM's independent market monitor
  attributes 63% of the 2025/26 capacity price increase (~$9.3B) to data-centre load. Estimated +$16/month in Ohio,
  +$18 in western Maryland. Today's order changes no rate, so the fight passes to the states.
- **Big question:** Why is my electricity bill going up?
- **So what chain:** AI needs data centres -> grid must promise power for them years ahead -> that promise is priced
  in an auction everyone pays into -> your bill rises before the building even switches on.
- **Thesis:** Everyone is talking about data centres raising power bills, but the mechanism reveals you're paying for
  the grid's *promise* of power to them, which matters because that charge lands on your bill even if the data centre
  is hundreds of miles away.
- **Frame 0:** "You're paying for AI's electricity reservation."
- **Hooks:** "Your power bill has a line for a building you'll never see." / "Both parties want data centres to pay
  their own power. A regulator just said no." / "You're not paying for the electricity AI uses. You're paying for a reservation."
- **Key image:** scale: a vast glowing data centre on the horizon, power lines draped across a whole map to tiny
  houses. Cozy: a family at the kitchen table opening the bill, a little "RESERVED" sign on an empty seat at their table.
- **Evidence:** FERC order and Trump post well documented (Utility Dive, Microgrid Knowledge, Citizens Utility Board,
  The Hill). Attribution of bill increases is **contested**: monitor's method is disputed; PJM itself put 2026/27 bill
  effects at 1.5-5%; Energy Secretary Wright and SemiAnalysis argue policy, not AI, is the main driver. The figure
  "$29.4B / 46%" appears only in secondary blogs; don't use it until found in the monitor's report.
- **Risks:** heavy political charge 9 days before the midterms; must stay strictly bipartisan (both sides agree on the
  goal). US-only (PJM region). Newest hook is 21 Oct (4 days). Possible overlap with tech scout's angle on AI infrastructure.
- **Sources:** The Hill, https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/6161874-trump-mandates-data-center-power/ (21 Oct 2026);
  Spectrum News, https://spectrumlocalnews.com/us/snplus/politics/2026/10/21/trump-suggests-data-centers-pay-for-their-own-electricity (21 Oct 2026);
  Utility Dive, https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-pjm-large-load-interconnection-iras-byonc/832716/ (Oct 2026);
  Citizens Utility Board, https://www.citizensutilityboard.org/blog/2026/10/18/ferc-rejection-of-pjm-data-center-plan-makes-state-action-last-hope-to-protect-consumers/ (18 Oct 2026);

## 4. 17 million "missing" deaths: why Social Security thought so many people were still alive (SSA, 20 Oct 2026)

- **News:** Tue 20 Oct, the Social Security Administration announced what it calls the most comprehensive cleanup of
  its number database in its 91-year history: 17 million death dates added to records that lacked one, corrections to
  nearly 15% of all active SSNs; also ~25M records given missing citizenship data via DHS SAVE and 8.5M corrected
  (Reuters via US News, Click2Houston/KPRC).
- **Twist / mechanism:** the viral reading is "17 million dead people on the books = fraud". The boring truth: SSA only
  records a death when someone reports it. Millions of people who died decades ago, often before electronic death
  reporting, simply never had one entered. A 2015 inspector-general audit found 6.5M people aged 112+ with no death
  recorded, of whom only 266 were getting benefits; SSA has said these records are not paying anyone. Adding a date
  doesn't recover money; it closes a door for identity thieves using old numbers. Critics (a former SSA employee to
  Congress; Sens. Warren and Blumenthal) worry the reverse error is now possible: living people wrongly marked dead.
- **Big question:** Is the government paying dead people?
- **So what chain:** death only exists in the database if someone tells it -> millions never got told -> it looks like
  fraud but mostly isn't -> the real risks are stolen old numbers, and you being marked dead by mistake (which cuts off
  benefits and banking).
- **Thesis:** Everyone is talking about 17 million dead people on Social Security, but the cleanup reveals the
  database only knows you died if someone tells it, which matters because the real danger isn't ghosts collecting
  cheques, it's the living getting marked dead.
- **Frame 0:** "17 million dead people. Almost none paid."
- **Hooks:** "America's Social Security list had 17 million people who never officially died." / "The government
  doesn't know you're dead until someone tells it." / "The scary part isn't dead people getting paid. It's being declared dead."
- **Key image:** scale: an endless archive hall of filing drawers, a lamp moving along them stamping dates. Cozy:
  one old index card for a woman born in 1890, a candle beside it.
- **Evidence:** the announcement and numbers are SSA's own claims (Reuters/US News, 20 Oct); not independently
  verified. Benefit figures for no-death-date records come from older OIG audits (2015, 2023) and SSA statements;
  need the 2026 SSA release itself. The 2.7M Death Master File claim is disputed (SSA denies).
- **Risks:** **highest partisan risk of the four** (DOGE, citizenship data, fraud narratives). Only viable if told
  purely as "how a database works", no villains. US-only. Consider as reserve.
- **Sources:** US News/Reuters, https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-10-20/us-social-security-agency-corrects-nearly-15-of-records-in-database-cleanup (20 Oct 2026);
  Click2Houston, https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/10/21/social-security-announces-major-records-cleanup-heres-what-it-means-for-you/ (21 Oct 2026);
  SSA OIG audit A-06-14-34030, https://oig-files.ssa.gov/audits/full/A-06-14-34030_0.pdf (2015);
  Bloomberg Opinion, https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2025-02-24/the-truth-about-social-security-and-dead-people (2025).
