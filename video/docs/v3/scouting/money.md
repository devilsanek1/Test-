## 1. AI is eating the memory chips, so your next laptop costs more (and computers had been getting cheaper for 20 years)

**News.**
- 12 Oct 2026: Gartner, preliminary Q3 2026: worldwide PC shipments down 22.4 % year on year to 55.4 million. Analyst
  Namrata Banerjee: DRAM costs roughly quintupled year on year in Q2 and vendors passed it to buyers; all six biggest
  vendors shipped less; HP down 30.9 %.
- Same week: IDC -20.1 % (62.7 m units), Counterpoint -21 % (57 m, forecasts -27 % in Q4; report covered 23 Oct),
  Omdia -21.2 % (58.1 m, forecasts -24 % in Q4). Omdia: memory and storage are now nearly 40 % of a PC's build cost,
  versus a usual ~15 %.
- 14 Oct 2026 (Sept CPI): "computers, peripherals and smart home assistants" up 9.7 % year on year, the biggest rise
  since at least 2006; software and accessories +28.4 %. (NBC reported "record" increases in these categories.)
- Spillover: Intel raised PC processor prices ~10 % on 5 Oct (third 2026 hike). Earlier in 2026: Mac mini $599 to
  $799, Xbox and PS5 Pro price rises, IDC sees phone prices up to 8 % (cheap phones hit hardest).
- Next peg: SK hynix Q3 results 27/28 Oct; Samsung end of Oct.

**Sources.**
- Gartner press release, 12 Oct 2026: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-12-gartner-says-worldwide-pc-shipments-declined-22-point-4-percent-in-third-quarter-of-2026-as-component-cost-inflation-raised-asps
- Tom's Hardware, Q3 shipments: https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/pc-shipments-tumble-over-20-percent-in-3q26-as-chip-shortages-bite-top-three-pc-vendors-ship-11-6-million-fewer-units-year-over-year
- Technology.org (Counterpoint), 23 Oct 2026: https://www.technology.org/2026/10/23/global-pc-shipments-q3-2026-down-21-percent-memory-costs/
- 24/7 Wall St., 24 Oct 2026: https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/24/pc-sales-just-crashed-21-as-ai-eats-the-worlds-memory-chips
- NBC News on Sept CPI: https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/inflation-jumped-september-gas-diesel-prices-squeezed-consumers-ahead-rcna603202
- ROIC.ai summary of CPI computers +9.7 %, 14 Oct 2026: https://www.roic.ai/news/ai-boom-fuels-sharpest-computer-price-inflation-in-two-decades-10-14-2026
- VnExpress on phones/consoles: https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/tech-news/smartphone-gaming-console-prices-surge-globally-amid-memory-chip-shortage-5089018.html

**Big question.** Is AI going to make my life more expensive?

**So what chain.** AI data centres need huge amounts of high-bandwidth memory -> the three big memory makers move
factory lines to AI chips -> less ordinary RAM for laptops and phones -> RAM costs ~5x -> laptop makers raise prices and
drop cheap models -> computers, which got cheaper every year for two decades, are up ~10 % in a year -> a student or a
family replacing a dead laptop this Christmas pays more or gets less, and analysts don't expect relief before 2027-28.

**Thesis.** Everyone is talking about AI taking jobs, but this new PC sales crash reveals AI is already taking something
smaller: the memory chips in your laptop, which matters because the one thing that always got cheaper is now getting
pricier, and the cheapest models are disappearing first.

**Frame-0 text.** "AI just made your laptop pricier"

**Hooks.**
1. "Computers got cheaper every year for 20 years. This year they jumped 10 %. Blame a chatbot."
2. "The world just bought 20 % fewer computers. Not because nobody wants one."
3. "There's a chip inside your laptop that AI wants more than you do."

**Twist.** It's not a chip shortage in general: memory makers are choosing to sell to AI data centres because they pay
more, so consumer devices lose an auction they didn't know they were in. And computers are just 0.3 % of the CPI, so
this barely moves "inflation" while being very visible to anyone buying a laptop.

**Key image.** Scale: a giant data centre as a hungry whale, swallowing a river of tiny glowing RAM sticks while a
little laptop at the river's edge gets a trickle. Cozy: a kid at a desk lamp, old laptop, a price tag that grows.

**Evidence.** Strong. Four analyst firms independently report a ~20-22 % Q3 drop and blame memory costs. The CPI figure
(+9.7 %, largest since 2006) is from secondary write-ups; confirm against BLS table (series CUUR0000SEEE01 or the
"computers, peripherals and smart home assistants" line). IDC partly blames front-loaded stockpiling in H1, not only
price. The Fed reportedly notes quality-adjustment quirks in the index.

**Risks.** Two AI-adjacent videos in a row (video 2 is Google military AI): this is a prices story, but the word "AI"
is in the hook. Don't say "AI is raising prices on everything": computers are a tiny CPI weight. Phone/console price
hikes are from earlier in 2026; keep them as supporting, not as "this week". Don't name a villain; memory makers selling
to the highest bidder is ordinary market behaviour.

## 2. Cocoa crashed, so why is Halloween candy still pricier? (The answer: you're eating last year's cocoa)

**News.**
- Halloween is Sat 31 Oct. NRF projects record Halloween spending of $13.5 bn, $4.1 bn on candy.
- BLS candy and chewing gum CPI: +7.7 % in the year to Sept 2026 (Newsweek, citing BLS), on top of +9.8 % the year
  before; other outlets cite +8.1 % (NBC via Entrepreneur, 16 Oct; Business Model Analyst). Chocolate specifically up
  11-15 % in some estimates; NIQ Halloween-chocolate data shows only +1.7 % (Quartz). One local report: a 100-piece bag
  $16.96, +3.5 % on last year and +71 % on 2021.
- Cocoa futures: record above $12,000/t in late 2024 / early 2025, fell ~75-78 % to a bottom near $2,850-3,240 in March
  2026, rebounded to ~$5,700 by mid-Aug / early Oct (CNBC, 5 Oct 2026).
- Mechanism: Hershey hedges commodities 3 to 24 months ahead; told analysts in Feb 2026 it was hedged above market and in
  May saw "no change in the pricing environment"; points to relief and a return to more real chocolate in 2027.
  Mondelez CEO: "We're already covered for '26; there's not a lot we can do anymore," and expects chocolate margins to
  rise considerably in 2027. PwC's Ali Furman (NBC): makers buy cocoa months, sometimes two years, ahead, "shoppers are
  paying for that spike now."

**Sources.**
- Newsweek (BLS candy CPI 7.7 %): https://newsweek.com/halloween-candy-popular-map-price-changes-inflation-12569648
- Entrepreneur, 16 Oct 2026 (NBC/PwC, +8.1 %): https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/candy-prices-are-way-up-but-thats-not-scaring-americans-away-from-buying-tons-of-halloween-candy
- CNBC, 5 Oct 2026, cocoa climbing again: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/05/cocoa-prices-are-climbing-again-heres-why-this-time-is-different.html
- Axios, 22 May 2026, "Cocoa prices have tumbled. Chocolate prices haven't.": https://www.axios.com/2026/05/22/cocoa-chocolate-hershey-prices
- Quartz (15.5 % vs NIQ 1.7 %): https://qz.com/halloween-chocolate-prices-cocoa-shock-101226
- Benzinga, Sept 2026: https://www.benzinga.com/Opinion/26/09/61631494/cocoa-crashed-this-year-so-why-is-your-halloween-candy-still-this-pricey
- WJLA / Sinclair stations, Halloween 2026 prices: https://wjla.com/newsletter-daily/halloween-candy-prices-2026-trick-or-treating-chocolate-costs-cocoa-shortage-inflation-grocery-hersheys-reeses-pieces-shrinkflation-consumer-tariffs-money-saving-tips-shopping-deals
- Hershey 10-Q (hedging policy): https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000047111/000162828026028682/hsy-20260329.htm

**Big question.** Why don't prices come back down when costs fall?

**So what chain.** Chocolate makers lock in cocoa prices up to two years ahead to avoid surprises -> they locked in near
the 2024-25 record -> cocoa then crashed, but this Halloween's chocolate was paid for at the peak -> the candy bag in
your cart is priced off a cocoa price from a year or more ago -> relief, if it comes, arrives around 2027, and companies
say margins will widen first. The same lag (hedging, contracts) explains why many shelf prices lag their raw costs.

**Thesis.** Everyone is talking about cocoa prices crashing, but this Halloween's candy bill reveals you're paying for
cocoa bought at the peak a year or two ago, which matters because when costs fall, the relief reaches the shelf last,
and sometimes companies keep part of it.

**Frame-0 text.** "Cocoa crashed 75 %. Candy still went up."

**Hooks.**
1. "Cocoa is 75 % cheaper than its peak. Your Halloween candy still costs more. Here's the trick."
2. "The chocolate in your trick-or-treat bowl was paid for when cocoa cost a record."
3. "Chocolate companies said it out loud: 'We're already covered for '26.'"

**Twist.** The price you pay is set by a bet made long ago (hedging), not today's cocoa market. Hedging protected
companies on the way up, and now it delays the drop; Mondelez openly expects fatter chocolate margins in 2027. Also:
cocoa has already bounced back up to ~$5,700, so the "crash" was partly temporary.

**Key image.** Cozy: a kid in a costume holding out a pillowcase at a warm, lamp-lit doorway; the candy in the bowl has
a tiny timestamp "bought 2025". Scale: a giant cocoa price line plunging off a cliff while the candy price line on a
shop shelf keeps calmly climbing stairs.

**Evidence.** Good on mechanism (company filings and earnings-call quotes; cocoa futures are public). Weaker on the
exact candy price rise: 7.7 % (Newsweek/BLS) vs 8.1 % (NBC) vs chocolate 15.5 % (PwC estimate) vs 1.7 % (NIQ, Halloween
chocolate). Pick one primary number from BLS before scripting. "75 % crash" depends on the peak and trough used; say
"fell by about three quarters from its record" with dates, and add that it has since partly rebounded.

**Risks.** Freshness: the mechanism has been reported since May; the news peg is Halloween (6 days away) plus the
latest CPI. Needs to publish before 31 Oct. Avoid "greedy companies" framing; hedging is normal risk management. Watch
for Hershey (~29-30 Oct) and Mondelez (~27-28 Oct) Q3 results, which could add or change a fact.

## 3. Diesel costs $2 more than gas, and it's not the oil, it's the refineries

**News.**
- EIA weekly update, 20 Oct 2026 (week of 19 Oct): on-highway diesel $6.276/gal, up 9.7 c; regular gasoline $4.284,
  down 4.7 c. Gap about $1.99. Diesel ~$2.6-2.7 above a year ago. California diesel ~$8.24.
- The widest weekly diesel-gas gap in EIA's series (since 1994) was about $2.05, week of 21 Sept 2026; diesel's record
  $6.53 the same week (secondary sources reading EIA data).
- Diesel crack spread (refining margin over crude) passed $100/bbl for the first time in 2026, about $113 on 20 Oct;
  gasoline crack ~$44-46, close to normal. So crude explains the gas price; refining explains the diesel premium.
- Causes reported: US refineries above 95 % utilisation for months, US diesel stocks ~13 % below 5-year average, Gulf
  diesel exports down to roughly a quarter of pre-war (IEA Sept report, via secondary), attacks on Middle East refineries,
  Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining, Europe pulling US cargoes, and heating season starting (heating oil and jet fuel
  come from the same "middle distillate" barrel).
- Consequence: truck fuel surcharges track the weekly DOE diesel price; ATRI's 2026 survey ranks fuel costs the
  industry's top concern. USDA forecasts all food prices +2.8 % in 2026.

**Sources.**
- EIA weekly retail prices: https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/pet_pri_gnd_dcus_nus_w.htm
- Blue Book Services, "Diesel climbs again as regular gas prices ease": https://www.bluebookservices.com/diesel-climbs-again-as-regular-gas-prices-ease/
- IndexBox, EIA update 20 Oct 2026: https://www.indexbox.io/blog/eia-weekly-fuel-update-gasoline-falls-diesel-rises-as-of-october-20-2026/
- Yahoo Finance, refining margins hit a record: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/gas-and-diesel-prices-likely-to-stay-elevated-as-oil-refining-margins-hit-a-record-high-194000714.html
- RBN Energy, 2026 record crack spreads: https://rbnenergy.com/daily-posts/blog/what-2026s-record-crack-spreads-tell-us-about-refining-economics
- Euronews, 5 Sept 2026, why fuel is expensive though crude isn't at a record: https://euronews.com/business/2026/09/05/why-are-petrol-and-diesel-so-expensive-despite-crude-oil-not-reaching-record-highs
- MSU Today, Oct 2026 expert Q&A: https://msutoday.msu.edu/news/2026/10/gas-price-forecast
- StoneX, Europe heating oil season: https://www.stonex.com/en/insights/europe-s-heating-oil-season-arrives-as-diesel-supply-remains-squeezed/
- Tank Transport / ATRI: https://tanktransport.com/2026/10/trucking-fuel-costs/

**Big question.** Why is everything still getting more expensive?

**So what chain.** Almost nothing you buy moves without diesel (trucks, trains, ships, tractors) -> diesel is a
different product from gasoline, made in a different part of the refinery -> the world lost diesel-making capacity
(Gulf exports, damaged refineries) while winter heating demand starts -> refiners' margin on diesel is at a record, so
diesel sits ~$2 above gas -> truck fuel surcharges rise every week with it -> those costs land, with a lag, in grocery
and delivery prices even when the gas price at your local pump is easing.

**Thesis.** Everyone is talking about gas prices, but this week's numbers reveal the real squeeze is diesel, priced by
refineries not oil wells, which matters because diesel moves your groceries, so it can keep pushing prices up even while
your pump price falls.

**Frame-0 text.** "Gas fell. Diesel just went up. Uh-oh."

**Hooks.**
1. "Gas is getting cheaper. Diesel just went up again. That's bad news for your groceries."
2. "Diesel costs $2 more than gas right now. The biggest gap in over 30 years."
3. "Same barrel of oil. Two prices, $2 apart. Here's why."

**Twist.** Crude oil isn't at a record; the bottleneck is refining. A barrel only yields so much diesel, and diesel
competes with jet fuel and heating oil for the same slice, so winter makes it worse even if oil gets cheaper.

**Key image.** Scale: a gigantic refinery tower cut open like a dollhouse, the oil splitting into floors (gasoline,
jet, diesel), with a tiny crowded diesel floor and a queue of trucks, ships and tractors waiting. Cozy: a truck cab at
night on an empty highway, warm dashboard light, a fuel receipt.

**Evidence.** Pump prices are EIA primary data (re-check on the EIA page; next update 27 Oct). The "widest gap since
1994" claim comes from a secondary explainer reading EIA data; verify by computing from EIA's weekly series. Crack spread
figures differ by source/method; use "record" only with the RBN/Yahoo framing. Diesel-to-grocery pass-through is a
logical mechanism with a lag, not a measured number: say "pushes up costs" not "will raise grocery prices by X".

**Risks.** Very US-centric (the brief's audience is broad; works in Europe too, where diesel is the main car fuel, but
numbers differ). Gas prices are a political topic before the 3 Nov midterms: stay neutral, don't attribute blame. The
gap number moves weekly; script must use the latest EIA reading.

## 4. The data centre isn't in your town, but you may be paying for its power anyway (weaker freshness, contested)

**News.**
- 19 Oct 2026: Citizens Utility Board (Illinois) explainer: PJM capacity price went from $28.92 to $329.17 per MW-day
  between 2024-25 and 2026-27; delivery and capacity costs are spread across the whole footprint, so blocking a local
  data centre wouldn't shield residents.
- 21 Oct 2026: Ohio's consumer advocate warns data-centre growth is driving costs; FirstEnergy proposal adds $4.26/month
  from 2027 rising to $12.78 by 2029 for Ohio Edison customers.
- 22 Oct 2026: coverage that data-centre energy costs are moving midterm voters in Ohio and Michigan.
- Background (July 2026): PJM 2028/29 auction cleared at the $325/MW-day cap, $16.4 bn total; the market monitor
  attributes ~$6.3 bn to data centres, ~$30 bn cumulative across recent auctions (NPR, 1 Oct: ~$29 bn over two years for
  ~67 million people). Monitor's Joe Bowring: "This is not something the data centers are actually paying themselves."
- Counter-evidence: Rutgers State Policy Lab ("mostly not, yet"); LBNL / CRA-for-EEI: outside PJM customers largely
  shielded; E3 (May 2026) on multiple drivers (grid hardening, gas prices).

**Sources.**
- CUB, 19 Oct 2026: https://www.citizensutilityboard.org/blog/2026/10/19/data-centers-and-your-electric-bills/
- Richland Source, 21 Oct 2026: https://www.richlandsource.com/2026/10/21/data-center-surge-driving-up-electric-costs-across-ohio-consumer-advocate-warns/
- NPR, 1 Oct 2026: https://www.npr.org/2026/10/01/nx-s1-5984949/data-centers-ai-ratepayers-congress
- Sierra Club on PJM auction, July 2026: https://www.sierraclub.org/press-releases/2026/07/pjm-capacity-auction-hits-price-cap-third-consecutive-time
- Industrial Info ($6.3 bn): https://www.industrialinfo.com/iirenergy/industry-news/article/report-data-centers-create-about-us63-billion-in-electric-costs-for-pjm-customers--360334
- Rutgers counterpoint: https://policylab.rutgers.edu/publication/are-data-centers-raising-your-electric-bill-mostly-not-yet/
- Bloomberg feature, power bills as a midterm issue: https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-power-bills-midterm-election-issue/

**Big question.** Why is my electricity bill going up?

**So what chain.** Data centres need reserved power years ahead -> the grid runs an auction for "standby" capacity ->
data-centre demand pushed the price to the legal cap -> that cost is shared by every customer across 13 states plus DC,
not just the data centre or its town -> it shows up as a line in your supply charge.

**Thesis.** Everyone is fighting about data centres in their backyard, but the grid's own monitor shows the cost travels
through an auction to everyone's bill across 13 states, which matters because saying no to one in your town doesn't
take it off your bill.

**Frame-0 text.** "No data centre nearby? You're still paying."

**Hooks.**
1. "Your town said no to the data centre. Your power bill didn't get the memo."
2. "There's an auction most people have never heard of. It just set part of your electric bill."
3. "$6 billion. That's what one auction says data centres added to people's power bills."

**Twist.** The cost isn't the electricity data centres use; it's the price of guaranteeing backup capacity for them,
set in one auction and spread across everyone.

**Key image.** Scale: one glowing data centre on a hill with power lines fanning out like roots under thousands of tiny
house roofs, each house lit with a small coin. Cozy: a kitchen table at night, a bill under a lamp, one line circled.

**Evidence.** Contested. The monitor's estimate is solid as an estimate, but the residential bill effect varies by
utility and state, and several studies find small effects outside PJM. Only valid for the PJM region (13 states + DC).

**Risks.** Freshest items (19-22 Oct) are advocacy blogs and local news; the hard numbers are from July. Highly
politicised before the 3 Nov midterms. Easy to slide into "data centres are raising your bill" without the regional
caveat. Rank below 1-3.
