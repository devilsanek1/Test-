# Video 3 script: "The data center on your price tag" (core line B, second person)

Draft, 2026-10-25. Not committed. It's built on `draft_personal.md` around the locked core line (`../CORE_LINE.md`, pick B):
**"AI is making your next laptop pricier."** The story starts at the viewer's own desk (the old laptop is dying, the
replacement is in the cart), pulls back once to the size of an AI data center, then comes home to the same price tag.
Facts come only from `../research/factlock.md` (FL) and `../research/widen.md` (W). The voice is American English.

## At a glance

- **Frame-0 text (all 3 hooks):** **AI IS MAKING YOUR NEXT LAPTOP PRICIER**
- **Length:** 110 spoken words with H1, 111 with H2 and 112 with H3. That's about 43 s at 160-182 wpm plus the written pauses.
- **Proof order:** (1) it's real (B3), (2) it's mostly memory (B4), (3) data centers pay more, so it's an auction (B5-B6),
  (4) why "next" (B7-B8), then the callback (B9).
- **"AI data centers" in the voice:** about 4.1-5.1 s with H1 (about 4.9-5.9 s with H3, the longest hook).
- **Jolt for a plain line:** the frame-0 data center fills the window right behind the laptop. The cart pop is the
  happiest sound in shopping, and the price tag bumps up on it. Line 2 is "you and an AI data center want the same
  memory chips", with two hands reaching for one chip. That opens "wait, what does AI have to do with my laptop?" in 4 s.

## Frame 0 and the first second

- **Frame-0 image (same for all 3 hooks, mid-action, cozy enclosure):** the desk nook at night, boxed in by a bookshelf
  on the left and a tall frosty window with string lights on the right, all inside a warm lamp pool. The old laptop has
  tape on its hinge and a "1%" battery. The viewer's thumb is already landing on **Add to cart** under a shiny new laptop
  on their phone. Right outside the window, so close it fills the glass, an AI data center looms like a lit tower block.
  A giant scoop arm lifts a glowing stream of memory chips off a conveyor into it. The text sits in bold ink in the
  upper-middle third, well clear of the top edge.
- **Positive phone sound: `phone_cart_add` at 0.10 s (frame 3 at 30 fps)**, exactly as the thumb hits the button. It's a
  0.41 s soft pop with a bright tick at the end. On the tick (about 0.35 s), the new laptop's price tag on the phone
  bumps up a notch. The voice comes in at 0.50 s, after the tick, and the music is almost silent under it.
  - **Why this sound:** it's the exact moment the viewer is in, buying the replacement, and it's the happiest sound in
    online shopping. Because the tag jumps on the tick, the good sound delivers the bad news before a word is said.
    The image is the same under every hook, so it works with all three, and the last shot drifts back to the same
    button, so the loop lands on this pop.

## Cued script (H1 route)

Times assume about 160-182 wpm plus the pauses in the cues.

[plain, a touch incredulous, like telling a friend news about THEM; quick (~182 wpm), level pitch, not loud; voice in at 0.50 s, after the cart tick; lean on "NEXT"; no smile; 200 ms beat after; DON'T: no headline or anchor read, no big inhale]
> AI is making your NEXT laptop pricier.

- **Image (cozy enclosure, frame 0):** the nook described above. On "NEXT", the camera nudges toward the new laptop on the phone.

**B2 · The jolt / stakes · 3.0-6.7 s**

W 9 VERIFIED: the GB300 rack also carries about 17 TB of LPDDR5X, the memory type in many thin laptops. ## Rules check

- Frame 0 shows the core line as text, mid-action, with a data center next to the laptop. There's no greeting, no intro and no "I started reading".
- H1 is the core line verbatim. H2 (a question) and H3 (a personal condolence) are different openings that land "your next laptop" plus "pricier" within about 2-3 s, under the same frame-0 text.
- `phone_cart_add` lands at 0.10 s. A second positive sound (`phone_notify_ding`) comes at about 30 s.
- The structure runs hook (0-3 s), stakes (3-7 s), it's real (7-16 s), explanation (16-25 s), twist (25-30 s), the payoff for the viewer (30-37 s) and the callback (37-42 s). It ends on the callback, with no "follow for more".
- AI-tell pass:
  - There's one comparison line ("less a shortage than an auction") and no "it's not X, it's Y".
  - No "here's the thing", "turns out", "delve", triplets, colon reveals, self-answered questions or em-dashes in the voiceover.
  - The connectives are so, and, because.
- Facts:
  - Every line maps to a VERIFIED FL/W row, or to REPORTED with soft wording (B8).
  - The only US figure (+9.7%) says "in the US" out loud and on the tag.
  - The 5x stays on the chip, never next to a laptop.
  - Never "every year", "record", "ever", "only", "double", "AI is raising prices on everything" or "than your last one".
- No villain: the makers sell to whoever pays more (a shrug in the cue), and the data center is a calm, polite giant.

## Open points for the owner

1. **"Five times last year's price"** is the natural spoken form. FL 4.1's exact safe wording is "about five times what
   they did a year earlier" (+3 words, about +1 s). The on-screen chip dates it to Q2 2026 either way.
2. **The 40% is on screen only.** If the owner wants it spoken, B4 becomes "Mostly the memory chips. They cost about
   five times last year's price, and they're now almost forty percent of a PC's parts cost." That's +10 words (about +3.5 s),
   so H3 would run about 47 s. It only fits if a line is cut.
3. **Joke and callback** both pay off B2 ("you and an AI data center"). If together they feel like two punchlines,
   drop "and you're bidding against a building" (-6 words, about -2 s). The auction image still shows the tiny paddle.
4. **No BLS series check is needed:** the script never says "biggest in 20 years".
