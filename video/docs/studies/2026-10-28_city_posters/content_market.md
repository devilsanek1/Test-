# Content lens A: the market for place-led content (2026-10-28)

**Question:** what place-led content (cities, architecture, buildings, infrastructure) works on short-form and for
visual-explainer studios, and what does it mean for a tech/AI news channel with an illustrated, poster-like city look?
**Method:** 39 web searches on 2026-10-28. Every fact was read that day. The date given with a source is the date it was
published or the date of its data. WebFetch could not reach socialpruf.com, vidiq.com or newsroom.tiktok.com from this
container (network allowlist), so the figures come from the search tool's reading of the cited pages. Channel counts
from third-party trackers are marked "est.": trackers disagree by roughly 5-20 %. The source keys [Sn] resolve in §6.
Our own numbers come from the playbook's Performance log. Re-check any number before it goes into a script: this file
is for strategy, not for a fact lock.

**Top findings (one screen):**
1. Place-led explainers are a proven genre in long-form YouTube: the B1M has ~4M subscribers, Wendover ~4.9M, Practical
   Engineering ~4.7M and Johnny Harris ~7.8M (some are tracker figures, est.). We found no short-form numbers for any
   of these studios, and the best-known illustrated studio has ~25M on YouTube against ~1.9M on TikTok (est.) [S9].
   Neither the look nor the genre carries reach on short-form by itself; the opening does. v1 and v3 were both drawn
   worlds: v1's skip rate was 32 %, v3's 64.5 %, and the owner read v3's opening as an ad (playbook).
2. The place stories that travel have a real, named place, a conflict and an everyday consequence. They come in three
   kinds: neighbours against a machine (data-centre noise and wells, honking robotaxis), a promise against the place
   (NEOM, Intel Ohio), and "your place, recognised" (the NYT dialect quiz, city-pride trends). A postcard with no
   consequence is the trap.
3. Tech and AI arriving in towns is a live and growing fuss. Local opposition blocked or delayed ~$64B of US
   data-centre projects in the 2 years to May 2025, and a tracker counted ~$130B in Q1 2026 alone [S22]. Its facts are
   contested, though (water, "267 %"): the strongest place stories are also the easiest to get wrong.

**What the table says.**
- Place-led explainers built their studios on long-form YouTube (the B1M, Wendover, Practical Engineering, Johnny
  Harris). On short-form, the evidence is hashtags and individual creators (PlanningTok tags get tens of millions of
  views), not studios. We found no TikTok or Reels figures for any of the big place-led studios, the B1M included [S1].
- Short-form's breakout animated channel (Zack D. Films, ~29M) does curiosity facts: not places, and not news.
- The biggest single place items are about conflict or identity: a promo everyone argued about (NEOM), a quiz about
  where you're from (NYT), a parking lot of honking robotaxis (Waymo), a family whose well changed next to a data centre
  (MPU). Our own best performer fits the pattern: v1 drew the internet as a town of buildings (its baseline sheet)
  and carried a news hook.

## 2. Story shapes and hooks

| Shape | Example (evidence) | Why it gets shared | The trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Your place, recognised** | NYT dialect quiz [S17]; the "things in my city that just make sense", "lunch in my city" and Chicago "love letter" trends [S38]; fans' country redraws of the Lofi Girl scene [S32] | identity: people send what names their place, and sends per reach is one of Instagram's top signals, weighted most for non-followers [S34] | local pride without news makes a city account, not us; one named city narrows the audience |
| **The neighbour and the machine** | Georgia wells and bills next to Meta's site [S19]; the turbine hum in Vineland, NJ [S20]; xAI's Memphis turbines (up to 35 seen in April 2025 images; a permit later granted for 15) [S24]; Waymo's honking lot [S18] | high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) predict sharing [S36]; one place, one family, an everyday consequence | contested facts: the water claims are disputed and Meta denies them [S19]; never a manufactured villain (brief §18-19) |
| **The promise vs the place** ("X did Y, here's what happened") | NEOM's 14M-view promo vs "the rise and fall" [S16]; Intel Ohio, where production promised by the end of 2025 is now set for 2030-31 [S25] | a built-in before/after and a scale moment; the comments argue | snark and punching down (brief: never ridicule people affected) |
| **Before / after on one street** | @betterstreetsai, with its requests for "my street" [S15]; Google's AI traffic lights: up to 30 % fewer stops at 70 junctions in 12 cities [S27] | the contrast reads with the sound off, and it invites "do mine" | an AI render is not a plan; the results come from tests (2022-23), not from a whole city |
| **The hidden thing around you** | Practical Engineering (515M views) [S5]; undersea cables (Vox, 3.2M) [S12]; "people fascinated to learn how the internet works" [S28] | awe, and "I never knew" facts people repeat | without a news peg it fails our freshness test; peg it to an event (the Red Sea cable cuts, Sept 2025 [S28]) |
| **The map that explains it** | Bloomberg's map of power prices near data centres (wholesale up to +267 % at some nodes, 2020-25) [S23]; Johnny Harris, Map Men [S4] [S8] | each viewer looks for their own place on it | compression: "bills up 267 %" was rated a misrepresentation, since wholesale is ~30-50 % of a bill [S23] |
| **The picture that moved people** | a photo angle made a Lawson under Mt Fuji a crowd spot, and the town put up a 20 m screen (2024) [S30]; an AI news clip sent a couple 300+ km to a cable car that doesn't exist (2025) [S29]; robotaxis became a San Francisco attraction [S26] | travel longing plus a twist, with the platform itself in the story | the postcard: a pretty place with no consequence gets "so what?" |

**What we read from it.**
- Shares come from two engines: identity (my place) and arousal (awe, anger, worry). The poster look already brings
  awe (the one hero landmark, the scale), so the story has to bring the identity or the conflict.
- The trap is the travel postcard. The #architecture tag is huge [S14], but the top videos under the urbanism tags are
  critiques ("do not paint American cities in a positive light" [S13]). What rises is a place with a problem or a
  change.
- In the market, the fuss around places and AI skews negative (data centres, noise, fakes). Positive place stories exist
  (AI timing traffic lights [S27], robotaxis as an attraction [S26]), but we found less evidence that they travel. A
  positive pick needs the stronger frame-0 picture.

## 4. The illustrated city look in video

**Who does illustrated places well, and what they prove.**
- **Kurzgesagt:** one consistent flat look from a studio of ~70-77 people [S9] proves that a look can be a brand on
  YouTube. Off YouTube, the same look has ~1.9M followers on TikTok and ~1.6M on Instagram (est.) [S9]: about 13x
  fewer than its YouTube base, even though a 2022 grant partly paid for TikTok content [S9].
- **Lofi Girl:** an illustrated window onto a real city (the Croix-Rousse in Lyon) [S32]. Fans redrew the scene for
  their own countries ("60 Lofi Girl redraws from around the world") [S32]. Our reading: the place in the window became
  an invitation to make "my country's" version.
- **Tiny Glade:** a cozy, hand-made-looking town spread through short clips and sold 616K copies in its first month,
  made by 2 people [S21].

**Does the look perform on short-form? Evidence, not taste.**
- We found no measured case of an illustrated city reel or animation with millions of views. The closest is one
  illustrator's self-reported 12.9M views across reels, undated [S39]. The look's short-form record is unproven.
- Animation isn't the barrier, since animated shorts can scale (Zack D. Films, ~29M [S10]). Our reading is that the
  opening is. Our v1 (a drawn city with a news hook) had a 32.2 % skip rate. v3 (also a drawn world; the owner read its
  opening as an ad) had 64.5 % (playbook, Performance log, 2026-10-26 and 2026-10-28).
- The look is no longer rare. AI image tools turned illustrated restyles into a mass trend (the Ghibli moment [S31]) and
  made cozy illustrated loops cheap (an Adobe Firefly loop case study [S39]), so flat, textured city art is everywhere
  in that stream. What stays rare is a drawn world that moves, explains and is true.

**The series idea as a brand asset.**
- **The grid is now 3:4.** Instagram moved profile thumbnails from squares to 3:4: it tested this in Aug 2024, and it
  was the default by 2025-01-20 (Mosseri called the old square crop "pretty brutal") [S33]. In the grid, a reel's 9:16
  cover shows as a centred 3:4 crop, and the creator can move the crop [S33]. The posters are ~3:4, so a grid of our
  covers can read as a set of posters at no extra cost.
- **Carousels earn the most engagement per post** on Instagram (0.55 %, against Reels 0.50 % and images 0.45 %; data
  from 2023-24) [S35]. A film's best frames as a poster carousel make a cheap second post (we found no source comparing
  a carousel's reach with a reel's).
- **A recurring, named format** is how place-led creators compound (Map Men [S8]; Wendover's fixed cadence for ~11 years
  [S3]). TikTok's 2026 forecast calls the platform a discovery engine ("Curiosity Detours") [S37], which suits a "one
  place, one tech story" series.

## 5. Implications for us (ranked)

1. **Pick place stories for the conflict at a named place, never for the postcard.** Evidence: the items that travelled
   paired a place with a consequence (MPU's wells [S19], the honking lot [S18], NEOM [S16], the Mt Fuji screen [S30]),
   and the urbanism tags rise on critique [S13]. Our v1 shows that the place can also be a metaphor (websites drawn as
   houses) when the story is news. In our films: the G0 scout gives each candidate a "place line" (one real or
   metaphorical place, who lives there, what changes for them), and DIRECTION's home place becomes that place, drawn
   new and never traced. Cost S. Risk: conflict stories skew negative, and the slate needs a positive story next
   (playbook, Taste signals). The positive place stories we found (AI traffic lights, the wonder of robotaxis) need a
   stronger frame 0.
2. **Let the place be the share trigger.** Evidence: sends per reach is one of Instagram's top three signals and weighs
   most for non-followers [S34]; identity content gets shared (the NYT quiz [S17], city trends [S38], the Lofi redraws
   [S32]); our v1 had 0.7 % shares and v3 had 0 % (playbook). In our films: name the place in the first spoken line
   when the story allows. Prefer stories where many places share the pattern (a map of every town with a data centre,
   every Waymo city) so each viewer finds theirs. Cost S. Risk: none under the owner's rules as long as it stays
   implicit. The brief bans asking ("no 'send this to...'", ENGAGEMENT LAYER).
3. **Add two place devices to the toolkit: a map pin that turns into a poster street, and a before/after on one
   street.** Evidence: the map stories [S23] [S4] [S8] and the before/after accounts [S15] [S27]. In our films: two G3
   recipes (a pin drops and the camera dives into the drawn street; a wipe or a time-of-day change on a fixed street),
   then helpers in `video/src/lib`. Map labels follow the "few and big" words rule (the place's name only). Cost M.
   Risk: a number on a map compresses badly (the "267 %" case [S23]), so the fact lock names the denominator of every
   mapped number (brief §18).
4. **Make every cover a 3:4 city poster, and name a series only after 3 place-led films.** Evidence: the 3:4 grid [S33]
   and carousels' engagement [S35]. In our films: a cover rule in `video/docs/POSTING_KIT.md`: the 3:4 centre crop
   holds the hero landmark and the core line. The place's name goes in the posters' title zone only if it reads at
   phone size. The posters' tiny letter-spaced titles would not, and the owner's "few and big" words rule wins. Add a
   poster carousel per film. Cost S. Risk: a cover can't save a weak first 2 s (v3 lost 60 % by 2 s); never polish covers
   before the story lock.
5. **Don't expect the look to bring reach; measure it on saves and shares.** Evidence: Kurzgesagt's 25M vs 1.9M [S9];
   v1 vs v3 (playbook); illustrated restyles are now mass-produced [S31] [S39]. In our films: G0 scoring stays
   relevance-first (TOPIC_CRITERIA). The look's test is saves and shares on the next 3 place-led films, against v1's
   0.4 % saves and 0.7 % shares. Cost S. Risk: none; this guards against picking by style.
