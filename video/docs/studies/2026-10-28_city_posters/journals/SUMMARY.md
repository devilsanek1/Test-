# Which big-journal stories fit our look

**The answer:** about 1 big-journal story in 10 fits our drawn worlds. The ones that fit happen TO a real place: new
finds about the past, space and sky, nature and people saving it, and floods and storms.

**Kinds that fit** (fit with our look, out of 10):
- Floods, storms, quakes, 8.9: the best fit, but all bad news this month (California's sea is up to a foot higher).
- New finds about the past, 7.4: an Arctic ice cap had started a new ice age; our warming stopped it.
- Space and sky, 7.2: Japan sent a probe to bring back soil from a moon of Mars.
- Nature, and people saving it, 7.2: an island turns off its lights so young seabirds find the sea.

**5 best ideas now** (each checked on the web):
1. Japan's probe to Mars's moon Phobos; its soil comes back around 2031 (launched 20 Oct).
2. The ice age we stopped: Canada's Barnes Ice Cap (Science, 22 Oct).
3. A volcano's stone forest becomes America's newest national park (20 Oct).
4. The island that goes dark for baby seabirds (the Azores, every autumn).
5. A telescope made of Antarctic ice wins the Nobel (6 Oct; the ceremony is 10 Dec).

**What it means:** tech news on its own almost never fits (AI and web stories average 1.3). Tech fits when the machine
sits in a weird place: the ice telescope, Finland's nuclear-waste tomb in 1.9-billion-year-old rock (licensed 22 Oct),
the Mars probe. Data centres this month were all anger and money, none in a weird place. City news draws well but had
no big WOW.

**Your call:** should the next shortlist come from new finds about real places (history, space, nature), or from tech in
weird places (the ice telescope, Finland's waste tomb)?
