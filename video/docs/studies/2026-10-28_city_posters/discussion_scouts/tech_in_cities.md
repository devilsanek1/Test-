## Cases in full

### 1. London dug a second river under the Thames; the sewage in the real one fell 86%
- **Problem and fix:** London's Victorian sewers overflowed into the Thames when it rained (21+ million m3 a year). The
  Thames Tideway Tunnel, a 25 km "super sewer" deep under the river bed (about 7 m wide, up to about 60 m deep, est.),
  opened in 2025. In 2025: overflows just under 3 million m3, -86% (modelled, not metered); days with a raised health
  risk on the river 75.7% -> 10.4%; about 84 t of wet wipes and other sewage litter kept out; sewage items on the
  foreshore 30% -> 18% of all litter (Thames21).
- **Why now:** the first annual Tideway Benefits Report (mid-Oct 2026), as the tunnel's running passes to Thames Water.
- **The big view:** hero: the tunnel as a cutaway under the river, crisp; above it the Thames bends past Tower Bridge
  and the Shard. Far out: the estuary opening to the North Sea. Nature: a seal on the mud, eels, gulls. Frame 0: rain,
  drains gush a brown plume into the river (expected); the ground peels open and the plume turns down into a giant tunnel.
- **So what:** a river people can row and swim near again; every old city has these sewers. **Series:** the hidden
  machine under the city (Tokyo's flood "cathedral", Paris's tank for the Seine swims, Copenhagen's rain tunnels).
- **Ad or news?** News. First line: "London built a second river under the Thames. Last year the sewage in the real one fell by 86%."
- **Truth:** Thames Water, Oct 2026 https://www.thameswater.co.uk/news/2026/oct/86-drop-in-sewage-discharges-into-thames-as-the-operation-of-thames-tideway-tunnel-transfers-to-thames-water ;
  Positive News, 16 Oct 2026 https://www.positive.news/society/good-news-stories-from-week-42-of-2026/ . The operator's
  own report; 86% is a model; 2025 was dry (the report says the drop beats rainfall alone); the promise was about 95%.
- **Mood:** positive. **Risks:** "sewage" can read gross; one dry year; Thames Water is unpopular; tunnel size from memory.

### 2. London's hospitals fly children's leukaemia samples over the traffic
- **Problem and fix:** a sample stuck in a courier van is a doctor waiting. Guy's and St Thomas' are 2.1 km apart, but
  the road run between their labs took 30+ minutes; since Oct 2024 drones do it in 2-4 minutes. The network (run by
  Apian, founded by NHS doctors; Wing and Matternet fly) now links three hospital trusts, a south-west London route
  (since Feb 2026) and Synnovis's Blackfriars lab (20,000 samples a day), which now gets bone-marrow samples for
  leukaemia diagnosis by air, children's among them. Robot dogs are being tested to carry samples from the drone pad.
- **Why now:** 20 Oct 2026: Great Ormond Street (children) joins, "up to 90% faster" (Matternet's claim). Same day
  Apian counted 13,000+ items flown and showed two years of flight paths: one orange band from London Bridge to
  Westminster Bridge.
- **The big view:** hero: one small white drone with a sample box, crisp, crossing the river. Far out: the Thames
  winding east past the City towers to Canary Wharf. Nature: river, plane trees, gulls at drone height. Frame 0: a
  courier van crawls in a red river of brake lights (expected); a drone lifts off the hospital roof and is across first.
- **So what:** faster answers for sick children's families; people want this one (a Massachusetts town voted 510-13
  against drones dropping toothpaste). **Series:** Sweden's defibrillator drones; Fort Collins's firehouse drones reach
  any street in about 90 s (Route Fifty, 27 Oct 2026); Michigan Medicine will test organ flights (DroneXL, 26 Oct).
- **Ad or news?** Brand risk. First line: "In London, children's blood tests now fly over the traffic." No company on screen.
- **Truth:** DroneXL, 20 Oct 2026 https://dronexl.co/2026/10/20/apian-drone-flight-paths-guys-st-thomas-london-render/ ;

### 3. Bears are walking into Japan's towns, so the towns hired AI, drones and robots
- **Problem and fix:** record attacks: 238 people wounded and 13 killed in fiscal 2025, 62 wounded and 6 killed in
  the first five months of fiscal 2026 (environment ministry). Villages empty, bears move in and walk into towns for
  food. The fixes: B Alert AI cameras spot a bear and alert officials (29 municipalities incl. Sendai; "up to 99.9%"
  is the maker's claim); Showa village (1,000 people) flies a thermal drone that sees a bear's warm trail under the
  trees (nine caught); a University of Tokyo startup tests four-legged patrol robots; an AI map of likely encounters.
- **Why now:** The Washington Post, republished 16 Oct 2026 ("AI, 4-legged robots and pepper-spraying drones");
  encounters on pace to beat last year's record; autumn is the peak.
- **The big view:** hero: a bear at a town's edge at dusk by a persimmon tree, crisp. Far out: forested ridges to snow
  peaks. Nature: maples, persimmons, mist. Frame 0: a quiet street at dawn, a man steps out for the paper (expected);
  a bear is by the gate, then a drone's thermal view finds three more on the hill.
- **So what:** "would you want AI watching your street for bears?" splits people, shareably. **Series:** partly
  (cities' tech against wildlife: LA's wildlife bridge over the 101 opens 2 Dec 2026).
- **Ad or news?** News. First line: "Bears are walking into Japan's towns. So the towns hired drones, AI and robots."

### 4. Indonesia starts a 575 km wall against the sea, and the first thing it builds is trees

  Java, about 20 years, said to protect about 55 million people (government); Jakarta's share grew from 12 to 19 km;
  up to about US$80bn (search summary). The capital itself is moving to Nusantara on Borneo.

### 6. Venice's hidden wall: 78 gates rise from the seabed, and may be choking the lagoon (weird place)
- **Problem and fix:** high tides flooded Venice for centuries (1966: 194 cm; Nov 2019: 187 cm; both est.). Since Oct
  2020, MOSE: 78 hollow steel gates lie flat in the seabed at the lagoon's three inlets; for a tide forecast over 110 cm,
  air is pumped in and they swing up, cutting the lagoon off from the Adriatic. Raised well over 100 times (sources:
  about 130 to 156); 24 times in the first 50 days of 2026; EUR 200,000-300,000 a raise (search summary).
- **Why now:** on 20 Oct 2026 the city forecast 110 cm for 21 Oct, "MOSE activation possible", the season's first high
  tide. The twist: a 2026 OGS study (Scientific Reports): the gates hold the sea back, but frequent closures make the
  lagoon's water renew much more slowly. (The 21 Oct raise is not confirmed in what I read.)
- **The big view:** hero: one yellow gate swinging up out of the water at the Lido inlet, crisp. Far out: the Alps'
  snow line across the lagoon on a clear day (est.); the Adriatic behind. Nature: salt marsh, egrets. Frame 0: water
  seeps up between St Mark's paving stones (expected flood); cut to sea: a wall stands up out of the waves.
- **So what:** a city that switches off the sea, at a cost. **Series:** big barriers (Java, case 4; Thames; Rotterdam).
- **Ad or news?** News. First line: "Venice has a wall you've never seen. It lies on the seabed, and this week it stood up."
- **Truth:** ilmeteo.net, 20 Oct 2026 (quotes the city's alert and the study, Melaku Canu et al., Sci Rep 2026) https://www.ilmeteo.net/notizie/previsioni/minimo-sul-golfo-di-venezia-e-scirocco-sull-adriatico-torna-l-acqua-alta-a-venezia-in-azione-il-mose.html ;
  costs: L'Indipendente, 19 Feb 2026 https://www.lindipendente.online/2026/02/19/venezia-il-mose-costa-200mila-euro-ogni-alta-marea-ma-piazza-san-marco-e-sempre-allagata/ .

### 8. These new homes are heated by the flooded coal mines under their feet (weird place)
- **Problem and fix:** Seaham, a former pit town on the Durham coast. Its old mines filled with water, which is still
  pumped up and cleaned at the Dawdon works to protect drinking water. It comes up at 18-20 °C all year; a heat pump
  lifts it to heating temperature. It now heats about 750 new affordable homes at Seaham Garden Village (about 6 MW
  potential; 2,600 t CO2 a year saved, their estimate). About 25% of Britain's homes sit above old coal mines.
- **Why now:** 6 Oct 2026, the government: the network is live and commissioned (residents moved in from January).
- **The big view:** hero: a cutaway from a new street down to a flooded mine gallery, the water glowing warm, crisp.
  Far out: the North Sea horizon from the cliffs. Nature: limestone cliffs, a beach known for sea glass (est.), gulls.
  Frame 0: a coal fire in an old miner's grate (the expected warmth); the floor drops away to the same mines, now full
  of warm water, piping heat into new homes.
- **So what:** steadier, cheaper heat from what the mines left (energy prices are high in 2026). **Series:** yes:
  Gateshead (2023), Wales, Heerlen in the Netherlands; the authority runs 80+ mine-water pumping schemes.
- **Ad or news?** News; councils and a public body. First line: "These new homes are heated by the flooded coal mines under their feet."
- **Truth:** GOV.UK (Mining Remediation Authority), 6 Oct 2026 https://www.gov.uk/government/news/flicking-the-switch-to-heat-seaham-homes-with-mine-water-energy ;
  ThinkGeoEnergy, 6 Oct 2026 https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/mine-water-geothermal-heat-project-goes-online-at-seaham-county-durham-uk/ .
  A press release plus trade press; one estate, not a city; the heat pump still needs power.

  fines start at GBP 100 (up to 1,000 in court). 683 drivers were fined in 2021-24, Lamborghinis top the list (The
  National, 2024).
- **Why now:** 15 Oct 2026, the council: the cameras fired almost 1,400 times in Aug-Sep, the loudest car at almost
  114 dB, "rock-concert volumes" (search summary; the release blocked us).
- **The big view:** hero: one supercar, crisp, under a small box on a lamppost. Far out: the London skyline past Hyde
  Park. Nature: Sloane Street's plane trees, autumn leaves, the moon. Frame 0: a sleeping street (expected quiet); a
  roar, drawn sound waves fill the air and converge on the box, which flashes.
- **So what:** sleep for residents; would you want one on your street: many would. **Series:** yes: New York (1,691
  fines in 2025), Paris's noise radars, Victoria's "hoon cameras" by end 2026.
- **Ad or news?** News. First line: "In London's richest streets, a camera now listens for supercars."
- **Truth:** RBKC release, 15 Oct 2026 (title only) https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/newsroom/hundreds-fined-noisy-drivers-hit-rock-concert-volumes ;
