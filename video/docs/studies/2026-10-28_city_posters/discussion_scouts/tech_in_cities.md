## Cases in full

### 1. London dug a second river under the Thames, and the sewage in the real one fell 86%
- **Problem and fix:** London's Victorian sewers overflowed into the Thames whenever it rained (pre-Tideway average:
  more than 21 million m3 of raw sewage a year). The fix: the Thames Tideway Tunnel, a "super sewer" about 25 km long
  (16 miles), dug deep under the river bed, that catches the overflows and carries them east to the treatment works.
  Opened in 2025. Size: about 7 m wide, up to about 60 m deep (est., Tideway's published figures, not re-checked).
- **Numbers (first full year, 2025):** overflow volume just under 3 million m3, down about 86% (modelled, not
  measured). Days with a raised health risk on the river: 75.7% of days before, 10.4% in 2025. About 84 tonnes of
  wet wipes and other sewage litter kept out (about 130 t across the wider system). On the foreshore, sewage items fell
  from 30% to 18% of all litter (Thames21's counts). One oxygen-crash event linked to overflows all year.
- **Why now:** the first annual Tideway Benefits Report, mid-Oct 2026 (in Positive News's 16 Oct roundup), published as
  the tunnel's running passes from Tideway to Thames Water.
- **The big view:** hero: the tunnel, drawn as a cutaway under the river, a dark tube the size of a road tunnel.
  Above it, the Thames bending past Tower Bridge and the Shard. Far out: the estuary opening to the North Sea, the
  river's whole length. Nature: the water itself, a seal on the mud, eels, gulls. Frame 0: rain on London, drains
  gush into the river (the expected brown plume); the ground peels open and the plume turns down into a giant tunnel.
- **So what:** a river people can row, swim near and fish in again; would they want it at home: yes, every city with
  old sewers has the same problem.
- **A series?** Yes: the hidden machine under the city (Tokyo's flood "cathedral", Paris's Austerlitz tank for the
  Seine swims, Copenhagen's cloudburst tunnels, Hong Kong's tank under a racecourse).
- **Ad or news?** News; no product. First line: "London built a second river under the Thames. Last year the sewage in
  the real one fell by 86%."
- **Truth:** Thames Water release, Oct 2026
  https://www.thameswater.co.uk/news/2026/oct/86-drop-in-sewage-discharges-into-thames-as-the-operation-of-thames-tideway-tunnel-transfers-to-thames-water ;
  Positive News, 16 Oct 2026 https://www.positive.news/society/good-news-stories-from-week-42-of-2026/ . The 86% is a
  model, not a meter; 2025 was unusually dry (the report says the drop beats rainfall alone); the project once promised
  about 95%. Thames Water is a disliked company facing re-nationalisation: this is its own report.
- **Mood:** positive. **Risks:** "sewage" can read gross; one dry year; the source is the operator; London-centric.
- **Scores:** R 7, T 8, V 9, M 9, F 8, S 7; weighted 8.0. WOW 8.

### 2. London's hospitals fly children's leukaemia samples over the traffic
- **Problem and fix:** a blood or bone-marrow sample stuck in a courier van in London traffic is a doctor waiting.
  Guy's and St Thomas' sit 2.1 km apart; the road run between their labs took more than 30 minutes. Since Oct 2024
  drones fly it in under 2 minutes (Wing's aircraft) or about 4 (Matternet's). The network now spans three hospital
  trusts plus a south-west London route (Raynes Park to St George's, Tooting, since Feb 2026), run by Apian, a startup
  founded by NHS doctors.
- **Numbers:** more than 13,000 clinical items flown since Oct 2024 (Apian's count, 20 Oct 2026); the first phase
  carried 6,000+ samples and cut costs by 28% on average (Apian's claim). Synnovis's Blackfriars lab, which tests
  20,000 samples a day, now gets bone-marrow samples for leukaemia diagnosis by drone, including children's from the
  Evelina. Robot dogs are being trialled at Great Ormond Street to meet the drones and trot samples to the lab.
- **Why now:** 20 Oct 2026, Great Ormond Street Hospital (children) joins: Matternet drones fly its pathology samples to
  The Doctors Laboratory, "up to 90% faster" (Matternet's claim). Same day, Apian published a render of two years of
  flights: hundreds of paths stacking into one orange band from London Bridge to Westminster Bridge.
- **The big view:** hero: one small white drone with a sample box, crisp, crossing the river. Far out: the Thames
  winding east past the City towers to Canary Wharf; below, the jammed bridges. Nature: the river, plane trees, gulls
  at drone height. Frame 0: a courier van crawling in a red line of brake lights (expected); a drone lifts off the
  hospital roof and is across the river before the van moves.
- **So what:** faster answers for sick children's families; yes, people would want it (DroneXL's contrast: a
  Massachusetts town voted 510 to 13 against Amazon drones dropping toothpaste; London said yes for a child's blood).
- **A series?** Yes: medical drones in Swedish towns (defibrillators, Everdrone; a Stockholm launch due this autumn),
  Rouen, Fort Collins (drones on firehouse roofs, anywhere in the city in about 90 s; Route Fifty, 27 Oct 2026).
- **Ad or news?** Risky if it opens on a brand. First line as news: "In London, children's blood tests now fly over the
  traffic." Name no company on screen.
- **Truth:** DroneXL, 20 Oct 2026 https://dronexl.co/2026/10/20/apian-drone-flight-paths-guys-st-thomas-london-render/ ;
  DroneDJ, 20 Oct 2026 https://dronedj.com/2026/10/20/matternet-nhs-drone-children-hospital/ ; Synnovis release,
  7 Sep 2026 https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/synnovis-and-apian-partner-to-advance-drone-and-autonomous-robotics-in-healthcare-logistics-20260907 ;
  robot dogs: ianVisits, 5 Feb 2026
  https://www.ianvisits.co.uk/articles/flying-drones-and-robot-dogs-being-used-to-move-medical-samples-between-nhs-hospitals-87342/ .
  Two trade outlets plus company releases; no NHS-wide figures; the speed and cost numbers are the companies'. A pilot
  in command watches every flight; it is a few routes, not citywide.
- **Mood:** positive. **Risks:** reads as a drone ad; small scale; "faster sample" is not "faster cure"; v4's

### 3. Bears are walking into Japan's towns, so the towns hired AI, drones and robot dogs
- **Problem and fix:** Japan's bear attacks hit a record: 238 people wounded and 13 killed in fiscal 2025, then 62
  wounded and 6 killed in the first five months of fiscal 2026 (environment ministry). Villages empty out, bears move
  in, and they now walk into towns for food. Towns answer with tech: Sendai and 28 other municipalities run B Alert, AI
  cameras that spot a bear and alert officials (trained on about 60,000 images; "up to 99.9%" accuracy is the
  maker's claim); Showa village (1,000 people) flies a thermal drone that sees a bear's warm trail under the trees
  (nine bears caught); a University of Tokyo startup tests four-legged robots to patrol the edge of town; Sophia
  University's AI map of likely encounters is about 70% accurate; Shibetsu's hunters share GPS positions live.
- **Why now:** The Washington Post, republished 16 Oct 2026 (headline: AI, four-legged robots and pepper-spraying
  drones); encounters are on pace to beat last year's record; autumn is the peak (bears feed before winter).
- **The big view:** hero: a bear at a town's edge at dusk, crisp, by a persimmon tree. Far out: forested mountains
  folding back to snow peaks; the town's lights below. Nature: autumn maples, persimmons, mist. Frame 0: a quiet
  street at dawn, a man steps out for the paper (the expected morning); a bear is standing by the gate, then a
  drone's thermal view finds three more on the hill.
- **So what:** "would you want AI watching your street for bears?" splits people in a good, shareable way.
- **A series?** Partly: cities using tech against wildlife (Mumbai's leopards, Florida's AI mosquito traps, LA's
  wildlife bridge opening 2 Dec 2026).
- **Ad or news?** News. First line: "Bears are walking into Japan's towns. So the towns hired drones, AI and robot dogs."
- **Truth:** The Washington Post via Anchorage Daily News, 16 Oct 2026
  https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2026/10/16/as-bear-attacks-rise-japan-turns-to-ai-4-legged-robots-and-pepper-spraying-drones/ ;

  One fresh outlet (syndicated); the accuracy figures are the makers'; robots are a test; fiscal vs calendar years
  differ; captured bears are usually killed. An expert warns bears get used to new deterrents.
- **Mood:** mixed (fear, culls; tech that protects people). **Risks:** animal death; fear-led; the robot is only a

### 4. Indonesia starts a 575 km sea wall to save its sinking north coast, and the first thing it builds is trees
- **Problem and fix:** Jakarta and Java's north coast are sinking (groundwater pumping; parts of north Jakarta drop
  several cm a year, est.) while the sea rises; tidal floods reach homes, ports and airports. The fix: the Giant Sea
  Wall, about 575 km of offshore and onshore dikes plus mangroves from Banten to East Java, about 20 years of work,
  said to protect about 55 million people (government figure). Jakarta's own share grew from 12 to 19 km. Cost: up to
  about US$80 billion (search summary). The capital itself is moving to Nusantara on Borneo.
- **Why now:** 7 Oct 2026, the infrastructure ministry: the groundbreaking is "this October", "within one or two
  weeks", starting with mangroves; the concrete ("grey") works start in 2027. (Jakarta had targeted September.)
  Whether the ceremony happened by 28 Oct is not confirmed.
- **The big view:** hero: a section of sea wall, crisp, with a mangrove belt in front. Far out: the Java Sea to the
  horizon one way; inland, volcano cones (Gede-Pangrango, est.) behind Jakarta's towers. Nature: mangroves, egrets,
  mud flats, monsoon clouds. Frame 0: a fishing village's street at high tide, water ankle-deep (expected: flood);
  the camera rises and the whole coastline is lower than the sea beside it.
- **So what:** one of the biggest bets any country has made against the sea; would you want it: it splits people
  (some call it a costly folly; mangroves first is the hopeful part).
- **A series?** Yes: big barriers (Venice's MOSE, the Thames Barrier, Rotterdam's Maeslantkering, New York's plans).
- **Ad or news?** News. First line: "Indonesia is starting a 575-kilometre wall against the sea. The first thing it
  builds is trees."
- **Truth:** ANTARA (state news agency), 7 Oct 2026 https://en.antaranews.com/news/434454/indonesia-to-begin-giant-sea-wall-project-with-mangroves ;
  The Jakarta Post, 22 Jan 2026 https://www.thejakartapost.com/indonesia/2026/01/22/jakarta-to-break-ground-on-giant-sea-wall-in-september ;
  The Star, 24 May 2026 https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/05/24/indonesia-presses-ahead-with-giant-sea-wall-project-along-java-coast ;

- **Why now:** the 3 Sep launch (25 days before the window); in the window: Minneapolis's council voted 7-6 on 1 Oct to

### 6. Venice's hidden wall: 78 gates rise from the seabed, and now they may be choking the lagoon
- **Problem and fix:** high tides ("acqua alta") flooded Venice for centuries (1966: 194 cm; Nov 2019: 187 cm; both
  est.). Since Oct 2020, MOSE: 78 hollow steel gates lie flat in the seabed at the lagoon's three inlets; when a tide
  over 110 cm is forecast, air is pumped in and they swing up, cutting the lagoon off from the Adriatic. Raised well
  over 100 times since 2020 (sources differ: about 130 to 156); 24 times in the first 50 days of 2026; each raise costs
  about EUR 200,000-300,000 (L'Indipendente, Feb 2026).
- **Why now:** the autumn season's first high tide: on 20 Oct 2026 the city forecast 110 cm for 9:20 on 21 Oct, "MOSE
  activation possible" (Comune di Venezia), after months of calm. The twist: a 2026 OGS study in Scientific Reports
  finds MOSE does hold the sea back, but frequent closures make the lagoon's water renew much more slowly. (A raise on
  21 Oct itself is not confirmed in what I could read.)
- **The big view:** hero: one yellow gate swinging up out of the water at the Lido inlet, crisp. Far out: on a clear
  autumn day, the Alps' snow line across the lagoon behind the city (a real view, est. visibility); the Adriatic on the
  other side. Nature: the lagoon's salt marshes, egrets, the tide itself. Frame 0: St Mark's Square with water rising
  between the paving stones (the expected flood); cut out to the sea: a wall of gates is standing up out of the waves.
- **So what:** a city that switches off the sea; would you want it: yes, but the study says the cure has a cost.
- **A series?** Yes: big barriers (Java's sea wall, case 4; the Thames Barrier; Rotterdam's Maeslantkering).
- **Ad or news?** News. First line: "Venice has a wall you've never seen. It lies on the seabed, and this week it stood
  up."
- **Truth:** ilmeteo.net, 20 Oct 2026 https://www.ilmeteo.net/notizie/previsioni/minimo-sul-golfo-di-venezia-e-scirocco-sull-adriatico-torna-l-acqua-alta-a-venezia-in-azione-il-mose.html
  (quotes the city's alert and the study: Melaku Canu et al., "Cumulative impact of climate change and adaptation
  measures on Venice and its lagoon", Sci Rep, 2026); costs: L'Indipendente, 19 Feb 2026
  https://www.lindipendente.online/2026/02/19/venezia-il-mose-costa-200mila-euro-ogni-alta-marea-ma-piazza-san-marco-e-sempre-allagata/ .
  St Mark's Square still floods below the threshold (glass walls protect the basilica). The 2019 and 1966 levels are
  from memory (est.): check before use.
- **Mood:** positive with a twist. **Risks:** the 21 Oct raise needs confirming; Venice is a familiar story (2020's
  first raise went viral); the study needs reading in full before any claim.

### 7. Cities are building subway lines in the sky
- **Problem and fix:** hillside districts that buses crawl up and metros can't reach. Cable cars run as public
  transport on a normal ticket: La Paz and El Alto (11 lines, about 79 million trips in 2024, search summary); Mexico
  City (Line 2 in Iztapalapa, 10.5 km, called the world's longest urban line); Paris's first, Câble C1 (4.5 km,
  5 stations, opened 13 Dec 2025, about 10,000 riders a day and 1.8 million trips by Aug 2026, search summary).
- **Why now:** 15 Oct 2026, Panama's president awarded the contract for a 6.6 km, six-station cable car in San
  Miguelito, one of the country's densest districts (2,800 riders an hour each way; service from 2029). In Santiago,
  Chile's first public-transport cable car (Providencia to Huechuraba in 13 min, about US$80m) is 85% built; it won an
  infrastructure award on 23 Oct and should open in 2027, amid a fight over its fare.
- **The big view:** hero: one cabin, crisp, gliding over rooftops. Far out: in La Paz, Illimani (6,438 m) above the
  city; in Paris, the Eiffel Tower on the horizon; in Panama, the canal's ships. Nature: the steep hillside, eucalyptus
  or palms, clouds below the cabin. Frame 0: a bus stuck on a hairpin road up a slope (expected); a line of cabins
  floats over it, straight up the hill.
- **So what:** an hour's commute cut to minutes for people on the hills; yes, people want it (ridership beats
  forecasts in Paris).
- **A series?** Yes, it is one (Medellín, Bogotá, Santo Domingo, Toulouse, Ajaccio, Uruapan).
- **Ad or news?** News; no brand on screen (the cable makers are big firms). First line: "Some cities are building
  their newest subway line in the sky."
- **Truth:** La República, 20 Oct 2026 https://www.larepublica.co/globoeconomia/doppelmayr-construira-teleferico-de-6-6-kilometros-en-san-miguelito-panama-4506917 ;
  Pauta, 23 Oct 2026 https://www.pauta.cl/actualidad/2026/10/23/teleferico-bicentenario-recibe-premio-de-infraestructura-y-proyecta-apertura-para-2027.html ;

  Paris and La Paz figures are search summaries (the operators' sites blocked us). The fresh pegs are a contract and a
  build, not an opening.
- **Mood:** positive. **Risks:** no opening in the window; the "world's biggest network" title is disputed (La Paz vs
  Mexico City); a cable maker could look like the hero (ad risk).
