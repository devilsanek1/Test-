### 2. A fridge-size data centre in orbit that can only think for 15 minutes (Google's test satellite)

Power about 1 kW of solar (est., from reports). Planned life about a year; two more satellites in 2027 to test laser links. The project lead: not cheaper than the ground "in the next five years". Context: Starcloud flew an Nvidia H100 in Nov 2025; SpaceX filed on 30 Jan 2026 for up to one million data-centre satellites.
- **Why now:** launched 1 Oct 2026; Google confirmed contact and normal operation (technews.tw, 2 Oct; search summary).

### 4. Stockholm's internet lives 30 m under granite, in a Cold War bunker (Pionen) [owner's pick]

## Weird places as a recurring kind? (the owner's question)

Yes, with conditions. Supply: at least 10 real places in this file alone: a Cold War bunker (Stockholm), a skate-park bunker (Gothenburg, Q1 2027), a chapel (Barcelona), pools (Devon, Manchester), a working mine (Dolomites), the seabed (Shanghai), orbit (the fridge), a cathedral's bomb shelter (Helsinki), coal pits (Kuzbass, proposed) and port hulls (Japan, concept); backups: a mine in a Norwegian fjord (Lefdal), a former NATO munitions bunker near Stavanger (UpCloud, Jan 2026).

That is a clean spine per film and a promise viewers can learn.
It fits the look: each place is a poster view with a far-out anchor (Stockholm's islands, the Dolomites, Barcelona to the sea, the Earth's curve).
Conditions: one place per film, never a list; a current peg every time (an opening, a vote, a strike, a launch); alternate the mood; never lead with the operator (they love the PR); vary the reveal (drop through rock, dive under the sea, rise to orbit, open a church door) so the "hidden server hall" beat doesn't repeat.
Risks: most places are old news with thin pegs (chapel and pool: May 2026), and a cool place with no "why" goes flat. A possible run: orbit (1 Oct) -> bunker (war peg) -> Helsinki heat (winter) -> chapel -> mine -> seabed.
