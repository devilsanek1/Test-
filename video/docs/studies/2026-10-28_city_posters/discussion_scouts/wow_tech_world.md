### 1. A tower in Texas will try to catch a spaceship falling from orbit
- **What happened:** 28 Sep 2026: SpaceX's Starship (about 121 m tall, the biggest rocket ever) reached orbit for the
  first time on its 14th test flight and dropped off 26 Starlink satellites. The booster splashed down in the Gulf of
  Mexico; the ship came down early in the Pacific and blew up seconds after touching the water. 20 Oct: on SpaceX's
  results call, Elon Musk said Flight 15 (expected in November) will try to catch BOTH stages with the launch tower's
  arms. Boosters have been caught since Oct 2024; a ship coming back from orbit never has.
- **Why now:** the 20 Oct call; the attempt is due in November (no date, FAA licence pending).
- **The big view:** hero: the launch tower (about 145 m, est.) with its two "chopstick" arms. Far out: the ship's
  glowing streak across the whole sky, from orbit (about 27,000 km/h, est.) down to the coast. Nature: the dunes and
  salt flats of Boca Chica, the Gulf, the Rio Grande's mouth. Frame 0: a rocket landing the expected way (legs on a
  pad, or a splash in the sea), then the arms swing shut around a falling ship.
- **So what:** a rocket you can catch and refly like a plane is what makes space cheap: satellite internet, NASA's moon
  lander (it is a Starship).
- **Ad or news?** News, if the line is the event: "Next month, this tower tries to catch a spaceship falling from
  orbit." Never open on the brand or on Musk.
- **Truth:** CNN live, 28 Sep 2026 https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/28/science/live-news/spacex-starship-flight-14-launch ;
  NPR, 28 Sep https://www.npr.org/2026/09/28/nx-s1-5983418/spacex-starship-first-orbital-flight-14-nasa ; NBC
  https://www.nbcnews.com/science/space/spacex-starship-rocket-launches-orbit-first-time-rcna600222 ; Space.com on the
  20 Oct call https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-will-attempt-1st-ever-double-starship-rocket-catch-on-next-test-launch-a-profound-technology-milestone-elon-musk-says .
  Flight 14 is fact; the catch is a company plan (Musk: "more likely than not" it works), date unset.
- **Mood:** positive (wonder); polarising owner.
- **Risks:** the date slips or it fails (then the story is "they tried"); Musk in the comments; booster catches are old
  news, so the film must be clear that this is the SHIP, back from orbit; a space story needs the coast as its world.

### 2. Germany built a wind turbine taller than the Eiffel Tower, on old coal land
- **What happened:** Schipkau, Lusatia (eastern Germany, a coal-mining region for decades): GICON's pilot turbine
  reached full height in late Sep 2026: 365 m to the blade tip, hub at 300 m, 3 m shorter than Berlin's TV tower
  (the Eiffel Tower is about 330 m). No crane reaches that high: the turbine was fitted at about 150 m and a hydraulic
  lift borrowed from bridge building hauled it up, about 9 m an hour. Built for SPRIND, Germany's agency for
  breakthrough ideas. Small (3.8 MW); grid connection due in November.
- **Why now:** Euronews 30 Sep and 2 Oct, Electrek 2 Oct, New Atlas 9 Oct; switch-on in November.
- **The big view:** hero: the one giant turbine, a lattice tower with the rotor on top. Far out: ordinary turbines that
  barely reach its waist, and the region's coal plant cooling towers on the horizon (Schwarze Pumpe, about 30 km
  east, est.). Nature: lakes in flooded open-pit mines, pine forest, sand. Frame 0: a normal wind turbine on a field;
  the camera tilts up and the tower keeps going, past where turbines stop.
- **So what:** the wind 300 m up is stronger and steadier, so old wind farms could grow up instead of out: more power
  from the same land.
- **Ad or news?** News: "Germany just built a wind turbine taller than the Eiffel Tower." Name GICON once, late.
- **Truth:** Euronews, 30 Sep 2026 https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/30/worlds-tallest-wind-turbine-being-built-in-germanys-former-coal-mining-region ;
  Electrek, 2 Oct https://electrek.co/2026/10/02/the-worlds-tallest-wind-turbine-just-reached-1197-feet/ ; New Atlas
  https://newatlas.com/energy/gicon-telescoping-wind-turbine-world-record-height/ . Height is built fact; "about
  double the output" is the developer's claim, not yet measured. Work stopped in late 2025 over faulty steel parts.
- **Mood:** positive (a coal region turned to wind, clean energy).
- **Risks:** one pilot, small output; the "tallest" is a height record, not a power record (China's 26 MW prototype is
  bigger); November grid date could slip.
- **Scores:** R 7, T 8, V 9, M 9, F 9, S 7; weighted 8.1. WOW 8.

### 3. Norway will blast a tunnel through a mountain, for ships
- **What happened:** Norway's Coastal Administration (Kystverket) is building the world's first full-size tunnel for
  sea-going ships through the Stad peninsula on the west coast: about 1.7 km long, about 50 m high and 36 m wide, big
  enough for the coastal ferries. Cost frame NOK 8.6 billion (parliament, June 2026); builder AF Gruppen; work starts
  early 2027 and takes about five years. The sea off Stad is the coast's most dangerous stretch: waves from several
  directions at once, ships waiting days for calm.
- **Why now:** 7 Oct 2026, the government's 2027 budget proposes NOK 1.05 billion to start construction (vote in Dec).
- **The big view:** hero: the tunnel mouth cut into a sheer mountain wall, a coastal ship sailing in. Far out: the
  open Atlantic around the cape, storm waves, the long coast. Nature: fjord, cliffs, green slopes, sea birds. Frame 0:
  a ship heads for the mountain (expected: it turns out into the storm around the cape), then it sails straight in.
- **So what:** ferries and freight that run in any weather, and fewer ships in danger on a deadly bit of sea.
- **Ad or news?** News: "Norway is about to blast a tunnel through a mountain, for ships."
- **Truth:** Kystverket project page https://kystverket.no/en/fairway/stad-ship-tunnel/ ; regjeringen.no, 2027 budget,
  7 Oct 2026 https://www.regjeringen.no/no/statsbudsjett/2027/fylkesoversikten/statsbudsjettet-2027-more-og-romsdal/id3175019/ ;
  Marine Insight https://www.marineinsight.com/construction-on-worlds-first-ship-tunnel-to-begin-in-2027-as-norway-secures-funding .
  Planned and funded, not built; the budget isn't adopted yet; older articles give other sizes (use Kystverket's).
- **Mood:** positive.
- **Risks:** the peg is a budget line, not an event; it opens about 2032 (est.); "world's first" needs its qualifier
  (canal tunnels exist; this is the first for sea-going ships); cost overruns in its past.
- **Scores:** R 7, T 8, V 10, M 8, F 7, S 8; weighted 8.0. WOW 9.
