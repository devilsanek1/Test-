### 1. Germany built a wind turbine taller than the Eiffel Tower, on old coal land
- **What happened:** in Schipkau, Lusatia (eastern Germany's coal country), GICON's pilot turbine reached full height in
  late Sep 2026: 365 m to the tip, hub at 300 m (the Eiffel Tower: about 330 m). No crane reaches that high: a lift
  borrowed from bridge building hauled it up from about 150 m, 9 m an hour. Built for SPRIND, Germany's breakthrough
  agency; a small 3.8 MW pilot.
- **Why now:** full height reported 30 Sep to 9 Oct; grid connection due in November.
- **The big view:** hero: the one giant turbine on its lattice tower. Far out: normal turbines that reach its waist, the
  coal plant's cooling towers on the horizon (Schwarze Pumpe, about 30 km east, est.). Nature: lakes in flooded
  open-pit mines, pine forest, sand. Frame 0: a normal turbine on a field; the camera tilts up and the tower keeps going.
- **So what:** the wind 300 m up is stronger and steadier, so wind farms could grow up instead of out.
- **Ad or news?** News: "Germany just built a wind turbine taller than the Eiffel Tower." GICON named once, late.
- **Truth:** Euronews, 30 Sep 2026 https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/30/worlds-tallest-wind-turbine-being-built-in-germanys-former-coal-mining-region ;
  Electrek, 2 Oct https://electrek.co/2026/10/02/the-worlds-tallest-wind-turbine-just-reached-1197-feet/ ; New Atlas
  https://newatlas.com/energy/gicon-telescoping-wind-turbine-world-record-height/ . The height is built fact (3 m short
  of Berlin's TV tower); "about double the output" is the developer's claim, unmeasured. Work stopped in late 2025 over
  faulty steel parts.
- **Mood:** positive (coal land turned to wind). **Risks:** one small pilot; a height record, not a power record
  (China's 26 MW prototype is bigger); the November date could slip.

### 2. Norway will blast a tunnel through a mountain, for ships
- **What happened:** Norway's Coastal Administration (Kystverket) is building the world's first full-size tunnel for
  sea-going ships, through the Stad peninsula: about 1.7 km long, 50 m high, 36 m wide, big enough for the coastal
  ferries. Cost frame NOK 8.6 bn (parliament, June 2026); builder AF Gruppen; about five years of work from early 2027.
  The sea off Stad is the coast's most dangerous: waves from several directions at once, ships waiting days for calm.
- **Why now:** 7 Oct 2026: the government's 2027 budget proposes NOK 1.05 bn to start building (vote in December).
- **The big view:** hero: the tunnel mouth cut into a sheer mountain wall, a coastal ship sailing in. Far out: the open
  Atlantic around the cape, storm waves, the long coast. Nature: fjord, cliffs, green slopes, sea birds. Frame 0: a ship
  heads for the mountain (expected: it turns out into the storm around the cape), then it sails straight in.
- **So what:** ferries and freight that run in any weather, and fewer ships in danger on a deadly bit of sea.
- **Ad or news?** News: "Norway is about to blast a tunnel through a mountain, for ships."
- **Truth:** Kystverket https://kystverket.no/en/fairway/stad-ship-tunnel/ ; regjeringen.no, 2027 budget, 7 Oct 2026
  https://www.regjeringen.no/no/statsbudsjett/2027/fylkesoversikten/statsbudsjettet-2027-more-og-romsdal/id3175019/ ;

- **Mood:** positive. **Risks:** the peg is a budget line; it opens about 2032 (est.); "world's first" needs its

### 4. The software that kept the first Moon landing going
- **What happened:** Margaret Hamilton, who led MIT's team writing Apollo's flight software, died on 30 Sep 2026, aged
  90. The story it brings back (NASA history, to check): minutes before Apollo 11 touched down in July 1969, the
  computer flashed "1202" alarms: overloaded. The software dropped the less important jobs and kept landing.
- **Why now:** her death, reported 8-9 Oct by AP, NPR and WBUR; NASA's chief paid tribute.
- **The big view:** hero: the lunar lander dropping toward grey craters. Far out: the Earth hanging in the black sky
  (the posters' "something WAY far out", literally). Nature: Moon dust, long shadows. Second place: Mission Control.
  Frame 0: a lander drifting down (expected: a calm landing), then alarm lights flash, and keep flashing as it lands.
- **So what:** her idea, that software must expect its own errors and survive them, keeps planes and phones running.
- **Ad or news?** No ad risk: "Minutes before the first Moon landing, the computer started to overload."
- **Truth:** AP via WSB-TV https://www.wsbtv.com/news/science/margaret-hamilton/HTJQSSWQS43AZCZN355UMVWOBQ/ ; NPR, 8 Oct
  https://www.npr.org/2026/10/08/nx-s1-5994972/margaret-hamilton-obituary-software-engineer-apollo ; WBUR, 9 Oct
  https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/10/09/margaret-hamilton-nasa-apollo-mit-software-engineering-obituary . Credit is her
  team's (others at MIT designed the computer's scheduler), not hers alone.
- **Mood:** bittersweet; the story is a triumph. **Risks:** a death as the peg (a tribute, never "RIP" bait); history,
  not new tech; no likeness (a small figure, no face, beside her famous stack of code); the "she alone saved it" myth.
- **Scores:** R 7, T 8, V 9, M 7, F 8, S 8; weighted 7.8. WOW 8.

- **So what:** clean power day and night, from heat that lies under most places, not just volcano country.
