# Content fit: which stories put cities and buildings on screen, for our channel (content lens B)

**Lens:** OUR FIT. The owner's question (2026-10-28): "which type of content would be best fit into videos where we have
more of cities / architectures / buildings / etc". Study brief: `README.md` (this folder). Analysis only; nothing here is
a rule until the owner approves it. Written 2026-10-28 (content lens B); web facts from search summaries (section 3C).

## 1. Our evidence (and its limits)

(`img/content_fit_place_share.jpg`; tags in the sheet's colour bars): **P** = a street, building or skyline is the
frame's subject; **A** = a building's blueprint; **B** = a text board leads; **O** = an object or screen leads.

**What the places did in each film** (our reading, not the owner's words):
- **v1's city was the mechanism, not a backdrop.** Not a real city: each website is a building (S01's civic buildings
  are the government sites that go dark; S03's street "built from one blueprint" is the 7 in 10 sites on PHP; S06's lane
  is PHP's own workshop: `docs/v1/scenes/`). Every "so what" happens TO a building, so place, story and payoff are one
  picture: places that DO the story.
- **v2's canal was the real place where it happened** (her office's bank, the towpath, her bank; Arlington for the one
  scale reveal: `docs/v2/THROUGHLINE.md` §1.4), the look the owner likes. But the argument rode on text boards (6 of 20
  frames) and nothing in the canal changes because of the story: a stage, not the mechanism. The owner found the story
  weak, and the place did not rescue it.
- **v3's place was behind the product.** The data-centre town leads in one frame (20 s); everywhere else a shopping
  screen leads, and frame 0 read as an ad (the owner's read, playbook Audience and goal).

**What can be concluded.** (1) The ranking by place share (v1 82 % > v2 65 % > v3 5 %) agrees with the one performance
ranking we have (v1 > v3). (2) Most of the measured gap is in the first 2-3 s: v3 lost ~60 points by ~2 s, v1 ~24; after
~5 s both curves slide slowly. Instagram's skip rate is the share of views that leave within the first 3 s (added to
Insights in 2025: https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/ , https://marketing4ecommerce.net/en/retention-and-skip-rate-reels/ ,
search summaries, accessed 2026-10-28), so our numbers speak to **frame 0 and the first line**, not to the film's share
of places. (3) A place that IS the mechanism (v1) sits with our best numbers; a place as a stage for boards (v2) is the
look the owner likes with the story they found weak.

**What cannot be concluded:** that places help. v1 and v3 differ in almost everything at once: topic (a security event
vs prices), hook (news vs a sales question read as an ad), frame 0 (buildings vs a shopping app), length (55 vs 41 s),
line (v1's ink vs the clean pen), window (~24 h vs an early check), account age (post 1 on 25 Oct vs post 2 on 28 Oct,
est. from the logs), posting hour (not logged). v2 has no numbers. Two data points with no control cannot separate
"places" from "not an ad", and Instagram's distribution is noisy from post to post (est.).

**The owner's hypothesis, stated as one:** *videos whose world is cities, architecture and buildings will look better and
perform better for us.* Split it so it can fail:
- **H1 (the opening):** a frame 0 where a place changes (a building goes dark, a street floods, a skyline lights up)
  lowers the skip rate against a frame 0 led by an object or screen, for the same story and the same first line.
- **H2 (the film):** films where the place carries the mechanism (v1's way) hold viewers better after 5 s and earn more
  saves and shares than films where places are a stage (v2's way).

**What the next 2-3 videos can measure** (cheap; no control film made on purpose):
1. **One film, two openings, as Instagram Trial Reels** (shown to non-followers only, read after ~24-72 h; tested May
   2024, launched Dec 2024: https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/31/instagram-tests-trial-reels-that-dont-display-to-a-creators-followers ,
   https://business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/instagram-introduces-trial-reels-feature-what-it-is-how-it-works-more-124121100429_1.html ,
   accessed 2026-10-28; eligibility unclear, some guides say 1,000+ followers: check the account). Opening A: the place
   event at frame 0; B: the same line over the thread object in close-up; only the first ~3 s differ (cost S: one extra
   shot). Compare skip rate, 3 s hold and average watch at the same hour. Tests H1.
2. **One log line per film, always the same** (an addition to the playbook's Performance log: the orchestrator's call):
   place share (P frames at 1 per 2 s, as in the sheet), frame-0 type (place event / object / person / screen), the
   ad-or-news verdict, archetype (section 2), length, post day, hour and number; Instagram at **exactly 24 h** (v1's
   numbers are 24 h, v3's an early check: not comparable): views, % non-followers, skip rate, retention at 2 / 5 / 15 s,
   average watch, shares and saves per 1,000 views. Tests H2 over time.
3. **A decision rule set before the results (est.):** if the place-led opening beats the object-led one by 5+ skip-rate
   points in 2 of 2-3 trials, "a place event at frame 0" becomes a default; if not, places stay a look choice (the owner
   likes it: reason enough), not a growth lever. Sampling error (95 %, binomial, est.): about ±2 points at 2,000 views
   per opening, ±4 at 500; the bigger noise is which audience Instagram picks.

**1. Nature against the city, with tech on the city's side** (quake alerts, storm and flood forecasts, fire cameras).
The force is the one scale moment (the storm over the Gulf, the ring of shaking), the town under it the home place, its
skyline the hero, the warning itself the thread (the forecast line, the alert racing ahead of the shaking, a wisp of
smoke). The hook is physical: the expected thing (every model says Louisiana), then reality overtaking it (the storm
turns; phones light up before the cups rattle). An event, not a product, so it passes "ad or news?" while the event
leads. Mood: hopeful when the warning worked, gentle around deaths. Supply: high this month (Isaias and Panama's M7.7 on
9 Oct, Japan's false alarm 16 Oct, California's fire cameras 14-15 Oct); est. 2-4 pegs a month in season. Risks: one
storm proves little; vendor-on-vendor sources (Google's AI, Google's alerts); maps and arrows instead of a drawn world (r4).

**2=. The system drawn as a city** (v1's form: an invisible system, such as the web, a network or your phone's apps,
drawn as a town where each building is one part). The only archetype we have numbers for (v1: 82 % place-led frames,
skip rate 32.2 %, with section 1's limits). Every "so what" happens to a building, so it reads with the sound off. Hook:
the building does what the news says (it goes dark; it opens its door to a stranger). Mood: mostly bad news (outages,
flaws, hacks): the slot after v4. Supply: weekly (est.; the ideas bank's "a backup that's a twin"). Risks: the metaphor
must map one-to-one and read cold (playbook: "never a metaphor the facts don't support or that needs explaining"); a
made-up town lacks the posters' real-city charm unless it borrows one city's flavour; PHP again is out (a past topic).

**2=. The hidden machine under the city** (data centres, subsea cables, chip fabs, substations, cooling water). The
posters' patchwork city with one giant hidden among ordinary roofs, shown by a cutaway or the one pull-back; the thread
is what travels (a message as light, a watt, a drop of cooling water). Hook: the everyday act (you hit send), then
reality (it crosses an ocean in a cable about as thick as a garden hose: verify first); as news it needs an event at the
machine (a cut cable, a town's vote, a heat deal). AI's build-out is the fuss of 2026 (bills, water, noise), but mostly
as costs. Supply: weekly (est.). Risks: v3 was data centres and your wallet (a new angle only, never through a shopping
screen); logos (draw it unbranded); a grey box is dull unless the reveal is the event.

**4. The street that works differently now** (robotaxis, delivery and defibrillator drones, driverless shuttles). One
crossing the viewer knows is the home place, the new machine the thread moving through it; life is built in (cafés,
bikes, crowds: the posters' density). Hook: the expected street (a red light, a driver), then the change (nobody at the
wheel; a box lands before the ambulance). Mood: mixed (safety vs jobs and trust). Supply: weekly (Waymo's Toronto,
Madrid and Las Vegas news 16-27 Oct, `v4/scouting_r4/tech.md`; the drone-AED study 25 Sep). Risks: a branded robotaxi in
frame 0 is an ad; pilots and company claims (T 6); a weak "so what" outside that city.

**5. Two places, two choices** (one city bans, another adopts: smart glasses at pools, phones in schools, age checks).
The fuss is built in and fairness gets its own place, but two places halve the coziness unless drawn as one street with
two sides (CRAFT §a: at most 3 places). Hook: "here it's banned; across the river it's handed out" (news if both
decisions are real). Supply: est. 2-4 a month. Risks: a listicle feel; politics. v4's glasses have this shape.

**6=. The place where it happened** (a real place anchors a story about something else). It charms (v2's canal is the
look the owner likes), but the mechanism lives elsewhere (a contract, a fear), so the place becomes a stage and boards
carry the argument (v2: 6 of 20 frames). Risks: generic travel content, the company's logo on its HQ, a real person's
likeness. The default setting for any story, never the reason to pick one.

**6=. A building as the protagonist** (a data centre heating a town, a 3D-printed block, a fusion hall). The purest
poster match, but tech news rarely makes a building the news (none found in the window: section 3C), such stories are
often curiosities without a "so what", and they sit close to the builder's press release (ad risk). Best as the hero
inside #2.

**8. Your city from above** (satellites and AI map roofs, trees, heat or lost cities). The orbit-to-roof zoom is a superb
scale move, but a top-down map is the opposite of the posters' raised three-quarter view and flattens depth (CRAFT §f),
surveillance worries come with it, and nothing fresh turned up. One scale shot inside another archetype at most.

**9. The map story** (one event across many places). The weakest fit: many places and no home break the through-line
card, and maps drift into infographics. Only as one home place plus one pull-back to the globe.

## 4. Recommendation and open questions

**The lesson under all of it:** our best film did not show a pretty city, it made the story happen TO buildings (v1);
the look the owner likes put a lovely place behind a story whose mechanism lived on boards (v2). So pick stories whose
mechanism can happen to a place, then draw that place the way the posters do (one hero landmark, one home, one sky).
Place fit is a test of the story, not of the set.

**Directions for the channel, in order:**
1. **Nature against the city, tech on the city's side** (rank 8.12): the best place fit that also supplies positive,
   AI-led news now; one town under one sky is both the posters' composition and our through-line card (one home place,
   one scale reveal, the warning as the thread). Start v4 here.
2. **The system drawn as a city, and the hidden machine under it** (7.76 each): the place IS the mechanism (v1's way);
   the home for the channel's core tech stories (outages, flaws, AI's power and water), mostly bad news, so the slot
   after v4. A real place (a real cable station, a real data-centre town) where the facts allow; never via a shop screen.
3. **The street that works differently now** (7.40): robots, drones and driverless cars in one real street; the most
   life and density, but weaker truth and real ad risk: only when a public result or a person leads, never a launch.
   Not directions: the place as a stage (v2), the map story, the view from above; a building as the hero lives in #2.

**Candidates now (v4 needs a positive story):**
- **The AI that called the hurricane** (rank 7.96, place fit 9): the most place-led checked story on the shortlist, AI
  as the hero, kept fresh by NPR (19 and 26 Oct). Frame 0: every forecast line points west, then the storm bends toward
  one Panhandle town.
- **Panama's quake, then its phones** (F1, 8.08): the quake-alert story with the real-quake peg r4 asked for; needs the
  fact lock (Panama in Google's 15 Oct list; no phone-warning claim for 9 Oct; not AI on Google's pages: AI-adjacent).
- **The glasses** stay first even with place fit (8.13): the study does not overturn r4's pick, it adds the place-led
  alternative; if picked, stage them place-first (frame 0: the ban sign on a pool gate, then the café street).

**The experiment** (section 1): v4 place-led with a place event at frame 0, logged the section-1 way at exactly 24 h;
two openings as Trial Reels if the account qualifies; over v5-v6, one film where the place is the mechanism and one
where it is only a stage (H2). The decision rule is fixed before the numbers come in.
