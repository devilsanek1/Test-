# v8 frame card: scene 1, "She quit Google." (planned 2026-10-31 before building; FRAME_CARD.md)

The owner on v7: "it does look better overall but we lost the feeling ... the scene now is about a dark alley whete
nothing happens but the life and action is rather on the background". This card starts from the feeling; every answer
is a choice for THIS beat. Numbers appear only where they name a camera or a position, never as a target.

## 1. The moment and the feeling
- **What happens:** she has just quit Google. She pushes out through the lobby's glass door onto the canal-side street
  and takes her first steps into her own evening.
- **What the viewer should feel:** the quiet thrill and nerve of stepping out on her own; the evening opening up in
  front of her; a little surprising; alive. Why here: this is frame 0 of the film. The hook turns on "quitting should
  set you free" and the next line takes that away ("her contract bans criticizing it, for life"). So this frame has to
  sell the freedom (open, warm, moving) for the twist to land. A lonely dark street says the opposite.
- **What they understand in one second, sound off:** a young woman walking out of a big glass office into a beautiful
  evening by the water.

## 2. The idea
- **"Out of the glass, into the gold."** Behind her, the office: a tall cool glass wall with a warm lit lobby and its
  door still swinging. In front of her, the canal runs away to a low sun, and the evening light finds her as she steps
  out: it rims her hair and face, and the wind off the water lifts her hair and coat. The city is out enjoying its
  evening; she is about to join it.
- **Why stop scrolling:** the first breath after a big decision, a moment people recognise and wish for, shown as warm
  light on a person under a big glowing sky.
- **Belongs to the film:** the canal on one October evening (the film's home place), the Google building, her.

## 3. Life and action
- **Her (the action, in front):** mid-stride, a few steps out of the door, walking toward the light; chin lifted,
  looking out toward the sun; hair and long coat lifting in the breeze.
- **The life around her (supporting, small, warm, low contrast):** people on the canal-side further along (a couple
  strolling, someone on the canal edge, a cyclist), a narrowboat on the water with a lit window and a wisp of chimney
  smoke (Pip hidden on its roof), lit windows on the far bank, birds toward the sun, a few leaves blowing past her feet.
  They say "the evening is alive and ordinary"; they never compete with her (smaller, softer, backlit like her).

## 4. The eye
- **First:** her, where the hardest value step (her dark coat against the bright water and sky), the hottest light (the
  sun's gold on her hair and face) and the motion meet.
- **Second:** the sun at the end of the canal, where she is looking and going (kept calm: low, half behind the far roofs).
- **Third:** the caption above; then the glowing door behind her (where she came from); then the life.
- **What leads:** her gaze and stride point at the sun; the canal, the kerb and the glass lines run to it; the sun's
  path on the water and her long shadow on the stones lead back to her.
- **One place to look:** her. The building is one quiet mass, the sky and the near street the two quiet fields.

## 5. Space and scale
- **Camera:** on the canal-side street, a few metres from her, at the eye height of someone sitting at a café table
  there (a little below hers), so she stands up into the big evening sky.
- **Distance:** near (the street's stones, her long shadow, a leaf), middle (her, the door, the canal edge, the boat),
  far (the far bank, a bridge, the city's towers in the haze under the sun). Overlap (she overlaps the canal and the far
  bank), air (the far planes paler and closer to the sky), perspective (everything runs to the sunset).
- **Scale:** the glass building goes up out of the frame; the canal and the city go on past the sun; she is a person at
  her real size inside that.

## 6. Light, value and colour
- **Time and light:** the golden half hour before an October sunset. The low sun at the end of the canal is the key and
  the story light: it rims her (hair, face, shoulders, coat edge), lays a warm wash on the stones with long shadows
  toward us, and a glitter path on the water. The lobby's warm lamps behind her are a step under it (a pool on the
  stones, a warm edge on her back). The office glass stays in its own cool shade.
- **How light or dark, and why:** a luminous middle key: a bright sky and water, a warm lit street, soft rose-violet
  shade. The darks are few and they serve the eye: her coat, her shadow, the door's frame. Nothing in front of her is a
  dark slab. Why: the feeling is opening and hope, not solitude.
- **Colour key:** the sunset's own family (gold, peach, coral-rose, lilac in the shade and at the top of the sky). The
  one hot accent is the sun's gold on her. The glass building is the one cool field (slate violet): the office she
  leaves.

## 7. Shapes, edges and paint
- **Shape language:** the office tall, straight and hard; everything ahead of her soft and flowing (hair and coat in the
  wind, rippling water, long clouds streaming toward the light).
- **Crisp:** her, the door's frame. **Painterly:** the far bank, the far city, the clouds. **Painted by hand:** the sky,
  the water, the stones, the glass and stone of the building.

## 8. Words
- "She quit Google." in the caption band over open sky, seated right of the building's edge. No words in the picture.

## 9. Motion and sound
- **What moves:** the door swinging shut behind her, her stride, her hair and coat in the breeze, leaves skittering,
  glints on the water, the boat, birds. The camera is still.
- **Sound:** the door's soft close behind her, then the evening opening up (water, distant voices, a bike bell); the door
  is the one sound that meets the picture.

## 10. The hand-off
- Her gaze and the light pull forward, toward the sun: "what happens to her now?" In the film the next shot dives into
  her office window; from this camera that window is above the frame, so the join needs a tilt up the glass (noted, not
  solved in a still).

## 11. The test (to fill in after building)
- The feeling it gives, in three words, at phone size; does it match section 1? Worth pausing on? One whole picture?
- Mistakes: caption readable in its band; the app's buttons and bars clear of anything that must read; nothing
  important cut by an edge; no banding.
