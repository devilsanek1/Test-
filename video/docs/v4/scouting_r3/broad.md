## 1. Every model said Louisiana. The AI said Florida, before the storm even existed
- **What happened, when, who:** Hurricane Isaias (2026 Atlantic season) became a depression on 6 Oct, was named on 7 Oct, became the season's first hurricane on 8 Oct (tying the 1905 record for the latest first hurricane), peaked at Category 3, and made landfall near Destin, Florida (western Panhandle) on Fri 9 Oct ~8 pm ET (01:30 UTC 10 Oct) as a Category 2, 105 mph. Nine US deaths, at least $2 bn damage, ~900,000 power customers out across FL/AL/GA. Early on, the physics-based models (US GFS, European IFS) pointed at a tropical-storm landfall in Louisiana or Mississippi; Google DeepMind's AI model (and other AI models) favoured Alabama/Florida and a possible major hurricane, before the system had even formed (Wikipedia, citing the forecast record). NPR's 26 Oct feature (in window): the DeepMind model called the western Panhandle "right out of the gate". The US National Hurricane Center (NHC) has blended DeepMind's model into its official forecasts since 2025; 2026 is its second season.
- **What AI did / didn't do, stage:** Did: the best TRACK (where the storm goes). Didn't: intensity (how strong) trailed NHC's own blended forecast; it doesn't decide anything (human forecasters blend AI and physics models and issue the warnings every 6 h); its runs jump around more than NHC's; nobody can say why it is right ("black box"). Stage: in operational use, measured. Season verification by former NHC branch chief James Franklin (via Michael Lowry's Substack, data to 6 Sept): DeepMind the most accurate track guidance in the Atlantic and Pacific, up to ~30% better than NHC's official 5-day track forecast. That verification is independent but informal (not NHC's official end-of-season report, not peer-reviewed).
- **Sources:** NPR, Jessica Meszaros, 2026-10-26, https://www.npr.org/2026/10/26/nx-s1-6012820/ai-hurricane-forecast-google-deepmind-isaias (read via text.npr.org); CNN 2026-10-08 "The National Hurricane Center's big bet on AI" https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/08/weather/hurricane-isaias-ai-models-google (headline/snippet only); Wikipedia "Hurricane Isaias (2026)" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Isaias_(2026) (storm facts, forecast section); NPR 2026-10-09 landfall https://www.npr.org/2026/10/09/nx-s1-5996396/hurricane-isaias-updates; Fix the News summary ("outperformed on path tracking but struggled with storm intensity"); Michael Lowry, Eye on the Tropics, 2026-09-10 https://michaelrlowry.substack.com/p/the-ai-model-that-continues-to-blow; NOAA Q&A with NHC's Wallace Hogsett https://www.weather.gov/news/261102-AI-Hurricane-Forecasting ("None of the models are perfect, and they never will be.").
- **So-what for an ordinary person, and when:** a better 3-5-day track = the right towns evacuate and the wrong ones don't (time, money, trust in warnings). It is already in the forecasts you see on TV this season (season ends 30 Nov); AI models improve in weeks, not years. NHC Director Michael Brennan: intensity errors are "about half of what they were" at Hurricane Katrina.
- **Why it's hopeful:** forecasting is getting faster at getting better. Lowry: AI could compress "a decade of progress into a year", and he calls it "a boon for our business", not a replacement for forecasters.
- **Real person, own words:** Michael Lowry, Miami hurricane specialist who has advised FEMA and the NHC (NPR): "That model has been, by far and away, the best performing computer model that we've ever seen. Period." Also Brian McNoldy (U. Miami): "It makes us a little uncomfortable, because we like to know why you're getting the right answer."
- **Frame-0 line:** "Every forecast said Louisiana. Except one." (6 words) / question hook: "Where would you have evacuated?"
- **The twist:** the winning model knows no physics. Lowry: "The AI models don't really know anything about the atmosphere other than the current picture." It learned from decades of past storms and beat the equations; the experts can't explain why, so humans still make the call (the blend).
- **Drawable key image:** the spaghetti plot: a fan of grey forecast lines all bending into Louisiana, one warm line peeling off to the Florida Panhandle, and the storm's real path drawing itself over the warm line.
- **Evidence strength / doubts:** STRONG on the Isaias track call (NPR x2, CNN, Wikipedia, Fix the News agree) and on NHC using it. MEDIUM on the season numbers (one independent verifier, via a Substack, to early Sept). Doubts: one storm is an anecdote (NHC: other storms went the physics models' way in 2025); the storm itself is 9-10 Oct, so the in-window peg is NPR's 26 Oct feature; 9 deaths, so the tone must stay respectful; Google did not answer NPR's questions; the exact lead time (days before landfall) of the first AI call is not given in what I read.

## 2. (DRAFT, being cross-checked) A gene editor no one found in nature, invented by AI, now protects rice
- **What happened, when, who:** University of Missouri (Bing Yang's lab; first author Ajay Gupta) used OpenCRISPR-1, a gene-editing protein designed by AI at the California company Profluent and released open source, to edit rice so it resists bacterial blight (a disease that can wreck rice harvests in parts of Asia and Africa). Paper: "AI-designed OpenCRISPR-1 enables efficient targeted mutagenesis and prime editing in rice", aBIOTECH (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.abiote.2026.100054. Phys.org (University of Missouri release) 2026-10-27.
- **What AI did / didn't do, stage:** AI designed the editing tool (Profluent, earlier work; previously shown in animal cells); the Missouri team adapted it to rice and did the editing. AI did not pick the gene, grow or test the plants. Stage: LAB line only; no field trial, no yield numbers in the release; "may be several years" before farmers grow it (needs commercial varieties, regulators, industry partners).
- **Sources:** Phys.org 2026-10-27 https://phys.org/news/2026-10-rice-devastating-disease-gene-ai.html (read); second source pending.
- **Real person, own words:** Bing Yang, Curators' distinguished professor, University of Missouri: "Bacterial blight can wreak havoc on rice crops, reducing both yield and quality"; "OpenCRISPR-1 is open source and free for research and commercialization of crop genome editing".

## 3. (DRAFT) Florida's worst dengue year in ~a century; an AI trap names the mosquito the same day
- **What happened, when, who:** University of South Florida (Ryan Carney, Sriram Chellappan) startup Digitomy: a trap whose two cameras photograph caught mosquitoes and an AI names species, sex and whether it has fed on blood (98%+ species accuracy in LAB tests), results the same day instead of 2-3 days under a microscope. Tested with Anastasia Mosquito Control District (St. Johns County, FL); field validation with Hillsborough County being prepared. Tech Xplore / USF release, 2026-10-26.
- **Stage / doubts:** no peer-reviewed field study, no trap count, no dengue impact shown ("still being human-led"). Weak as a hopeful headline; kept as a backup.
