# Video 4 ideas, sequence B ("The open machine"): THE FRAME LOOP, evaluation round 1

Fresh evaluator (built none of it). Order kept: the sheet first (full size, then phone size and squint size), these
lines written from the pictures alone, before the brief, B.md or the pipeline were read.

## 1. The feeling each picture gives (from the picture alone)

- **B-B1** ("70,000 homes?"): The feeling it gives: a hushed, cold night with something secret glowing under it; a molten gold seam splits the snow and pulls the eye down the street to a lit gate. Quiet curiosity and a little mystery ("what is that?"), more intrigue than surprise; the big "70,000" reads, but the picture shows a few warm windows, not a city of homes.
- **B-B2** ("giant computers, data centers."): The feeling it gives: cold, empty, sterile depth; a long grey corridor with a tiny figure at the end. A little lonely, a little pull from the perspective, but flat (all mid-greys, nothing very dark or very light) and generic, like any stock server aisle; the snow falling indoors reads as odd rather than magical.
- **B-B3** ("really hot."): The feeling it gives: a warm amber glow, like walking down a lit toaster or a sauna; cosy and pleasant, not alarming. The jump from B2's grey to orange is a clear "it got hot" across the cut, but inside the frame nothing rises, shimmers or bothers the person. A stray bar over "hot." looks like a mistake.
- **B-B4** ("40 kettles,"): The feeling it gives: a mild, whimsical "huh": a giant cabinet of small steaming kettles beside a small person. Quiet and orderly, like a shop display or a vending machine; at phone size the kettles shrink to dots and the cabinet reads as a speaker or a locker, so the astonishment rests on the words.
- **B-B5** ("That's a lot of tea."): The feeling it gives: stiff and puzzling: a big, flat, mannequin-like figure on a stool holds a tiny mug up to the kettle wall. I cannot tell whether she is toasting, pouring or reaching; the face is blank and the gold stripe down her back reads as a crack of light, not hair. No smile; a little lonely, a little awkward.
- **B-B6** ("big fans blow that heat outside."): The feeling it gives: murky, smoky gloom: three glowing vents pour grey plumes into the night like smokestacks. It reads as pollution or something burning, heavier than a pang; the swirling flakes are pretty at full size but read as ash or sparks at phone size, and the smoke smears into the words.
- **B-B7** ("But Helsinki wants that heat."): The feeling it gives: quiet, wistful longing: a small figure on a grey quay points toward a far plume of steam while a gold seam glows in the snow in front of us. Calm and a bit melancholy, not a turn and not delight; the city is absent (a ferry and an empty quay).
- **B-B8** ("a plan, starting next year."): The feeling it gives: a thin, technical "plan": a white wireframe box drawn over the same quay, a dashed path, a "2027" sign. A blueprint overlay, informative more than hopeful; it is nearly B7 again, so the eye feels a repeat.
- **B-B9** ("So how would that work?"): The feeling it gives: a threshold: a figure silhouetted in a bright warm doorway, her shadow running toward us. Invitation and curiosity, "go on, step in"; the strongest pull on the sheet, though the shapes inside the door read as a bar chart.
- **B-B10** ("with water in pipes."): The feeling it gives: plain, satisfying clarity: a cold blue pipe snakes up the face of a rack and leaves glowing orange, "oh, the water picks the heat up". But it is a cutaway diagram, dry and textbook; the person is lost among dark boxes.
- **B-B11** ("pipes under the streets into people's homes."): The feeling it gives: cosy arrival: a gold line winds up the snowy street past a tram, climbs the wall and ends at a lit window where someone stands. Warm, safe, homecoming; it closes the loop with B1's seam.
- **B-B12** ("heat an apartment in Helsinki."): The feeling it gives: a personal link, "that's mine": a hand, a phone, a gold line running from the chat to a lit window where someone waves. Tender, a little wink; but the hand and sleeve are a dark mitten-blob, and "heat" crashes into "an" in the words.
- **B-B5alt** ("That's a lot of tea."): The feeling it gives: a small smile and a breath: one dark mug of glowing tea, steam blooming, in front of a soft wall of kettles. Calm, cosy, funny in its scale; clean at a glance.
- **B-B6alt** ("Normally, blow that heat outside."): The feeling it gives: hazy, drifting, a little sad: soft steam columns rise from scattered glowing vents across a blurred grey land. Dreamy, but it reads as hot springs or chimneys more than fans or waste; at phone size it is mush, and the cut-down words read like an order.
- **B-B7alt** ("But Helsinki wants that heat."): The feeling it gives: warm, lively, communal and funny: a whole building of glowing windows thrown open, people leaning out and reaching for the drifting warm air, a chorus of "we want it". Delight; yet the same figure is cloned nine times and they look pasted on the facade, legs hanging below the sills.
- **B-B12alt** ("heat an apartment in Helsinki."): The feeling it gives: snug, peaceful home: a black cat asleep on a sill above a glowing radiator, snow outside, a little "..." bubble by the phone like a wink. The coziest frame on the sheet, an ending that lands; "heat" again crashes into "an".

The paint-over: `B_EVAL_r1.png` (pink = what holds a frame back; honey = a value or colour correction painted on, in
B2, B3, B6 and B8; a verdict pill on each frame and a "gives:" line under each; the board read in the empty corner).

## 2. Each frame: the feeling intended beside the one it gives, and the verdict

**B-B1 · The warm line.** Intended: surprise and curiosity, a cozy snowy city and an odd true idea. Gives: hushed
curiosity and a little mystery; cold more than cozy; few homes. **ALMOST.** The thumb-stopper is real: a molten honey
seam through cool snow, pulling the eye to the lit hall.
1. Give "seventy thousand homes" something to land on: the Jugendstil row running deep along the quay, window after
   window warming in a wave from the seam's branches. Why: the claim's payoff and the "cozy city" both live in those
   windows; now one block shows about eight warm windows and the rest is empty grey quay, so the number lands on
   nothing and the frame feels cold.
2. Draw the hall at the quay's end in the film's pencil, its computer lights painted inside (B.md already offers this).
   Why: at phone size the painted hall reads as a lit gate that already stands and already heats the city. That
   contradicts the film's own device six beats later (B7: the line stops at an empty lot), shows as built what the facts
   call a plan, and misses the chance to teach "pencil = planned" at frame 0, so that B8 reads at once.

**B-B2 · A cathedral of computers.** Intended: a little awe; AI is a real, huge, physical thing. Gives: cold, empty,
sterile depth; a stock server aisle; a little lonely. **ALMOST.** The depth and her small figure give some scale; the
cathedral is not felt.
1. Put the light at the far end: the open door onto the snowy night as the brightest shape, the floor a step down
   (painted on the paint-over). Why: awe in a nave comes from walking toward a light; now the eye drops to the floor,
   because the drift and the floor are the lightest things below the band and the far end is a dull dark box
   (measured, to explain it: L* ~70 and ~61 against ~36). My first line called the frame "flat": its range is wide,
   but the light sits on the wrong things.
2. Make the machines tower: a lower eye, near her knee, so the walls climb out of sight. Why: now they stop at about
   three times her height under a flat ceiling and read as cabinets, not giants.
3. Give the machines their own lights, and keep the snow at a door: rows of tiny, regular cool points on the racks;
   the blown-in snow as a drift at a visible threshold, with her cart's tracks. Why: the rack lights are the same white
   dots as the falling snow, so the walls read as snowy, and the drift reads as a pale rug laid across the floor.

**B-B3 · Too hot for snow.** Intended: the heat rising; warm, a touch of alarm. Gives: a cosy amber glow, a toaster or a
sauna; pleasant, nothing rising. **ALMOST.** The cut from B2's grey to this orange is a strong "it got hot".
1. Make the heat rise and show the frame's own idea: haze lifting off both walls to the ceiling vents, the flakes above
   her turning to drops before they reach the machines. Why: "too hot for snow" is the best idea here and it is not in
   the picture: the flakes just float, and heat that does not move reads as a warm, cosy light.
2. Put the heat in her body: beanie pulled off in her hand, hair lifting in the draught, coat open. Why: the touch of
   alarm is hers to show; now her raised hand reads as horns on her head.
3. A hotter core: whiter honey where the aisle meets (painted on the paint-over), deeper at the near grilles. Why: the
   even amber reads as lit wooden doors, not as heat that is too much.
4. Remove the bar over "hot.". Why: it reads as a typo; the lit hand is the stress.

**B-B4 · Forty kettles in one.** Intended: playful astonishment at the size of it. Gives: a mild, whimsical "huh"; an
orderly display, like a vending machine. **ALMOST.** The idea is the sheet's best invention: one computer opened on forty
boiling kettles, true and readable.
1. Make them boil: forty plumes merging into one big cloud rolling out of the cabinet's top. Why: "boiling non-stop" is
   the astonishment; now each kettle has a small soft puff and the wall is calm and orderly.
2. Draw the door swung wide toward us (the dashed outline on the paint-over). Why: the film's device is a warm opening;
   with no door the cabinet reads as a shelf or a vending machine, not a computer opened.
3. Closer and lower, so the kettle wall towers over her, and give her a reaction (a step back, a hand up). Why:
   astonishment is felt through a person's scale and reaction; she stands still and small at its side.

**B-B5 · One cup.** Intended: a smile and a breath; the joke lands. Gives: stiff and puzzling; a mannequin on a stool.
**NOT THERE.** The joke is the scale (forty kettles, one cup), and the staging inverts it: the person is the biggest,
heaviest shape in the frame and the cup a tiny dark dot at the edge of the kettle wall. The figure (a helmet of a head,
no face, no neck, a gold stripe down her back like a crack) reads as a mannequin, so the toast does not read and nothing
is funny. The rework is on the sheet: B-B5alt.

**B-B6 · Snow falling up.** Intended: a small pang, all that warmth thrown into the cold night. Gives: murky, smoky gloom;
smokestacks; heavier than a pang. **ALMOST.** Snow lifted back up over a fan is a real idea and will read in motion; the
hinge is that the plume reads as smoke.
1. Paint the plume as warm air, not smoke: honey at the fan's mouth, thinning to clear night as it rises (painted on the
   paint-over). Why: a pang is watching warmth vanish; a grey, opaque plume reads as exhaust or burning, which gives
   gloom and alarm (measured, to explain the murk: 95 % of the frame below the band is darker than L* 66; only the fan
   mouths are light).
2. Clear the band and give the warmth a big dark sky to vanish into, above one near fan. Why: the plume climbs into the
   band behind "blow that heat outside", and with three fans crowding the frame there is nowhere for the warmth to go.
3. Lift the snow only inside the plume, bright and clear; let it fall everywhere else. Why: the streaks are scattered
   over the whole upper frame and read as ash at phone size, so the "falling up" is lost.
If the warm plume does not turn the gloom into a pang, this frame goes to rework next round.

**B-B7 · The city reaches.** Intended: the turn, a spark of delight, the city reaching for it. Gives: quiet, wistful
longing; grey and still; no city. **NOT THERE.** It is the story's hinge ("But") and it does not turn: the frame is as
grey and still as the waste before it. The city that "wants" is not in the picture (a ferry's bow and an empty quay),
the line's "spark" is a small glow with nothing marking the untouched snow as a gap, and one small figure pointing at a
faint far plume reads as longing, not delight. A rework direction that keeps the concept: the city itself reaching, on
this lot. The Jugendstil row at the lot's edge has its windows flung open, people leaning out toward the plume (which
stays far across the ice), while below them the city's warm line races to the lot's edge and stops in a burst of light
and steam. That is B7alt's life with this frame's gap, and B8 keeps its view with one change.

**B-B8 · The missing piece, drawn.** Intended: hope and anticipation; real, and coming soon. Gives: a thin, technical
"plan"; a blueprint overlay; nearly B7 again. **ALMOST.** "The plan is drawn, not painted" is a good device and nearly
reads.
1. Draw a building standing on the lot, its facade and big door facing the line's end, in soft pencil; not a wireframe
   box. Why: the hard white lines draw an open room floating over the water, which reads as a hologram or a CAD
   diagram, not "a hall will stand here"; they are also the nearest thing on the sheet to ink lines.
2. Give the hope a light: a faint warm glow in the drawn door (painted on the paint-over), or along the dashed pipe as
   it draws itself. Why: the drawing is cold, so the frame stays as grey as B7 and the arc does not lift on "a plan".
3. Bring the "2027" tag in from the edge at the type guide's size for it, or drop it. Why: its "2" is cut by the frame's
   left edge, it sits in the side margin, and its figures are about half the guide's size (about 36 px at frame scale),
   so the year does not read at phone size.
It follows B7's rework: the same view, one change.

**B-B9 · A drawn door opens.** Intended: leaning in; a door opens. Gives: a threshold, an invitation, "go on, step in";
the strongest pull on the sheet. **THERE.** Polish: the shapes inside the door read as a bar chart (stepped heights,
lines on top): draw them as machines, with small lights and pipes rising from their tops, even in height and in
perspective. Let her lean: a hand on the door's edge, her body tipped forward.

**B-B10 · Cool in, honey out.** Intended: clarity and satisfaction, "oh, that's how". Gives: plain clarity, cool in and
hot out; but a dry cutaway diagram, the person lost. **ALMOST.** It reads; it is not yet worth a pause.
1. Open the machine: its door swung wide, the pipe winding over its shelves of chips. Why: a pipe drawn on the face of a
   closed black box is the textbook heat-exchanger icon, the dry diagram the owner rules out, and the film's device is
   looking into warm openings.
2. Show temperature, not a colour code: frost on the cold pipe at the foot, a breath of steam where the warm pipe
   leaves. Why: blue-to-orange reads as a legend; the "oh" comes from seeing the cold become warm.
3. Put her at the valve, near, her hand on it, lit by the warm pipe. Why: she stands back among the racks, dark on
   dark, so the frame has no human centre.
4. Quiet the back rows and let the drawn walls show the snowy night and the city's warm windows, the warm pipe leaving
   toward them. Why: the middle is a busy cluster of boxes, stubs and drips, and the heat's destination is not in the
   picture.

**B-B11 · Under the snow, home.** Intended: cozy relief, warmth travelling under the snow to homes. Gives: cozy arrival,
warm and safe; it closes the loop with B1's seam. **THERE.** Polish: the seam's climb up the facade reads as a glowing
rope hung on the wall; soften it into a thaw in the plaster rising to the radiator under her window. The dog by the
manhole reads as a small table: make it a dog, nose down to the steam, or drop it.

**B-B12 · Your chat, a warm window.** Intended: personal warmth and a wink; your own chat warms a home; back to the
start. Gives: a personal link, tender, a little wink; the hand a dark blob; the words collide. **ALMOST.**
1. Set the words as the type guide's landing-word lockup (R4): the lead-in, then "heat" on its own row, then the rest,
   with clear space. Why: "heat" now runs into "an apartment", and the payoff word must read in one glance.
2. Draw a gloved hand holding the phone: the thumb over its edge, the cuff, a little snow on it. Why: the "you" lives in
   that hand; now it is a dark mitten-blob that reads as a shadow.
3. Make the wink readable: her raised mug clear against her window's glow (a nearer window or a larger figure), or
   B5alt's mug on her sill. Why: the toast is the wink, and a stick figure with a raised arm reads as a wave at best.

**B-B5alt · One cup, close.** Intended: a smile and a breath. Gives: a small smile and a breath; calm, cozy, funny in its
scale. **THERE**; take it for B5. Polish: the kettle wall's hard top edge right under the band and the pale strips at
its sides and bottom frame it like a pasted picture, so let the wall run out of the frame. Let the mug carry the cast:
it is her mug, and it comes back on her sill or in her hand in B12 (the echo B.md wanted from her toast).

**B-B6alt · Everywhere, up it goes.** Intended: a small pang. Gives: hazy, drifting, a little sad; hot springs or
chimneys; mush at phone size. **NOT THERE.** "Normally, everywhere" needs halls and fans you can name, and nothing here
names them. Soft plumes rise from small glowing dots in a blurred grey land, so it reads as hot springs in fog, and at
phone size it is grey mush. Its words ("Normally, blow that heat outside.", the middle of the line dropped) read as an
order. B6 stays the base.

**B-B7alt · The city leans out.** Intended: the turn, a spark of delight, the city reaching for it. Gives: warm, lively,
communal and funny; delight; the people look pasted on. **ALMOST**; its life is the rework direction for B7.
1. Put real, varied people in the windows (an old man, a child, a raised mug, mittens hung out, a cat), leaning on the
   sills with their bodies inside. Why: one figure is cloned nine times and stands in front of the facade, legs hanging
   below the sills, so it reads as paper dolls, not a city.
2. Keep the plume far across the ice, out of reach. Why: now it drifts right up to the windows, which says the heat
   comes by air (it comes by water in pipes) and settles the wish before B8's plan.
3. Keep the city's warm line stopping at the lot's edge in the frame. Why: without the gap, B8 has no missing piece to
   draw.

**B-B12alt · The cat on the warm sill.** Intended: personal warmth and a wink. Gives: a snug, peaceful home; the coziest
frame on the sheet; an ending that lands. **THERE.** Polish: the same "heat"/"an" collision as B12 (the R4 lockup). The
gold stroke across the cat's back reads as a stick; make it the radiator's warm rim light, or drop it. Let the "..."
bubble rise from the phone (tilted up, its screen lit); now it floats beside a dark slab. Lift the sill and the radiator
a little: their lower part sits under the app's bottom bar, and the radiator's glow is the heat's read. Against B12 it
trades the POV "you" for her phone, and the loop back to B1 then needs a pull out through the window (the hall's lights
are already in it). It is the more pause-worthy ending; the choice is the art director's.

## 3. BOARD READ (the twelve in order, sound off, as a viewer)

- **The story from the frames alone:** mostly yes. A honey seam in a snowy harbour city leads into a hall of computers;
  they glow hot; one opens on kettles; fans throw the warmth into the night; on the quay a warm line stops short and a
  plan is drawn; a door opens; water carries the heat; it runs under the street into a window; your phone. Two links
  need the words: B7 (why she points at a far plume) and B8 (what the white box is).
- **One world:** yes, firmly. One snowy harbour night, cool greys and one honey heat, one woman, one type; the hall is
  reached through doors with the snow blowing in. Nothing looks borrowed from another film.
- **One thread:** yes. The heat is in every frame from B3 on. The seam carries it B1 > B7 > B8 > B11 > B12, and the pipe
  hands it from the hall to the street.
- **One arc of feeling:** it climbs at both ends and sags in the middle. What the frames give: curious > cold scale >
  warm > huh > stiff > gloom > wistful > a diagram > step in > "oh" > cozy > mine. B5-B8 are the problem: no smile, gloom
  for the pang, no turn on "But", a blueprint for hope. The story's hinge (B7) is as grey and still as the waste frame
  before it, so the viewer never feels the film turn. From B9 on, the arc climbs cleanly to the end.
- **The concept:** half clear. "The open machine" reads: the hall's door, the open cabinet, the drawn door, the window,
  the phone. "One piece missing" does not read yet. B1 shows the whole system built and already heating the city, B7's
  gap is invisible, and the pencil reads as white CAD lines. With the hall pencilled in B1 and B7's stop made visible,
  this would be the clearest part of the concept.
- **Rhythm and hand-offs:** three same-view pairs (B2/B3, B4/B5, B7/B8) and four frames in the hall make the first half
  the least varied stretch; B5alt's close-up already breaks it. The hand-offs are well designed: the seam into the door,
  the same-view changes, the steam up through the roof, the drawn door stepped through, the pipe out to the street.

## 4. The sequence's verdict: ALMOST

One world, one thread and good hand-offs: this is one film, and its second half (B9-B12, with B12alt beside B12)
already lands. It is not THERE because the middle does not carry the turn. In order of weight:
1. B7: rework. Show the city's people reaching on the lot, keep the line's stop, keep the plume far.
2. B5: take B5alt, with her mug as the echo in B12.
3. B6 and B8: the warm plume; the drawn building with a warm door.
4. B1: the hall in pencil and many homes.
5. Open up B2-B4 and B10: the far door's light, the heat rising, the kettles boiling behind a door swung wide, the
   machine opened.
6. The "heat"/"an" lockup in B12 and B12alt.
