# Video 4 ideas, imagination A: THE FROSTED CITY (lens: the city as the story)

**Files:** this page; `A_sheet.png` (A-B1 to A-B12 in beat order, then A-B4alt, A-B7alt, A-B9alt, A-B10alt);
`A_src/` (`frames.py` draws every thumbnail as one whole 1080 x 1920 frame, `sketchlib.py` its pieces, `sheet.py` the
sheet, `A_phone_strip.jpg` the film in order at phone size). Regenerate: `cd video/docs/v4/ideas/A_src && python3
frames.py && python3 sheet.py --phone` (about 30 s; full-size renders go to a temp folder, `A_OUT` to move it).
Thumbnails are value sketches: cool greys, ONE warm accent (the heat, honey), the caption seat as a light band with
the beat's words in it. People are silhouettes here; the people in use come at the key frames.

## THE FILM CONCEPT

**In one line:** one winter night, Helsinki is a city of frosted windows, and the one building in town with no
windows at all, the data center at the harbour's edge, is full of heat it throws into the sky; the film carries that
heat from the building with no windows to the windows that want it, and the FROST on Helsinki's windows tells the story.

**The world (real and specific):** a harbour neighbourhood of Helsinki on a deep winter night, from the real South
Harbour and its streets: stone apartment blocks of five and six storeys in soft stucco, a Jugendstil corner tower,
deep double windows with a candle or a paper star, snow on every sill, roofs crowded with small chimneys; a tram line;
the frozen bay with ice sheets, a dark channel that steams in the cold (the real "sea smoke"), an ice fisher on his
stool; across the ice, at the city's edge, one long blank block with cold lights and big roof fans. Snow falling.

**The central device: the frost on Helsinki's windows,** the city's own thermometer and sketchbook. A window is a home;
frost on it is the cold; when warmth arrives the frost clears and the pane goes honey. The data center is the one
building with NO windows: heat with no home. Every beat is a window of the city (looked at, looked through, drawn on,
opened), and the frost moves the story: it **clears** in the promise (B1), **stays** while the heat is wasted (B6),
is **drawn on** as the plan (B8: a finger in a window's frost, "2027"), **is** the plan until it is real (B9-B10: the
frost-drawn data center, its door, its hall; only the water painted), and **clears for real**, window by window, as
the warm water arrives (B11-B12). **Painted = real, frost lines = planned** (the plant is a plan): the warm water is
the one thing that crosses from the frost drawing into the painted city.

**The thread (the heat, always honey):** a warm wave through 70,000 windows (B1) -> the cold street of giant machines
inside the blank building (B2) -> their heat, glowing and steaming (B3) -> forty kitchens' kettles (B4) -> one cup
(B5) -> a warm plume blown off over the frozen bay (B6) -> the city throwing its windows open to it (B7) -> the plan's
line in the frost (B8) -> the frost door (B9) -> the water taking the heat, cool in, honey out (B10) -> the warm water
under the snow, windows clearing up the street (B11) -> your chat, and the one window a step warmer (B12).

**The arc of feeling:** a cozy, curious night ("wait, what?") -> the cold awe of a street of giant machines -> heat, a
touch of alarm -> playful astonishment (a whole block boiling) -> a smile (one cup) -> a pang (warmth drifting away
over frosted panes) -> delight (the city throws its windows open) -> hope (the plan drawn in the frost, with a date)
-> leaning in (a frost door opens) -> "oh, that's how" -> cozy relief (frost clearing up the street) -> a warm wink.
**Its light:** night all film, one colour key; the frost chapter (B8-B9) is the film's one light, airy moment (hope),
then back into the dark for the mechanism, warming to the end.

**One person, one "you":** the city's people are its homes, so the film keeps ONE resident, a young woman in her
twenties whose window is the one window: walking home in the wide (B1), at her sill with one cup (B5), drawing the plan
in her frost (B8), opening its door with a fingertip (B9), at her warmed window (B12). The "you" is POV only, once:
your gloved hand and phone (B12). Everyone else small: a technician, an ice fisher, neighbours, walkers.

**The home view and the loop:** one view holds the first and last frame: from the snowy near shore, across a narrow
arm of frozen harbour, her block with the one window in the golden box, the city's blocks stepping up the hill behind,
the blank building at the harbour's edge on the left. B1 opens on it frosted and warming; B12 closes on it warm with
your phone in front (the deliberate match), then un-builds into frame 0.

## THE BEATS (each planned on FRAME_CARD, feeling first; library devices named after the frame was imagined)

**A-B1 · The city wakes warm** (0.13-4.2 s) · *Sees:* a snowy Helsinki harbour at night, the city's windows frosted
pale, and a wave of warm honey rolling out from a blank building at the water's edge, along the shore, through her
block and up the hill, frost clearing window after window. *Gets:* AI, that building, is to warm the city's homes.
*Feels:* cozy, curious, "wait, what?" · **The frame:** the hook made physical as one bold event (the wave), the payoff
in the band by ~2 s ("to heat 70,000 homes?"); the eye: her window (the hardest step, the golden box) -> the wave's
bright crest -> its source, the blank building -> the resident walking home on the near snow (rear three-quarter, her
breath showing). Layers: near snow (the quiet dark field), the frozen arm with its steaming channel, the shore road
and its warm run, the blocks, the hill city, the dome, the sky. Sound: a rising run of soft chimes as the panes clear.
*Devices:* D7 pattern with its break (a texture of frosted panes, the warm sweep), E8 the accent travelling with the
thread, N6 depth stacked, T for frame 0's build. *Hand-off:* the warm run leads the eye back along the shore to the
blank building's door.

**A-B2 · A street of giant machines** (4.28-8.6 s) · *Sees:* inside the building with no windows: a long aisle of
cabinets three times a person's height, their rows of tiny cold lights like a city's windows, a lone technician
walking toward the one huge door at the end, which frames the night, the frozen harbour and the city's warm windows.
*Gets:* AI is real: giant computers in a big building. *Feels:* a little awe, cold, a hum · **The frame:** the
machine city rhymes with the home city (lights in rows like windows), and the home city waits at the end of the
aisle: the cold place and the warm place in one picture. Dark key; the door the one lit opening. *Devices:* B2 the
vanishing point closed by the door (E7), C1 + E2 the lit opening, D2 scale, E1 one small figure where the lines meet.
*Hand-off:* the same aisle, held.

**A-B3 · The machines run hot** (8.76-10.9 s) · *Sees:* the same aisle a step closer, every cabinet's vents now
glowing honey, warm air and steam rolling up under the ceiling and out of the door into the frozen night; the
technician wipes his brow. *Gets:* they get really hot. *Feels:* heat rising, a touch of alarm · **The frame:** the
first warm since B1 appears at the source; haze softens the far door. Sound: a low hiss swelling. *Devices:* N14 the
deliberate match (B2 with one change: cold to hot), a push in. *Hand-off:* the steam pours out of the door into the
street, and "to put it into perspective" pulls us out after it.

**A-B4 · One computer, a whole block boiling** (10.96-17.5 s) · *Sees:* out in a snowy street, ONE giant computer
stands on the pavement (a cabinet four times a neighbour's height, melting its own pool in the snow, its own column of
steam); across the street an apartment block of forty kitchen windows, a kettle boiling on every sill, forty curls of
steam gathering into one cloud as big as the computer's. *Gets:* one computer = forty kettles boiling non-stop.
*Feels:* playful astonishment · **The frame:** the comparison told by the city's own building: a grid of forty warm
windows (one texture) with one break, the machine; the neighbour and her dog stop and look up. Sound: forty tiny
whistles in a chorus on "FORTY", the lit "forty" in the band. *Devices:* D7 pattern with one break, E1 a human anchor
for scale. *Hand-off:* a push across the street into one lit window opposite.

**A-B5 · One cup** (17.76-19.2 s) · *Sees:* inside, behind her: she stands at her kitchen window in a big knit, hair
up in a claw clip, one small mug of tea steaming in her hand, looking out at the block across the street boiling away
in all forty windows. *Gets:* the joke. *Feels:* a smile and a breath · **The frame:** her one curl of steam against a
building's worth; warm lamp at her shoulder, frost in the pane's corners, the radiator under the sill. Calm, close,
quiet values. Sound: a small sip, a clink. *Devices:* C1 frame within a frame, E10 the near figure looking in.
*Hand-off:* the block's cloud rises, and we rise with the steam over the roofs to the harbour.

**A-B6 · Warmth drifting away** (19.26-23.4 s) · *Sees:* the one wide: the frozen bay under a huge night sky; on the
blank building's roof three big fans blow warm columns that join and bend away over the ice and over the city's
frosted windows, greying and thinning into the dark; far along the shore two other data centers' plumes thin away
too; one ice fisher on his stool looks up. *Gets:* normally the heat is just blown away. *Feels:* a small pang ·
**The frame:** the warmth (the accent) is only at the fans' roots and fades as it drifts: you watch it get lost above
cold homes. *Devices:* D1 negative space, Z1 the one scale moment, E6 atmospheric layers, E1 the tiny anchor.
*Hand-off:* the plain cut on "But".

**A-B7 · The city throws its windows open** (23.46-25.5 s) · *Sees:* the waterfront blocks close and climbing away,
windows flung open one after another, neighbours leaning out into the cold with their arms up into the warm drift
overhead, one holding up a mug; a child on the shore road with both arms up. *Gets:* Helsinki wants that heat.
*Feels:* the turn: a spark of delight · **The frame:** the drift's underside lit warm by the opened windows; frosted
panes swung out catching the light; breath in the cold. Sound: casements clacking open in a cascade, a delighted
ripple. *Devices:* the plain cut (F, on "But"), B1 leading lines up the facade into the drift, D7 the pattern broken
window by window. *Hand-off:* a push into one window that stayed shut: hers, frosted.

**A-B8 · The plan, drawn in the frost** (25.54-28.3 s) · *Sees:* inside her window: the pane thick with glittering
frost; with a fingertip she has drawn the plan in it, a little box with a door and two fans at the harbour's edge (the
new data center), a line along the shore and up to a little window (home), and now writes "2027"; through every line
the real night shows, its lights. *Gets:* the city has a plan, starting next year (a plan: a drawing, not built).
*Feels:* hope and anticipation · **The frame:** the film's one light, airy picture; the warm wall above (the band),
the candle and a plant on the sill, the cold radiator under it. Sound: the squeak of a finger on frost. *Devices:* Z4
the insert (the frost is the paper, one number big), C1, N1. *Hand-off:* a push in on the little box she drew.

**A-B9 · The frost door opens** (28.34-29.5 s) · *Sees:* the frost drawing fills the frame; her fingertip has just
swung the drawn door open, a sheet of frost on its hinge, and behind it a deep doorway with a cool glow, tall dark
machines and a pipe of cool water waiting. *Gets:* we are going inside to see how. *Feels:* leaning in, a door opens ·
**The frame:** the night shows through the drawn walls, full of the city's lights; the candle warms the frost from
below. Sound: a soft creak. *Devices:* C1 / B4 the threshold, a push. *Hand-off:* through the door (a push-through).

**A-B10 · Cold in, warm out** (29.56-34.4 s) · *Sees:* inside the frost drawing: a big hall drawn in frost lines on
the dark, a row of giant machines glowing warm inside, a tiny frost-line person at the far end; one pipe, the only
painted thing: the water comes in cool and pale along the left, runs to the far end, turns and comes back along the
machines' feet, each machine handing its heat down into it, and leaves honey toward us. *Gets:* water in pipes takes
the heat out of the computers. *Feels:* clarity, "oh, that's how" · **The frame:** two big legs at the near edge, pale
on the left and honey on the right, so in and out read at a glance; the water never touches the machines' insides.
Sound: a cool flow note warming to a hum. *Devices:* E9 the subject travels the line, B2. *Hand-off:* the honey leg
runs out of the drawing's edge and becomes the real warm water under a real street.

**A-B11 · Home by home, up the street** (34.46-38.4 s) · *Sees:* her street at a person's eye height, blocks
shoulder to shoulder on both sides, her block closing the far end; along the near kerb a soft painted cut in the
snowbank shows the twin pipes under the street, the warm one honey; past it the warm water shows as a melt line
running to every door; the near windows already warm, the middle ones clearing, the far ones still frosted: the warmth
climbing toward her door; a tram going up the street, walkers, a dog. *Gets:* pipes under the streets carry the heat
into homes. *Feels:* cozy relief · **The frame:** enclosure on both sides, the street's one long sight line ended by
her block; snow falling. Sound: the tram's bell, a warm hum. *Devices:* B2 + E7 the closed vista, B1 the melt line
leading to her door, D7 the windows warming in order. *Hand-off:* the line reaches her door; we pull back to the shore.

**A-B12 · Your chat, her window** (38.48-42.24 s) · *Sees:* the home view again, the whole city warm; your gloved
hand holds your phone up from the bottom left, a chat bubble pops, a pulse runs along the shore from the plan's
building (still drawn in frost lines: "could soon") and her window goes a step warmer, her silhouette in it.
*Gets:* your own chatbot could soon heat an apartment in Helsinki. *Feels:* personal warmth and a wink, back to the
start · **The frame:** the film's hardest step on her window ("apartment" lit in the band); the pop on the bubble, the
warm step on her pane. *Devices:* N14 the deliberate match with B1, E11 the POV hook, E2. *Hand-off:* the loop: the
city un-builds into frame 0's frosted night.

## ALTERNATES (the four beats I am least sure of)

- **A-B4alt · The seesaw in the snowy courtyard:** in a little playground held by frosted blocks, a seesaw rests
  level: the one giant computer on one end, forty kettles stacked in a pyramid on the other, the same steam rising off
  both; a child with a sled stops. Cleaner to read at phone size than forty windows (one picture of "as much as"), but
  a balance can read as weight; the steam carries "heat". Flows into B5 by a push into a lit window over the yard.
- **A-B7alt · The city's own pipe reaches out:** along the shore road the city's warm line races under the snow toward
  the blank building, every manhole on its way lifting its lid with a warm puff in a cascade, people turning to watch.
  The turn told by the true mechanism (the network reaching) rather than by people; less delight, more "real". (The
  puffs are a playful stretch: Helsinki's pipes carry hot water, not steam.)
- **A-B9alt · She opens the window on the night:** the frosted casement with her drawing and "2027" on it swings into
  the room, the cold night pours in, the candle flame leans; she points across the frozen harbour to the empty snowy
  site beside the blank building. A real door (a real window) instead of a drawn one; leads straight into B10alt.
- **A-B10alt · One machine, one pipe:** inside the frost drawing, one giant machine big and close; the water comes in
  pale at its foot, climbs through it warming, and leaves honey at its top, running off toward the city. The plainest
  possible "oh, that's how", at the cost of "a big new data center" (one machine, not a hall).

## THE LOOK AT THE SHEET (at phone size, in order, as one film)

**What I saw on the first pass, and redrew:** B1's wave swept one block and the hill city hid behind the near belt
(raised the hill: the wave now crosses a whole hillside, with a bright crest and panes half-cleared ahead of it); B3's
steam crossed the caption band (kept under it); B4's computer read as another dark building (a cabinet now: rounded,
a glass door with trays, a plinth, its own melt pool, a neighbour beside it for scale); B5's figure was a lumpy
silhouette and her cup did not read (her hair up in a claw clip, a light mug with its warm curl against the glass);
B6's plume had no source (three warm columns off the fans now, bending away); B7's people were specks (staged close:
neighbours leaning out, arms up into the drift, the casements as slim frosted panes); B8's frost read as grey noise
and the hand as a beak (a glittering pane, a real hand and sleeve); B9 looked like a diagram (her fingertip on the
frost door, the night's lights through the drawn walls, the candle's warmth); B10's "cold in" was invisible (a loop
with two big legs, pale and honey); B11's sides were modern slabs (blocks of varied heights shoulder to shoulder);
B12's source looked built (the plan's building in frost lines).

**Read as one film now:** one world (one Helsinki night), one thread (the honey heat, from the wave to her window),
one device (the frost, strongest from B5 to B11), one arc (the dark middle, the light frost chapter for hope, warmth to
the end). Each frame says its line with the sound off; the frost chapter is the most pause-worthy stretch (B8 above all).
**What still holds back, honestly:** B9 is the plainest picture (a 1.2 s hinge; its alternate is a real window); B4 is
busy (its alternate is cleaner); in the wides the frosted panes read as "cold pale windows" rather than frost (the
frost itself shows from B5 on); the people are silhouettes until the people in use are drawn at the key frames.

## TRUTH, PIP, THE PLACE

- **Truth:** the plant is a plan (frost lines in B8-B10 and B12's source; "2027" = next year); no names or logos; the
  homes are flats (apartment blocks); the water stays inside its pipe and heats homes (radiators under the sills,
  windows clearing), never a tap; "normally" is shown on generic data centers (B6), and nothing claims Helsinki is the
  first; one AI cabinet = forty 3 kW kettles. The heat pump (the small box) is not voiced, so not drawn; a key frame may
  add it, unlabelled, in B11's cut.
- **Pip** (not drawn at thumbnail size): on a snowy balcony of the next block (B1, B12), on a sill among the frosted
  windows (B7, off the eye path), on a balcony on the right side of the street (B11); none in B2-B6 closes or the frost
  chapter.
- **The place, from real photos** (references only, kept out of the repo): the South Harbour at night with sea smoke
  over open water lit warm by the city, snowy roofs crowded with chimneys, a ferry in sea smoke, an ice fisher on the
  frozen bay with apartment blocks behind (finland.fi, "Snow brings light and life to Helsinki",
  https://finland.fi/life-society/snow-brings-light-and-life-to-helsinki/); a snowy evening street with people in long
  dark coats (Commons, public domain, "Keskuskatu on a snowy evening in January 2024").
