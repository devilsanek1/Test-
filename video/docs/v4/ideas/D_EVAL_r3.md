# Video 4 ideas, sequence D, THE FRAME LOOP evaluation round 3 (fresh evaluator)

Sheet judged: `D_sheet.png` as it is now (2314 x 2350, written 14:51). I looked at it whole, then at each thumbnail at
native size (360 x 640, about a phone viewport), enlarged 2x, and squinted (small and blurred). The lines below were
written from the pictures alone, before I read BRIEF.md, D.md or the earlier rounds.

## First look: the feeling each frame gives (from the picture alone)

- **D-B1** The feeling it gives: a hushed, cold night at home. She sits in the dark looking at a hole melted in the
  frost, and through it a whole city glows; curiosity and a small promise of warmth. The lower third is one dark mass
  (chair, throw, floor) where she nearly disappears.
- **D-B2** The feeling it gives: uncanny and a little lonely. Her small warm corner (armchair, lamp, tea) is set down in
  an endless cold corridor of blinking machines; her chat has a huge other end.
- **D-B3** The feeling it gives: a grim, heavy block steaming in the snow, dripping and melting a dark pool around
  itself: "something industrial out there". It reads damp and grey more than hot, and the fence and empty snow make the
  bottom half static.
- **D-B4** The feeling it gives: absurd, cosy plenty. Her kitchen is packed wall to wall with steaming kettles in a
  golden fog, a smile at the excess; but she melts into the beige haze, so it is a warm blur more than a picture with a
  centre.
- **D-B5** The feeling it gives: a deadpan, contented sip. Eyes closed, cup at her lips, kettles lined up on the sill;
  calm and a little funny, soft and pale, gentle rather than striking.
- **D-B6** The feeling it gives: a dull, slightly sad waste. A black box of fans breathes a grey plume up into the night
  over little snowy houses; the heat leaving, not landing. Flat and grey; the box floats with no clear ground and no
  link to her.
- **D-B7** The feeling it gives: eager longing with a wink. A whole building leans out of its windows, hands reaching
  for golden ribbons of warmth drifting past (a cat too): the neighbourhood wants it. Lively; the strongest "people"
  frame.
- **D-B8** The feeling it gives: quiet hope. She stands at her open window; across the dark bay a building drawn in
  lines of light sits on the shore, a gold thread running from it toward her roofs: a promise not built yet.
- **D-B9** The feeling it gives: an invitation and a little mystery. In the building drawn in light, a door swings open
  on a warm glow, footprints leading in: "come in and see". Clean, calm, the clearest focal point on the sheet.
- **D-B10** The feeling it gives: plain and explanatory, a cutaway. A cold white pipe in, a copper pipe out with a puff
  of steam, a faceless worker at the rack; clear but cool and diagram-like, the least magical frame.
- **D-B11** The feeling it gives: a gentle discovery from above. She leans out and looks down a steep snowy street where
  a dark melted line with warm glows runs under the snow toward the lit buildings: the warmth travelling home. The
  melt line reads more as a trench than as warmth.
- **D-B12** The feeling it gives: snug payoff. Back in her armchair, the frost gone, the city clear, her socked feet up
  on a glowing orange radiator, phone in hand; warm and complete, it rhymes with the first frame.
- **D-B2alt** The feeling it gives: cold awe at scale. A gallery view over a city of fan-topped machine blocks with tiny
  figures walking an aisle; impressive but impersonal, no home and no her.
- **D-B7alt** The feeling it gives: a personal reach. She leans out of her window, arm up, catching a warm glow drifting
  from the steaming city across the bay; yearning, but dark on dark and quieter than the whole building reaching.
- **D-B8alt** The feeling it gives: sparse and wistful. A building drawn in light on an empty snowfield, a thin gold line
  to a far window across the ice; distance and promise, but mostly empty grey at phone size.
- **D-B11alt** The feeling it gives: snug and communal. A doll's-house cutaway of a whole block, every flat with its
  people and a glowing radiator fed by one warm pipe; comforting and clear, but tidy and diagram-like, the bottom third
  empty earth.

## Per frame: the intended feeling beside the one it gives, and the verdict

Full size = 1080 x 1920; the app's bars from `LAYOUT.md` §1 (bottom bar y > 1520, right rail x > 950 at y 900-1500).
Measurements appear only where they explain a gap my eye found.

**D-B1 "The frost clears onto seventy thousand windows": THERE.** Intended: surprise and curiosity, a cozy snowy city
and an odd true idea. Gives: a hushed cold night, curiosity, the clearing a lit promise. Curiosity and the cozy city
land, and the "wait, what?" is the frost caught mid-melt (a bright rim, drops): the oddest, brightest shape in the row.
Polish: the radiator's waking fin and its pipe sit half under the app's bottom bar (the fin runs to y ~1617): lift the
radiator so the "ding" is seen.

**D-B2 "Her armchair among the giants": THERE.** Intended: a little awe, AI as a real, huge, physical thing. Gives:
uncanny and a little lonely, her warm corner in an endless cold corridor. That is awe round a lonely-cozy core; it reads
in a second and is the film's device at its clearest. No notes.

**D-B3 "The building the snow can't touch": ALMOST, at its cap (two adjust rounds used).** Intended: the heat rising,
warm, with a touch of alarm ("really hot"). Gives: a grim block steaming in the snow, damp and grey more than hot. The
alarm lands (a black mass, hard steam, the melted ring), but the warmth does not.
1. The five white streams off the roof edge read as frozen downpipes or icicles (solid, even, hooked over the edge),
   the opposite of hot. Round 2 turned the icicles into streams, but they still read that way. Drop them: the vapour,
   the bare wet ground and the rain already say the snow can't touch it.
2. "Really hot" has no warmth at its source: the vents' mouths are four specks (its warm share is as small as B2's or
   B8's quiet frames, though its word is "hot"). Give each mouth a bigger warm glow that lights the base of its own
   vapour column and shines in the puddles below (the film's one vapour paint: warm at the mouth, white above). It is
   painted on the paint-over.
At its cap, both notes go into its key frame, and the owner sees the frame with them.

**D-B4 "Forty kettles in her kitchen": THERE.** Intended: playful astonishment at the size of it. Gives: absurd, cosy
plenty, a smile at the excess. The playfulness and the forty land; the astonishment is gentle (soft puffs, and she is
calm). Polish: (1) her warm-brown knit sits in the warm haze, so in colour she is one more mid-tone; in grey she is
already the darkest shape in the middle (knit ~L* 38 against the steam ~65). Keep her the coolest, darkest shape on the
lightest steam, as B5 does, so the eye lands on her first, as her card wants. (2) Give a few near spouts a jet and lift
a few lids, so "boiling non-stop" has energy; the build animates the rest.

**D-B5 "Tea for one": THERE.** Intended: a smile and a breath, the joke lands. Gives: a deadpan, contented sip. It
matches. No notes.

**D-B6 "Out her window, up into the night": ALMOST** (judged THERE in rounds 1 and 2 and unchanged since, so one adjust
is available). Intended: a small pang, all that warmth thrown into the cold night. Gives: a dull, slightly sad waste,
flat and grey, with a toy-like box and no link to her. The waste reads; the pang barely does, because nothing is seen
being thrown.
1. The fans blow nothing. The plume is a separate cloud above and behind the box, and no column rises from any fan.
   Draw a column out of every fan (warm at its root, white above), merging over the box into the one plume that thins
   to nothing, so "big fans blow that heat outside" is one continuous shape. It is painted on the paint-over, and with
   it the beat reads at once.
2. The box reads as a toy brick with eight glowing studs: no hold on the land and nothing to measure it by. Set it on
   the land (a contact shadow along its base, darker than the wind streaks) and give it one human-scale cue (a door in
   its side, a person or a van at its foot), so "big" reads.
3. Her window, the beat's link to her (her open window lets the heat out like the fans do), sits with the near roofs
   entirely under the app's bottom bar (y ~1690-1920). Lift the near roofs above the bar so her one lit, open window and
   its own wisp read as the measure; otherwise the pang has no "her" in it.

**D-B7 "The whole block reaches": THERE.** Intended: the turn, a spark of delight, the city reaching for it. Gives:
eager longing with a wink, a whole building reaching, her hand the brightest warm. It matches and is the board's
strongest people frame. Key-frame polish: the neighbours are still black blobs that differ only by shape; the people in
use give them faces, knits and palms.

**D-B8 "Across the bay, a plan": THERE.** Intended: hope and anticipation, real and coming soon. Gives: quiet hope, a
promise drawn in light with a gold thread to her roofs. It matches. Polish: her head and shoulders sink into the dark
bay; a lamp-warm rim from the room behind separates her from the night.

**D-B9 "The plan's door opens": THERE.** Intended: leaning in, a door opens. Gives: an invitation and a little
mystery, the clearest focal point on the board. It matches. Polish: the glimpse inside reads as an ornament (a
symmetric chevron of fins); tall cabinets receding into the light would set up B10's reveal.

**D-B10 "Blue in, honey out": THERE (the plainest frame on the board).** Intended: clarity and satisfaction, "oh,
that's how". Gives: plain and explanatory, a cutaway, clear but cool. The clarity lands; the satisfaction is quieter.
Polish:
- The honey pipe reads as a telescope aimed at the city (short, steep, tapering to a point). Run it lower and longer,
  resting on its posts, so it reads as a pipeline leaving for the city.
- Its end, the arrival at her city, sits on the right rail's edge (x ~960, y ~1050). End it inside the stage.
- The worker is a pictogram. The people in use and the snowy lot drawn as a place keep it from becoming a diagram.

**D-B11 "Up her street, under the snow": THERE.** Intended: cozy relief, warmth travelling under the snow to homes.
Gives: a gentle discovery from above, the warmth travelling home. It matches, a little cooler than "relief". Polish:
the melt strip reads as a dark trench, and dark says cold. A thin veil of steam along its whole length and an unbroken
warm sheen of the windows on its wet surface would make the line itself read warm and travelling.

**D-B12 "Socks on a warm radiator": THERE.** Intended: personal warmth and a wink, back to the start. Gives: a snug
payoff, warm and complete, rhyming with B1; it is the warmest step by eye. Polish: her phone straddles the app's bottom
bar (y ~1440-1560); lift her hand and phone above it so the bubble's pop on "chatbot" is seen.

## BOARD READ (all frames in order, sound off, at phone size)

- **The story reads from the frames alone.** She is at home with her chat; her chair stands among the machines; their
  building steams in the snow; her kitchen fills with kettles; she sips; the fans blow heat into the night; her whole
  block reaches for it; a plan drawn in light sends a warm line to her; its door opens on warm light; a cold pipe goes
  in and a hot pipe comes out; the warmth comes up her street; her feet rest on a hot radiator.
- **One world.** One night, one woman with her bun, one window and radiator, one Helsinki. The near roofs of B6, B8 and
  B7alt, and B11's tall street, are not yet B1's long, low Helsinki; that is the world kit's job at the key frames, as
  `D.md` says.
- **One thread.** The warm accent sits at the focal of every frame and travels: lamp, vents, kettles, fans, hands, chalk
  line, door, pipe, melt strip, radiator.
- **One arc.** Curiosity, awe, (alarm, only half: grey), laughter, breath, (pang, only half: the fans blow nothing),
  delight, hope, leaning in, clarity, relief, warmth. The arc sags twice, at the two machine exteriors, B3 and B6, the
  only frames that are both grey and empty of her.
- **The film concept is clear.** "The warmth comes home": B1's frost and cold radiator are answered by B12's clear glass
  and hot radiator. Her home is in eight of the twelve frames; the other four follow the heat (and B6's link to her is
  lost under the app's bar).
- **The joins hand on.** The chair carries B1 into B2 and back in B12, the steam carries B3 into B4 and B5 into B6, the
  window is the gate from B7 to B8, and the pipe becomes the melt strip from B10 to B11.
- **The value rhythm alternates well.** Dark, then stark, then the pale lamp-lit kitchen as the lift in the middle, then
  grey, night, pale chalk, and warm night. B9's door is the biggest light; B12 is the warmest colour.
