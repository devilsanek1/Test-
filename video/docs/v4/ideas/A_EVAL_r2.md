## 1. The feeling each thumbnail gives (picture alone, written first)

- **A-B1** The feeling it gives: a hushed, cold winter night across a frozen bay, one block of the city glowing warm

- **A-B3** The feeling it gives: the same canyon turned stifling: everything glows amber, steam billows overhead, the

- **A-B4** The feeling it gives: whimsical and warm; a whole block where every window holds a steaming kettle, and one

- **A-B5** The feeling it gives: cozy, wry intimacy; a woman at home, back to us, lifts her tea toward the window of

- **A-B6** The feeling it gives: quiet waste and loneliness; three fans on a dark building puff warm steam that turns
  grey and drifts off over cold homes while a lone ice-fisher sits on a huge empty frozen bay; melancholy, a bit bleak

- **A-B7** The feeling it gives: joy and welcome; a whole block throws its warm windows open, people with arms up

- **A-B9** The feeling it gives: wonder and invitation; a frosted door swings open, its frame glowing warm, onto a

- **A-B10** The feeling it gives: hushed, cold and technical; a dark corridor drawn in thin white lines, one warm pipe

- **A-B11** The feeling it gives: homecoming and everyday warmth; a snowy street of blocks with warm windows, glowing

- **A-B7alt** The feeling it gives: longing, wishing; every frosted window of a block has a warm sun drawn in it, a

- **A-B9alt** The feeling it gives: curious, toy-like; a finger lifts the roof of a glowing outline house to show rows

## 2. Per thumbnail: the feeling it gives beside the beat's, and the verdict

**A-B1 · The city wakes warm** (the hook). *Gives:* a calm, cold postcard, one block glowing in the snow, a gentle
curiosity. *Wants:* surprise and curiosity, "wait, what?": a cozy snowy city and an odd true idea, made physical as one
bold event. **ALMOST.** The cozy snowy city is there. The jolt is missing because the still doesn't show the event the

1. The wave reads as a soft glow around one block. Show it mid-sweep as one front across the whole hillside: frosted

2. The source is a low dark slab at the left edge. Its small warm door is the only link to the glow, and at phone size

3. The city is a thin strip between the caption band and a large empty field of near snow, so the event is small at

   Check: her feet sit just under the line of the app's bottom bar. Lift her a touch.

**A-B2 · A street of giant machines.** *Gives:* cold awe at scale, a tiny person in a dark canyon of towering

**A-B4 · One computer, a whole block boiling.** *Gives:* whimsical and warm, a smile; the kettles are small at phone

2. The machine stands at the left edge, half over the first column of kitchens, so it reads as a dark pillar beside
   the block. Bring it into the stage, clear of that column, with the neighbour and her dog at its feet. Why: the

**A-B5 · One cup.** *Gives:* cozy, wry intimacy, a toast to the boiling block, a smile. *Wants:* a smile and a breath:

## 3. BOARD READ (the whole board in order, sound off, at phone size, as a viewer)

- **Does the story read from the frames alone?** Mostly. Frame by frame: a snowy city with a warm glow; a hall of
  giant machines; they run hot; one machine against a block of boiling kettles; one cup raised to it; fans blowing
  the warmth away over a frozen bay; the city cheering at its windows; a fingertip writing "2027" and the plan in the
  frost; a frost door opening into the machine hall; pipes, one cold and one warm; warm water along a street into
  homes; your phone and her warm window. Two links break without the captions, and they are this round's main notes:
  - B1 doesn't show that one strange building is warming the city, so "AI heats homes" is only in the words;
  - B10 doesn't show the heat going into the water.
- **One world?** Yes: one Helsinki night (the stucco blocks, the harbour ice, the tram, the ice fisher, snow), one
  honey accent and one resident. The frost chapter changes medium inside her window, and B8 and B9 carry it (her hand,
  the honey lines, the city's warmth through the frost). B10 is the one frame that looks like another film (a
  blueprint on black). Its rework brings it back.
- **One thread?** Yes. The honey heat is in every frame, from the glow to her window, and it crosses into the frost
  drawing as the one painted thing.
- **One arc of feeling?** In shape, yes: cozy-curious, awe, heat, astonishment, a smile, a pang, delight, hope,
  leaning in, the "oh", relief, the wink. The light arc reads at phone size: dark, then the airy frost chapter, then
  warming to the end. Three beats sag:
  - the start (B1 is a calm postcard where the hook needs a jolt);
  - the astonishment (B4 is a smile, not a "whoa");
  - the "oh" (B10 is a hush).
- **Is the film concept clear?** It becomes clear at B8: the frost is the city's sketchbook, and the plan stays frost
  until it is real. Before that, at phone size, the frosted panes of B1, B6 and B11 look like plain white windows, so
  "the frost tells the story" doesn't land until mid-film. B1's front (frosted ahead, honey behind) fixes this at the
  source: the frost clearing becomes the first thing the film shows.
- **Hand-offs:** every frame hands on to the next:
  - the steam out of B3's door pulls us into B4's street;
  - the push into her window takes B4 to B5;
  - B7's one frosted window becomes B8's pane;
  - the box she drew opens as B9's door;
  - the honey leg runs out of B10's drawing into B11's street;
  - the line reaches her door and we pull back to B12;
  - B12 matches B1, closing the loop.
- **For the art director:**
  - the giant computer is drawn four ways (B2, B3, B4, B10) and should become one object at the key frames, so the
    viewer recognizes it each time;
  - the frost lines are the plan's drawing (white frost, part of the story), never an outline on the painted world.
    Keep them that way, since the build carries no ink line.
