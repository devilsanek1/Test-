## 1. The feeling each frame gives (written from the picture alone, before reading anything)

- **A-B2** The feeling it gives: cold, dwarfing awe; a canyon of black machine-towers dotted with lights like
  tower-block windows, one tiny figure facing the snowy city through a window at the far end. Big and still, and a

- **A-B3** The feeling it gives: close, building heat; the same aisle now glows amber like toaster grilles, steam

- **A-B4** The feeling it gives: a whimsical, warm surprise; a whole block where every window holds a steaming kettle,

- **A-B5** The feeling it gives: wry, cosy warmth; a woman with her back to us in a lamp-lit room lifts her steaming cup

- **A-B6** The feeling it gives: wistful waste; three glowing fans on a low shed pour warm steam that turns grey as it

- **A-B7** The feeling it gives: rising eagerness; a long block with windows flung open, silhouettes stretching their

- **A-B9** The feeling it gives: curious wonder at a threshold; a hand swings open a frosted door and a gold-rimmed

- **A-B10** The feeling it gives: a cool, schematic calm; a corridor drawn in thin white frost lines on black, a pale

- **A-B11** The feeling it gives: homely relief; a snowy street between two lit blocks, gold lines running along both

- **A-B12** The feeling it gives: a tender, quiet ending; a hand holds a phone with a chat on it, and across the frozen

- **A-B4alt** The feeling it gives: a dry, clever weighing; on a seesaw in a snowy yard one tall dark machine balances

## 2. Per frame: the feeling wanted beside the feeling given, and the verdict

**A-B1 · The city wakes warm: ALMOST**
- Wanted: surprise and curiosity, a cozy snowy city and an odd true idea ("wait, what?"). Given: a hushed, cozy calm
  with one warm glow. The coziness is there; the surprise is not, because the still never shows the hook's event or its
  odd half.
  1. The wave reads as one block glowing in a soft haze. Make the front one bold sweep across many facades: the panes
     ahead of it plainly frosted, the crest the brightest honey, the cleared panes warm behind, so "warmth is racing
     through the city" is the first read at phone size. The hook has to stop a thumb, and a glow does not.
  2. The source does not read: the windowless building is a low dark strip at the left edge, so "AI" is not in the
     picture and the "wait, what?" (a machine heating homes) has nothing to land on. Make its lit door and the warm run
     leaving it the second read, the run plainly feeding the front.
  3. Mistake: the walker's feet dip under the app's bottom bar (about y 1545 of 1920; the bar starts at 1520). Lift her.

**A-B2 · A street of giant machines: THERE**
- Wanted: a little awe; AI is a real, physical, huge thing. Given: cold, dwarfing awe in a canyon of machines with the
  warm city at the end of the aisle. It reads in a second; the cabinet lights in rows like a facade's windows are this
  film's own rhyme, and the door makes the cold place and the warm place one picture.
- Polish: the floor's thin honey streaks read as scratches at phone size; one soft reflection of the door's light.

**A-B3 · The machines run hot: THERE**
- Wanted: the heat rising, warm, a touch of alarm. Given: close, building heat; the grilles glow, steam rolls, he wipes
  his brow. The same aisle with one change: the cleanest hand-off on the sheet.
- Polish: the haze's light wedges cut into the band's lower corners; keep the band's edge clean.

**A-B4 · One computer, a whole block boiling: ALMOST**
- Wanted: playful astonishment at the size of it. Given: a whimsical, warm surprise (a block of boiling kettles), but
  "this one machine = all of that" takes a second look, and the comparison is the whole beat.
  1. At phone size the computer reads as a dark post at the left edge, half against the facade, and the two clouds read
     as the building's steam. Step the computer clear of the facade into its own space, and stage its cloud and the
     block's one gathered cloud side by side, the same size, with sky between them: one machine, one cloud; forty
     kitchens, one cloud.
  2. The lower third is empty ground while every kettle is small: come closer (the block bigger, the street shorter) so
     each kettle and its curl read at phone size.

**A-B5 · One cup: THERE**
- Wanted: a smile and a breath; the joke lands. Given: wry, cozy warmth, a little toast to the boiling block. One cup
  raised against a building's worth is the joke in one glance; the calm values give the breath.
- Polish: her back is the frame's biggest shape and reads flat grey; give it the lamp's rim and the knit, and a glimpse
  of her cheek lifting, so the smile is seen, not implied (the people in use, at the key frame).

**A-B6 · Warmth drifting away: THERE**
- Wanted: a small pang. Given: wistful waste, warm at the fans and grey as it drifts over the cold. The plume's colour
  tells the story by itself, and the empty ice is the right silence.
- Polish: the ice fisher looks up at the plume (the pang gets a witness), lifted a touch off the app's bottom bar.

**A-B7 · The city throws its windows open: THERE**
- Wanted: the turn, a spark of delight, the city reaching for it. Given: rising eagerness, windows flung open and arms
  up. The gesture reads at phone size and lands the turn on "But".
- Polish: the warm drift they reach for sits behind the roofline, half under the band; bring it lower over the roofs so
  the arms reach into it. Mistake: the man in the street has his legs under the app's bottom bar (feet near y 1680);
  lift him.

**A-B8 · The plan, drawn in the frost: THERE**
- Wanted: hope and anticipation; real and soon, a plan. Given: playful, intimate hope. The best frame on the sheet:
  "2027" and the little line from the machine-house to her home read in a second, the glow makes the plan warm, the
  frost makes it a plan, and her hand makes it human.
- Polish: at phone size the drawn machine-house (a box with two discs) could read as a camera; a curl of steam off its
  fans, or blades in them.

**A-B9 · The frost door opens: THERE**
- Wanted: leaning in; a door opens. Given: curious wonder at a threshold; the honey-rimmed opening and the deep
  corridor pull the eye in. The round-1 rework landed: a place you step into, not a diagram.
- Polish: the frost in the lower third is blotchy (beige-grey mottling); calm it so the door is the one busy thing.

**A-B10 · Cold in, warm out: NOT THERE**
- Wanted: clarity and satisfaction, "oh, that's how". Given: a cool, schematic calm, clear in parts but empty.
- Why the idea fails its feeling: the beat's whole content is the heat leaving the machines into the water, and the
  picture does not show it. The cool pipe and the honey pipe run along opposite walls, the turn that joins them is a
  speck at the vanishing point, and only one cabinet glows, faintly. What remains is thin white lines on black, the dry
  diagram the owner's second rule rules out, and at phone size it is mostly black, so not worth pausing on. Round 1
  refined the lines and colours without changing that. The approach (a long hall in line art on a void) is the
  problem, so the frame gets its one rework.
- For the rework (the builder's call): A-B10alt's staging is the stronger base (one machine close, the water in cool
  at its foot and out honey at its top, read in a glance). Then make it a place: the hall lit from within by the
  machines' honey on the frost, the warm city glowing through, her frost-hand person with a hand on the machine, the
  pipe warming as it climbs.

**A-B11 · Home by home, up the street: ALMOST**
- Wanted: cozy relief; warmth travelling under the snow to homes. Given: homely relief in a warm, busy street, but the
  tram pulls the eye more than the warmth does.
  1. The tram's two headlights are the brightest whites in the frame, dead centre, so the eye lands on the tram and
     not on the warm water reaching the doors. Move the tram aside or back, or dim its lights, so the eye runs the warm
     line to the doors and on to her block, the street's one sight line.
  2. "Under the snow" does not read: the warm water is a lit stripe lying on the kerbs (neon kerb lights, or rails).
     Show it under: snow melted in a line over the pipe, the honey glowing up through it and turning in at each door.

**A-B12 · Your chat, her window: ALMOST**
- Wanted: personal warmth and a wink; your own chat warms a home, back to the start. Given: a tender, quiet ending,
  "my message, her warmth", and the loop to B1 reads.
  1. The payoff's two reads, your bubble and her window, are both small at phone size (her figure is a sliver). Step
     the view in on her block, or raise the phone a little, keeping the bubble, the shore and her window on one
     diagonal, so the cause and its warmth are the two biggest honeys.
  2. The wink is missing: the frame is tender but has no smile. One gesture would give it: she lifts her cup toward
     you, B5's toast returned.
- Polish: the plan's building drawn in white frost lines reads as a neon gate at phone size; draw it in B8's
  glittering finger hand, or let it go.

## 3. Board read (the whole board in order, sound off, as a viewer)

- **The story from the frames alone:** mostly yes. Sound off, a viewer follows: giant machines -> they glow hot -> one of
  them is a whole block of kettles -> a toast -> the heat thrown away over the ice -> the city wants it -> a plan for
  2027 -> a door into the plan -> (B10: lines) -> warm water up the street into homes -> your chat, her warm window. Two
  soft spots: without its caption, B1 does not say "AI heats homes" (the source is invisible), and B10's "how" only
  half reads.
- **One world:** yes. One Helsinki winter night, one palette, one honey accent, the frozen harbour, stucco blocks, a
  tram. The frost chapter's light frames (B8-B9) are tied in by her window and her hand. B10 is the one frame that looks
  like another film's graphic (white lines on black).
- **One thread:** the board's strength. The honey heat is in every frame and travels: wave -> glowing grilles -> kettles
  -> cup -> plume -> open windows -> frost lines -> door rim -> pipe -> kerb -> bubble and window.
- **One arc of feeling:** it moves one step per frame through the middle (awe, heat, astonishment, smile, pang, delight,
  hope, leaning in). It opens on its quietest note (B1 is a calm glow where the hook needs an event) and sags at B10,
  where "oh, that's how" should brighten. B11-B12 land relief and tenderness, the wink still missing.
- **The film concept:** clear: the heat of the one building with no windows, carried to the windows that want it, with
  the frost telling the story. Windows carry B4, B5, B7, B8, B11 and B12; frost as the plan carries B8-B10 and B12.
  Before B7, frost reads as pale cold windows rather than frost; that is fine at that scale, and B7 brings it close.
- **Rhythm (for the shot plan):** five frames share the same central vanishing-point composition (B2, B3, B9, B10, B11),
  and three share the same harbour wide with a big empty foreground (B1, B6, B12). Reworking B10 around one machine,
  close, also breaks the run of corridors.

## 4. The sequence's verdict: ALMOST

Seven frames THERE (B2, B3, B5, B6, B7, B8, B9: polish only), four ALMOST (B1, B4, B11, B12: their last adjust round),
one NOT THERE (B10: its one rework, from B10alt's staging). The sequence is not THERE because the hook (B1) and the
mechanism (B10) need more than polish. The concept, the world and the thread are sound; nothing here asks for a
different film.
