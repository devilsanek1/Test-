## THE FILM CONCEPT: "The open machine" (one piece missing)

**In one line:** Helsinki already has warm-water pipes under its snowy streets, and AI's giant computers already make
heat by the hall-full. The film opens that whole warm machine for us one door at a time, and shows the one piece still
missing between the machines and the homes being drawn in.

**The world:** one snowy harbour neighbourhood of Helsinki on a winter night, the real Katajanokka quay (from the photos
in `B_src/refs/`). It has Jugendstil stone blocks with a corner turret and steep tin roofs, lamps hung on wires across
the street, the green tram, and shovelled snow banks with bollards. The state icebreakers are moored at the quay (pale
decks over black hulls). There is the frozen harbour with a winter swimmer's ice hole by a ladder, and, at the quay's
end, an empty snowy lot by the water. Everything cold is night-blue and snow-white. The only warm colour in the film is
the heat.

**The central device: the machine seen through warm openings, and the one opening still missing.** Every frame's place
to look is one warm opening onto the mechanism in a cold, shut winter: a hall's big door, one computer's cabinet door, a
drawn door, a soft thaw in the snow over a pipe, a home's window, your phone's screen. The heat writes its own route.
Where it flows, the snow melts into a dark wet seam with the warm showing through, and the manholes puff steam. Where
the snow lies untouched, the heat isn't getting through. Between the city's warm line and the harbour lot lies one
stretch of untouched snow: the missing piece. The plan is the only thing in the film drawn instead of painted, as pale
pencil lines over that snow (painted = real, pencil = planned). Inside the drawn hall the machines are painted, and the
night shows through its line walls.

**The thread's journey (the heat):**
1. Born in the giant computers: their backs go honey-hot.
2. Measured: one cabinet opens on forty boiling kettles.
3. Thrown out the wrong way: roof fans blow it up into the snowing night, where it lifts the snowflakes and is gone.
4. Wanted: the city's own warm line races to the edge of the empty lot and stops, reaching for the plume across the ice.
5. Drawn in: a pencil hall and a dashed pencil pipe across the gap, with a 2027 tag.
6. Carried: a drawn door opens; inside, cool water runs in and honey water runs out.
7. Home: under the snow, up the wall, into one window's radiator.
8. In your hand: your chat, below her warm window. Then the loop back to frame 0.

**The arc of feeling:**
- Curiosity: a cozy snowy quay lit by an odd building made of computer lights.
- Awe: a cathedral of giant computers.
- Warm, a touch of alarm: the heat pouring off them.
- Playful astonishment: forty kettles inside one.
- A smile: one cup of tea raised to them.
- A pang: warmth blown into the night, the snow falling upward.
- A spark of delight: the city's warm line racing to the gap.
- Hope: the missing piece drawn in pale pencil.
- Leaning in: a drawn door opening on warm light.
- Clarity: cool in, honey out.
- Cozy relief: warmth under the snow, arriving home.
- A wink: your chat, and her toast from the warm window. Back to frame 0.

**One cast:** the one person near the camera is her, a young woman who tends the machines on the night shift and lives in
the old house at the quay. She walks home in B1, works in the hall in B2-B6 (small in B2, B3 and B6, beside the open
computer in B4, only her hand and her mug in B5), reaches toward the plume in B7, stands by the plan in B8, leans in at
the drawn door in B9, turns its valve in B10, and is in her warm window in B11-B12. Her mug (dark, a pale band) is the
toast in B5 and comes back raised in her window in B12: the wink. The city's people lean out of their windows in B7
(and B7alt). The one "you" is POV only: your gloved hand and phone in B12. Pip (hidden, for G3) could be on the
icebreaker's rail (B1, B7, B8) and in a tram window (B11).

**Two views carry the change (the library's N14, one change each):**
- The quay along to the hall: B1, the hook, the wish.
- The quay's empty lot: B7, the city's line stops short; B8, the plan pencils itself across the gap.

The machine's inside (B2-B5, B9-B10) is always reached through a door, so the snowy city stays in reach.

**Truth** (`video/docs/v5/research/factlock.md`): the new data center is only ever a plan.
- B1 and B12 show the wish, under the voice's "wants" and "could soon".
- B7-B8 show the lot empty, then a drawing with a year on it.
- B9-B10 show how it WOULD work, inside pencil walls.
- B2-B6 are data centers in general. Their roof fans are the usual way to throw the heat out.
- One cabinet = forty kettles (FL 2.2).
- The heat goes into a radiator, never a tap.
- No company name or logo anywhere. The plan's site is a generic empty lot, never a real one.

The hall at the quay's end is drawn in pencil wherever it appears (B1, far in B12), its computers painted inside: frame
0 already says "planned", so B8's drawing reads at once.
