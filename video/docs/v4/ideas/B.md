The sheet: `B_sheet.png` (B-B1 to B-B12 in beat order, then B-B5alt, B-B6alt, B-B7alt, B-B12alt). Source: `B_src/`
(`lib.js` the drawing kit, `frames.js` every frame, `pages/<id>.html` one page per frame, `make_sheet.py` the sheet; the

## THE FILM CONCEPT: "The open machine" (one piece missing)

**In one line:** Helsinki already has warm-water pipes under its snowy streets, and AI's giant computers already make
heat by the hall-full. The film opens that whole warm machine for us one door at a time, and shows the one piece still
missing between the machines and the homes being drawn in.

**The world:** one snowy harbour neighbourhood of Helsinki on a winter night, the real Katajanokka quay (from the photos
in `B_src/refs/`). It has Jugendstil stone blocks with a corner turret and steep tin roofs, lamps hung on wires across
the street, the green tram, and shovelled snow banks with bollards. The state icebreakers are moored at the quay (pale
decks over black hulls). There is the frozen harbour with a winter swimmer's ice hole by a ladder, and, at the quay's
end, an empty snowy lot by the water. Everything cold is night-blue and snow-white. The only warm colour in the film is
the heat.

**The central device: the machine seen through warm openings, and the one opening still missing.** Every frame's place
to look is one warm opening onto the mechanism in a cold, shut winter: a hall's big door, one computer's cabinet door, a
drawn door, a soft thaw in the snow over a pipe, a home's window, your phone's screen. The heat writes its own route.
Where it flows, the snow melts into a dark wet seam with the warm showing through, and the manholes puff steam. Where
the snow lies untouched, the heat isn't getting through. Between the city's warm line and the harbour lot lies one
stretch of untouched snow: the missing piece. The plan is the only thing in the film drawn instead of painted, as pale
pencil lines over that snow (painted = real, pencil = planned). Inside the drawn hall the machines are painted, and the
night shows through its line walls.

**The thread's journey (the heat):**
1. Born in the giant computers: their backs go honey-hot.
2. Measured: one cabinet opens on forty boiling kettles.
3. Thrown out the wrong way: roof fans blow it up into the snowing night, where it lifts the snowflakes and is gone.
4. Wanted: the city's own warm line races to the edge of the empty lot and stops, reaching for the plume across the ice.
5. Drawn in: a pencil hall and a dashed pencil pipe across the gap, with a 2027 tag.
6. Carried: a drawn door opens; inside, cool water runs in and honey water runs out.
7. Home: under the snow, up the wall, into one window's radiator.
8. In your hand: your chat, below her warm window. Then the loop back to frame 0.

**The arc of feeling:**
- Curiosity: a cozy snowy quay lit by an odd building made of computer lights.
- Awe: a cathedral of giant computers.
- Warm, a touch of alarm: the heat pouring off them.
- Playful astonishment: forty kettles inside one.
- A smile: one cup of tea raised to them.
- A pang: warmth blown into the night, the snow falling upward.
- A spark of delight: the city's warm line racing to the gap.
- Hope: the missing piece drawn in pale pencil.
- Leaning in: a drawn door opening on warm light.
- Clarity: cool in, honey out.
- Cozy relief: warmth under the snow, arriving home.
- A wink: your chat, and her toast from the warm window. Back to frame 0.

**One cast:** the one person near the camera is her, a young woman who tends the machines on the night shift and lives in
the old house at the quay. She walks home in B1, works in the hall in B2-B6 (small in B2-B4, near in B5), reaches toward
the plume in B7, stands by the plan in B8, leans in at the drawn door in B9, turns its valve in B10, and is in her warm
window in B11-B12. Her toast in B5 comes back as the wink in B12. The one "you" is POV only: your gloved hand and phone
in B12. Pip (hidden, for G3) could be on the icebreaker's rail (B1, B7, B8) and in a tram window (B11).

**Two views carry the change (the library's N14, one change each):**
- The quay along to the hall: B1, the hook, the wish.
- The quay's empty lot: B7, the city's line stops short; B8, the plan pencils itself across the gap.

The machine's inside (B2-B5, B9-B10) is always reached through a door, so the snowy city stays in reach.

**Truth** (`video/docs/v5/research/factlock.md`): the new data center is only ever a plan.
- B1 and B12 show the wish, under the voice's "wants" and "could soon".
- B7-B8 show the lot empty, then a drawing with a year on it.
- B9-B10 show how it WOULD work, inside pencil walls.
- B2-B6 are data centers in general. Their roof fans are the usual way to throw the heat out.
- One cabinet = forty kettles (FL 2.2).
- The heat goes into a radiator, never a tap.
- No company name or logo anywhere. The plan's site is a generic empty lot, never a real one.

A judge may still read B1's hall as built. If so, draw its walls in pencil there too, keeping the computers inside
painted.

## The beats (one idea each, beat order; times from `voice_words.json`)

Each beat gives what the viewer sees, gets and feels, and how it hands on. Then its card in short: the feeling, the
light, the eye, the library devices it already uses, and the words.

**B-B1 · The warm line** (0.13-3.92) "Did you know Helsinki wants AI to heat seventy thousand homes?"
- Sees: the Katajanokka quay at night from a person's eye: an icebreaker moored on the left, the Jugendstil row with its
  turret on the right, snow falling, her walking home. At the quay's end stands a building whose whole face is the
  small cool lights of giant computers, its big door open on rows of them, steam rolling off its roof. A warm seam runs
  out of that door under the snow toward us (manholes puffing), branching into the blocks, whose windows glow.
- Gets: AI could heat Helsinki's homes. Feels: "wait, what?".
- Frame 0's event: the face lights blink on at "AI" (1.5 s), the seam fires on "heat" (2.14 s), and the windows pop warm
  in a wave on "seventy thousand homes".
- Hands on: the seam leads the eye back into the open door (B2).
- Card: cozy and curious; a cool night, warm points spreading. Eye: the seam, the lit door, the turret's window.
  Devices: B1 leading lines, B2 one point closed by the hall (E7), E1 her small figure, E2 the lit window. Words:
  "70,000 / homes?" (R3).

**B-B2 · A cathedral of computers** (4.28-8.22) "AI actually runs on giant computers, in buildings called data centers."
- Sees: an aisle running away between two walls of giant computers, taller than a person, thousands of small lights,
  cable trays overhead, the floor shining. She walks small down it pushing a cart; snow blown in over the threshold lies
  in a drift with her cart's tracks; far at the end, a door stands open on the snowy night.
- Gets: AI is real machines, huge, in a building. Feels: a little awe.
- Hands on: she reaches the aisle behind the machines, where it runs hot (B3).
- Card: awe; a cool, even light, a long tunnel of value. Eye: the far door, then her. Devices: B2 one point, D2 scale,
  D7 repetition, E7 the closed vista. Words: "giant computers," / "data centers."

**B-B3 · Too hot for snow** (8.76-10.64) "Those computers get really hot."
- Sees: the hot aisle behind the machines: both walls of grilles glow honey, and a warm haze rises off them to the
  vents. She stands in the middle pulling off her beanie, her hair lifting in the hot draught; flakes blown in behind her
  turn to drops before they reach the machines.
- Gets: they make heat, a lot. Feels: warm, a touch of alarm.
- Hands on: she turns to one computer's front door (B4).
- Card: heat rising; the warmest frame of the film. Eye: her, between the glowing walls. Devices: B4 tunnel, E1 the
  figure where the lines meet. Words: "really / hot." (R4, "hot" lit).

**B-B4 · Forty kettles in one** (10.96-17.34) "To put it into perspective, just one of these computers makes as much heat
as FORTY kettles, boiling non-stop."
- Sees: one giant computer's front door swung wide. Inside, where its trays should be, stand forty electric kettles in
  eight rows of five, their water gauges glowing, all boiling, their steam rolling up into one cloud. She steps back to
  take it in; the rest of the row stands shut. On "To put it into perspective," the door swings; on "FORTY" the kettles
  are counted on, row by row.
- Gets: one computer = forty boiling kettles. Feels: playful astonishment.
- Hands on: the steam rolls down to her one mug (B5).
- Card: astonishment; the cabinet a lit, steaming opening in a dark row. Eye: the kettle wall, then her. Devices: C1
  frame in a frame, D7 "how many", D2 her scale. Words: "40" (R3) / "kettles," then "boiling non-stop."

**B-B5 · One cup** (17.76-18.84) "That's a lot of tea."
- Sees: closer. She sits on a stool in profile beside the open cabinet of forty steaming kettles and raises ONE mug to it,
  its tea warm and its tag hanging: a toast.
- Gets: forty kettles' worth, one cup. Feels: a smile and a breath.
- Hands on: the kettles' steam rises out of the top of the frame, up through the roof (B6). Her toast comes back in B12.

**B-B6 · Snow falling up** (19.26-22.88) "Normally, data centers just use big fans to blow that heat outside."
- Sees: on the hall's roof at night, one big fan near us (and its neighbours behind) blows the warm air straight up.
  Above it the falling snow is lifted back UP in streaks and melts away, and the warm plume thins into the dark. She
  stands small between the fans, looking up. Beyond the roof's parapet: a snowy land, other halls far off each sending up

- Gets: normally that heat is just thrown away into the sky. Feels: a small pang.
- Hands on: the plain cut on "But" to Helsinki's quay, where that plume rises across the ice (B7).
- Card: a pang; the darkest frame, the warm only at the fan's mouth, fading to grey. Eye: the big fan, up its plume,

  "blow that heat outside."

**B-B7 · The city reaches** (23.46-25.22) "But Helsinki WANTS that heat."
- Sees: closer down the quay to its empty lot by the water. The city's own warm line comes racing from under our feet
  and stops with a spark of warm and a burst of steam where the untouched snow begins. She stands beside it, one hand

- Gets: Helsinki's own warm pipes want that heat. Feels: the turn, a spark of delight, the city reaching.

**B-B8 · The missing piece, drawn** (25.54-27.96) "The city has a plan to use it, starting next year."

**B-B9 · A drawn door opens** (28.34-29.56) "So how would that work?"

**B-B10 · Cool in, honey out** (29.56-33.98) "A big new data center would take the heat out of its computers with water

## Alternates (the four beats I am least sure of)

**B-B5alt · One cup, close** (for B5)
- Sees: no one. ONE big mug steams on the open door's ledge, its tea warm and its tag hanging, against the soft pale
  wall of forty steaming kettles.
- Why: the cleanest read in 1.08 s (N9 isolation, D7's one break). It trades her charm for that clarity.

**B-B6alt · Everywhere, up it goes** (for B6)
- Sees: the wide. Halls scattered over a snowy land at night, every roof sending its heat up in plumes that thin away,
  and Helsinki a small warm cluster on the far coast.
- Why: it says "normally, everywhere" as the film's one scale moment, where the main B6 says "big fans" up close. It is
  murkier at phone size.

**B-B7alt · The city leans out** (for B7)
- Sees: a Jugendstil facade front-on. Window after window swings open, and people lean out toward a warm plume drifting
  in over the harbour: a hand held out, a mug raised, mittens hung out, a cat on a sill.
- Why: the most delight and the most life of any turn I drew. But it is a feeling, not the mechanism, and it bends the
  physics a little (warm air drifting to the city). It also loses the gap that B8 fills.

**B-B12alt · The cat on the warm sill** (for B12)
- Sees: inside her flat. A cat sleeps on the deep sill above the warm radiator, her phone beside it pops your chat
  bubble, and through the window are the quay, the city's windows and the hall's lights far away.
- Why: cozier and more intimate, and the cat is a wink anyone reads. It trades the POV "you" for her phone, and the loop
  then needs a pull out through the window.
