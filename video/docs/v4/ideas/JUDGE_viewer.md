# Video 4 ideas: the cold viewer's judgement

A fresh judge (built and reviewed none of this). The eye: a regular person scrolling short videos at night who has never
heard of this project. Part 1 was written from the four final sheets alone (`A_sheet.png` to `D_sheet.png`, each frame
cropped and seen at phone size in beat order, sound off, then imagining the voice), before any notes were read.

## Part 1: from the frames alone

### A · The Frosted City
**The film it makes:** a grey snowy city across a frozen harbour, a warm beam sliding along one block. A canyon of
tower-sized machines with a tiny person; they glow and steam; a black machine beside an apartment block whose every
window holds a boiling kettle (I grin); a woman raising her tea to that block from her window; the heat drifting off as
clouds over the frozen bay, one ice-fisher below; a block throwing its windows open, arms up; a finger drawing "2027" and
a little plan (building, pipe, home) in a window's frost (lovely); a frosty door opening on a corridor of machines; a
pipe going in cold and out warm beside a pale, frost-white figure; a street whose kerbs glow warm; my phone with a chat,
and across the water one bright window with a woman waving. With the voice I follow the whole story, but it plays calm
and grey from end to end: good illustrations of each sentence more than one rising story. The heat changes shape every
frame (beam, glow, clouds, kettles, cup, cheering windows, frost lines, kerb glow), so there is no one thing my eye
follows, and the frost arrives only at B8.
**The arc of feeling:** B1 a calm postcard, mild curiosity (the beam is pretty, I can't tell what it is) -> B2 quiet awe
-> B3 warm, faint alarm -> B4 a grin (the kettle block) -> B5 a small toast -> B6 grey and empty, the pang barely there ->
B7 cheer -> B8 tender hope (the finger in the frost) -> B9 lean in -> B10 half-clear (cold in, warm out, but who is the
white ghost?) -> B11 cozy -> B12 a sweet loop back to B1's view.

### B · The Open Machine
**The film it makes:** a real-looking snowy harbour street (a ferry, an old brick building with a tower) with a glowing
crack of warmth running through the snow at my feet, "70,000 homes?" huge in the pale sky: I stop. A cathedral of black
computers, a tiny figure at the bright end; the same aisle glowing orange, a man in the middle with his hat off, steaming
and fanning himself (I laugh); one rack that is a stack of forty glowing kettles; one mug held up in front of a wall of
mugs; a round fan in a roof puffing sparks into the dark; the harbour house with everyone leaning out of the windows
toward a warm plume over the water, the glowing line ending in a spark at a woman's feet; the same house with a "2027"
sign on the outline of a building that isn't there yet; a drawn door opening, light pouring out, a woman in it; a woman
turning a valve while a pipe snakes white-in, gold-out through a rack (an instant "oh, that's how"); the line glowing
under the snow past a tram to a lit window; my phone, the line running from it up the street to a window where someone
raises a cup. I understood every frame with the sound off: the glowing line is a fuse I follow from the first frame to
the last. But many frames are half pale sky (the picture lives in the bottom half), and B5 and B6 are thin.
**The arc of feeling:** B1 "wait, what?" (a glow in the snow) -> B2 awe -> B3 a laugh (the sweating man) -> B4
astonishment (forty kettles) -> B5 a mild smile (the mug is lonely on a white page) -> B6 a quiet, slightly abstract pang
-> B7 the turn, delight -> B8 hope, but it is B7 again with a sign -> B9 lean in -> B10 "oh, that's how" -> B11 cozy
relief -> B12 the warm payoff, the line from my hand to her window.

### C · The Warm Current
**The film it makes:** a polished navy night: my own hand and phone in front of a harbour street where a hole in the snow
glows and steams; a corridor of computers like a street of office windows; one rack glowing orange in a dark wall, a man
flinching; the rack's face made of forty steaming kettles; then the rack wearing a giant knitted tea cosy while a little
man pours himself a cup from its spout (the best laugh of the four); rooftop fans blowing gold plumes away over the
roofs; a harbour street where manholes light up one by one; a chalk drawing of a building floating over the ice, "2027"
and a chalk pipe swallowing a gold stream; a chalk door opening on warm light; a rack with a pipe zigzagging blue to
gold; the gold line running up the street to a facade's door, its windows glowing; my phone again and a big golden
window with a woman and her cup. It reads, it is elegant and one world, and the gold line travels. But the frames are
sparse (big empty dark sky and ground), the places generic, the people tiny silhouettes, and B7-B8 lose me (the manholes
are a path, not a city wanting anything; the chalk scene is busy).
**The arc of feeling:** B1 curiosity -> B2 mild awe (another corridor) -> B3 heat, a little abstract -> B4 astonishment
-> B5 a real laugh (the tea cosy) -> B6 a pretty pang -> B7 flat: a path, no turn, nobody delighted -> B8 confused hope
(the chalk) -> B9 lean in -> B10 clarity -> B11 cozy -> B12 warm, back to B1's phone.

### D · The Warmth Comes Home
**The film it makes:** a woman seen from behind in her armchair at a frosted window, phone in hand; the frost thaws in a
patch and the city's warm windows show through. Her armchair, lamp and all, in the middle of an endless hall of giant
computers (funny and surreal: her chat lives here); a black building so hot the snow melts into a pond around it and
icicles drip; her kitchen crammed with boiling kettles, steam everywhere; she sips one cup, eyes closed, calm in the
middle of it (a smile); a fan block on a roof blowing a plume over little snowy houses; a whole block leaning out of
their windows to catch gold ribbons of warmth drifting past (delight); she at her window looking across the bay at the
outline of a building, a gold line running from it to her house; a door of light opening in the outline, footprints
going in; a man by a rack, an icy pipe in, a copper pipe out on poles toward the city; she looks down on her street
where a warm line glows under the snow; her feet in socks on the hot radiator, phone in hand, the city in the window. I
understood it and I cared: it is her story. The weak spots: the first frame (dark and quiet, a back in an armchair: a
picture to linger on, not one to stop a thumb), the heavy grey bar across the top, and a dim, murky palette in several
frames.
**The arc of feeling:** B1 quiet, cozy intrigue (the frost thawing) -> B2 surprise and a grin (the armchair among the
giants) -> B3 alarm (the snow melting away) -> B4 playful astonishment (the kettle kitchen) -> B5 a smile and a breath
(her sip) -> B6 a soft, grey pang -> B7 delight (the block reaches) -> B8 hope (her plan across the bay) -> B9 lean in ->
B10 clarity, but who is this man? -> B11 cozy relief (her street) -> B12 warm and personal, the "aww" I would send
(socks on the radiator).

## Part 2: what the notes change (read after Part 1: each `X.md` and its last `X_EVAL_r3.md`)

- **A:** the frost is the device (frost = planned, cleared = warm) and one resident runs through the film. Neither
  reached me cold: I saw frost only from B8, and never knew the hand in B8, the white figure in B10 and the toast in B12
  were one woman. The concept lives on the page more than in the pictures; its evaluator's THERE on all twelve is beat
  by beat, while my doubt is the whole (calm from end to end).
- **B:** the pick for B6 is B6alt, "Only the fox gets it" (the sheet's B6 slot shows the retired one): it mends my
  thinnest middle frame with a wry smile. "Her" is one night-shift worker in every chapter; I read B3's figure as a man,
  so the cast does not read yet. Its evaluator's two ALMOSTs (B5 cold and a robot-looking glove, B9's bar chart) are
  exactly where I faltered; both have a round left.
- **C:** your question flying as a cyan spark from your phone to the computer would tie B1's phone to B2's hall, but it
  lives only in the shot plan; on the sheet the link is missing. Its evaluator's soft spot (B6-B7, the gentlest frames at
  the turn) is where I went flat.
- **D:** the joins (the room becoming the hall round her chair, her window pushed open and the camera rising with the
  steam, the push through her window) promise a film that flows better than the sheet, which raises it for me. B3 and B6
  (ALMOST; B3 at its cap) are where I got bored.

## Part 3: the ranking, through the cold viewer's eye

1. **B, The Open Machine:** the only frame 0 that stopped me with a plain "wait, what?" (a glowing seam in the snow at
   my feet, "70,000 homes?" huge), the only film I understood frame by frame with the sound off, and one physical
   thread I could follow from my feet to my phone to her window; two laughs in the hall (the "phew", the kettle rack)
   and a wry fox in the middle. What it lacks: a person to care about, and three thin frames (B5, B8, B9).
2. **D, The Warmth Comes Home (a close second):** the heart of the round; one woman I followed and smiled with most
   often (her armchair among the giants, her kitchen of kettles, her whole block reaching, her socks on the radiator)
   and the most specific Helsinki. But a dark, quiet opening I might scroll past, and the middle sags twice into grey
   machines without her (B3, B6).
3. **C, The Warm Current:** elegant, one clean gold thread, and the best single laugh of the round (the tea cosy); but
   sparse frames, generic places and tiny people, and its turn and plan (B7-B8) are the flattest stretch of any film:
   nobody is delighted.
4. **A, The Frosted City:** a coherent, specific grey city with the round's most tender picture (her finger drawing
   "2027" in the frost) and a clever kettle block; but the calmest film: a soft hook (a beam), the heat changing shape
   every frame so I follow no one thing, and a frost device I never decode, whose chapter (B9-B10, the white ghost)
   feels like another film.

## The one I would make: B, The Open Machine

From the viewer's seat it is the one I would stop for, follow without the sound, and watch to its last frame, because
the warm seam runs from frame 0 to my own phone. To become the one I send to a friend it must gain a heart (her) and
lose its three thin frames, all inside its own concept: the quay, the warm seam, the hall, the pencil plan, her.

### Its beats that most need to improve, and how (most important first)

1. **Her, across the film (B1, B3-B5, B9, B10, B12): the heart.** I followed the seam, never a person. Make her the
   same person at a glance in every chapter (the people in use, one coat, one beanie, the banded mug) and show her face
   where the beat is a feeling: B3's "phew" (eyes shut, the beanie fanning), B5's smile, B12's toast turned to me. Then
   the woman walking home in B1 is the one who raises her mug to my phone, and the payoff is a person, not only a line.
2. **B5 One cup (a smile and a breath):** my weakest frame: a lonely mug on a pale page, held by a grey glove that reads
   as a robot's, raised to a cold grey wall; I barely smiled. Raise it to B4's blaze (the same open machine behind, its
   forty kettles still boiling honey through the steam), make her hand a person's (a knit glove, the tea's warm light on
   its knuckles, her coat's cuff) and let a cheek lift at the frame's edge; the mug stays the one dark crisp shape. The
   joke is one cup against a machine's worth, so the machine must still be blazing.
3. **B9 A drawn door opens (leaning in):** inside the door I saw a bar chart, a dry diagram exactly where the film
   promises the real thing. Draw the film's own machines in there, an aisle of B2-B4's cabinets running back into the
   warm light with the pipes along their tops, so the door pulls me in with her.
4. **B8 The missing piece, drawn (hope):** as a still it is B7 again with a sign: the paper hides the drawing and the
   dashed pipe across the untouched snow (the missing piece itself) is a few faint dots. The drawing is the beat's one
   change, so make it what I see: the pencil hall's roofline, big door and windows over the lot, the dashed pipe bold
   across the gap with a faint warmth along it, the "2027" paper small on its door.
5. **B6alt Only the fox gets it (a pang with a wry smile):** it made me smile, but at phone size the fox is a grey cat.
   Its shape must say fox (the brush tail over its nose, the pointed mask), because it is the punchline, and the melt
   must visibly lead nowhere, so the waste is felt.
6. **B1 The warm line ("wait, what?"):** keep what stopped me, the luminous snowy sky and the seam at my feet. One gap:
   the hall at the quay's end reads as a glass pavilion, so the seam seems to come out of a pavilion; let its rows of
   small computer lights read, so "AI" has a picture on the voice's "AI".
7. **B10 Cool in, honey out ("oh, that's how"):** clear, but the machine is an empty dark box (a boiler) and the cold
   is only a white pipe. Its trays and small lights behind the pipe make it a computer; the frost and icicles at its foot
   and the breath of steam at the valve must be felt at phone size.

Smaller, for the key frames: B4's empty grey right half gets the row's other closed cabinets ("just ONE of these
computers"); B12's phone lifted clear of the app's caption zone; and across the film the pale field above each picture
becomes the world's own calm (the snowy sky, the hall's high light, the steam), so every frame is one picture from top
to bottom, never a picture under a page.
