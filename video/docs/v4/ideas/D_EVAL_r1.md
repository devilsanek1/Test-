## First read, from the picture alone (written before BRIEF.md and D.md)

- **D-B1** The feeling it gives: a quiet, snug-but-cold winter night at home: someone alone in an armchair looking out at a snowy city, the glowing radiator the only warm thing; calm, a little lonely; nothing happens yet (the frost just sits in the window corners, the hook is in the words, not in the picture).
- **D-B2** The feeling it gives: cold, vast, impersonal: an endless dark aisle of blinking machines dwarfing a tiny figure; awe with a chill. Reads "data centre" at once, but it is the familiar stock picture, and nothing in it ties to her (no phone, no home, no warmth).
- **D-B3** The feeling it gives: an ominous industrial block pouring plumes into the night, the snow melted away in a dark ring around it; "this thing is hot" reads (orange glow at the vents, icicles, the bare ring), but the plumes read as factory smoke, so it leans to pollution and threat more than to wasted warmth.
- **D-B4** The feeling it gives: a gentle absurd joke: a pale kitchen overrun by steaming kettles on every surface while she sits calmly with a mug; whimsical and readable at a glance, but washed-out cool grey daylight, so the heat feels faint rather than hot; it reads "lots of kettles", not a number.
- **D-B5** The feeling it gives: calm, droll contentment: a close side view of her sipping, eyes closed, kettles lined up behind; a soft, pale punchline beat with almost no warmth in it for a beat about tea and heat.
- **D-B6** The feeling it gives: melancholy waste: a black fan box breathing a tall plume up into the night over a small village of snowy houses, more plumes on the horizon; a clear big-against-small picture, slightly ominous (again smoke-like); the clearest "the heat goes nowhere" frame on the sheet.
- **D-B7** The feeling it gives: longing with a comic edge: she stands at the window with both arms flung out, one hand toward a plume over the city; "she reaches for the heat" reads, but the pose reads as a stretch or a scarecrow, and the pale fog in the room and the cold grey radiator make the moment cool and flat.
- **D-B8** The feeling it gives: quiet, hopeful waiting: she leans on the sill looking at a ghostly white outline of a building across the bay; "something will be built there" reads, but the outline is small and faint at phone size; calm, static, the same window and the same back view as B7.
- **D-B9** The feeling it gives: curiosity inside an empty diagram: a white-outlined box in the snow, a door swung open onto three line-drawn racks and a spill of light; inviting ("let's look inside") but thin and schematic; nothing to pause on.
- **D-B10** The feeling it gives: clean, logical "how it works": a cold white pipe in, a warm orange pipe out of a black rack, steam rising; reads in a second, but it is a dry explainer diagram on a blank snowfield, with no person and no home in it.
- **D-B11** The feeling it gives: anticipation and connection: from high above, a warm line runs down a snowy night street between lit apartment blocks toward her at the bottom; real depth, the most alive of the "how it works" frames; the warm line can read as a road's centre marking.
- **D-B12** The feeling it gives: snug, warm contentment: socked feet up on a glowing radiator, phone in hand, the snowy city outside; the warmth has arrived; cosy and complete, the strongest frame on the sheet and a clear echo of B1.
- **D-B2alt** The feeling it gives: intimate curiosity: she holds her phone up to the window and it lines up with a steaming building across the bay, its screen a grid of machine lights; a clever, quiet "my chat lives over there"; the phone-in-the-picture is small but reads.
- **D-B7alt** The feeling it gives: playful, collective craving: a whole lit block, every window a warm orange box with a silhouette reaching a pole toward drifting steam clouds; bold, graphic, warm, worth pausing on; the figures are generic pawns and the arms read as sticks or fishing rods.
- **D-B8alt** The feeling it gives: an intimate daydream, but mostly blank: a finger sketch on steamed glass with drips; the sketched shape reads as a table or a comb, not a building, and the dark hand at the right is an unclear blob; pale and empty at phone size.
- **D-B11alt** The feeling it gives: a quiet homecoming: a warm line runs straight down a snowy street to a steaming manhole and her lit window at the end; symmetric and clear, but sparse and cold (few warm windows); anticipation, slightly lonely.

## Per frame: the feeling wanted, the feeling given, the verdict

Read after the first lines above, beside BRIEF.md's beats, D.md's concept and PIPELINE_V2's FRAME LOOP, ONE FILM and
RICH WORLD sections. The thumbnails are value sketches, so the verdicts judge the idea and the whole frame, not the
finish. The marked-up sheet: `D_EVAL_r1.png`.

### D-B1 "The frost clears onto 70,000 windows": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** surprise and curiosity: a cozy snowy city and an odd true idea ("wait, what?"). **Gives:** cozy, calm, a little lonely; nothing happens yet.
- The whole frame is right and worth pausing on (her from behind, the chat, the radiator, the window as the film's gate, the domes over the roofs). What holds it back:
  1. **The hook's event is not in the picture.** The frost sits in two corners. Let the frost BE the event: the pane frosted over at frame 0 and clearing onto the city (the key frame caught mid-clear), because "wait, what?" has to be a visible change from the first frame, not a mood.
  2. **Seventy thousand homes are a thin line of specks.** Bring the far city up and wider in the glass, a sea of windows across the bay, so the wave of windows warming reads as one big event at phone size: it is the claim made physical.
  3. **Her radiator is already hot.** That spends the payoff (B12's "one change: the warmth") in the first four seconds; measured, B1 already shows more than half of B12's honey. Keep it cold here, at most its first fin waking on the "ding", so the home's warmth belongs to the end.

### D-B2 "Her chat's other end: a city of machines": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** a little awe: AI is a real, physical, huge thing. **Gives:** cold, vast awe, but the familiar data-hall aisle, with nothing of her in it.
- Keep the high catwalk view and the tiny walkers: they carry the scale. What holds it back:
  1. **The eye has nowhere to land.** The far door, meant as the focal, is a grey speck and its warm city is invisible at phone size. Make the door the frame's one light, big enough to show her city's warm windows in it, because it is the focal, the thread's warm accent and the link to her chat.
  2. **It is the stock picture.** Push D's own idea, "a city of machines": rack tops like roofs, aisles like streets with walkers, the lights like a city's windows at night, so it is this film's picture and visibly rhymes with B11 (her warm street from above).

### D-B3 "The building the snow can't touch": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** the heat rising: warm, a touch of alarm. **Gives:** an ominous works pouring smoke; "hot" reads, "warm" does not.
- Keep the black mass on the white field and the melted ring. What holds it back:
  1. **The heat reads as smoke.** Four grey columns with fiery roots read as chimneys burning something: it bends the facts (nothing burns there; it is warm air and vapour) and makes the thread's hero look like pollution. Paint it as heat: soft white vapour off a bare wet roof, a shimmer, drips from the icicles.
  2. **"The snow can't touch it" is the title, not yet the picture:** the snow falls evenly over the building. Let the flakes melt to drops in the warm air above it and the banks slump wet at its walls, so the alarm is "look how hot it is".
- Polish: the plumes rise into the caption band; keep them under it.

### D-B4 "Forty kettles in her kitchen": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** playful astonishment at the size of it. **Gives:** a gentle absurd joke that reads at once, but cool and faint: the heat is not felt.
- Keep the pattern with one break (her) and the forty readable kettles in her small kitchen. What holds it back:
  1. **The room reads cold** (a cool grey fog, like a winter morning) in the beat about heat. Make it hot: the pendant lamp's warm light glowing through the steam, the kettles' lights blooming in it, the window streaming, because "as much heat as forty kettles" has to be felt as heat (painted on the paint-over).
  2. **It reads as daytime in a night film.** Keep the night in the fogged window (dark behind the fog, the city's lights blurred through it), so the kitchen belongs to the same evening as B1-B3 and B6.
  3. **Nothing carries the astonishment:** she sits calmly and the kettles sit politely. Let them boil hard (lids lifting, spouts jetting) and let her be surrounded (feet pulled up off a floor of kettles, the mug held close), so B4 astonishes and her calm sip in B5 becomes the punchline.

### D-B5 "Tea for one": THERE
- **Wanted:** a smile and a breath: the joke lands. **Gives:** calm, droll contentment: one sip among forty kettles.
- Polish: a touch of warm light on her face from the mug, so the breath is cozy, not clinical; the kettles behind her a little more of a crowd, so the joke's scale carries into the close.

### D-B6 "Out her window, up into the night": THERE
- **Wanted:** a small pang: all that warmth thrown into the cold night. **Gives:** melancholy waste: a plume rising over small snowy homes says it at a glance.
- Polish: the plume as white vapour thinning to nothing, warm only at the fans' mouths, never a grey smoke column (B3's paint); its top under the caption band; her small open window at the bottom reads as a chimney: make it her lit, open window.

### D-B7 "She reaches for it": NOT THERE
- **Wanted:** the turn: a spark of delight, the city reaching for it. **Gives:** longing with a comic edge; the pose reads as a stretch.
- Why it fails: both arms flung wide, from behind, reads as a stretch or a yawn, not a reach; the touch (the plume's warm tip at her fingers) is small, at the window's edge, away from the eye; the room is pale and cool with a cold radiator, so the film's turn gets no lift after B6's pang. The idea may live, but the staging has to be new: close, her leaning OUT of the open window into the snow, one arm out, a warm wisp curling to her hand (honey on her fingers and face), the night and the plumes beyond. Or take D-B7alt, which gives this beat's feeling better (below).

### D-B8 "Across the bay, a plan": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** hope and anticipation: real, and coming soon (a plan, not built). **Gives:** quiet, hopeful waiting; the plan is small and faint.
- Chalk for the plan is the right, truthful idea. What holds it back:
  1. **The plan barely reads:** a faint outline far across the bay, cut by the window's bar, fading into the fog. Make it bigger and nearer, clear of the bar, so "a big building, drawn, not built" reads in a second.
  2. **Its warm line runs away to the right.** Draw it across the bay toward her window: the hope is that the warmth is coming here.
  3. **It repeats B7's picture** (the same window, the same back). With B7 restaged close, or B7alt taken, this wider view becomes the first return to her window and stands on its own.

### D-B9 "The plan's door opens": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** leaning in: a door opens. **Gives:** curiosity in an empty diagram.
- What holds it back:
  1. **It is thin:** white lines on a blank snowfield, no place, no scale (built thin, against the owner's third rule). Put the camera at the threshold, the door towering, the real snowy lot around it (stakes, snowbanks, the frozen bay and her city's windows through the chalk walls), so the door opens in a world.
  2. **There is nothing inside to lean toward.** Make the light from inside the frame's one light, the chalk giants glimpsed lit, because "leaning in" needs a pull.
- For the key frame: the chalk must look like chalk in the world (grainy, drawn on the night), never a pen outline (the build has no ink lines).

### D-B10 "Blue in, honey out": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** clarity and satisfaction: "oh, that's how". **Gives:** clean logic, but a dry diagram.
- Keep the cold pipe and the hot pipe side by side: that is the clarity. What holds it back:
  1. **The honey pipe leaves the frame and ends nowhere.** Lead it through the chalk wall and across the shore to her city's windows, inside the frame, because "oh, that's how" lands when we see where the warmth goes (and it hands on to B11).
  2. **A diagram on blank snow** (the owner's second rule: never a dry diagram): no person, no place, no scale. Make the giant computer giant against something human (a worker, the door's height) in the lot at night, so it is a picture worth pausing on.

### D-B11 "Up her street, under the snow": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** cozy relief: warmth travelling under the snow to homes. **Gives:** anticipation and connection; the warm line reads as a road marking.
- Keep the view down from her window, her in the corner, the rhyme with B2 and the street's life (the tram, the walkers). What holds it back:
  1. **The stripe reads as a painted centre line,** so "under the snow" does not read. Make it a melt strip: the snow melted into a wet dark ribbon over the pipe, the lit windows reflected in it, banks slumping beside it, the manhole's warm puff bigger.
  2. **The warmth should arrive at her:** the melt's front coming up to her door below the window, the windows nearest her warming, because the relief is "it's coming home".
- Polish: her head sits in the bottom app bar's reach; the right block's windows show through the caption band.

### D-B12 "Socks on a warm radiator": THERE
- **Wanted:** personal warmth and a wink; back to the start. **Gives:** snug, warm contentment: the warmth has arrived.
- Polish: the socks read lumpy (like bread): feet in wool socks, crossed at the ankles. The "one change" from B1 only lands if B1 keeps its radiator cold (B1, note 3).

## BOARD READ (B1 to B12 in order, sound off, as a viewer)
- **The story reads, as one film.** Her chat at night, a hall of machines, a hot building, forty kettles, a sip, the heat blown off over homes, she reaches, a plan across the bay, its door, cold in and hot out, the warmth up her street, her feet on a warm radiator. One world (one snowy night in her harbour neighbourhood), one "you" (from behind or in profile, the measure of every big thing), one thread (the heat), and the concept "her small evening acts out each big thing" is clear from the frames. B2 and B11 rhyme: two cities seen from above, one cold, one warm.
- **The thread fades in the middle.** The heat is the warm accent at the focal in B1, B10, B11 and B12, and a speck from B2 to B9 (measured, the honey share drops to almost nothing in B2, B4, B7, B8 and B9; the map is on the paint-over), so with the sound off the middle loses the thread. Each frame's focal should carry its bit of the heat: the city in B2's door, the vents' warmth in B3, the kettles' glow in B4, the wisp at her hand in B7, the chalk line in B8, the giants inside in B9.
- **One night, four frames that look like day:** B4, B5, B7 and B8 (pale grey rooms) sit between night frames and read as a time jump. Keep each frame's lightness for its feeling (airy for the joke), but keep the night in the windows and the lamp light warm.
- **The arc has the right shape and three flat spots:** a quiet hook (B1's event lives only in motion), a turn that does not spark (B7), and two diagrams just before the payoff (B9, B10), so the film loses its pull right before it lands. And B1 already shows B12's warmth, so the bookends are too alike: B1 cold, B12 the one warm change.
- **The heat reads as smoke three times** (B3, B6, B7): one paint decision for the whole film: white vapour, warm only at its root.
- **The alternates:** bring in B7alt (with its notes) for the turn; B2alt, B8alt and B11alt stay out.

## Sequence verdict: ALMOST
The concept is truly whole (one home, one person, one thread, one arc), and every frame belongs to it. It is not THERE yet:
the hook (B1), the turn (B7) and the mechanism frames (B9, B10) need more than polish, and the warmth has to be carried
through the middle. Next round: adjust B1-B4 and B8-B11 on the notes above; restage B7 or take B7alt with its notes;
polish B5, B6 and B12.
