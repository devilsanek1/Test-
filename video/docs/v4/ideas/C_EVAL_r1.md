# Video 4 ideas, sequence C ("The warm current"): THE FRAME LOOP, evaluation round 1

Fresh evaluator (built none of it). Looked at `C_sheet.png` first, at full size (each thumbnail at 2x) and at phone
size (the sheet at 430 px wide, each frame at 135 x 240), before reading anything else.

## 1. The feeling each picture gives (written from the pictures alone, before reading the brief)

- C-B1 (the snow wakes warm). The feeling it gives: a hushed snowy night street, my phone in my hand, and one warm seam of light running from my feet into the distance toward a row of lit windows; quiet wonder and a pull to follow it. Beautiful and calm, more "come with me" than a jolt.
- C-B2 (a street of computers). The feeling it gives: a long, cold, dim hall of machines, one small person walking away, a tiny spark arcing overhead; lonely and big but murky. At phone size it is mostly dark and "giant" does not hit: it reads as an ordinary server corridor.
- C-B3 (the heat climbs). The feeling it gives: one machine glowing like an oven door with heat wobbling off its top and a person raising an arm beside it; "warm" more than "really hot", and the arm reads as a raised fist (knocking? cheering?) rather than flinching from heat.
- C-B4 (forty kettles inside). The feeling it gives: a happy surprise: the door swings open and the machine is stacked full of steaming kettles. A playful gag that reads at once, the brightest and boldest frame; a little flat, like a pattern on a wall.
- C-B5 (the teapot computer). The feeling it gives: a small, dry, cosy smile: the machine wears a tea-bag tag and a person sips tea beside it. But it is dark and quiet, the tag is small and could be a luggage label, so the joke whispers right after the kettles shouted.
- C-B6 (thrown into the night). The feeling it gives: heat drifting up off a roof into the snowy sky with the city beyond; a soft, wistful "there it goes", gentle rather than thrown away. The glowing spiral fans also read as stove rings.
- C-B7 (the city reaches back). The feeling it gives: the same roof, now with golden roots growing from the city across the ice toward the heat; a hopeful turn, but quiet. The gold branches read as lines drawn on top of the picture (a network chart) more than the city reaching, and little changes from the frame before.
- C-B8 (a plan in chalk). The feeling it gives: an empty, pale snowfield with a thin white outline of a building, a "2027" sign and a person holding the end of a glowing thread; patient promise, "not built yet". At phone size the chalk lines vanish and it reads as an empty grey field.
- C-B9 (a door in the plan). The feeling it gives: warm curiosity and welcome: a lit doorway open in the cold, light pouring onto the snow, someone leaning in to look, the gold thread leading to it. Reads in a second.
- C-B10 (blue in, gold out). The feeling it gives: a clean "oh, that's how": a cold blue pipe snakes up the machine and comes out gold. Satisfying to understand, but cool and schematic: a cutaway diagram with no one in it.
- C-B11 (warm under the snow). The feeling it gives: a cosy house at night with lit windows and a warm glowing dot under the street; arrival, relief. But the bottom third is a black band with two circles that reads as a diagram, and the thin line from the dot to the door barely shows.
- C-B12 (your chat, her warm window). The feeling it gives: a tender, warm payoff: my phone in hand, looking up at a glowing window where a woman sits snug with a cup, the gold line running to her building. The strongest feeling on the sheet; it closes the loop with the first frame.
- C-B1alt (the waterfront wakes). The feeling it gives: a pretty postcard of a whole lit-up city across the frozen water, my phone in front; calm, cosy, "so many homes". Lovely but still: nothing is happening, so no jolt.
- C-B5alt (the tea cosy). The feeling it gives: a big daft smile: the machine wearing a giant knitted tea cosy (or a woolly hat) and a person raising a mug to it. Funnier than the tea tag, but dark on dark: at phone size it is a grey bell.
- C-B7alt (the street opens a way). The feeling it gives: the first frame's street with a line of manholes opening one after another, each glowing and steaming; magical, a path of welcome lights. But the warmth seems to come out of the ground, so "the city wants the heat" is not what it says.
- C-B11alt (the melt line). The feeling it gives: a warm line melted through the snow, leading straight to a house with lit windows; simple, cosy arrival, "the heat came home". The thread from the first frame lands at the door. Reads in a second.

## 2. Each frame: the beat's feeling beside the one it gives, and the verdict
Judged against BRIEF.md's beats, C.md's concept and the frame card, at phone size. Each note is one change and why.
Measurements only explain what the eye saw (below the caption band: the share darker than L* 25, and the share in the
warm honey hues, a rough count).

**C-B1 "The snow wakes warm": ALMOST.** Gives: hushed wonder, a pull to follow. Beat: surprise and curiosity, "wait, what?" (the hook).
1. The seam is a hard, even, straight stripe: at phone size it reads as a road marking or a runway light, so nothing is odd. Paint it as warmth under the snow (a soft, ragged melt channel, the snow sagging dark and wet along it, steam lifting off it), because the "wait, what?" is "why is the snow glowing?".
2. The picture shows the end state, so it is calm. Draw the run itself: the seam lit from the harbour end to a bright running head near mid-street, the windows bloomed only behind it, the near street still cold. A hook is an event, a picture caught mid-event is the one a thumb stops for, and it shows the warm coming from somewhere else, not out of your phone.

**C-B2 "A street of computers": ALMOST.** Gives: a dim, lonely, ordinary server corridor, mostly dark. Beat: a little awe: AI is a real, physical, huge thing.
1. The murk leaves nothing to be awed by at phone size (87 % of it is darker than L* 25, against 38 % in B1). Light it as the concept says, a canyon glowing with thousands of small cool lights, because awe needs the size to be seen.
2. The concept's own picture does not show: it reads as the stock data-centre aisle. Give the near computers B1's facade rhythm (a roofline, window-like grids of lights, cable trays strung like tram wires), so "street of homes, street of computers" is seen and the idea is this film's.
3. Two warm spots (a haze on the left, the strip on the right) split "the only warm thing": keep the one answering computer warm, so the eye knows which one is ours.
4. The engineer is a far speck: bring her to the middle distance with a computer towering over her, so "giant" is felt against a person.

**C-B3 "The heat climbs": ALMOST.** Gives: a machine glowing like an oven door, warm more than hot; the raised arm reads as a fist. Beat: the heat rising: warm, a touch of alarm.
1. The heat is a few squiggles on the lid: make it pour, a tall wavering column off the top that bends the hall's lights behind it, because "really hot" is heat you can see climbing.
2. Her gesture reads as a raised fist (knocking? cheering?): make her recoil, leaning away with a forearm up against the heat, so the touch of alarm is in a person; and bring her in from the right edge, where the app's buttons sit.
- Polish: the glowing border round the cabinet makes it a lit doorway; let the glow come from inside the grille (hottest at its middle), so it is a machine heating up and B9 keeps the film's one door.

**C-B4 "Forty kettles inside": THERE.** Gives: a happy surprise, a gag that reads at once; the boldest frame. Beat: playful astonishment at the size of it.
- Polish: a rim of light and spout steam on each kettle, so they read as forty boiling kettles, not a printed pattern; her lean-back (now a calm reach), in from the right edge.

**C-B5 "The teapot computer": ALMOST.** Gives: a small, dry, cosy smile that whispers; the tag could be a luggage label. Beat: a smile and a breath (the joke has about one second).
1. The punchline is too small to find in a second: make the tag big, lit and swinging on its long string, the first place the eye lands.
2. It is the darkest frame on the sheet right where the film wants a smile (90 % darker than L* 25; its warm 0.5 % right after B4's 20.8 %): let the computer's heat glow through its mesh like tea steeping, so the joke is warm and the swell (B3-B5) does not die before B6.
3. It repeats B3's set-up (the same frontal cabinet, the figure at the right): go wider, as the concept says, the giant teabag and her tiny mug with its tiny tag side by side, so giant-and-tiny reads in one look.

**C-B6 "Thrown into the night": ALMOST.** Gives: a soft, wistful "there it goes"; the glowing fans read as stove rings. Beat: a small pang: all that warmth thrown into the cold night.
1. The pang is warmth wasted above homes that need it, and the homes are a thin far strip: bring the blocks nearer, their windows dark, and let the plumes drift over them and thin away, so the loss is felt against who needs it.
2. The near fans sit at the bottom edge, under the app's bottom bar, and glow like stove rings: raise the row into the picture and draw dark blades against a warm core, so they read as fans and the plumes' warm roots stay seen.

**C-B7 "The city reaches back": ALMOST.** Gives: a quiet, hopeful turn; the gold branches read as a network chart drawn over B6. Beat: the turn: a spark of delight, the city reaching for it.
1. The reach reads as a chart (branches joining into one trunk that ends at the roof): draw it as a reach, warm lines leaving the city's doors and opening out like fingers as they near the plumes, stopping just short, so "wants" is a gesture.
2. The change from B6 is too small to feel like "But": let the city answer where the lines leave it (its doors and kerbs catching the warm), so the delight has a source and the frame is more than B6 with lines added.

**C-B8 "A plan in chalk": ALMOST.** Gives: patient promise, "not built yet"; at phone size the chalk vanishes into an empty pale field. Beat: hope and anticipation: real, and coming soon.
1. The plan is hairlines: draw the chalk bold and faintly glowing, with the planned rows of computers and the pipe inside, so the building is "almost there" and reads in a second.
2. The warm is nearly gone (0.1 %, less than the waste frame B6's 1.3 %): make the gap between the warm tip and the plan's chalk pipe the focal, close and lit, because anticipation is two ends about to touch.
3. The line "still being drawn" is a dot at a far corner: put its bright chalk tip on the eye's path, so "it's happening, soon" reads.

**C-B9 "A door in the plan": THERE.** Gives: warm curiosity and welcome, read in a second, the gold thread leading to it. Beat: leaning in: a door opens.
- Polish: bolder chalk (as B8), so it is a door in the plan, not a lone shed.

**C-B10 "Blue in, gold out": ALMOST.** Gives: a clean "oh, that's how", but cool and schematic: a cutaway diagram with no one in it. Beat: clarity and satisfaction.
1. It reads as a diagram (the owner's rule 2): the arrows on the inlet and the ladder of identical vertical lines are chart marks. Drop them and paint the place the concept describes (the chalk walls with the snowy night through them, the rows going on), so it is a view of the world.
2. Nobody is in it: the engineer beside it, her gloved hand on the pipe where blue turns gold, so "cold in, warm out" is felt through a person.
3. Show the computer relieved: the shelves the pipe has passed going from amber to cool, so satisfaction is felt as relief, not only understood.

**C-B11 "Warm under the snow": ALMOST.** Gives: a cosy lit house and a glowing dot under the street; the bottom third a black band that reads as a cross-section. Beat: cozy relief: warmth travelling under the snow to homes.
1. The cut shows the pipe end-on (a dot), so nothing travels, and it spends the bottom third on a flat black band. The thin melt line is what carries "travelling under the snow" here, and B11alt makes it the whole idea: take B11alt.
2. The woman in the window (in both) is a tiny silhouette caught on the window bars, like someone climbing: bigger, at the glass, looking out, so the warm arrives at a person.

**C-B12 "Your chat, her warm window": THERE.** Gives: tender warmth, the payoff, the loop to B1 (the same hand and phone). Beat: personal warmth and a wink.
- Polish: she is a flat brown shape: her face turned to the window (the people in use) and the mug's steam showing (the wink back to the tea); the radiator under the sill readable (the heat's last stop); the phone up off the app's bottom bar (C already flags it).

## 3. The alternates
- **C-B1alt "The waterfront wakes": NOT THERE.** A postcard: a city with lit windows at night is what every city looks like, so nothing is odd and nothing is happening: no "wait, what?". Keep B1.
- **C-B5alt "The tea cosy": ALMOST.** The bigger laugh, but it reads as a giant beanie, dark on dark. A spout and a handle poking out of the cosy (at once a teapot under its cosy: the "teapot computer" itself), and the knit lit warm from the heat beneath, so the bell reads against the hall. With those two I would take it over B5: a new silhouette that reads in a split second and breaks the B3-B5 sameness.
- **C-B7alt "The street opens a way": ALMOST.** Magic, a runway of welcome lights, and the home place back at the turn; but the steam over the open manholes says the street is already warm, not that it wants the heat. Drop the steam, keep them as lights pointing down the street, and show the far plumes plainly at its end, so the path is the city reaching for the thrown-away heat. With that it gives more delight than B7 (B7 keeps the stronger match with B6: the art director's call).
- **C-B11alt "The melt line": THERE.** The warm line in the snow leads to the door: cozy arrival, the thread from B1 landing home, read in a second. Polish: the woman (B11's note 2); a little street life and a slight angle, so the block is a place, not a front elevation.

## 4. BOARD READ (rows 1-2 in order, sound off, at phone size)
A warm line under a snowy street to lit homes, my phone in hand -> a dark hall of machines -> one machine glowing ->
forty kettles inside -> (a tag on the machine: easy to miss) -> heat rising off a roof over the city -> gold lines from
the city to the roof -> (a faint outline on an empty lot, 2027) -> a warm door in it -> water turning gold through a
computer -> warm under the street into a house -> my phone and a warm window with a woman and her tea.
- **The story reads from the frames alone:** yes, start to end, with three soft spots where the picture goes quiet at
  phone size: B2 (murky), B5 (the joke does not show), B8 (the plan does not show).
- **One world:** yes. One snowy Helsinki night, one cold palette, one warm; the hall frames are the same night's
  inside, the outside frames share the snow, the harbour and the low clouds.
- **One thread:** yes, and it is this sequence's strength: the warm is always where the eye lands and hands on from
  frame to frame (seam, grille, kettles, plumes, lines, tip, door, pipe, melt line, window).
- **One arc of feeling:** the shape is right (born, swells, lost, turned, home) and the peaks land (B4, B9, B12), but it
  sags where it should lift: the swell dies at the joke (B5), the turn (B7) is quiet, and hope (B8) is the palest frame
  with the least warm on the sheet; the concept's own rule ("how much of a frame is warm follows the arc") breaks at
  B5 and B8. And B3, B4, B5 are one frontal set-up three times running: B5 should change the picture.
- **The concept clear:** yes: follow the warm, from a computer to a home, in one night. What does not show yet is where
  the trip starts: your question leaving your phone as a cool spark is in neither B1 nor B2 at phone size (B2's dotted
  arc vanishes), so "your chat makes this heat" is left to the voice until B12. Make the spark visible once (leaving the
  phone, or landing on the one computer in B2).
