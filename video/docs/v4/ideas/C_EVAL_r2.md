# C, THE WARM CURRENT: evaluation round 2 (fresh evaluator)

## First look, from the picture alone (written before reading anything else)
Looked at `C_sheet.png` at full size (each thumbnail cropped and enlarged) and at phone size (each thumbnail at
160 x 284, and the whole sheet at 40 %), in that order.

- **B1**: The feeling it gives: a hushed, cold winter night on a city street, with a small puzzling warm glow steaming out of the snow in the middle of the road; a quiet "huh, what's that?", calm and cosy rather than startling; at phone size the glow is a small spark under a big question, and the phone in your hand says "you just asked something".
- **B2**: The feeling it gives: hushed awe at scale; a long, cold, blue-white canyon of blinking machines with one small person walking it, like a city street at night made of computers; one warm strip on the right hints the warmth is in here somewhere.
- **B3**: The feeling it gives: heat building up to too much; a cabinet glowing orange from the inside, heat shimmering up into the ceiling lights, a man leaning back with his arm raised against it, "whoa, that's hot"; the glow is a soft blob, so it reads as heat more than as a machine, and the man's recoil only reads at full size.
- **B4**: The feeling it gives: a funny reveal; a man opens the cabinet and it is stacked full of steaming kettles, an instant, playful "ha, that many?", the clearest read in the top row.
- **B5**: The feeling it gives: a small, dry smile; a glowing cabinet treated like a mug with a giant tea-bag tag hanging over its rim, and a little man sitting beside it sipping his own tea; gentle and cosy, but at phone size the joke shrinks to a white tag on an orange slab, and the slab seems to float above its own glow.
- **B6**: The feeling it gives: a quiet, slightly wistful "it just goes up and away"; warm plumes rise off rooftop fans into the snowy night while the dark apartment blocks behind sit cold; calm, more mood than sting.
- **B7**: The feeling it gives: a soft turn of hope; five warm pipes curl down from the edge of the city towards the same fans, like fingers reaching; gentle, but it reads as nearly the same picture as the frame before, and the pipes stop short of the fans, so the reach is faint.
- **B8**: The feeling it gives: a cool, sketchy promise; a white chalk outline of a building drawn over the dark harbour, a "2027" sign, a man standing by it, the little warm comet of heat arriving along a chalk path; hopeful but thin and blueprint-cool, the emptiest-feeling frame of the twelve.
- **B9**: The feeling it gives: curious welcome; a real door standing open in the middle of the chalk plan, warm light spilling across the snow, a person leaning in to look, "let's see inside"; one of the most inviting pictures on the sheet.
- **B10**: The feeling it gives: a calm "aha"; one cabinet in a chalk aisle with a pipe zig-zagging across its face, blue going in at the bottom and gold coming out at the top, a man with his hand on it; clear and satisfying, but it leans towards an explanatory drawing more than a moment.
- **B11**: The feeling it gives: quiet relief as warmth arrives; the golden line runs up the street, under a hatch and in at the front door, and the building's windows glow floor by floor with someone at the middle window; cosy and steady, a little static and symmetrical.
- **B12**: The feeling it gives: tender, close warmth; your phone in your hand in the cold street and, right beside it, a golden window with a woman curled in a chair with a mug, the heat line from the street leading to her; a soft, satisfying landing.
- **B1alt**: The feeling it gives: a quiet, wider wonder from up high; looking down from your balcony at a long snowy street where a warm seam glows under the snow and branches into the side streets; calm and beautiful, the warm line reads, the phone in your hand less so.
- **B5alt**: The feeling it gives: a laugh-out-loud cosy absurdity; a giant cabinet dressed in a knitted tea cosy with a pompom and a spout, a tiny man reaching up to catch tea from it in his cup; it reads instantly at phone size, the strongest single picture on the sheet.
- **B7alt**: The feeling it gives: anticipation and welcome; the same street as the opening, its manhole covers lifting open one after another and glowing, a lit path leading back towards the far steam, "the street opens a way"; a clear, hopeful turn.
- **B11alt**: The feeling it gives: the same quiet arrival as B11; it is the same picture with only the figure in the middle window changed, so it offers no second option.

## Per frame: the beat's feeling beside the one it gives, and the verdict
Read after the first look: `BRIEF.md` (the beats), `C.md` (the concept, the ideas, "Round 1 changes"), `PIPELINE_V2.md`
(THE FRAME LOOP, ONE FILM, A RICH, BEAUTIFUL WORLD) and `LAYOUT.md` §1 for the hard zones. The marked-up sheet:
`C_EVAL_r2.png` (red = fix, amber = the change, cyan = polish). Measurements only explain what the eye found; frame
pixels are the 1080 x 1920 frame (a thumbnail pixel x 3.6).

### B1 "The snow wakes warm": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** surprise and curiosity, "wait, what?", and the hook rule (a bold visible event from frame 0).
  **Gives:** a calm, cosy-cold "huh, what's that?". The curiosity is there. The surprise is missing.
- 1. The running head is a small, far spark. Its bright warm is about 0.6 % of the frame, at y ~1080-1360, smaller than
  your phone. **Change:** bring it near and make it two to three times bigger, its melt channel widening toward you in
  perspective, steam thrown up at its head, the snow ahead still white. **Why:** the hook has to be an event a thumb
  sees at phone size, racing AT you. Today it reads as a little fire in the road.
- 2. The bloomed windows on the right compete with the head (together about three quarters of its bright area; the two
  big near panes sit in the right rail). **Change:** keep the near blocks dark and let the bloom sit only far behind
  the head, as the idea says. **Why:** the head must be the one place the eye lands first.
- 3. **Change:** have the run already under way at frame 0, not starting at the card's "~1.5 s". **Why:** a still
  street for 1.5 s breaks the hook rule.

### B2 "A street of computers": THERE
- **Wanted:** a little awe: AI is physical and huge. **Gives:** hushed awe at scale, a canyon street made of machines with
  one small person in it. The feeling is achieved.
- Polish (key frame): the cyan spark on its curved trail sits on top of the thin warm strip, and together they read as a
  street lamp (head, arm, pole). Give the warm a computer's whole face (a block of amber among the cyan) with the spark
  landing on it.

### B3 "The heat climbs": THERE
- **Wanted:** heat rising, warm, a touch of alarm. **Gives:** heat building to too much, and a recoil. The feeling is
  achieved at full size.
- Polish: her recoil reads only at full size. Clear up the silhouette (forearm across the face, a lean away, air between
  her and the cabinet). Her feet run under the app's bottom bar.

### B4 "Forty kettles inside": THERE
- **Wanted:** playful astonishment. **Gives:** a funny reveal that reads at once, the clearest read in the top row.
- Polish: she stands in the right rail and over the bottom bar. Pull her in.

### B5 "The teapot computer": ALMOST, take B5alt
- **Wanted:** a smile and a breath: the joke lands. **Gives:** a small, dry smile at full size. At phone size the joke
  shrinks to a white tag on an orange slab, and the slab seems to float above its own glow.
- **Change:** take B5alt (the tea cosy) into the film as B5, and bring B5's best detail into it: her tiny mug, with its
  own tiny tag, held up under the spout. **Why:** the tag-over-the-rim joke needs a second look and the beat has one
  second. The cosy lands at a glance and keeps the calm, wider breath.

### B6 "Thrown into the night": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** a small pang: all that warmth thrown into the cold night. **Gives:** a quiet, wistful "it goes up and
  away". It has mood but no sting.
- 1. The plumes die out at frame y ~1100, about 400 px under the roofs (y ~700). The warm never reaches the sky over the
  homes, so only the caption says it is wasted. **Change:** let the plumes pour up past the roofline and thin to
  nothing in the sky above the homes, fading out just under the caption band. **Why:** the pang is warmth lost right
  over homes that need it.
- 2. The homes' windows are a pale lilac grid that reads as lit. **Change:** make them dark, cold windows, with at most
  a very few dim ones. **Why:** the cold homes have to be right there, wanting.
- 3. At phone size the fans read as warm pies. **Change:** add a dark housing ring and a grille, with the warm coming up
  through the blades. **Why:** "big fans" has to read as fans.

### B7 "The city reaches back": ALMOST, take B7alt
- **Wanted:** the turn: a spark of delight, the city reaching for it. **Gives:** a soft turn of hope.
- What holds it back: at phone size B7 is B6 plus five thin lines, so the "But" is not felt. The lines end at the far
  ledge, a long way from the fans, so they hang like hooks instead of reaching.
- **Change:** take B7alt (THERE) as B7. It gives the turn at a glance and moves from the roof to the home street, which
  breaks the run of three similar frames (B6-B8). If the art director keeps the B6/B7 match instead, two changes:
  (a) the city visibly answers (its doorways lit, the lines wide warm melt channels with steam); (b) the fingertips run
  across the roof's snow and stop a finger's breadth from the nearest plume.

### B8 "A plan in chalk": ALMOST
- **Wanted:** hope and anticipation: real, and coming soon. **Gives:** a cool, sketchy promise, the emptiest frame of
  the twelve. The plan and "2027" read. The hope is thin, and the anticipation does not read.
- 1. The almost-touch (the film's anticipation) is two dots at frame y ~1390-1460, below the read zone and next to the
  app's bottom bar. At phone size it reads as a comet. **Change:** make the gap the focal, near and big in the read
  zone (about y 900-1200): the city's warm channel coming in from one side, the plan's chalk pipe from the other, a
  small bright gap between them. **Why:** the anticipation lives in that gap.
- 2. **Change:** let the warm tip's light fall on the chalk tip and on the snow round the gap. **Why:** hope is warm, and
  this frame is blueprint-cold (warm covers under 1.5 % of it below the band).
- Polish: the "2027" digits are about 36 px tall, body-text size on a phone. Make them twice that, or let the caption
  carry "next year".

### B9 "A door in the plan": THERE
- **Wanted:** leaning in: a door opens. **Gives:** a curious welcome, "let's see inside". One of the strongest frames.
- Polish: make the chalk as bold as B8's.

### B10 "Blue in, gold out": THERE
- **Wanted:** clarity and satisfaction, "oh, that's how". **Gives:** a calm "aha". This is the most graphic frame.
- Polish (key frame): the gold main's pointed chalk end reads as an arrow, so round it. Paint the pipe as a pipe with
  water in it, with the film's winter signs (frost on the blue, a breath of steam off the gold), so it is a place you
  feel and not a diagram.

### B11 "Warm under the snow": THERE
- **Wanted:** cozy relief: warmth travelling under the snow to homes. **Gives:** quiet relief as the warmth arrives.
- Fix (hard constraint, caught by the zones): the roof and its chimneys sit inside the caption band (frame y ~530-600,
  under "homes."). Lower the block or crop the roof.
- Polish: the woman is a small bust; make her bigger at the glass, as the idea says. Paint the frost on the top floors as
  frost; today it is pale fills that read like lit, cold windows.

### B12 "Your chat, her warm window": THERE
- **Wanted:** personal warmth and a wink, back to the start. **Gives:** tender, close warmth and a soft landing.
- Polish: move the phone's screen above the app's bottom bar (its lower edge reaches ~1565). Hang a tiny tea tag over
  her mug's rim to land the wink back to "a lot of tea". Keep her window the film's warmest, brightest honey: with
  B5alt in, the cosy becomes the film's biggest warm mass.

### The alternates
- **B1alt "From your balcony": ALMOST, keep B1.** Wanted the hook's surprise; it gives a calm, wider wonder. The warm
  seam is a thin line, and its right-angle branches make it read like a map; the phone is a pale card at the edge. It
  is no stronger than B1, and B1 holds the eye-level street and the phone that B12 returns to.
- **B5alt "The tea cosy": THERE, take it as B5** (see B5). Wanted a smile and a breath; it gives a laugh and a calm,
  centred breath. It is the strongest single picture on the sheet at phone size.
- **B7alt "The street opens a way": THERE, take it as B7.** Wanted the turn; it gives anticipation and welcome at a
  glance: the street that woke warm in B1 lays a lit path toward the heat being thrown away. Polish: the couple
  on the pavement visibly turning to look.
- **B11alt "The melt line": the same picture as B11.** B11 was taken from it in round 1; only the figure in the window
  differs. It is not an option, so drop it from the alternates row and let the board show only real choices.

## BOARD READ (the whole board in order, sound off, at phone size)
A warm thing in the snow of a night street, my phone in hand -> a canyon street of machines, one warm -> it glows hot,
someone flinches -> it is full of kettles -> a glowing slab with a tea tag, a man with a cuppa -> roof fans steam into
the night -> the city's warm lines curl toward them -> a chalk plan, 2027, a warm spark arriving -> a warm door in the
plan -> water turning gold up a machine -> a warm line under the street into a lit block -> my phone and a warm window,
a woman with a mug.
- **The story from the frames alone:** yes. Machines make heat, it is usually thrown away, the city wants it, there is
  a plan, water and pipes carry it, it reaches a home, and you are at both ends.
- **One world:** yes. One snowy Helsinki night, one cold palette with one warm accent, one cast, one type for the words,
  and the street of homes rhyming with the street of computers (B1/B2) at a glance. The world is still generic
  (repeated block units in B6/B7); the Katajanokka specifics belong to the world kit and the key frames.
- **One thread:** yes, and the board's strength. The eye always lands on the warm, and from B8 on it is literally one
  line handed from frame to frame (B8's tip, B9's line to the door, B10's gold main, B11's line under the street,
  B12's line to her block), always entering from the lower right.
- **One arc:** it rises and lands warm, but it sags at the turn. B6, B7 and B8 read alike: cool exteriors with small
  warm accents (warm under 2.2 % of each frame below the band, against 15 % in B5). The drop (pang), the swing ("But")
  and the lift (hope) are three soft frames instead of a fall and a rise. With B5alt (the swell's peak), a truly cold
  B6, B7alt (a change of place and a lit path) and B8's gap as its focal, the arc would swing where the story turns.
- **The concept:** clear. "The heat is the one warm thing, and it travels" reads from the board at a glance. Its
  second colour, your question as a cyan spark, is only a speck in B2. If the loop "your chat -> this heat -> her
  home" should read from the pictures and not only the voice, the shot plan can show the spark leaving your phone in
  B1's hand-off.

## THE SEQUENCE'S VERDICT: ALMOST

their own alternates (B5 -> B5alt, B7 -> B7alt). Three frames are held back by specific, fixable things: B1 (the
hook's event too small and far), B6 (the warm never leaves over the cold homes) and B8 (the almost-touch too small and
too low to be the focal). These are composition changes, not new ideas: the concept, the world and the thread stand.

band; B11alt dropped from the alternates. The polish notes go to the key frames.
