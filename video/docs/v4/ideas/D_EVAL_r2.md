# D, "The warmth comes home": THE FRAME LOOP, evaluation round 2

A fresh evaluator (built none of it). The sheet judged as it is now (`D_sheet.png`, 2314 x 2350, 16 thumbnails of
360 x 640), looked at in full, frame by frame at 2x, then as a phone-size strip (each frame ~150 px wide, colour and
grey). Paint-over: `D_EVAL_r2.png`.

## 1. The feeling it gives, from the picture alone (written before reading the brief or D.md)

- **D-B1** The feeling it gives: a hushed, cold night and a pull of curiosity: she sits in the dark and a warm glowing
  city shows through a hole in the frosted glass, as if something wonderful is out there; the room itself stays cold
  and dim (one orange pipe), and the cleared patch reads as a dark smudge or cloud more than as frost melting away.
- **D-B2** The feeling it gives: cold, endless, mechanical scale: an aerial maze of long grey blocks with tiny walking
  figures; awe with a little dread; at a glance it could be a city of buildings seen from above as easily as a hall of
  computers; the warm city through the far opening is small and far, and she is not in it.
- **D-B3** The feeling it gives: a lonely black building on a snowy night breathing out warm steam: quiet, mildly
  curious ("something warm is going on in there"), not "really hot"; the fence and the empty snow make the bottom half
  calm and still.
- **D-B4** The feeling it gives: a warm, funny, absurd surprise: her kitchen floods with golden steam and dozens of
  glowing kettles while she sits in the middle of it calmly holding a cup; the first time the film turns warm and
  light, a smile. (The two dark side pillars make it look framed or letterboxed next to its neighbours.)
- **D-B5** The feeling it gives: a calm, content sip, a gentle joke: eyes closed, tea at her lips, kettles lined up on
  the sill and steaming at her feet; soft, a pause to breathe; quiet rather than glowing (mostly greys).
- **D-B6** The feeling it gives: cold waste: a black box of glowing fans heaves a column of steam up into the empty night
  above a sleeping snowy village; a little ominous and sad (the warmth leaves, the little houses stay cold); the box
  seems to float on a grey plain, its place and size unclear.
- **D-B7** The feeling it gives: longing, a whole block wanting: silhouettes lean out of every window, arms up, reaching
  for ribbons of air drifting past (even a cat); hopeful and a little funny; the ribbons read as cool fog more than as
  warmth, and the reachers are faceless black shapes.
- **D-B8** The feeling it gives: quiet hope and anticipation: from behind, she leans on her open window and looks across
  the dark bay at a ghostly chalk outline of a building, a golden thread already running from it to the roofs below
  her; still, private, a promise far away.
- **D-B9** The feeling it gives: an invitation and a little wonder: a door in the chalk building swings open on a warm
  glowing room, footprints lead to it through the snow, "come and see"; what glows inside (rows of bars like organ
  pipes) is beautiful but abstract.
- **D-B10** The feeling it gives: a calm, clear "oh, I see": an icy pipe goes in, a warm copper pipe comes out and runs
  off to the city; understanding more than feeling, the closest frame to a clean diagram (a chalk box room, one
  machine, one generic black man).
- **D-B11** The feeling it gives: anticipation, the warmth on its way to her: over her shoulder we look down a snowy
  street where a glowing strip melted into the snow runs between lit buildings straight toward her house; hopeful,
  moving, "here it comes".
- **D-B12** The feeling it gives: snug contentment, the circle closes: same chair, same window now clear of frost, her
  socked feet up on a glowing orange radiator, phone in hand; cosy and satisfied, though the room around her stays
  mostly dark, so the warmth sits in the radiator rather than wrapping her.
- **D-B2alt** The feeling it gives: surreal, dreamy awe around a lonely-cosy core: her armchair, lamp and steaming mug sit
  in an endless corridor of blinking machines, as if her chat had carried her inside them; readable in a second and
  memorable.
- **D-B7alt** The feeling it gives: personal longing and wonder: she leans out of her open window, one arm stretched to
  touch a warm spark in a ribbon of steam drifting from the city; intimate and hopeful.
- **D-B8alt** The feeling it gives: a sparse, cold, distant hope: a chalk outline drawn on an empty snowfield (cut by the
  left edge), one golden line to one lit window far off; calm and emptier.
- **D-B11alt** The feeling it gives: cosy order, warmth reaching every room: a doll's-house cutaway of a block, an orange
  pipe climbing from under the street to a radiator in every flat, little people eating, sleeping, sitting; neat and
  satisfying, a little like a diagram.

## 2. Per frame: the feeling wanted, the feeling given, the verdict

Judged as ideas drawn as value sketches (the idea, the world, the light, the people, the words as one picture), each in
the board, with the loop's one-film questions (its world, the thread, one step of the arc, the hand-on). This is the
second adjust round for every ALMOST below, so the last one the loop allows.

### D-B1 "The frost clears onto 70,000 windows": ALMOST
Wanted: surprise and curiosity, a cozy snowy city and an odd true idea ("wait, what?"). Gives: curiosity and a hush; the
surprise is held back. Round 1's three notes landed (the frost caught mid-clear, a sea of windows, the radiator cold but
its first fin). It belongs, starts the thread and hands on (her lit phone).
1. **The clearing reads as a dark cloud over the city.** Its edge is a soft dark halo inside scratchy frost, so at phone
   size it reads as smoke, the one thing the heat must never look like, and the hook's event (the frost melting) does
   not show. Give the edge a bright, lacy crystal rim, wet, with drops running down from it, and keep the cleared glass
   clean (no frost lines across the lit windows): "the frost is melting away onto a warm city" (painted on B1).
2. **The far city is a generic wall of towers.** Helsinki across the bay is long and low (Jugend blocks, steep tin
   roofs, the domes); drawn so, wide rather than tall, it is the cozy snowy city the beat asks for and still a sea of
   windows. B12's view follows it (the bookend).
Polish: the waking fin sits at the edge of the app's right rail and in the bottom bar; bring it a fin or two inward.

### D-B2 "Her chat's other end: a city of machines": ALMOST (or take D-B2alt, THERE)
Wanted: a little awe, AI as a real, physical, huge thing. Gives: awe at a cold, endless scale with a little dread, but it
reads as a city of buildings under a night sky as easily as a hall of computers: no roof, no walls, the blocks running
off the frame like streets, their lights like windows. Round 1's notes landed (the far door the one light, the rhyme
with B11), and that rhyme is what blurs the read.
1. **Show it is inside.** Draw the hall's roof (trusses with rows of lights running to the door, painted on B2) and give
   the blocks one machine detail buildings never have (fan grilles on their tops, cable trays over the aisles), so
   "giant computers" reads in a second and the awe is about machines; the layout keeps the rhyme.
Or take D-B2alt (below): it reads at once, carries the film's device and joins B1 on her armchair; it gives up the high
angle's rhyme with B11, which a viewer feels far less than the read.

### D-B3 "The building the snow can't touch": ALMOST
Wanted: the heat rising, warm, a touch of alarm. Gives: a quiet, lonely building breathing out warm steam; mildly
curious, not "really hot", no alarm. Round 1's notes landed in part (one vapour paint, warm only at the mouths, no
smoke, clear of the band), but the rain where the snow should be does not read at phone size (thin streaks among the
flakes), and three things say "cold":
1. **The icicles.** A full row along the roof edge says "frozen", the opposite of the beat. Replace it with meltwater
   pouring off the roof edge and steaming as it falls: the building is too hot to hold snow.
2. **Black on black.** Its top and sides sit on the night sky (measured to explain it: the wall L* 16 against the sky's
   19; only the snow in front is light, L* 75), so the "hot dark mass in a white field" never forms. Lift the sky behind
   it (the vapour lit by the city's glow, or the snowfield rising behind it), so it reads as a black, steaming mass in
   white: stark, a touch of alarm (painted on B3).
3. **The melted ring reads as a shadow.** Make it wet: puddles catching the vents' warm glow, steam lifting off the
   bare ground, the banks slumped at its edge, so "the snow can't touch it" reads without the title.

### D-B4 "Forty kettles in her kitchen": THERE
Wanted: playful astonishment at the size of it. Gives: a warm, funny, absurd surprise, the film's first light and warm
frame, a smile. Round 1's notes all landed (lamp-warm steam, the night in the fogged window, a floor of kettles boiling
hard, her up on the table). Polish: the two dark side pillars read as black bars (pillarboxing) at a glance: give them
the room's light and surface if they are a door jamb, or drop them; the middle rows a touch crisper through the steam.

### D-B5 "Tea for one": THERE
Wanted: a smile and a breath. Gives: a calm, content sip, a gentle joke; the night now in its window keeps the one
night, her profile still dark on the lit fog. No notes.

### D-B6 "Out her window, up into the night": THERE
Wanted: a small pang. Gives: cold waste, the warmth heaved into the empty night over a sleeping village. Polish: seat the
fan box on a roof edge (it floats on a grey plain, its place and size unclear), and let her window at the bottom (the
measure, the steam's source) be a touch brighter than its neighbours.

### D-B7 "The whole block reaches": ALMOST
Wanted: the turn, a spark of delight, the city reaching for it. Gives: longing, a whole block wanting, hopeful and a
little funny: the turn reads (the plain cut from the plume to a facade of open windows), the delight not yet. Round 1's
four notes landed in shape (sleeves and open hands, bodies leaning out, varied neighbours, her window top left, the
facade turned for depth) but not in light:
1. **The drifting air reads as cool fog.** Its warm thread is too faint at phone size, so the block reaches for grey
   mist. Give it a warm core that reads at a glance, warmest where it touches the hands (painted on B7): the delight is
   "the warmth is right there".
2. **The reachers are black cut-outs.** Catch the passing warmth on their up-turned faces and open hands (a lit cheek, a
   lit palm), so the reach turns from wanting into delight; kept below the payoff's warmth.
3. **Her hand, the one difference, is a speck.** Make the warmth arriving at her hand the brightest warm in the frame,
   so the eye lands there first and the push into her window (B8) has its target.

### D-B8 "Across the bay, a plan": THERE
Wanted: hope and anticipation, real and coming soon. Gives: quiet hope, a promise across the water with its warm line
already coming to her. Round 1's notes landed (the plan big and near through the open window, the line across the ice to
her, the room lamp-warm at night). Polish: a warm rim from the room's lamp on her head and shoulders (her dark head
nearly merges with the dark bay).

### D-B9 "The plan's door opens": THERE
Wanted: leaning in, a door opens. Gives: an invitation and a little wonder; the warm doorway the one light, our
footprints leading in. Polish: the giants inside as rows going back into the light (the symmetric fan of bars reads as an
ornament, an organ or a radiator), so the door opens on B10's subject.

### D-B10 "Blue in, honey out": THERE
Wanted: clarity and satisfaction, "oh, that's how". Gives: a calm, clear "oh, I see": icy pipe in, copper pipe out, the
worker the measure, snow falling through the unbuilt roof. The sparest frame, but clarity is its feeling, and round 1's
notes landed. Polish: the hot pipe tapers on its posts into the far windows, left of the app's right rail (its flare now
sits under the rail and reads as a beam hitting the horizon); drop the headlamp wedge (a second pale diagonal that
competes with the cold pipe).

### D-B11 "Up her street, under the snow": ALMOST
Wanted: cozy relief, warmth travelling under the snow to homes. Gives: anticipation, "here it comes"; the melt strip now
reads at once (round 1's note 1 landed), the relief does not:
1. **The arrival is hidden behind her head.** Round 1's note 2 was drawn (the strip turns in to her door), but her head
   covers it. Move her to the right third, or lower, so the strip's front turning in at her door, with a warm spill
   there, is in view: relief is the arrival.
Polish: drop the pale wedge at the top right (a stray beam crossing the caption band).

### D-B12 "Socks on a warm radiator": THERE
Wanted: personal warmth and a wink, back to the start. Gives: snug contentment, the circle closed: the same room, the
frost gone, socks on a hot radiator, the chat in her hand; the board's warmest step. Polish: let the radiator's warmth
fall on her, her socks, the chair and the floor (the room is almost all deep dark, 77 % of it under L* 25, so the warmth
sits in the radiator instead of wrapping her; painted on B12); the far city follows B1's note 2.

### The alternates
- **D-B2alt "Her armchair among the giants": THERE.** Wanted (B2): a little awe. Gives surreal, dreamy awe around a
  lonely-cosy core, readable in a second: her chat's other end, measured against her, the film's own device. The
  stronger B2 of the two.
- **D-B7alt "She leans out and catches it": NOT THERE as B7.** Gives personal longing and wonder: "she wants it", not
  "Helsinki wants it", and it repeats B8's view one beat early. B7 carries the beat; nothing to escalate.
- **D-B8alt "Her window, seen from the plan": ALMOST, not needed.** Gives a sparse, cold, distant hope: the plan cut by
  the left edge, the lower half empty snow, her a dot. B8 (THERE) carries the hope.
- **D-B11alt "The house opened like a doll's house": ALMOST, not needed.** Gives cosy order, the warmth into every flat,
  the plainest "into people's homes"; held back by the dim rooms, the black cut-out people and the square-on cutaway (a
  diagram's look). B11 stays the pick (her view, the journey home, the rhyme with B2).

## 3. BOARD READ (B1 to B12 in order, sound off, at phone size, as a viewer)
- **The story reads from the frames alone:** a woman at her frosted window, a warm city beyond; a hall of machines; a
  hot building in the snow; her kitchen of forty kettles; her sip; the steam blown into the night; her block reaching
  for it; a plan across the bay; its door; cold in, warm out; the warmth up her street; her socks on the radiator. The
  weak reads without captions: B2 (a city or a hall?) and B3 (a building steaming, not a hot one).
- **One world:** one snowy night throughout, one woman (the bun, from behind or in profile), one window as the gate (B1,
  B8, B11, B12). The one break: her city is drawn three ways, a tower skyline (B1, B12, B2's door), a village of cottage
  roofs (B6, B8, B12's foreground) and tall plain blocks (B7, B11), never yet the specific Helsinki the card names
  (Jugend blocks, the tram, the domes, the icebreakers). One real place for the world kit; in the ideas, B1/B12's far
  city at least (B1, note 2).
- **One thread, the clearest of the board:** the heat at every focal, changing form: vapour (B3), steam and kettle
  lights (B4-B5), the plume (B6), the drifting warmth (B7), the warm chalk line (B8), the door's light (B9), the hot pipe
  (B10), the melt strip (B11), the radiator (B12).
- **One arc:** curiosity, awe, (quiet where alarm belongs), laughter, a breath, the pang, (longing where delight
  belongs), hope, leaning in, "oh, that's how", anticipation, contentment. The light carries it: dark, the light comic
  peak at B4-B5, the dark middle, the warm end, B12 the warmest step. Two flat steps, B3 and B7: exactly the two frames
  whose notes are about feeling, not polish.
- **The concept is clear:** "The warmth comes home", one home, one person: B1 and B12 are the same room cold and warm,
  and her window is the gate the story leaves and returns through. "Every big thing met in one of her small things"
  reads in her kettle (B4-B5), her window (B8), her street (B11), her radiator (B12) and in B2alt; in B2, B6 and B10 her
  home is only a speck (her city in the door, her window at the bottom, the pipe's far end), fine where the camera
  follows the heat, and one more reason to take B2alt.

## 4. The sequence's verdict: ALMOST
Seven of the twelve are THERE (B4, B5, B6, B8, B9, B10, B12) and so is B2alt; five are ALMOST with concrete notes (B1,
B2 or its alt, B3, B7, B11). Not THERE yet because two frames miss their beat's feeling rather than polish (B3: no heat,
no alarm; B7: no delight at the turn) and the hook frame still reads its event as smoke. The concept, the thread and the
arc are whole, the world one night and one home (its city still to be made one real Helsinki); nothing needs a rework,
and this is the last adjust round for these frames.
