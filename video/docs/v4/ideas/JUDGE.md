# Video 4 ideas: the judges' synthesis (2026-10-31)

A fresh judge (built and reviewed none of this). It read the three judges (`JUDGE_viewer.md`, the cold viewer;
`JUDGE_art.md`, the art director; `JUDGE_story.md`, the story editor), then looked at the four sheets as one board,
in beat order, at phone size, with the sound off, to settle where they split. The board is `JUDGE_board.png`: each film's
twelve picks, built from the sequences' own frames, each checked against its sheet tile. B's B6 is B6alt, its pick.

## 1. The four films, as a viewer would tell them to a friend
- **A · The Frosted City:** a frosty Helsinki night where the AI building's heat drifts off over the ice, until the city
  throws its windows open, a woman draws the plan in her window's frost, and the warmth clears the frost up her street.
- **B · The Open Machine:** a warm crack glows in the snow of a Helsinki quay; we follow it into the AI machine door by
  door (a furnace of computers, one cabinet full of forty boiling kettles), find the one stretch of pipe still missing,
  watch it drawn in for next year, and follow the warm line home to her window and your phone.
- **C · The Warm Current:** one little stream of heat crosses a blue Helsinki night: born in a computer, so hot it's
  forty kettles (it gets a tea cosy), thrown into the sky by fans, then led back down a street and under the snow into a
  warm window.
- **D · The Warmth Comes Home:** one woman's evening acts it all out: her armchair among the giant computers her chat
  runs on, forty kettles boiling in her kitchen, her whole block leaning out for the warmth the fans throw away, and the
  heat coming up her street until her socks are on a warm radiator.

## 3. The base: B, "The Open Machine"
**The strongest film for this voice.** The voice is a surprising claim, then "So how would that work?", answered.
B's whole device is that question turned into pictures: the film opens the machine one warm door at a time and shows
the one piece still missing being drawn in.
- **Its device is the fact.** Helsinki's warm pipes already run under the snow. The computers already make heat. The
  plan is the stretch between them, drawn in pencil over an empty lot (painted = real, pencil = planned). So "a plan,
  starting next year" is true in every frame, and B is the most careful of the four on the facts. Its pencil also rhymes
  with the house opening (frame 0 drawn on, then the colour floods in): the colour floods into everything real and
  stops at the plan.
- **One thread with the sound off.** The warm seam in the snow at frame 0 is the heat's own route. The eye follows it
  from your feet into the hall, to the gap, and home to her window and your phone ("a fuse I follow", the viewer).
- **Every big line has its clearest picture here.** The hook is an event, and the hall is a cathedral of computers that
  becomes a furnace. One cabinet opens on forty kettles, in the voice's order. The city's line stops at the gap, and the
  gap is drawn in. Cool water goes in and honey water comes out through one machine.
- **Its gaps are frames, not ideas.** The missing "AI" in the hook, three thin frames and a heroine who doesn't read yet
  can all be fixed inside its concept. The others' gaps are in their ideas: A's unreadable device, C's quiet turn, D's
  quiet hook and missing computer.

What B must gain: a heart (her, its own night-shift worker who lives at the quay) and the art director's eye (every
frame one whole picture, top to bottom, in a specific place). The base is B's twelve as picked, with B6alt (the fox) at
B6. Its hero frame is B1, the quay: it shows the most of the world, and the loop returns to it.

## 4. Inside B: the beats to improve, and how (most important first)
1. **B1 The warm line (surprise, "wait, what?").** Keep the seam at your feet and the huge "70,000 homes?". Make the
   seam alive from frame 0 (the snow lifting along it, steam). Let "AI" read: the drawn hall at the quay's end shows its
   painted computers in rows of small cool lights, drawn as a pencil sketch of a building, never a glass pavilion. Stay
   true to the plan: the city's painted line stops short of the drawn hall, with a dashed pencil stretch between, so the
   gap is planted at frame 0, and the shot plan never enters the drawn door before B9.
2. **Her, across the film (the heart).** One woman at a glance in every chapter: the people in use, one coat, one
   beanie, her banded mug. She is a small full figure acting with her pose, as the card's one person near the camera.
   She walks home in B1. In B3 she fans herself with her beanie, coat flung open (today she reads as a man). She sips
   in B5, stands by the plan in B8, leans in at the door in B9 and turns the valve in B10. In B12 she raises the same
   mug in her warm window, looking out over the quay, never at us. Then the payoff is a person, not only a line.
3. **B5 One cup (a smile and a breath).** Plan it again, whole. Her full figure stands before B4's open cabinet, still
   boiling its forty kettles in honey steam, as she takes one sip from her banded mug, eyes closed (a serene moment).
   One cup against a machine's worth is the joke, so the machine must still blaze. No pale void, no gloved hand filling
   the foreground.
4. **B7-B8 The turn and the plan (delight, then hope).** The gap is the story, so it must read at phone size. In B7,
   give the untouched snow between the line's burst and the lot its breadth. In B8, the drawing is the one change: the
   pencil hall's roofline, big door and windows over the lot, the dashed pipe bold across the gap with a faint warmth
   along it, and the "2027" paper small on its door. Today the paper hides the drawing.
5. **B9 A drawn door opens (leaning in).** Through the door, an aisle of the film's own cabinets runs back into warm
   light, pipes along their tops, with her leaning in: the promise of B10, never bars that read as a chart.
6. **B6alt Only the fox gets it (a pang, with a wry smile).** Its shape must say fox (the brush tail over its nose, the
   pointed mask), the melt must visibly lead nowhere, and a second hall far off says "normally".
7. **B4 Forty kettles in one (playful astonishment).** The row's other cabinets, closed and cool, stand around the
   one that is open ("just ONE of these computers"), filling today's empty grey half.
8. **B10 Cool in, honey out ("oh, that's how").** The machine is B4's cabinet, with its trays and small lights behind
   the pipe, never an empty box. Frost forms where the cold water comes in, and a breath of steam rises at the valve.
9. **B12 Your chat, a warm window (a wink, back to the start).** Put the loop in the picture as well as the camera: the
   drawn hall is still a drawing at the quay's end ("could soon"), the seam runs as in B1, her house is B11's facade,
   and her mug is B5's. Your hand stays cut by the bottom edge, with the phone's screen and its chat bubble above
   the app's bottom bar.
10. **The whole film (the world kit, then the key frames).** Every frame is one picture from top to bottom: the pale
   field becomes the world's own calm (the night sky over the quay with its snow, the hall's high lights, the steam),
   and the night gets its own colour, with honey kept for the heat. The hall becomes one specific place from a real data
   hall, its four frames four views (the cathedral, the hot aisle, the one cabinet, her sip). The quay keeps its real
   marks (the icebreakers, the turret block, the tram). The plan is a soft hand-drawn pencil sketch, never a white
   wireframe.
