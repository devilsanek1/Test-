# A · The Frosted City: THE FRAME LOOP, evaluation round 1 (fresh evaluator)

## 1. The feeling each picture gives (written from the sheet alone, before reading any doc)

Looked at in order: `A_sheet.png` whole, each thumbnail enlarged 2x, then each at phone size (the sheet's own size)
and at feed-glance size (160 px wide).

- **A-B1** The feeling it gives: a hushed, cold winter evening across a frozen river; one warm block of flats is a small comfort in a big grey dark and a lone figure stands on the bank; calm and a little lonely, but nothing is happening yet, so at phone size it is a pretty postcard, not a question that grabs.
- **A-B2** The feeling it gives: cold, dim, impersonal hush; a long machine hall with one small person in it, faintly ominous; "big" comes from the tiny figure, but it is the familiar server-aisle picture and so dark it nearly goes flat at phone size.
- **A-B3** The feeling it gives: the hall has turned to glowing embers: sweltering, stuffy, with a wry touch from the man wiping his brow; the first frame that pops at phone size, and the cold-to-hot turn from the frame before is felt at once.
- **A-B4** The feeling it gives: playful surprise: one dark machine beside a whole building of glowing windows, a steaming kettle in each; funny and cosy once you see the kettles, but at phone size they shrink to dots, it reads first as "a warm block of flats", and the neat 8 x 5 grid feels like a counting chart.
- **A-B5** The feeling it gives: private, wry and quiet: a woman at home with her own cup of tea looking out at the boiling block; an inward smile, but the room is so dark and she is such a heavy dark shape that it feels cold and heavy rather than cosy, and with no face there is no one to share the joke with.
- **A-B6** The feeling it gives: a sigh of waste and loneliness: warm steam pouring into a cold sky over an empty frozen lake where one ice fisher sits alone; melancholy and clear, but mostly empty grey, and the "big fans" are nowhere to be seen.
- **A-B7** The feeling it gives: lively, eager, shared joy: windows full of people throwing their arms up, the first crowd in the film; the turn from loss to "yes, we want it" lands, though what they reach for (a warm haze in the cloud) is faint, and open windows in falling snow puzzle a little.
- **A-B8** The feeling it gives: quiet, homely hope: a finger drawing the plan in the frost of a window, "2027" big and clear, a candle on the sill; tender and calm, but the drawing is a little icon diagram (box, line, box) and the grey striped arm reads slightly mechanical.
- **A-B9** The feeling it gives: a puzzle on a frosty whiteboard: curious but cold and clinical, the night city gone; a loop, two blobs and a door into a server cupboard; at phone size an abstract icon I cannot read in a second.
- **A-B10** The feeling it gives: an X-ray reveal: cool, technical, a little ghostly; the white line and the amber line say "cold in, warm out" clearly even at phone size, but it is a blueprint, not a place, and the stick figure is out of step with every other person on the sheet.
- **A-B11** The feeling it gives: warm, contented belonging: a whole street of lit homes, the glowing pipe running up between them, people walking home in the snow; satisfying and cosy, if static and symmetrical, and the lamp hanging in the middle reads as a gondola.
- **A-B12** The feeling it gives: a tender, personal close: the opening view again, now every window warm, a woman in the big window and my own hand holding a phone with a chat; it closes the circle gently, but the dark hand is heavy and the white wire box by the river is a puzzle.
- **A-B4alt** The feeling it gives: playful and fair: a seesaw in a snowy courtyard, one machine on one end and a heap of kettles on the other, a child with a sled looking on; "one weighs as much as forty" reads at once and is funnier than a chart, though the kettle heap is a dark lump at phone size.
- **A-B7alt** The feeling it gives: a quiet promise travelling toward us: a glowing line along the kerb, steam puffing from every hatch, two people on the pavement; inviting and a little magical, but calmer and less joyful than "wants", with fewer people and less warmth.
- **A-B9alt** The feeling it gives: opening up, a breath of curiosity: a hand swings the frosted pane open on the starry night and the lit city; intimate and quiet, but the marks on the pane (2027, a doorway) are too small to read, and opening a window in winter feels cold rather than inviting.
- **A-B10alt** The feeling it gives: a clean "aha": a cut-away cabinet, a white pipe going in and a warm pipe coming out; very clear at phone size, but sterile and schematic, a textbook drawing more than a moment.

## 2. Per frame: the feeling intended, the feeling given, the verdict

Read after the lines above: `BRIEF.md` (the beats), `A.md` (the concept and ideas), PIPELINE_V2 (THE FRAME LOOP, ONE
FILM, A RICH WORLD). Paint-over: `A_EVAL_r1.png` (badges, circles, arrows, the board read). Measurements only explain a
gap the eye found first.

**A-B1 · The city wakes warm: ALMOST (the board's first fix).** *Intended:* surprise and curiosity, a cozy snowy city
and an odd true idea ("wait, what?"). *Gives:* a calm, lonely postcard: the cozy half is there, the surprise half is
missing, for one reason: the wave, this frame's event, is not drawn.
1. Draw the wave as one honey front sweeping from the blank building along the shore and up the whole hill, its crest
   the brightest honey in the frame, cutting across facades mid-building; now the warmth stops at one block's edge, so
   it reads "one block is lit", not "something is spreading", and the spreading is the "wait, what?".
2. Make the source a strange building (no windows, cold lights, its fans steaming, its warm run glowing at its door);
   now a dark box with a faint smudge, and the viewer must ask "what is that?" before B2 answers.
3. Show the frost ahead of the crest as frost (ferned, glittering panes, not plain pale windows): the clearing is the
   visible event, and it plants the film's device in frame 0.

**A-B2 · A street of giant machines: ALMOST.** *Intended:* a little awe, AI is a real, physical, huge thing. *Gives:*
a cold, impersonal hush; the scale comes only from the tiny figure, and the frame sinks at phone size (91 % of it under
L* 25, half the value range of B1 and B3).
1. Make the door at the end big and lit, the warm city filling it: the cold machine street and the warm homes in one
   picture is this frame's idea, and now the door is a small grey square.
2. Set the cabinet lights in rows like a facade's windows: the machine city rhyming with the home city is what turns
   the familiar server aisle into this film's picture.
3. Let the door's light rake along the floor and the cabinet edges so the towering scale reads in a second.

**A-B3 · The machines run hot: THERE.** *Intended:* the heat rising, warm, a touch of alarm. *Gives:* sweltering and
wry; the cold-to-hot match with B2 is felt at once; the first frame that pops at phone size. Polish: steam rolling out
of the far door into the night (the hand-off to B4); the technician's wipe a touch bigger (the wry alarm).

**A-B4 · One computer, a whole block boiling: ALMOST.** *Intended:* playful astonishment at the size of it. *Gives:*
playful once read, but at phone size it reads first as "a warm block of flats", and in this film warm windows mean
"homes heated", so it risks saying "one computer heats forty homes".
1. Make the kettles read: each is about 8 px on the sheet, about 10 px on a phone. Fewer, bigger windows (the block
   closer), a bold kettle and a big white curl of steam in each, each kitchen a little different (a curtain, a plant, a
   hand at the kettle) so the neat 8 x 5 grid stops reading as a counting chart.
2. Make "as much as" visible: the forty curls gather into one cloud the same size and height as the computer's single
   plume, side by side (now a flat cloud on the roof against a tall column), so the eye compares two equal clouds.
3. Make the computer the one big break in the street, and lift the neighbour and her dog (the scale anchor, now at
   y ≈ 1470-1645 of 1920, under the app's bottom bar) into the read zone.

**A-B5 · One cup: ALMOST.** *Intended:* a smile and a breath, the joke lands. *Gives:* an inward smile, but cold and
heavy: the room is near-black (62 % under L* 25) and she is a dark mass with no face to share the joke.
1. Light the room: the lamp at her shoulder actually warm, her knit catching it; the cosy inside against the boiling
   block outside is the joke's frame.
2. Give her the gesture that lands it: her mug raised a little toward the forty windows, a toast (or her cheek turned,
   smiling), so the viewer smiles with her.
3. Let her read as her (the claw-clipped hair now reads as headphones; the knit's shoulder), lighter than the night
   outside, so she and her one curl are the joke's near half, not a black block.

**A-B6 · Warmth drifting away: ALMOST.** *Intended:* a small pang, all that warmth thrown into the cold night,
normally, everywhere. *Gives:* the pang is there (a sigh of waste, the lone ice fisher), but neither the fans nor
"everywhere" is in the picture, and it is mostly empty grey.
1. Show the fans as fans (big round housings, blades, tilted to us): the voice names them, and now they read as chimneys.
2. Make the plume's arc the subject: honey at the fans, greying as it bends over the frosted homes, its last wisps just
   above the windows that need it, so the pang is "so close, and lost".
3. Add two faint plumes far along the shore, so "normally" reads as everywhere.

**A-B7 · The city throws its windows open: THERE.** *Intended:* the turn, a spark of delight, the city reaching for it.
*Gives:* lively, eager, shared joy; the turn from B6 lands at a glance. Polish: the drift overhead lit honey on its
underside (the arms need something to reach for; now a faint haze); the film's stucco blocks with cornices and chimneys
(now plain slab facades, unlike B1, B11, B12); the figure on the street above the bottom bar; her one shut, frosted
window (the hand-off) a touch easier to find.

**A-B8 · The plan, drawn in the frost: ALMOST.** *Intended:* hope and anticipation, real and coming soon. *Gives:*
quiet homely hope, and "2027" reads at once, but the plan itself is a little icon diagram.
1. Draw the plan as her fingertip drawing: thick wobbly strokes, frost crumbs at their edges, a drip, her own block with
   its little tower at the end of the line (now clean boxes and a perfect curve, an infographic): the hope is a person's,
   and the owner's rule is never a dry diagram.
2. Let the night's warm lights glow through every cleared line and through "2027", so the plan glows honey: the thread
   and the hope in one picture (now dark lines with a few specks).
3. Her hand in the big knit sleeve from B5 (now a grey striped arm that reads mechanical): it is her plan.

**A-B9 · The frost door opens: NOT THERE.** *Intended:* leaning in, a door opens on the mechanism. *Gives:* a puzzle on
a frosty whiteboard, clinical, the night gone (85 % of it over L* 80, by far the whitest frame); at phone size an icon.
*Why the idea fails its feeling as staged:* the drawing is seen whole and flat with the door a small detail inside it,
so there is nothing to lean into, and the white field drops the night the whole film lives in. *Rework (a new staging,
keeping the frost device):* the door IS the frame: we stand at the drawn door's threshold, the sheet of frost swung
toward us, beyond it a deep dark doorway with a cool glow and the machines' rows of lights, the night's warm lights
showing through the frost walls on both sides, her fingertip at the door's edge; dark depth in a pale frame pulls the
eye in.

**A-B10 · Cold in, warm out: ALMOST.** *Intended:* clarity and satisfaction, "oh, that's how". *Gives:* an X-ray
reveal: "cold in, warm out" is clear even at phone size, but it is technical and ghostly, a blueprint more than a place.
1. Show the warming: the return pipe pale at the far end, getting warmer machine by machine as each pours its glow
   down into it, honey only at the near leg (now honey all the way from the far end, so the colours read but the "how"
   does not). B10alt has this moment.
2. Draw the hall in frost, not CAD lines: glittering, finger-drawn, the night's lights through the lines, so it stays
   her drawing and this film's world.
3. A frost-line person in the same hand as the hall (now a stick figure), and the cold leg a pale blue-grey quieter than
   the honey (now the white leg is the brightest thing in the frame and competes).

**A-B11 · Home by home, up the street: ALMOST.** *Intended:* cozy relief, warmth travelling under the snow to homes.
*Gives:* warm, contented belonging, but static: every side window is already lit, so it reads "a warm street", not
"warmth arriving".
1. Stage the arrival: near windows warm, the middle ones clearing, the far ones still frosted, her block at the end
   waiting, so the warmth travels.
2. Turn the warm line in at every door (a short melt branch, a glow at each entrance; now the lines cross the street
   like tram tracks), so "into people's homes" reads.
3. Draw the tram as a tram on its tracks, nearer and lit (now a box hanging from wires that reads as a gondola, right
   at the frame's centre): a Helsinki mark, and the centre stops being a puzzle.

**A-B12 · Your chat, her window: ALMOST.** *Intended:* personal warmth and a wink, your own chat warms a home, back to
the start. *Gives:* a tender close and the circle closes, but the wink (your chat causing her warmth) is faint.
1. Put the honey bubble on your phone and her window on one diagonal, the two brightest honeys in the frame, the pulse
   along the shore joining them, so "your chat warms her home" reads with the sound off.
2. The glove lighter, lower left and cut by the bottom edge (now a dark block in the centre-left), so the "you" is a
   gentle presence and the city stays whole.
3. The plan's building either clearly the frost-drawn data center (its fans and door in glittering frost: "could soon")
   or left out; now an unreadable white wire cube.

## 3. BOARD READ (the whole board in order, sound off, phone size, as a viewer)

- **The story reads** from the frames and their words, beat to beat: a snowy city and a claim, giant machines, they get
  hot, one machine against forty kettles, a cup, the heat blown away, the city wants it, a plan for 2027, cold water in
  and warm out, the warm line up the street, your chat and her window. Two places lean on the words: B9 (an icon) and
  B4 (the kettles do not read, so the caption carries "kettles").
- **One world:** yes. One snowy Helsinki night, cool greys and one honey, snow in every exterior, her window in B1, B7,
  B11 and B12, the first and last frames one view. It breaks in the frost chapter (B8-B10 turn into white icons, CAD
  lines and a stick figure: an infographic insert) and at B7's slab blocks.
- **One thread:** the honey heat is the clearest thing on the board, handed on frame to frame (the wave, the vents, the
  kettles, the cup, the lost plume, the windows, the pipe, the bubble, her window); it thins to specks in B8-B9.
- **One arc:** the right shape, and the pictures follow it from B2 to B8 and from B10 to B12 (cold awe, heat, play, a
  smile, a pang, delight, hope, "oh", relief, a wink). It sags at the start (B1 is calm where the hook must surprise:
  the board's biggest gap) and at B9 (clinical white where we should lean in).
- **The film concept: half clear.** "The city as the story" and the heat's journey to the windows read at a glance.
  "The frost on Helsinki's windows tells the story" does not: before B8 the frost is invisible (the frosted panes read as
  plain pale windows in B1, B6, B7 and B11, as A.md itself notes), so the frost drawing arrives in B8 as a new device
  instead of the city's own frost. One change fixes it and strengthens the hook: show the frost as frost from frame 0
  (ferned and glittering on the near panes) and clear it as the warmth arrives, so B8's finger draws in the frost the
  film has been showing all along.
- **Worth pausing on, at phone size:** B3, B7 and B11 already; B4, B8 and B12 once their notes land; B2 and B6 go dim
  and flat; B9 and B10 read as diagrams; B1 is a postcard.
- The light caption bar is the sheet's placeholder: in the key frames the band is each night frame's own sky (LAYOUT
  §1), which gives B1, B6 and B7 their skies back.

## 4. The sequence's verdict: ALMOST

The concept is strong and its own: the windows as homes, the building with no windows as heat with no home, the frost
as the plan (an honest way to show a plan that is not built yet); the world and the thread hold, and every hand-off is
planned frame to frame. It is not THERE: nine frames need their adjust notes and B9 needs a rework. **Fix in this
order:** B1's wave (the hook), B9 (the door as the frame), B4's kettles, then the frost shown as frost across the wides.
**Inside the sequence:** B4 with B4alt's equal clouds, B7, B9 reworked (not B9alt), B10 with B10alt's warming pipe.
