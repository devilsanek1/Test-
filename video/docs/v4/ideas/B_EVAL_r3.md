## 3. BOARD READ (the whole board in order, sound off, B6alt in B6's place)

- **The story reads from the frames alone.** A warm seam in a snowy harbour city leads to a drawn hall. Then a hall of
  computers, which turns hot. One computer holds forty boiling kettles, and a cup is raised to them. Fans throw the
  warmth out, and only a fox enjoys it. The neighbours lean out for it. A drawn hall marked "2027" fills the gap, and
  its door opens. Cold water goes in and warm water comes out. The warmth runs under the street into a window, and
  then it is your phone, the seam and her raised mug. Each step follows from the last with no voice.
- **One world.** One snowy harbour night in cool greys with one honey accent, and one cast (her in every chapter but the
  fox's, the neighbours, the "you"). The pencil device is held the same way in B1, B8, B9, B10 and far in B12. For the key frames:
  her house is one building in B11 and B12 (now two different facades).
- **One thread.** It is the heat, and it reads as one mark: the warm seam in B1, B7, B8, B11 and B12, and the honey of
  the machines, kettles, fans, door and pipe. B5 is the one frame where it goes cold (its note).
- **One arc.** Curious, then cool awe, hot, blazing, a breath, a wry pang, the warm turn, hope, the brightest opening,
  clarity, relief and a wink, each frame one step. The two deliberate pairs (B2-B3 cold to hot, B7-B8 empty to drawn)
  read as changes, not repeats. The only sag is at B5, which drops into cool grey a beat before the pang.
- **The concept is clear:** "the open machine, one piece missing". Every frame looks through one warm opening (the
  hall's door, the cabinet, the drawn door, the open machine, the window, your screen). The missing piece is plain on
  the board: the line stops short (B7), the piece is drawn in (B8), and we step inside the drawing (B9-B10). B1's
  drawn hall is the one place it is still a riddle, and for a hook that is fine.
