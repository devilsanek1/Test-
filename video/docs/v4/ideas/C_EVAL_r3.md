# Video 4 ideas, sequence C ("The warm current"): THE FRAME LOOP, evaluation round 3

Fresh evaluator, 2026-10-31. Sheet judged as it is now: `C_sheet.png` (the 14:29 build). The paint-over is `C_EVAL_r3.png`.

## 1. The feeling each thumbnail gives (written from the picture alone, before reading the brief or the concept)

Each frame was viewed at full size first, then at phone size (about 200 px wide) and at a squint (about 110 px wide).

- **C-B1** The feeling it gives: a jolt of curiosity in the cold. On a frozen, empty night street, right in front of my phone, a golden seam of heat splits the snow and steams. Something warm is waking up under the city and I want to know what. (At phone size it reads as a glowing crack or a small fire in the road.)
- **C-B2** The feeling it gives: cold, hushed awe. A canyon of blue-white lit "windows" runs to a vanishing point with one small person walking it. It feels like a city street made of machines, and big. One warm strip beside the person hints that the warmth came along.
- **C-B3** The feeling it gives: a flinch. A tall dark cabinet's grille glows like an oven door, heat wavers up off it, and a person rears back with an arm up as if hit by the blast: "whoa, that's hot". The recoil reads at phone size. The rising haze is faint and greyish.
- **C-B4** The feeling it gives: delighted surprise, a laugh. The cabinet door has swung open, it is packed with rows of steaming kettles glowing orange, and a person warms a hand at it. It is the brightest and fastest-reading frame in the row.
- **C-B5** The feeling it gives: a warm, cosy giggle. The machine is wearing a giant knitted tea cosy and glows like a lamp, and a tiny person cheerfully holds a mug up to its spout. Homely and silly at once.
- **C-B6** The feeling it gives: a quiet sigh of waste. Round roof fans push soft golden plumes up past a row of dark apartment windows into the night, and the warmth drifts off and is lost. It feels gentle and lazy rather than forceful, and the lower third is empty roof.
- **C-B7** The feeling it gives: hushed anticipation, an invitation. It is the opening's street again, now with a line of warm round hatches opened down its middle like runway lights receding to the city: the street is making way. It is calm rather than urgent, and the "wanting" comes across as an open path, not a reach.
- **C-B8** The feeling it gives: a hopeful but rather abstract promise. A chalk sketch of a building is drawn on the snowy harbour, with a "2027" stake and a chalk pipe catching the glowing warm stream, and a planner stands holding a rolled plan. It reads as "not real yet, but drawn". The bottom half is empty snow and the picture feels thin.
- **C-B9** The feeling it gives: curiosity and welcome. A real door stands open in the chalk outline, warm light pours out across the snow toward me, and a figure leans in to look. I want to step through.
- **C-B10** The feeling it gives: a calm "oh, I see". Cold blue water comes in low, zigzags up across a dark machine and leaves as gold, and a person lays a hand where it turns warm. It is clear and logical, but a little schematic and cool, with less wonder than the frames around it.
- **C-B11** The feeling it gives: arrival and relief. A gold line runs up the snowy street straight to a building's door, the windows glow warm, and a figure stands in the middle window: the heat got home. It feels stately but a bit static and frontal.
- **C-B12** The feeling it gives: tender, cosy closure. One warm window shows a woman curled in an armchair with tea, and my hand holds the phone in the foreground: my chat question has become her warm evening. Gentle and complete.
- **C-B1alt** The feeling it gives: excitement with a sense of scale. From my own balcony I watch a spark race down the long street, branching gold into doorways and warming windows as it passes, so the whole city seems about to light up. It is bigger and more of an event than C-B1, but the phone carries less of the story.
- **C-B5alt** The feeling it gives: a wry smile. The glowing machine is a giant tea bag with its tag swinging, and a person sits nearby with a mug. It is a neat pun, but sparse and less warm than the tea cosy.
- **C-B7alt** The feeling it gives: puzzled curiosity. Five glowing hooks curl from the edge of the buildings toward the roof fans, so I sense "reaching", but at phone size they could be cables or light streaks. The fans' plumes are gone, so the contrast with heat being thrown away is lost.

### C-B1 "The snow wakes warm": THERE
- **Wanted:** surprise and curiosity ("wait, what?"), plus the hook: a bold visible event from frame 0. **Gives:** a jolt of curiosity. Something warm is waking under the cold street, right in front of my phone.
- **Round 2's change landed.** The head is near, it is the brightest thing in the frame, and it is the one place the eye lands. The near blocks are dark, and the bloom (lit doors, far windows) sits behind it.
- Polish for the key frame:
  1. At phone size the head's jagged, flame-like edges read as a small fire in the road. Paint it as heat under snow (a soft wet melt edge, the snow lifting and cracking, steam), so the surprise feels warm and never dangerous.
  2. The phone's screen ends at frame y ~1570, about 50 px under the bottom bar. Lift it.
  3. Having the run already racing at you on frame 0 is the shot plan's job.

### C-B2 "A street of computers": THERE
- **Wanted:** a little awe: AI is physical and huge. **Gives:** cold, hushed awe. It is a street made of machines with one small person walking it.
- Polish for the key frame (round 2's note, still in the sketch): the spark on its curved trail sits on top of the thin warm strip, and together they read as a street lamp. I saw a lamp before I read anything. Make the warm into one computer's whole amber face among the cyan, with the spark landing on it, and let her look up at it. Today she walks away from us.

### C-B3 "The heat climbs": THERE
- **Wanted:** the heat rising: warm, with a touch of alarm. **Gives:** a flinch: "whoa, that's hot".
- Polish for the key frame:
  - Her recoil reads at phone size, but only just: she stands upright with one arm up, which could be a wave. Lean her away, with the forearm across her face.
  - Her feet end at frame y ~1656, under the bar. Lift her.
  - The hot air off the top is greyish and faint. Warm it at its root so the climb can be seen.

### C-B4 "Forty kettles inside": THERE
- **Wanted:** playful astonishment at the size of it. **Gives:** delighted surprise, a laugh. It is the fastest read of the top row (forty kettles, four by ten).
- Polish for the key frame (round 2's note, still in the sketch): her back runs into the right rail (to frame x ~990) and her feet end at y ~1674, under the bar. Pull her in and up.

### C-B5 "The tea cosy": THERE
- **Wanted:** a smile and a breath: the joke lands. **Gives:** a warm, cosy giggle. The giant and the tiny stand side by side, calm and centred.
- **Round 2's swap was right.** The cosy lands at a glance, and her tiny mug with its tiny tag carries the wink.
- Polish:
  - Her feet end at y ~1598. Lift her a little.
  - The cosy is now the film's biggest warm mass. A rough count of warm pixels below the band gives about 30 % of the frame here and about 17 % in B12. Keep B12's window the brightest honey in the film, so the payoff still lands as the warmest moment.

### C-B6 "Thrown into the night": THERE
- **Wanted:** a small pang: all that warmth thrown into the cold night. **Gives:** a quiet sigh of waste, as warm plumes drift off past dark homes. The pang is there, in a soft key.
- **Round 2's fixes landed.** The fans read as fans, the plumes climb past the homes, and the right-hand windows are dark.
- Key-frame notes. With B8, this is the frame the key frames must lift most.
  1. "Big fans ... blow" is not felt. The fans are small and sit in a row across the middle, nothing gives them scale, and the lower ~45 % of the frame is empty roof. Bring one fan near and big, its housing cut by the frame's edge, with its plume pouring up. That way "big" and "blow" read, and the near roof holds the fan instead of a blank.
  2. The haze over the three left facades makes their windows look warm, so only the right-hand block reads cold. Keep the plumes thin over the panes so the homes stay cold. The pang is warmth lost right over homes that need it.

### C-B7 "The street opens a way": THERE
- **Wanted:** the turn: a spark of delight, the city reaching for it. **Gives:** hushed anticipation and welcome. The opening's street, cold again, lays a lit path.
- The turn reads. The delight is quieter than the beat asks, and the reach reads more as a path laid down than as a reach.
- Polish for the key frame and the shot plan:
  1. What the street reaches for, the hall's plumes at its end, is a faint grey haze at the vanishing point at phone size. Make it read as B6's warm heat (warm at the root, the fans' roof just visible), so the lit path visibly points AT the thrown-away heat.
  2. The couple on the right pavement stand still. Have them turn and point (round 2's note): the delight is felt through people.
  3. The spark itself is motion: the lids slide open one after another on "wants". That belongs to the shot plan.

### C-B8 "A plan in chalk": THERE (the board's thinnest frame; its key frame must lift it)
- **Wanted:** hope and anticipation: real, and coming soon (a plan, not built). **Gives:** a hopeful promise that reads "not real yet, but drawn": the chalk building, its pipe meeting the city's warm line, 2027, the planner.
- **Round 2's change landed.** The meeting point is the focal and sits in the read zone (frame y ~1080-1150), with its light on the chalk and the snow.
- Key-frame notes:
  1. The lower ~40 % of the frame is blank snow, so at a glance the picture feels thin. It is also not yet the card's staging ("crouched low ... two ends about to touch close in front of us"): the gap sits mid-ground at a standing eye level.
     - **Change:** bring the camera low, as the card says, and run the city's warm channel in through the near snow from the lower right. That is the side the thread enters by in B9, B11 and B12.
     - **Why:** the near snow then carries the warm arriving, and the gap is near and big. The quiet field stays, painted alive.
  2. "2027" is about 45 px tall. Make it bigger, or let the caption carry "next year" (it already does).

### C-B9 "A door in the plan": THERE
- **Wanted:** leaning in: a door opens. **Gives:** curiosity and welcome; I want to step through. It is one of the board's strongest frames.
- Polish for the key frame (round 2's note, still in the sketch): its chalk is thin dashes, fainter than B8's. Make it as bold, so that B8 to B10 read as one plan.

### C-B10 "Blue in, gold out": THERE
- **Wanted:** clarity and satisfaction: "oh, that's how". **Gives:** a calm "oh, I see", felt through her hand where blue turns gold. It is the most schematic frame on the board: the pipe is a flat outlined zigzag, like an icon.
- Polish for the key frame (round 2's notes, still in the sketch):
  - The gold main's pointed end, top right, still reads as an arrow. Round it.
  - Paint the pipe as a pipe with water in it (frost on the blue, a breath of steam off the gold), so it is a place and never a diagram.

### C-B11 "Warm under the snow": THERE
- **Wanted:** cozy relief: warmth travelling under the snow to homes. **Gives:** arrival and relief. The gold line runs up the street to the door and the windows glow. It is stately, a little static and frontal.
- **Round 2's band fix landed.** The roofline now starts at frame y ~612, clear of the caption band.
- Polish for the key frame (round 2's notes, still in the sketch):
  - The woman is a small bust in the middle window. Make her bigger, right at the glass.
  - The top floors' pale panes read as cold lit windows. Paint them as frost.
  - Paint the line as a melt in the snow (dark, wet, steaming), not a lit stripe.
  - Add a little street life and a slight angle to wake up the frontal facade.

### C-B12 "Your chat, her warm window": THERE
- **Wanted:** personal warmth and a wink: your own chat warms a home; back to the start. **Gives:** tender, cosy closure. My phone and her warm evening, and the loop back to B1 reads.
- Polish for the key frame (round 2's notes, still in the sketch):
  - The phone's screen ends at frame y ~1566, under the bar. Lift it.
  - The wink back to "a lot of tea" isn't drawn yet. Add a tiny tea tag over her mug's rim.
  - Keep her window the film's brightest honey (see B5).

### The alternates
- **C-B1alt "From your balcony": ALMOST, keep B1.** The beat wants the hook's surprise; this gives excitement and scale, with the whole street warming. Two things hold it back:
  - The current is a thin line whose right-angle branches read like a map at phone size.
  - The phone is small, at the edge, and runs to frame y ~1688, under the bar.
  B1 stops the thumb harder, and it holds the eye-level street and the phone that B12 comes back to.
- **C-B5alt "The teapot computer": ALMOST, keep B5.** It gives a wry smile, but the tea-bag pun needs a second look that the beat's one second doesn't give. Beside the cosy, the picture is sparse.
- **C-B7alt "The city reaches back": NOT THERE for its beat.** It gives puzzled curiosity. At phone size the five gold hooks read as cables or light streaks, and with the fans' plumes gone there is no heat for them to reach for, so the "But" isn't felt. B7, the street, does the turn.

## 3. BOARD READ (the twelve film frames in order, sound off, at phone size and at a squint)

The board, frame by frame:
1. A warm thing bursts up through a cold street in front of my phone.
2. A canyon of machines, one warm sliver.
3. The machine glows like an oven; someone flinches.
4. It is full of boiling kettles.
5. It wears a giant tea cosy, and she holds up her mug.
6. Roof fans breathe the warmth away past dark homes.
7. The opening's street lays a lit path toward the far heat.
8. A chalk plan on the harbour, 2027, the warm line meeting its pipe.
9. A door in the plan opens warm.
10. Water turns gold up a machine under her hand.
11. The warm line runs to a block whose windows glow.
12. My phone and her warm window.

- **The story from the frames alone: yes.** Computers make heat, it is usually thrown away, the city wants it, a plan is drawn, water and pipes carry it home, and you are at both ends. The one link the pictures don't make yet is "your chat makes this heat" (B1 to B2). `C.md` already gives that to the shot plan: the spark leaving your phone.
- **One world: yes.** One snowy night, one cold palette with one warm accent and one small cast. The street of homes (B1, B7, B11, B12) rhymes with the street of computers (B2). The blocks are still generic units; the Helsinki specifics are the world kit's job.
- **One thread: yes, and it is the board's strength.** In every frame the eye lands on the warm, and from B8 on it is one line handed on from frame to frame.
- **One arc: yes.** The warm swells to the cosy and drops into the coldest stretch (B6 and B7: a sigh, then a lit path in the cold). It rises through the plan and the door, takes a cool breath at B10's clarity, and lands warm on the home.
  - As an example, the rough warm-pixel count below the band runs: B5 30 %, B6 2 %, B7 1 %, B8 6 %, B11 11 %, B12 17 %.
  - The soft spot is the turn. B6 and B7 are the gentlest frames in energy, so the "But" leans on B7's motion and on its far heat reading (see B7's polish).
- **The concept: clear at a glance.** "The heat is the only warm thing, and it travels" reads from the board in one pass.
