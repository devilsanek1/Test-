Date: 2026-10-27. This is the single fact file every later v4 step uses (core line, script, captions, quote cards, truth

**Verdicts.** **VERIFIED**: a primary document, or 2+ reputable outlets agreeing on the wording. **REPORTED**: one
reputable outlet, or a company's own unaudited claim; on screen it is attributed ("Deezer says"). **UNCERTAIN**:
vendor-only, trackers or blogs, conflicting sources, or absence of evidence; never on screen as fact.
**FALSE-as-worded**: the claim as worded is wrong or misleading, and the row gives the true version. *(derived)*: our
arithmetic on VERIFIED figures; say it as "most" or "at least", never as a sourced number.

## Corrections to the G0 framing
1. Spotify's 50M+ are "AI-generated slop and spam" (Spotify's words) kept out of recommendations this year: not deleted, and not all AI. "Likely-AI" is NBC's gloss. FL 1.1
2. The AI Persona badge was announced on 2026-08-11 and went live from mid-September. It isn't 20 October news. FL 1.3
3. James Blake's line is from his Instagram Story (22-23 June 2026), which NPR quoted on 27 Oct. It is about the whole industry being faked (labels buying bot plays, paid reviews), not about AI songs, and he never said it to NPR. FL 6.2
4. It is "a shared pot", not "one pot": there is one per country per month, split by each track's share of plays. A Premium play adds more than a free one, and the pot pays rights holders, who then pay artists. FL 3.1-3.6
5. Spotify pays no recording royalty to a track under 1,000 plays a year, and likely-AI tracks are 10x more likely to sit under that line. So most AI uploads take nothing from the pot. FL 3.9-3.10
6. Caught fraud is already taken out of the payout (Spotify, Deezer, Apple), and Deezer calls the impact on human artists' pay "minimal on Deezer". Only uncaught fraud drains the pot, and nobody has measured it. FL 4.1-4.2, 7.6, 8.1-8.2
7. Most of the fraud Deezer caught in 2025 was on tracks it did not flag as AI (derived), and 2026's loudest bot case boosted a human song. Bots are the drain; AI makes the fake catalogue cheap. FL 4.3, 4.5
8. Smith's harm is contested. His lawyers say no artist "suffered any appreciable loss", and Spotify says it held his Spotify take to about $60,000. The $8,091,843.64 is the agreed loss, which he forfeits. FL 4.14-4.15
9. Deezer's "over half" counts new uploads on one app, on peak days in June 2026 (39% in January, 44% in April). AI gets 1-3% of the plays. FL 2.1-2.3
10. Hayduk's quote, finished: "If the denominator has grown because of AI slop, the percentage that each legitimate rights holder or artist will receive is diminished." His firm sells detection. FL 6.1
11. "Up to 85%" is 2025's figure, "depending on the month" (the November 2025 figure was "up to 70%"). FL 2.4
12. The prosecutor's quote in `voices.md` drops a clause without an ellipsis: "and fake songs in the place of creativity". FL 6.3
13. G0's tell-a-friend line ("Half the songs being uploaded are AI ... every fake play takes a bit from real artists") is FALSE-as-worded. It holds for one app on peak days, and only for fakes that aren't caught. See "Never say".
14. (Truth check, 2026-10-28) Smith's fraud figure is "more than $8 million" (prosecutors at sentencing; he forfeits the agreed $8,091,843.64); the "$14M+" is everything royalty payers sent him, real plays included, so it is never "his take from fake plays". FL 4.10, section 9 step 5.

## 9. The safest causal chain
1. **AI makes songs almost free to make and upload, so they flood in.** On peak days in June 2026, over half of one app's new uploads were fully AI-made (nearly 90,000 a day). Spotify kept 50M+ "AI slop and spam" tracks out of its recommendations this year. FL 2.1, 1.1, 2.8, 2.9. **VERIFIED.**
2. **Real people barely play them, and most of the plays they do get are fake.** They get 1-3% of Deezer's plays, and up to 85% of those plays were fake in 2025. Deezer says fake plays are the main reason AI songs get uploaded; Smith's plan was "a TON of content with small amounts of Streams". FL 2.3-2.5, 4.11. **VERIFIED**, but on Deezer's numbers only (FL 8.4).
3. **Streaming doesn't pay per play.** Each month, in each country, the money sits in a shared pot, split by each track's share of the plays. FL 3.1-3.5. **VERIFIED (primary).**
4. **So every counted play, real or fake, grows the total and shrinks every other track's share.** Spotify: "as total streams grow, you need more streams to maintain the same share", and spam "can dilute the royalty pool". Apple: "This is a zero-sum game". Deezer: fraud "undermines payments to legitimate artists". FL 3.7, 7.4, 6.4, 7.6. **VERIFIED** (the pot's own design, in the platforms' own words).
5. **Only fake plays that slip past the filters drain it.** Caught fakes are stripped out (Spotify, Deezer, Apple), and on Spotify a track under 1,000 plays a year earns nothing, which most AI tracks miss. Yet some get through: Smith's fake plays brought him "more than $8 million" (prosecutors at sentencing; $14M+ in total, real plays included) over 7 years on four services before he was charged. FL 4.1-4.4, 3.9-3.10, 4.9-4.10. **VERIFIED** that the filters exist and have been beaten; how much gets through today is **UNCERTAIN** (FL 8.2).
6. **So a real artist's slice is thinner by the uncaught fakes' share of the plays: a little, by an amount nobody has measured.** FL 8.1-8.3, 8.8. **Inference** (arithmetic on a VERIFIED mechanism; the size is unknown).

**Where a hostile fact-checker attacks: the step from 5 to 6, "show me the uncaught fraud".** Deezer calls the impact on
human artists' pay "minimal on Deezer"; Spotify says it held Smith to about $60,000, and his lawyers say no artist lost
anything appreciable; most AI tracks never cross the pay line; most fraud Deezer caught wasn't on AI tracks. The defence:
claim only what the pot's design guarantees ("fake plays that slip through take a slice"), say "nobody knows how much",
and cast AI as the cheap fuel and bots as the thief. Second target: link 2 rests on one app's data, so say "on one app".
