# V4 research, lane 1: how streaming money and AI songs actually work (mechanism)

Date: 2026-10-27. Spotify, Deezer, SEC, NBC, MBW, THR and DOJ-quoting pages were read as raw page text; quotes are verbatim.
Tags: **VERIFIED** = 2+ reputable sources or a primary document. **REPORTED** = one reputable source. **UNCERTAIN** = weak,
vendor-only or conflicting. **DERIVED** = our own arithmetic from tagged facts (never say it as a sourced number).

## 1. The pot: how streaming pays
- **VERIFIED (primary).** No per-play rate: "No service pays a fixed amount per stream"; "you get paid based on your share of total streams — which is called 'streamshare.'" (Spotify Loud & Clear FAQ, 2026 edition with 2025 data, https://loudandclear.byspotify.com/faq/). Support page (updated 2025-08-19): "Spotify does not pay artist royalties according to a per-play or per-stream rate" https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/royalties/
- **VERIFIED (primary).** The split: "If your music accounts for 1% of all streams, you get roughly 1% of the royalty pool" (Loud & Clear, https://loudandclear.byspotify.com/process/).
- **VERIFIED (primary).** One pot per country per month, not one global pot: "Every month, in every country, we calculate streamshare like this: your streams in that market ÷ total streams in that market = your share of that market's royalty pool ... The total royalty pool in each market is based on the subscription and music advertising revenues generated there." (Loud & Clear FAQ, 2026).
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Size: "About two-thirds of that money goes into the royalty pool" (FAQ); about four-fifths to recordings, one-fifth to publishing (Royalties Guide https://artists.spotify.com/royalties-guide). Paid out "$11B+" in 2025, ~$70B since founding; "capturing one in a million of all Spotify streams in 2025 generated about $11,000" (FAQ).
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Spotify itself concedes the denominator effect: "Everyone gets paid based on streamshare, not per stream. So yes, as total streams grow, you need more streams to maintain the same share. But ... The total royalty pool is growing, too." (FAQ, 2026)
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Rights holders, not artists, get the money: "We don't pay artists or songwriters directly, we pay rights holders, who then pay you based on your agreement with them." (FAQ)
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Premium vs Free: "streams from Premium users contribute more to the total royalty pool than streams from Free users ... payouts may vary depending on factors like the listener's country, subscription plan" (Royalties Guide). SEC 6-K, Q1 2024: royalty costs are "recorded in each segment [Premium, Ad-Supported] based on specific rates for each segment"; "Premium Service royalties are based on the greater of a percentage of revenue and a per user amount"; some ad deals use "the greater of a percentage of relevant revenue and an amount for each time a track is streamed" https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639920/000163992024000007/spot-20240331x6xk.htm So the pot is mostly fixed by revenue, with contractual floors (a fact-checker could raise the floors; the dilution logic still holds).
- **VERIFIED (primary).** A play counts after 30 seconds (Spotify support, 2026-09-01, https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/how-your-streams-are-counted/).
- **VERIFIED.** Apple Music pays the same way and recycles caught fraud: "When we find fraud we remove the stream counts, we remove from the charts, and we take the money and put it back into the pool so that it goes to honorable artists." Oliver Schusser (Apple Music), The Hollywood Reporter 2026-01-30 https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-industry-news/apple-music-oliver-schusser-streaming-fraud-bad-bunny-1236488459/ ; 9to5Mac 2026-02-11.

### Spotify's 1,000-stream minimum (in force since April 2024)
- **VERIFIED (primary).** "Starting in April 2024, tracks must have reached a threshold of at least 1,000 streams in the previous 12 months to be included in the recorded music royalty pool calculation", plus a "minimum number of unique listeners" Spotify keeps secret (support 2024-05-02 https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/track-monetization-eligibility/ ; announced 2023-11-21 https://artists.spotify.com/en/blog/modernizing-our-royalty-system).
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Where that money goes: sub-1,000 tracks averaged "$0.03 per month" and were 0.5% of streams; "that money is reallocated by streamshare to increase the payments going to eligible tracks" (+0.5% each); "no change to the size of the music royalty pool". Spotify also says it stops "pennies from an extremely high volume of tracks" (same two pages).
- **VERIFIED.** Recording royalties only: neither the 1,000-stream nor the unique-listener rule applies to publishing royalties (Spotify, per MBW 2026-10-07; the support page names only the "recorded music royalty pool").
- **VERIFIED.** Spotify, 2026-10-20: "Our 1,000-stream royalty threshold ... has become a first line of defense for human artists against the wave of AI-generated content. Tracks with indicators showing a high probability of being AI-generated are 10 times more likely to fall below the threshold" (Spotify blog quoted by MBW https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-has-pulled-more-than-50m-ai-slop-and-spam-tracks-out-of-its-recommendations-this-year/ ; NBC: "far less common for AI tracks"). Careful: NBC writes "an artist must reach" 1,000 streams; the rule is per track.

### Other payout models
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Deezer "artist-centric" (with UMG, France from Q4 2023): a "double boost" for "professional artists" (at least 1,000 streams a month from at least 500 unique listeners) and for songs fans actively choose; noise (about 2% of streams) replaced and kept out of the pool; Deezer flagged about 7% of streams as fraudulent in 2022; "97% of all uploaders ... generated only 2% of the total streams" (Deezer 2023-09-06 https://newsroom-deezer.com/2023/09/universal-music-group-and-deezer-to-launch-the-first-comprehensive-artist-centric-music-streaming-model/ ; Variety; Music Week).
- **VERIFIED.** Deezer also caps each listener: "a monetization cap of 1000 streams per individual user per month"; plays above it claim nothing more from the pot, which blunts bot farms (Deezer notes quoted by MBW 2023-10-30 https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/deezers-artist-centric-model-now-has-a-new-element-dubbed-user-centric/). Still in force in 2026: **UNCERTAIN** (not re-checked).
- **REPORTED.** User-centric (SoundCloud "fan-powered royalties", since March 2021): each listener's money goes only to the artists that listener plays, so a fake account can only redirect its own fee; under pro-rata it can pull out more than it paid in. Working paper: H. Yu, arXiv 2601.09573 (2026), citing Ghosh et al. (2025): user-centric passes a "no profit beyond the subscription cost" test, pro-rata fails it https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09573 ; MBW 2023-10-30 for the two definitions.

## 2. What is TRUE to say (the precision questions)
- **"Every stream takes a slice of one shared pot"?** Fair as shorthand if (a) it's "a shared pot", not one global pot (it is one per country per month, and Premium plays are worth more than Free ones); (b) it means every play that counts (30 s+, a track over Spotify's 1,000-a-year line, not flagged as fake); (c) we accept that the pot pays rights holders, who then pay artists. Plain, safe version: "Streaming doesn't pay per play. The money goes into a shared pot, and each song gets a slice the size of its share of all the plays."
- **Per country / per tier?** Per country and month: yes (FAQ). Premium and Free money is booked separately at different rates (SEC) and Premium plays add more (Royalties Guide). Spotify never says publicly that there are literally two pots per country: **UNCERTAIN**, don't say it.
- **Does a bot play on a free account cost the same as on a paid one?** No. A Free play is backed by ad money and is worth less; a Premium play is worth more. So fraudsters pay for accounts: Smith's bots used family plans (prosecutors, MBW 2026-10-07), set up with "fake email accounts and fraudulently-obtained debit cards" (DOJ via Paste 2026-10). A paying bot puts its own fee into the pot but, by playing round the clock, takes out far more; Smith's agreed loss subtracted "money he paid back into royalty pools by buying the streaming accounts his bots used" (MBW). Deezer's per-user cap and user-centric models exist to close exactly this gap.
- **Does the 1,000-stream minimum change who gets the money from a fake that never reaches it?** Yes. On Spotify a track under the line is left out of the recording-royalty calculation, so it takes nothing from real artists (its would-be pennies go to eligible tracks). Exceptions: publishing royalties, and other services' own rules. The fakes that cost artists are the ones bots push over the line with enough fake "listeners". Smith, 2018 email: "we need a TON of content with small amounts of Streams" (MBW).
- **"Almost nobody listens to AI songs."** True on Deezer (1-3% of plays), but partly because Deezer hides them from recommendations, and up to 85% of those plays were bots (Deezer FAQ, 2026-07).
- **"Fake plays shrink every real artist's slice."** True only for fakes that are NOT caught. Spotify: detected artificial streams "do not earn royalties" and should cause no "second-order negative impact on legitimate streams" (FAQ); Deezer: "All fraudulent streams are removed from the royalty pool"; Apple: the money goes "back into the pool".

## 3. How AI tracks get made and uploaded
- **REPORTED.** Suno users make about 7 million songs a day ("a Spotify catalogue every two weeks"), from an investor deck Billboard obtained (Nov 2025, via DJ Mag https://djmag.com/news/ai-platform-suno-generates-entire-spotify-catalogues-worth-of-music-every-two-weeks). **VERIFIED:** 2M paid subscribers, $300M annual revenue (Suno CEO, 2026-02-25, MBW https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-hits-2m-paid-subscribers-300m-annual-revenue/ ; "far beyond" that, Music Ally 2026-10-02).
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Suno today: Free = 50 credits a day, "No commercial rights", "No monthly song downloads"; Pro ($10 a month, $8 billed yearly) = 2,500 credits a month ("up to 500 songs"), commercial rights, "20 song downloads per month"; Premier = 60 downloads (https://suno.com/pricing, read 2026-10-27). Caps in force since 2026-09-03 (MBW https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-limits-pro-subscribers-to-20-song-downloads-per-month), after Suno's 2025-11-25 settlement with Warner (AP via Claims Journal 2025-11-26). UMG and Sony are still suing Suno.
- **VERIFIED.** Udio stopped all downloads on 2025-10-30 after settling with UMG (a "walled garden") (AP via WSLS 2025-10-30; Digital Music News 2025-10-31).
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Uploading is cheap and unlimited: DistroKid "Musician" plan, $24.99 billed annually: "Upload unlimited songs, keep 100% of royalties, and distribute to 150+ stores and platforms" (https://distrokid.com/pricing/, read 2026-10-27).
- **REPORTED.** A Sonic Intelligence Academy registry study: 92.8% of AI tracks submitted were made with Suno; DistroKid distributed over three-quarters of those with a known distributor (FT via MBW 2026-10-05). It's a registry sample, not all uploads.
- Note: Deezer's June 2026 peak predates Suno's download cap; there is no post-September upload figure yet.

## 4. The scale
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Deezer AI uploads: about 60,000 a day, ~39% of deliveries (Jan 2026); ~75,000 a day, 44% (Apr 2026; TechCrunch 2026-04-20, Music Week); "over 50% of all new music uploads on Deezer at peak level in June, 2026", 90,000 a day on average (Deezer 2026-07-21; TechCrunch 2026-07-21). 13.4M AI tracks tagged in 2025. Deezer only, and "at peak".
- **VERIFIED (primary).** On Deezer AI is "between 1 and 3% of total streams" (FAQ, 2026-07-21). "Generating fake streams continue to be the main purpose for uploading AI-generated music" (Deezer, 2026-01-29). From July 2026 Deezer "will systematically take down AI-tracks used for streaming fraud, and those that haven't been streamed for at least 6 months." Deezer says its AI detector is 99.8% accurate (its own claim).
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Spotify removed "over 75 million spammy tracks" in the 12 months to 2025-09-25 and announced a spam filter (mass uploads, duplicates, SEO hacks, artificially short tracks) that tags uploaders and stops recommending them, because "these behaviors can dilute the royalty pool"; royalties treat "all music ... equally, regardless of the tools used to make it" (https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-25/spotify-strengthens-ai-protections/).
- **VERIFIED.** Spotify, 2026-10-20: 50M+ "AI-generated slop and spam" tracks taken out of recommendations so far in 2026 (still on the app); "thousands" of profiles carry the "AI Persona" badge and are left out of recommendations by default; "AI detection technology in particular has limitations, and it will only get harder as generative AI evolves" (NBC; MBW, both 2026-10-20).
- **VERIFIED.** Sony asked platforms to remove 260,000+ AI deepfakes of its artists by end Sept 2026 (135,000 by end March; ~20,000 a month): "it's becoming an uphill struggle" (FT 2026-10-05 via MBW https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sony-music-has-now-asked-platforms-to-remove-more-than-260000-ai-deepfakes-imitating-artists-like-harry-styles-adele-and-michael-jackson/ ; Music Week).

## 5. Bot fraud: shares, money, the Smith case
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Deezer: up to 85% of streams on fully AI tracks were fraudulent in 2025 "depending on the month"; fraud across the whole catalogue was 8% of all streams in 2025; every detected fraudulent stream is demonetised (https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/01/deezer-confirms-demonetization-of-ai-music/ , 2026-01-29; https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/ , 2026-07-21; THR 2026-01-30).
- **DERIVED.** AI tracks at most 3% of Deezer plays x at most 85% fake = at most ~2.6% of all plays, against 8% total fraud: at least two-thirds of the fraud Deezer caught in 2025 was on tracks not flagged as fully AI. Bots are older than AI songs; AI makes the fake catalogue cheap.
- Platforms' own caught-fraud rates: Spotify "less than one percent of all streams" (spokesperson 2023, via Billboard 2023-11-16) **REPORTED**; Apple 0.3% (2023 partner email, Billboard) **REPORTED**; Apple "less than half a percent", up to 2 billion fraudulent plays demonetised in 2025, "nearly $17 million" by a Manatt royalty calculator (THR 2026-01-30; 9to5Mac) **VERIFIED**; France: 1-3% of 2021 streams (1-3 billion) detected and removed, detected fraud only, data from Spotify, Deezer, Qobuz and labels (CNM study, Jan 2023, https://cnm.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/CP_CNM_Manipulation-des-streams_ENG.pdf ; Digital Music News 2023-01-18) **VERIFIED**.
- Estimates of all fraud, caught or not: Beatdapp, "at least 10%" of streams and about $2B a year misallocated (Bloomberg 2023-05 via Spokesman-Review; MBW 2024-07-09 https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/streaming-fraud-costs-the-global-music-industry-2bn-a-year-according-to-beatdapp-now-its-partnering-with-beatport-to-combat-the-trend/ ; Rolling Stone 2026-03-30): **VERIFIED** that Beatdapp says it, **UNCERTAIN** as a fact (it sells detection; method not audited). FT 2026-10-05: label executives estimate fraud could cost "up to USD $2.2 billion annually" **REPORTED**.
- **VERIFIED.** Penalties: Spotify withholds royalties on detected artificial streams and "charges labels and distributors per track when flagrant artificial streaming is detected ... only applied for very high rates" (support, 2026-09-17 https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/third-party-services-that-guarantee-streams/); **REPORTED**: trigger is over 90% fake plays, about $10.82 per track per month (Rolling Stone 2026-03-30). Apple raised its fine from 5-25% to 10-50% of the royalties at stake in Feb 2026 (THR; 9to5Mac).
- **VERIFIED.** Michael Smith (54, Cornelius, North Carolina): sentenced 2026-10-06 by Judge John G. Koeltl (SDNY) to 18 months plus two years' supervised release; forfeits $8,091,843.64 (the agreed loss); pleaded guilty 2026-03-19 to conspiracy to commit wire fraud; 2017-2024, up to 10,000 bot accounts on Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music and YouTube Music played 100,000+ AI songs over a billion times; he received $14M+ from royalty payers; prosecutors wanted at least 46 months; the first US criminal case of its kind (MBW 2026-10-07 https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/man-behind-8m-ai-song-and-bot-streaming-fraud-is-sentenced-to-18-months-in-prison/ ; Forbes 2026-10-07; MusicTech; Paste quoting DOJ).
- **VERIFIED.** His own maths (indictment, 2024-09-04): 1,040 bot accounts, about 661,440 plays a day, $1,207,128 a year, about half a cent a play (https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/north-carolina-musician-charged-music-streaming-fraud-aided-artificial-intelligence ; PopSci).
- **REPORTED.** April 2023: Smith's bots used YouTube Music family plans to play his AI songs 80.9M times, while Taylor Swift's entire catalogue got 9.3M family-plan streams that month (prosecutors, via MBW 2026-10-07). Get the filing before using it.
- **REPORTED** (MBW 2026-10-07, quoting filings). The fight over harm: the Music Fights Fraud Alliance: "[e]very fraudulent stream perpetrated by Mr. Smith diluted the value of every legitimate stream occurring during the same accounting period." The defence says the government does not dispute that "no recording artist or songwriter suffered any appreciable loss"; the government: "Every penny of loss was diverted to the defendant." US Attorney Jamie McDonald: Smith "robbed millions in royalty payments from genuine artists and their fans."
- **REPORTED.** Spotify told MBW (2024) its measures held Smith's Spotify earnings to about $60,000 of the ~$10M then alleged; the MLC halted his publishing payments in 2023 (MBW 2026-10-07).

## 7. What gets through, and what artists actually lose
- **VERIFIED.** Spotify, Deezer and Apple all say caught fakes earn nothing and their money stays with real plays (sources above). The only drain is fraud that slips through, and nobody can measure that directly.
- **VERIFIED (primary).** Deezer: "the overall impact on human artists' royalties is minimal on Deezer" (FAQ, 2026-07-21).
- Signs that some gets through: Smith ran for about seven years across four services before he was stopped (**VERIFIED**); the CNM figure counts only detected fraud, and its president reportedly thinks the real level is higher (**REPORTED**, 2023); vendors' 10%+ against the platforms' 1% or less; Spotify: detection "will only get harder" (2026-10-20).
- No measured per-artist loss exists. **DERIVED**, for illustration only: on Spotify's $11B 2025 payout, every 1% of counted plays that are uncaught fakes would move about $110M a year and make every real slice about 1% thinner (an artist due $1,000 gets about $990). The real % is unknown.
- **UNCERTAIN, don't use.** Forbes's "$4 billion fraud machine" (2026-05-05) seems to rest on the CISAC/PMP forecast that ~25% of creators' revenue (up to EUR 4B) is at risk by 2028 from AI replacing human work, which is not a fraud loss (Deezer cites the CISAC figure).

## 8. Never say -> say instead
- "Spotify pays X per stream" -> "nobody pays per play; it's your share of a shared pot".
- "One pot for all streaming" -> "a shared pot" (one per country, per month).
- "Every bot play takes from artists" -> "every fake play that slips past the filters".
- "85% of AI streams are fake" -> "up to 85% ... in 2025" (worst months). "Half of all songs are AI" -> "at its June peak, over half of new uploads to one app".
- "Spotify deleted 50 million AI songs" -> "pulled from recommendations". "AI music steals from artists" -> the villain is bot fraud; AI makes it cheap.
- Any dollar loss per artist -> none has been measured.

## Hook-worthy facts (all VERIFIED)
1. At its June 2026 peak, over half of all new songs uploaded to Deezer were fully AI-made, about 90,000 a day (Deezer; TechCrunch).
2. Yet AI songs get only 1-3% of Deezer's plays, and in 2025 up to 85% of those plays were fake, depending on the month (Deezer).
3. Streaming doesn't pay per play: each month, in each country, a pot of about two-thirds of Spotify's music revenue ($11B+ in 2025) is split by each song's share of all plays (Spotify).
4. Spotify has pulled 50M+ AI "slop and spam" tracks out of its recommendations this year; they're still on the app (Spotify via NBC, MBW).
5. Likely-AI tracks are 10 times more likely to miss Spotify's 1,000-plays-a-year pay line, which Spotify calls "a first line of defense for human artists" (Spotify via MBW; NBC).
6. On 6 Oct 2026 Michael Smith got 18 months and must forfeit $8,091,843.64 for using up to 10,000 bot accounts to play 100,000+ AI songs; he collected $14M+ (MBW; Forbes; MusicTech).
7. "This is a zero-sum game": Apple Music's Oliver Schusser on fraud; Apple stripped up to 2 billion fake plays out of its 2025 payouts (THR; 9to5Mac).
8. Sony has asked platforms to take down 260,000+ AI deepfakes of its artists, nearly double its March count (FT via MBW; Music Week).
