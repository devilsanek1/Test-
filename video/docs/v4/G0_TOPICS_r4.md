## Pick one

**1. A vaccine made from your own cancer** (77/90, new)
- What: 24 Oct, at ESMO (Europe's big cancer congress): Moderna and Merck's personal mRNA vaccine, in a trial of 1,137 people whose melanoma was cut out. Lead author: Georgina Long (Melanoma Institute Australia).
- What it means: AI reads the tumor's DNA and picks up to 34 marks only that cancer has (Moderna's own account). The shot trains the immune system to hunt the cells surgery missed. It's given after surgery, to stop the cancer coming back. Not a cure, not approved.
- Core line: "A vaccine made from your own cancer just passed its biggest test." Cover: A VACCINE MADE FROM YOUR OWN CANCER. Line 2: "Two years later, 82 in 100 were still cancer-free. On the usual drug alone, 70."
- Hopeful: a medicine written for one person's cancer worked in a big, fair trial (chance decided who got it), with side effects like a COVID shot: fever, a sore arm.
- Risk: AI is one step, on the company's word. No survival data yet; the paper is still to come; approval "may take several years". Never "cure".
- Fact status: CHECKED (NBC 23 Oct, Melanoma Institute Australia 23 Oct, EMJ 24 Oct, Moderna's blog). Fixed: not peer-reviewed yet (NEJM will publish it).

**2. The glasses some want banned** (72/90, new)
- What: Australia is fighting over camera smart glasses ("pervert glasses"): Victoria will ban them in schools (22 Oct), councils at pools and gyms, the Greens propose an import ban. ABC, 13 Oct: blind and deaf users say the same glasses changed their lives.
- What it means: an AI looks through the camera and says what's there. Chris Edwards, who is blind, has it read him a menu or his washing machine. Matt Henderson, deaf since birth, gets live captions. About A$600; the old aids cost thousands.
- Core line: "Some people want these glasses banned. Chris, who's blind, calls them life-changing." Cover: THE GLASSES SOME WANT BANNED: "LIFE-CHANGING". Line 2: "The camera people worry about? Its AI reads him the menu."
- Hopeful: it helps now, in shops, for anyone losing sight or hearing. Lawmakers are writing in exemptions for disability aids.
- Risk: the privacy worry (secret filming) is real and gets its say, with no villain. An Australian fight; the brand isn't named.
- Fact status: CHECKED (ABC 13 Oct, read in full; Canberra Times 19 Oct; EducationHQ 22 Oct). Fixed: the sources never say "creepy"; the import ban is only proposed.

**3. The AI that called the hurricane** (70/90, from round 3)
- What: Hurricane Isaias hit near Destin, Florida, on 9 Oct. NPR, 26 Oct: the usual models first said Louisiana; Google DeepMind's AI said Florida from the start.
- What it means: it got the path right, not the strength. People still issue the warnings; the US hurricane center blends it in.
- Core line: "The usual forecast models said Louisiana, and Google's AI said Florida." Cover: MODELS SAID LOUISIANA. AI SAID FLORIDA. Line 2: "And it was right. It pointed there before the storm even had a name."
- Hopeful: it's helping now. Expert Michael Lowry: "the best performing computer model that we've ever seen. Period."
- Risk: one storm proves little; nine people died; you saw it in round 3.
- Fact status: CHECKED in round 3 (NPR, CNN via Wikipedia); unchanged.

**Recommendation: 1, the vaccine:** the biggest, freshest good news, on the strongest evidence, told as 82 vs 70 in 100. For AI front and center, helping people today: 2.

## Fact-check notes (sources read 2026-10-28; feeds the fact lock)

**1. Vaccine (intismeran autogene, INTerpath-001).** NBC News (David Cox, 23 Oct, 2:42 pm EDT; read): "The trial
included 1,137 Stage 3 melanoma patients"; "757 of the participants also got the personalized vaccine; the remaining
380 got a placebo"; "Two years later, 82% of the patients who received both the new vaccine and the immunotherapy
hadn't had a recurrence", "compared with 70% in the immunotherapy plus placebo group"; "reduced the risk of cancer
recurrence and death by 42%"; "Each patient received their personalized vaccine in a little over nine weeks on
average"; "17% did not have tumor samples that were big enough for a vaccine to be created" (of 1,749 screened); "The
full data is yet to be published in a peer-reviewed journal"; the leak made investors react "poorly", "causing
Moderna's stock price to fall". Voices: Georgina Long: "This treatment is about mopping up the cancer cells we can't
see." / "This is just the proof of concept." Robert Vonderheide (Penn): "game changer"; "They're not on the shelf. They
have to be made for that patient." Shirin Bajaj (Mount Sinai): "It's amazing, but I think some of the logistics are
going to need to be worked out"; "it's hard to say whether at this point we have data to suggest that this is actually
the next blockbuster." EMJ Reviews (24 Oct; read): stages IIB/IIC or III/IV (so NBC's "Stage 3" is too narrow); "The
estimated 24-month recurrence-free survival rate was 82.2%" vs 70.0%; HR 0.58 (95% CI 0.45-0.75; p<0.001); "median
follow-up was 24.6 months" (data cut-off 30 June 2026); distant-metastasis-free at 24 months 88.5% vs 80.2% (HR 0.62);
fever 52.2% vs 8.2%; injection-site pain 51.0% vs 9.5%; grade 3+ side effects 23.6% vs 20.3%; ESMO abstract LBA1.
Melanoma Institute Australia (23 Oct, updated 25 Oct; read): 157 sites; "This is the first phase 3 study to show that
a treatment built from the unique 'fingerprint' of a patient's own tumour..."; "That fingerprint is read by sequencing
the tumour, then a 'wanted poster' of the cancer is made as mRNA..." (our picture, in the trial team's own words);
"Results will be published in the New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM)."; "These results now enter a regulatory review
process which may take several years, depending on the country." / "Until then the treatment is not available."; Long:
"This has not been achieved before in the history of cancer drug development." The AI step: Moderna's blog (19 Dec 2023;
read): "a series of fully integrated AI algorithms takes next-generation sequencing data from tumor and blood samples"
and "predicts up to 34 of those neoantigens that are most likely to elicit an immune response"; IBTimes (21 Aug 2026):
"Moderna says its AI-driven process can identify up to 34 neoantigens". NBC, MIA and EMJ don't mention AI, so it stays
"Moderna says": AI helps pick the targets; never "an AI-designed vaccine" or "AI beat cancer". Fixed: the scout's
"accepted by NEJM" becomes "NEJM will publish it" (not out yet); "a computer picks the targets" becomes Moderna's own
word, AI. Open: survival (too early); cost and scale; the other cancers in trials (scout: lung, kidney, bladder, via
STAT; not read here); the congress city.
