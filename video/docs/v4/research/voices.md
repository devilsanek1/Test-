**5. Jamie McDonald, U.S. Attorney (SDNY)**, on Michael Smith's sentence. On 2026-10-06 Smith got 18 months and was ordered to
  forfeit $8,091,843.64. He used thousands of bot accounts (up to 10,000 active) to stream "hundreds of thousands" of AI songs
  "billions" of times from 2017 to 2024, on Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music and YouTube Music; prosecutors call it the
  first criminal case of AI-assisted streaming fraud. Verbatim: "By flooding music streaming platforms with automated bots in
  the place of consumers, Smith robbed millions in royalty payments from genuine artists and their fans." **VERIFIED**: MBW
  2026-10-07 https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/man-behind-8m-ai-song-and-bot-streaming-fraud-is-sentenced-to-18-months-in-prison/ ;
  NPR 2026-10-27. Skip the statement's first line ("exploited super intelligence technology"), which is odd wording.
- Smith's own email, Oct 2018, from the court papers: "in order to not raise any issues with the powers that be we need a TON
  of content with small amounts of Streams." Another email says "a TON of songs fast to make this work around the anti fraud
  policies" (outlets hyphenate "anti fraud" differently). **VERIFIED**: MBW 2026-10-07; PC Gamer 2024
  https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/fraudster-charged-with-dollar12-million-in-stolen-royalties-used-1000-bots-to-stream-hundreds-of-thousands-of-ai-tracks-billions-of-times
- On screen: YES for both (an official statement and the court record). Smith's email explains why fakes arrive in floods:
  lots of songs, a few plays each, so no single track looks suspicious.

## 2. Fairness: the fraud is the villain, not the players
- **Spotify** pays nothing on streams it catches as manipulated (NPR). A track must reach 1,000 plays in 12 months to earn
  anything, a rule since 2024 (NBC 2026-10-20). It removed 75M+ "spammy" tracks in the 12 months to Sept 2025 (TIME; NBC),
  has kept 50M+ AI-looking tracks out of recommendations in 2026, and added the AI Persona badge (Aug) and Artist Profile
  Protection, which lets artists vet releases before they go live (TIME, 2026-03). **VERIFIED**. Per an NBC photo caption,
  300,000+ verified artists account for 99%+ of what listeners search for: **REPORTED**.
- **Deezer** tags AI tracks, strips fraudulent streams from the pool, and deletes AI tracks used for fraud or left unplayed for
  6+ months. It does not ban AI music (2026-07-21 release). **VERIFIED**.
- **Suno and Udio** make tools; they are not the fraudsters. The Smith court papers name no AI company, and the co-conspirator
  CEO is unnamed, so never name or draw anyone there. The labels have licensed them: UMG with Udio (Oct 2025), Warner with Udio
  and with Suno (Nov 2025). Sony and UMG-Suno are still unsettled (DMN 2026-04-09
  https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/09/suno-universal-music-lawsuit-settlement-impasse/). **VERIFIED** (widely reported).
  404 Media made its test track with Udio, but the loophole was the distributor's identity check (2026-09-17,
  https://www.404media.co/spotify-ai-music-scam/, **REPORTED**).
- **Distributors**: the weak point is the identity check, since anyone can claim "that's me" (Bender; 404 Media). Say "the upload
  system", not "DistroKid". DistroKid acted on Spotify's flag in Coleman's case, and the filters there hit a real artist.
- **AI-using artists** (Jones, Cork, Timbaland per TIME) are legitimate. Spotify's badge is about identity, not about how the music was made.
- **Avoid**: King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard. Their AI clone story (Dec 2025; Spotify removed it and paid "no royalties", DMN
  2025-12-09 https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/12/09/spotify-king-gizzard-ai-copycat/ **REPORTED**) sits on top of their
  protest at Daniel Ek's military-AI investment, which brings in politics and a villain. Reservoir's lawsuits: Anthropic is a
  co-target (see ai_frontier "dropped"), so leave them out entirely.

## 4. Real people, apps and logos on screen
- **Apps and logos**: naming Spotify and Deezer in the voice-over and captions is ordinary news commentary. Spotify's own partner
  rules say its logo must not be "modified, or added to" (https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design), and an altered
  logo used in criticism risks confusion or tarnishment claims (**UNCERTAIN**: general legal principle that varies by country;
  not legal advice). DO draw a generic music app in our own style (a play button, a wall of sleeves) with at most a colour nod.
  If a real logo appears, show it small and unaltered, as a source credit. DON'T crack, melt or robotise a logo, cast one as
  the villain, or put one on the thumbnail.
- **Real artists**: no caricatures. The UK has no image right but passing off covers implied endorsement; US publicity rights are
  state law with news exceptions (**UNCERTAIN**). Use quote cards (name, role, date, outlet). Never animate a real person
  mouthing words, and never voice their quote with a cloned or impersonated voice, which is exactly the harm the film is
  about. Our narrator reads it as a quote ("in her words...").
- **Small musicians**: use only what they said in public, never add a loss they didn't claim, and ask before drawing them. Never
  show a real fake's cover art or page (e.g. "Eduardo Arguelles").

## 5. Platform reach and audio
- **The topic itself**: we found no evidence that AI or music-industry talk is throttled on TikTok, Reels or Shorts (**UNCERTAIN**:
  absence of evidence). The real reach risks are below.
- **YouTube** (Help Center, primary, read 2026-10-27, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491): "Cloning one's own voice
  to create voice overs or dubs" needs no disclosure, and nor does non-realistic or animated AI. Disclosure IS required when
  AI "Creates music that's the main focus of the video". "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or
  impact its eligibility to earn money." **VERIFIED**.
- **TikTok** (Community Guidelines, effective 2026-09-24, https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity):
  creators must label "AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people", and
  "Unlabeled content may be removed, restricted, or labeled by our team". Misinformation that "may cause moderate harm" is
  ineligible for the For You feed. **VERIFIED** (primary). Our drawn look is not realistic, and the text doesn't mention voices.
  The claim that a label doesn't cut reach comes only from secondary guides: **UNCERTAIN**.
- **Optics**: a film about fake songs is narrated by a cloned voice (the owner's own). Keep the stance "fraud, not AI" so it
  can't read as hypocrisy, and be ready to say so in the comments.
- **Content ID**: a hummed or whistled copyrighted melody, or the song's lyrics on screen, can be claimed even with no recording
  (YouTube Creator Insider, as summarised by VidIQ https://vidiq.com/blog/post/cover-songs-on-youtube/ and TubeBuddy
  https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-news-song-copyright/; **REPORTED**). DO: original score and sound effects only; the
  "AI song" we play is our own bland sting. DON'T: hum, whistle or sing a real tune, use a sound-alike riff, a famous ringtone
  or sound logo, or put lyrics on screen. Don't use Suno or Udio output in the mix: YouTube requires disclosure when AI music
  is the main focus, the output may resemble real songs, and it contradicts the film.
- **Instagram/Threads**: since 2024, political content ("laws, elections, or social topics") hasn't been recommended to
  non-followers by default (Engadget 2024
  https://www.engadget.com/instagram-and-threads-will-no-longer-recommend-political-content-180521125.html); Meta said in Jan 2025
  it would add some back (Washington Times 2025-01-09). The 2026 state is **UNCERTAIN**, so keep bills and politicians out
  (e.g. the NO FAKES Act, Sen. Blackburn in NPR's piece).
