## 9. Common misconceptions (and the correction)
1. "Spotify deleted 50 million AI songs." No: it pulled 50M+ "AI slop and spam" tracks from recommendations. They stay on the app, and the count mixes AI tracks with non-AI spam.
2. "AI songs are taking over what we listen to." No: on Deezer they're about half of new uploads but under 3% of plays, and most of those plays are fraud.
3. "I'd know if I heard one." 97% failed a 3-song blind test, and 58% of streamers believe they've never been recommended one.
4. "An AI song topped the Billboard charts." Only niche charts: a ~3,000-download country sales chart, and No. 30 on an R&B airplay chart. None has been found on the Hot 100.
5. "The labels are fighting AI music." They sued, then Warner licensed Suno and Udio and UMG licensed Udio. UMG even distributes the AI "Papaoutai" cover. Sony, and UMG against Suno, are still in court.
6. "Bot fraud is an AI-song problem." 2026's loudest bot case boosted a human hit ("Earrings"). Bots go wherever the money is.
7. "AI music means no human made it." Usually a person writes, prompts, sings or curates (Xania Monet, Jacub, HAVEN.).
8. "If I never play AI songs, they cost artists nothing." Payouts come from one pot split by share of plays, so bot plays that go uncaught shrink everyone's slice. Artists' groups already say this (Say No To Suno). But caught fraud is already removed, and on Spotify a track under 1,000 plays a year earns nothing.

## 10. Which question is the public most worked up about?
| Question | How many share it | How angry | Can the one-pot mechanism pay it off in 40 s? |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Can I even tell? Tell me what's AI." | Widest. Labels wanted by 80% (Deezer/Ipsos), 77% in the Nordics (up from 58%) and 3/4 of the Dutch; 74% want a filter. Most 2025-26 viral stories are "we were fooled" stories, and every big platform shipped labels in 2026. | Medium: unease (52%), "We should have the choice" | Yes, as the way in: you can't tell, and what you hear matters less than who gets paid |
| "Are they stealing real artists' voices and work?" | High: 73% call it unethical | Highest. Imitation draws the most negative reactions (Luminate); see Sony's 260k fakes, Swift's trademarks, Isbell, Jorja Smith, SZA | No: it's a story about training, consent and likeness, not the pot |
| "Are real artists losing money?" | High but quiet: 70% see a threat to livelihoods; 69% say AI songs should pay less | Medium: the Chicago blackout, Say No To Suno | Fully, it IS the mechanism. People picture replacement, not dilution, which leaves room for a real "oh" |
| "Is my playlist fake?" | Medium | Low and falling (Spotify: "marked decrease in listener concerns") | Only as a myth to correct: AI is <3% of plays |

**Verdict.** The widest heat is "can I even tell?" (labels, choice), and the angriest is voice theft. The money question is
believed by most people but rarely explained. So ride the first into the third. Open on "you can't tell" (97% couldn't, in
one test). Turn on "you're barely hearing them anyway; bots are". Pay off with the one shared pot. Don't promise the theft
story (the pot can't pay it off), and don't promise "your playlist is full of AI" (false: <3% of plays).

## 11. What weakens or nuances the G0 framing
- Spotify's 50M is "AI slop **and spam**", not "50M likely-AI tracks": MBW says the figure isn't split and includes non-AI manipulation. Say "50 million AI-slop and spam tracks".
- Spotify pays nothing for tracks under 1,000 plays a year, and likely-AI tracks are 10x more likely to sit below that floor. So most AI uploads take nothing from the pot. The drain is bot plays that get past both the filters and the floor, as Smith's did.
