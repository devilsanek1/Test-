## 1. Cocoa got cheaper by half, so why is your Halloween chocolate more expensive?

- **News:** AP (2026-10-24): Halloween candy sales fell 6.7 % in the month to Oct 17 (NielsenIQ); candy and gum prices up 44 % in five years to September vs 22.5 % for all groceries. CBS (2026-10-26): record $13.5 bn Halloween spend (NRF), $4.1 bn on candy; chocolate ~8 % pricier than last Halloween (Wells Fargo Agri-Food Institute); 100-piece bag $16.96, +71 % vs 2021 (FinanceBuzz); Mondelez gummy packs +39 %, Ferrero chocolate packs ~+19 %.
- **Mechanism (the real story):** cocoa peaked at $10,710/t in Jan 2025 (AP/BLS; futures ~$12,906 Dec 2024 per Trading Economics), fell to $3,241 in March 2026, rebounded to ~$5,600-5,900 (Sept/Oct). But makers buy cocoa 3-24 months ahead (Hershey hedging window, per its filings as reported), so this October's chocolate was paid for at the peak. CBS: "candy makers are still using cocoa bought during the 2025 price spikes," per economists. PwC (via NBC): cocoa "fallen by about half, but lower costs take time to reach the shelf."
- **Twist:** the chocolate in your bag was bought a year ago; you are paying last year's cocoa price. Plus, some makers swapped cocoa butter for vegetable oil (CBS), so some bars legally became "chocolate candy." And prices tend to go up like a rocket, down like a feather.
- **Real person:** Phil Lempert (editor, The Lempert Report, CBS 2026-10-26): "We're going to see these prices go up through 2027 and probably 2028." Alt: Chris Costagli, NielsenIQ (AP).
- **Sources:** AP via NBC https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/halloween-candy-sales-are-slow-start-affordability-rcna605395 (2026-10-24); CBS https://www.cbsnews.com/news/consumers-face-scarier-halloween-costs-this-year-heres-why/ (2026-10-26); CNBC https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/05/cocoa-prices-are-climbing-again-heres-why-this-time-is-different.html (2026-10-05); Quartz (PwC 15.5 %) https://qz.com/halloween-chocolate-prices-cocoa-shock-101226
- **Frame 0:** "Cocoa halved. Your candy didn't."
- **Risks:** figures differ by method (PwC +15.5 %, NIQ +1.7 % Halloween-specific, Wells Fargo +8 %): pick one, attribute it. Hershey hedging window is from secondary reporting of the 10-Q; verify in the filing. Cocoa has partly rebounded, so say "about half off its peak," not "crashed." Seasonal: must ship by Oct 30-31 or reframe to "Christmas chocolate".

## 2. A power cut in one London building froze millions of bank cards

- **News:** 2026-10-20, ~06:08 UTC: "A power event caused an outage within europe-west2-b zone" (Google Cloud). Revolut, which runs on Google Cloud, lost card payments, transfers, ATM withdrawals and app login for hours across Europe (3.4 m customers in Ireland alone); Lloyds, HSBC and DPD users also reported problems. Google called it mitigated for most by ~14:00 UTC; Revolut still partial at ~17:00 GMT; normal by Oct 21. Same day, Treasury Committee chair Meg Hillier wrote to Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey and FCA chief Nikhil Rathi with nine questions (initial reply by 4 Nov).
- **Mechanism:** your "app bank" has no vault; it is software rented in someone else's data centre. One "zone" = one or a few buildings. Since 13 July 2026 the UK formally designates Google Cloud, AWS, Microsoft and Oracle as "critical third parties" to finance, precisely because one outage can hit many banks at once.
- **Twist:** your money was never at risk ("all our customers' money and data are safe"), but you couldn't touch it: your balance existed, your bank couldn't reach it. Whether your bank works depends on electricity in a building you've never heard of.
- **Real person:** weak. Revolut/Google statements are unattributed; Meg Hillier is quoted only via letter text ("The Bank of England, the PRA and the FCA all have a role in implementing the critical third party regime"). Would need a named customer quote (search local press).
- **Sources:** Irish Times https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/10/20/revolut-services-hit-by-outage-including-in-ireland/ (2026-10-20); UK Parliament Treasury Committee https://committees.parliament.uk/committee/158/treasury-committee/news/218364/google-cloud-outage-bank-of-england-and-fca-letter-published/ ; Computer Weekly https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366652388/MPs-ask-finance-regulators-how-they-will-deal-with-Google-Cloud-after-outage ; City AM https://www.cityam.com/revolut-hit-with-service-disruption-as-users-unable-to-make-payments/ ; Reuters (via Irish Times) for the Revolut-Google link.
- **Frame 0:** "One power cut froze millions of cards."
- **Risks:** Revolut never officially named the cause (Reuters source + timing); say "linked to." Durations differ by source. Google's post-incident report not yet out (could land mid-production and change details). UK/EU-centric for a US-voiced channel.

## 3. One drink a day vs two: the risk doesn't double, it multiplies by 40

- **News:** the federally commissioned Alcohol Intake and Health Study (Shield et al., Journal of Studies on Alcohol and Drugs 87(4):621) went viral again via ScienceDaily (2026-10-25), Forbes (2026-10-21), Nature World News (2026-10-26). Lifetime alcohol-caused death risk: ~1 in 1,000 above ~7 drinks/week (women >7.0, men >6.5), passes 1 in 100 at ~8.5/week, reaches ~1 in 25 at 14/week (two a day). No protective effect at any level.
- **Mechanism:** risk is a steep curve, not a straight line; the second daily drink costs far more than the first. Two a day was the old US guideline limit for men; the current 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines just say "limit alcoholic beverages," no number.
- **Twist:** the government commissioned this study for those guidelines, and the final guidelines dropped the numbers. Editorial: "Despite the study's adherence to its mandate, its findings were sidelined" (Robert M. Vincent).
- **Real person:** Kevin Shield (University of Toronto, lead author): "Even low levels of alcohol use come with health risks." / "We did not observe a significant protective effect of alcohol on health at any level of consumption."
- **Sources:** JSAD https://www.jsad.com/doi/10.15288/jsad.25-00435 ; ScienceDaily https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261024222030.htm (2026-10-25); STAT https://www.statnews.com/2025/01/14/alcohol-health-effects-new-federal-report-says-single-drink-daily-raises-risk/ (draft, 2025-01-14); Nature World News https://www.natureworldnews.com/articles/73646/20261026/two-drinks-day-may-carry-one-25-chance-dying-alcohol-over-lifetime-study-estimates.htm (2026-10-26).
- **Frame 0:** "Your second drink costs 40x more."
- **Risks:** FRESHNESS is the weak point: the draft was public Jan 2025, journal version earlier in 2026; this week is a viral recirculation, not new data. Population averages, wide confidence intervals; "40x" is approximate (1:1,000 to 1:25) and should be phrased as such. Guidelines angle can read partisan; keep strictly factual. Possible overlap with the science scout.

## 4. Microsoft just offered a quarter of its staff money to quit. Why not just lay them off?

- **News:** 2026-10-26: Microsoft offered a "Voluntary Exit Offer" to ~60,000 employees (~27 % of ~223,000), VP level (level 70) and below, in US, UK, Canada, India; Xbox, Windows, Copilot, Finance participating; data centre, sales, marketing excluded. Terms ≈ standard severance. Memo from Chief People Officer Amy Coleman: "The Voluntary Exit Offer is new for us. We'll learn from it." Earlier retirement buyout: >30 % of ~8,750 eligible took it; Microsoft expects lower uptake now. 4,800 cut in July.
- **Mechanism:** headline says "60,000 jobs," but this is an offer, not a cut; if uptake is like the last round, the real number is a fraction. A buyout lets a company shrink without mass-layoff optics; the classic risk is that the people most likely to accept are the ones who can easily get another job.
- **Twist:** Microsoft says AI is not driving the buyouts, while reports tie them to funding AI infrastructure spending.
- **Real person:** Amy Coleman (memo, quoted above).
- **Sources:** TechCrunch https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/ ; KUOW https://www.kuow.org/technology/2026-10-26/microsoft-offers-voluntary-buyouts-to-a-quarter-of-its-workforce ; Bloomberg https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/microsoft-to-offer-buyouts-to-60-000-workers-in-latest-cost-cut ; CNBC https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts.html ; NBC/Reuters https://www.nbcnews.com/business/corporations/microsoft-offers-buyouts-60000-employees-rcna605765 (all 2026-10-26).
- **Frame 0:** "Microsoft is paying 60,000 people to leave."
- **Risks:** uptake unknown, so the consequence is speculative; "best people leave" is general theory, not reported for Microsoft. Distant from an ordinary viewer's wallet unless tied to "your employer could do this next." Third AI-adjacent story in a row. Likely overlap with the jobs scout.
