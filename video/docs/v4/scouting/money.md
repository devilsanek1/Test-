## 1. Cocoa crashed. Your chocolate didn't.

**News.** AP (Dee-Ann Durbin), 2026-10-24: Halloween candy sales were **down 6.7 %** in the month to Oct 17 (NielsenIQ),
after an 11.7 % drop in mid-September. In the BLS CPI, candy and gum were **+7.7 %** a year in September (all items +3.7 %)
and **+44 % over five years** (all food at home +22.5 %). Cocoa futures set a record (above $12,000/t at the late-2024
peak; BLS monthly average $10,710/t in Jan 2025), fell to about $3,241/t in March 2026, and were around **$5,600/t on Oct 23**
(Yahoo Finance). That is still about half the peak.

**Mechanism (the twist).** Chocolate makers don't buy cocoa at today's price. Hershey's own 10-Q (Q2 2026, SEC) says: "We
generally hedge commodity price risks for 3- to 24-month periods." So today's bar was paid for with cocoa locked in
near the peak, up to two years ago. Shelf prices rise fast and fall slowly. In the meantime the bag shrinks: AP notes Aldi
sold a 135-piece Mars variety pack for $19.87, while five years ago a 250-piece pack cost nearly the same. Mondelez is
pushing bars filled with nougat, caramel and nuts, which use less chocolate (CEO Dirk Van de Put, Food Business News /
Baking Business, 2026). Cocoa has also rebounded since March (Iran war disrupting fertiliser in West Africa, plus El Niño).

**Thesis.** Everyone's talking about pricey Halloween candy, but cocoa already halved. Chocolate makers bought their cocoa
up to two years ahead, so you're eating 2025 prices in 2026 bars (fewer of them per bag), and that matters because it tells
you when prices may come down and why the bag got smaller first.

**Frame 0:** "Cocoa halved. Your chocolate didn't."

**Real person:** Chris Costagli, VP at NielsenIQ (AP, 2026-10-24), describing why shoppers are cutting back: "I can't
afford it this year because I've paid so much more for so many other things."

- Yahoo Finance, 2026-10-23, cocoa outlook: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/where-cocoa-prices-heading-2027-150045403.html

**Evidence and risks.** The hedging window comes from a primary SEC filing, so it's solid. Applying it to "today's bar"

vs monthly average), so say "about half its peak". The +15.5 % and +14 % chocolate figures aren't verified; use the BLS
candy +7.7 % and the Aldi pack comparison. Groundwork Collaborative's "+15.3 %" is from an advocacy group, so leave it out.
The story also expires after Oct 31; Hershey's and Mondelez's Q3 earnings (late Oct) could refresh it.

## 2. Europe's gas tanks: nobody was paid to fill them

**News.** Euronews (Piero Cingari), 2026-10-27: Dutch TTF gas was near **€96/MWh** on Oct 26 (briefly €97.64, the highest since
Dec 2022), against about €28 in January (**+234 %**). EU storage was **74.6 % full on Oct 24**, versus 82.9 % a year
earlier and 98.5 % in 2023. IEEFA says that's the lowest for the date since records began (2011). Wood Mackenzie
expects 17-20 % by the end of March. For a typical gas-heated home using about 10,000 kWh from November to March, this
means about **€600 extra, or about €715 with 20 % VAT**. Qatari LNG hasn't arrived since May (Hormuz). The EU bans
Russian LNG from January 2027.

**Mechanism (the twist).** Storage works like this: traders buy cheap summer gas, store it, and sell it in winter for more.
In 2026 that sum flipped. Summer gas cost **more** than winter gas (an "inverted" seasonal spread: Timera Energy, early
2026; ABN AMRO, summer 2026; Newsbase, late Aug 2026: summer at a €0.91-1.05/MWh premium to winter). So filling the tanks
lost money, and the tanks stayed emptier. The market itself told everyone not to prepare for winter.

**Thesis.** Everyone's talking about the war pushing up gas prices, but the real winter risk comes from the market itself:
for months, summer gas cost more than winter gas, so nobody was paid to fill Europe's tanks. That matters because a
gas-heated home could pay about €700 more this winter, and a cold snap could push it higher.

**Frame 0:** "Europe forgot to fill its gas tanks." (or "Your heating could cost €700 more")

**Real person:** Tom Marzec-Manser, director for Europe Gas & LNG at Wood Mackenzie (Euronews, 2026-10-27): "If it's a cold
winter – which is not the consensus because of El Niño – storage levels could be even lower." Or Ana Maria
Jaller-Makarewicz, IEEFA (same article): "Demand reduction remains the biggest lever within Europe's control." Practical tip
from her: lowering thermostats by one degree could cut energy bills by up to 10 %.

**Sources**
- Euronews, 2026-10-27: https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/27/european-natural-gas-prices-soar-what-it-means-for-your-winter-bills
- IEEFA, Oct 2026: https://ieefa.org/resources/eu-may-need-cut-winter-gas-demand-7-gas-storage-record-seasonal-low
- Timera Energy, inverted spreads: https://timera-energy.com/blog/inverted-seasonal-spreads-complicate-2026-outlook-for-storage-operators/
- ABN AMRO research, summer 2026: https://www.abnamro.com/research/en/our-research/gas-markets-find-relief-as-europe-races-to-refill-storage
- Newsbase, Aug 2026: https://newsbase.com/story/high-gas-prices-prevent-european-traders-from-filling-empty-tanks-464241
- Oxford Institute for Energy Studies, Jul 2026: https://www.oxfordenergy.org/wpcms/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/European-Storage-Refill-in-Summer-2026.pdf

**Evidence and risks.** The storage and price numbers are solid (GIE AGSI+ via Euronews/Bruegel). The inverted spread is
backed by several analysts, but they disagree on whether it was "inverted" or just "too flat to cover storage costs", so say
"barely or never paid". Euronews's €600-715 is an estimate of the wholesale effect. Fixed contracts and price caps delay it,
so say "could". The story is Europe-only, which suits the owner (DE/EU) but narrows a US audience. Don't say "shortage":
the EU's Gas Coordination Group saw no immediate supply risk (Sep 3).

## 3. The middle of the barrel: why diesel costs $2 more than petrol

**News.** EIA (released 2026-10-20, via FleetOwner 2026-10-21): diesel averaged **$6.276/gal**, up $2.66 on a year ago. Petrol
was **$4.284**, so the gap is about **$2** a gallon. AAA had diesel at about $6.32 on Oct 25. NBC (2026-10-13): since Feb 28,
diesel is up about 70 % and petrol about 45 %; the record is $6.53. Jet fuel hit about $4.75-4.90/gal, the highest single-day
average Argus has recorded and about double a year ago (NPR, 2026-10-23). Thanksgiving fares are up about 30 % and Christmas
fares more than 20 %, both 10-year highs (Hopper). Ryanair's Michael O'Leary warns European fares could rise 10-20 % next year.

**Mechanism (the twist).** Crude is one product, but each barrel is split into several fuels. Diesel, jet fuel and heating
oil come from the same "middle" cut of the barrel (middle distillates), and that is the part the world lost: Gulf
refined-product exports cut, Russian refineries hit by drones plus a Russian diesel export ban until Oct 31, China pausing
most fuel exports. So the middle of the barrel spiked far more than crude or petrol. You may never buy diesel, but
everything you buy arrived on it: trucks, trains, tractors, ships. Planes run on its twin. GasBuddy's Patrick De Haan:
"Truckers and farmers not going to see any relief anytime soon." The IEA and Goldman Sachs see it staying tight into 2027.

**Thesis.** Everyone's talking about petrol prices, but the real squeeze is diesel, which is $2 a gallon dearer because the
war took out the middle of the oil barrel. That matters because you pay it at the supermarket and on your holiday flight
even if you don't own a diesel car.

**Frame 0:** "You don't buy diesel. You pay for it."

**Real person:** Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy (NBC News, 2026-10-13): "$6/gal diesel may be here
to stay for now." For human texture: Rachel Stern of Frederick, MD (NPR, 2026-10-23), who is driving her kids instead of
flying because tickets are too expensive.

**Sources**
- FleetOwner (EIA data), 2026-10-21: https://www.fleetowner.com/emissions-efficiency/news/55413156/us-diesel-prices-climb-to-628-as-fleets-face-rising-fuel-costs-and-market-uncertainty
- NBC News, 2026-10-13: https://www.nbcnews.com/business/energy/diesel-futures-higher-prices-trump-moves-rcna603218
- NPR via OPB, 2026-10-23: https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/23/jet-fuel-prices-near-a-record-and-holiday-airfares-soar/
- CNBC, 2026-10-06, diesel "another squeeze" on consumers: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/diesel-prices-inflation.html
- Forbes, 2026-10-25: https://forbes.com/sites/eriksherman/2026/10/25/fuel-market-realities-will-hit-hardest-long-after-the-midterms

**Evidence and risks.** The price numbers are solid (EIA, AAA). The middle-distillate mechanism is standard refining
economics, but the specific supply-loss figures come partly from trader blogs, so cite the IEA and EIA directly before
scripting. The grocery pass-through is not measured: say "everything that travels on a truck" and don't put a figure on the
supermarket bill. The story is heavily politicised (the Russia diesel deal, the red-dye executive order, the midterms), so
leave the policy fights out. It's also mostly US; the Europe angle is weaker.

## 4. The fuel surcharge that rises when fuel doesn't

**News.** Reuters (Lisa Baertlein) via BNN Bloomberg, 2026-10-26: US retailers face record holiday delivery prices. Q4 ground
parcel rates are up 5.8 % and express up 13.8 % on a year ago (TD Cowen/AFS Freight Index). At $6.28 diesel, UPS's fuel
surcharge is **29.5 %** and FedEx's **29 %** of the base rate. USPS added its first-ever fuel surcharge (8 %) in April. Amazon
added 3.5 % to seller fees in April.

**Mechanism (the twist).** The surcharge isn't just fuel. Carriers keep rewriting the table that converts the weekly diesel
price into a percentage. AFS data via Reuters: in Q1 2021, diesel at $2.91 meant a 7.75 % surcharge. **At that same $2.91
today, it would be 23.75 %.** UPS added +1 point to every band in January and again in March 2026 (Sifted, Supply Chain
Dive). So a "fuel" fee has grown into a general price increase that stays even if fuel gets cheaper.

**Thesis.** Everyone blames the shipping-cost jump on diesel, but carriers rewrote their fuel-surcharge formula, so at 2021
fuel prices the fee would still be three times higher. That matters because almost a third of a parcel's delivery price is
now a "fuel" fee, and it ends up in what you pay for holiday orders and free-shipping thresholds.

**Frame 0:** "Same fuel price. Three times the fee."

**Real person:** Mingshu Bates, chief analytics officer at AFS Logistics (Reuters, 2026-10-26): "Fuel is front and center in
everybody's consideration and, again, no real good news there."

**Sources**
- Reuters via BNN Bloomberg, 2026-10-26: https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/international/2026/10/26/us-retailers-face-record-holiday-delivery-prices-from-surcharges-tied-to-costlier-diesel
- Supply Chain Dive, 2026: https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/ups-fuel-surcharge-table-increase-2026/814187/
- Sifted rate timeline: https://sifted.com/resources/2020-2026-fedex-ups-rate-increases-timeline/
- FedEx historical fuel surcharges (primary): https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/historical-fuel-surcharge.html

**Evidence and risks.** The 7.75 % vs 23.75 % comparison comes from AFS via Reuters and can be checked against the carriers'
published tables. It's a strong, sharp twist. But the consumer link is indirect: retailers pay the surcharge, and Reuters
doesn't say how much reaches shoppers. Say "lands in prices and shipping fees" carefully. It's B2B-flavoured, so it needs a
consumer frame (your parcel). UPS's and FedEx's earnings this week could add a fresh hook. Present the formula change as
fact, without implying wrongdoing; UPS says its pricing reflects premium service and investment.
