## 1. "To ban kids, they check everyone" (Türkiye, from Sunday 1 Nov; Greece, 1 Jan 2027)

- **News:** Türkiye's under-15 social media rules (Official Gazette, 10 Oct 2026) take effect **Sun 1 Nov 2026** for accounts
  created from that day: platforms must verify the age of **all new users** with an "age verification token" from the
  state e-Devlet portal (service run by the Cybersecurity Presidency). The token tells the platform only whether the
  user is 15+ (and 18+), no name or username. Existing accounts must be checked by **31 Mar 2027**. On **23 Oct 2026** the
  Presidency's Center for Combating Disinformation (DMM) warned of scam messages like "Your account will be closed" or
  "Verify your age immediately", and said to verify only via turkiye.gov.tr or the official app. Greece passed its
  under-15 ban late on **22 Oct 2026** (New Democracy alone in favour, PASOK abstained, five parties against), in force
  **1 Jan 2027**; when a platform can't confirm age it shows a QR code to scan with the state **Kids Wallet** app, which tells
  the platform only "15+: yes/no".
- **Big question:** Is the internet about to make everyone show ID?
- **So what:** You can't find the under-15s without checking everyone -> in Türkiye, every new account from Sunday goes through
  a government portal, adults included -> the design hides your name but adds a government step to signing up -> and the
  first side effect isn't censorship, it's scammers sending "verify your age now" links. For you: the next app sign-up may
  ask for a government-issued yes/no, and a new scam text is arriving with it.
- **Thesis:** Everyone is talking about banning kids from social media, but Türkiye's rules starting Sunday reveal that to stop
  kids you have to check everyone, through a government token that knows only one thing about you, which matters because
  signing up for an app now runs through the state, and scammers got there first.
- **Frame 0:** "From Sunday, adults need a token too."
- **Hooks:** (a) "From Sunday, a whole country needs a government token to open an Instagram account. Not just kids. Everyone."
  (b) "To keep kids off social media, Türkiye has to check every adult first." (c) "The new ID for the internet doesn't know your name. It knows one thing."
- **Twist:** The token is privacy-friendly by design (only "15+ yes/no"), and the first real-world problem isn't snooping,
  it's fake "verify your age" messages, which the government itself warned about before the law even started.
- **Real person:** Greek digital governance minister Dimitris Papastergiou, defending Kids Wallet: it tells a platform only
  whether a user has turned 15, "hands over no date of birth and monitors no pages or messages" (as reported, TechRadar /
  Greek Reporter). Türkiye's warning is institutional (DMM), not a named person.
- **Share reason:** practical and personal ("you'll need this if you sign up after Sunday"; "don't click this scam text"); a
  natural send to parents and friends in Türkiye/Greece/EU, where the EU is discussing similar rules.
- **Key image:** Scale: a city-wide queue of grown-ups at a tiny turnstile in front of a giant phone. Enclosure: a teen and a
  parent on a sofa holding up phones to the same small bouncer, while a fake bouncer slips a "VERIFY NOW" note under the door.
- **Evidence:** Good. Türkiye start date, token design, 31 Mar 2027 and scam warning: Türkiye Today (23 Oct), Kuwait Times,
  Caspian Post, Bianet. Greece vote: Greek Reporter, Engadget, TechRadar, Brussels Signal. Disputed: whether *existing* adult
  accounts must all verify by March or only suspected minors (sources differ); token technical spec not yet published.
- **Risks:** Politically sensitive (Türkiye's speech record, Greek opposition): stay factual, no villain. Say "every new account",
  not "every adult". Pick one country as the spine (Türkiye, because Sunday is the peg). Peg expires fast: post by ~2-3 Nov.

## 2. "43.9 million violations in a state of 2 million" (Meta, New Mexico, 21 Oct)

- **News:** 21 Oct 2026, Santa Fe District Judge Francis Mathew ordered Meta to pay New Mexico **$25B** under the Unfair
  Practices Act. A jury (Sep 2026) found 26 of 29 public statements misleading and counted **43.9 million** violations by
  multiplying by each statement's potential audience in the state (population 2.1M; ~1.4M Facebook users). Max at $5,000
  each: ~$219B; the judge cut it to $25B. Meta (CLO C.J. Mahoney): "We are confident we will prevail on appeal"; the case
  "seeks to impose a tax on speech"; New Mexico is "all but impossible to do business in". 22 Oct, Reuters-cited experts:
  Meta's best appeal argument is counting only people who actually saw a statement, each once. Money goes to education by
  state constitution; payment could take years.
- **New peg since v3:** none yet (no filed notice of appeal found). Still 6 days old.
- **Big question:** Can anyone actually make Big Tech pay for what it does with our data?
- **So what:** The size comes from the maths (people x promises), using an old consumer-fraud law other states also have ->
  an app's public promise gets a possible price per person in its audience -> the appeal will decide whether that maths survives.
  For you: privacy pop-ups and settings text become something companies can be billed for, per person.
- **Thesis:** Everyone is talking about the $25B, but the 43.9 million count reveals the real mechanism, every promise
  multiplied by everyone who could have heard it, which matters because that maths turns an app's promises to you into a
  per-person bill, if it survives Meta's appeal.
- **Frame 0:** "2 million people. 43.9 million violations."
- **Hooks:** (a) "About 2 million people live in New Mexico. A jury found Meta broke the law there almost 44 million times."
  (b) "The fine could legally have been $219 billion." (c) "Facebook's promises to you just got a price per person."
- **Twist:** The count is bigger than the population because it's people times promises, and nobody had to prove a single
  person saw them, which is exactly what Meta will attack on appeal.
- **Real person:** Meta CLO C.J. Mahoney: "We are confident we will prevail on appeal." (or NM AG Raúl Torrez).
- **Share reason:** jaw-drop maths ("how do you break the law 44 million times in a state of 2 million?").
- **Key image:** desert welcome sign "POP. 2,117,522" with a tally counter spinning past it; a kitchen grid multiplying.
- **Evidence:** Strong (AP/Reuters syndication, Bloomberg Tax, ABQ Journal, Source NM, PYMNTS 21 Oct).
- **Risks:** No fresh peg; consequence is indirect outside the US; never "43 million people misled"; always "ordered, under appeal".
  Don't mix with the March $375M NM child-safety verdict or the Aug 2026 multistate settlement (up to $17.1B, 52 AGs; NM did not join).

  for adults without cirrhosis, already suppressed, HBsAg <= 3,000 IU/mL. B-Well 1+2: functional cure 19% (233/1,220) vs 0%
