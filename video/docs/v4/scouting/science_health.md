## 1. The fish oil in your cupboard reached the brain, and did nothing

- **News:** Two-year randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, Keck Medicine of USC, published in *eBioMedicine* (2026; 129: 106316, DOI 10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106316); press wave 2026-10-20 to 10-22. 365 adults aged 55-80 at raised Alzheimer's risk (47 % carry APOE4), who rarely ate fish, took 2,000 mg DHA a day or placebo.
- **Twist:** The pills WORKED at the step everyone assumes is the hard one: DHA in the fluid around the brain rose 17 % on average. And still: no difference in memory/thinking tests, no slowing of hippocampus shrinkage, also none in APOE4 carriers. The omega-3 got in; the benefit didn't come.
- **Real person:** Dr Hussein Naji Yassine (director, USC Center for Personalized Brain Health): "our results do not support fish oil supplements as a preventive measure against Alzheimer's." Also: "Staying healthy throughout life remains the most powerful tool we have for reducing Alzheimer's risk, including regular exercise, quality sleep and a balanced diet."
- **So what:** Fish oil is one of the most-used supplements; many people (often parents/grandparents) buy it "for the brain". For that specific purpose, this is high-quality evidence it doesn't help, so that money can go elsewhere.
- **Thesis:** Everyone takes fish oil for their brain, but this new trial shows it actually reaches the brain and still doesn't protect memory, which matters because millions are paying monthly for a benefit that isn't there.
- **Frame 0:** "Fish oil reached the brain. Nothing changed."
- **Key image:** A tiny golden capsule riding down into a giant cosy brain-city; lights flicker on in the omega-3 pipes... and the memory library keeps quietly losing books.
- **Evidence:** Strong design (RCT, 2 yr, placebo, n=365). Limits: one population (older, low fish intake, at-risk); one omega-3 (DHA); doesn't speak to heart uses or to eating fish. Do not say "fish oil is useless".
- **Risks:** Over-generalising; supplement-industry pushback (some argue larger doses/earlier start). Keep it to "for brain/Alzheimer's prevention".
- **Sources:** ScienceDaily 2026-10-22 https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261020085427.htm ; Newswise (USC release) https://www.newswise.com/articles/fish-oil-supplements-may-not-prevent-alzheimer-s-related-decline ; NutritionInsight https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/omega-3-fish-oil-brain-health-claims.html ; Being Patient https://beingpatient.com/fish-oil-supplements-cognitive-decline-trial/

## 2. The "natural" sleep gummy that acts like THC, weeks before the US hemp crackdown

- **News:** University of Connecticut mouse study in *Drug and Alcohol Dependence* (Olivia Vanegas, lead; Steven Kinsey, senior), coverage 2026-10-24 to 10-26. Mice given CBN (the cannabinoid sold as a sleep gummy) twice daily for 3 days developed tolerance and showed withdrawal signs (head twitches, paw fluttering) when it stopped. Mice trained to recognise THC treated CBN as THC.
- **Twist:** CBN is sold in gas stations and online as the gentle, non-high sleep option, yet the brain reads it like THC. That matters right now: the 2025 federal law narrowing "hemp" (P.L. 119-37, Sec. 781) counts THC "and other cannabinoids with similar effects"; synthetic-cannabinoid part from 2026-11-12, total-THC / 0.4 mg-per-container cap from 2026-12-11 (reported delay; confirm). The law does not name CBN; how regulators treat it is open.
- **Real person:** Steven Kinsey (UConn): "Our findings suggest consumers should be aware repeated use may carry risks." Olivia Vanegas: marketing of minor cannabinoids is "largely running ahead of the science."
- **So what:** If you take a CBN gummy every night to sleep, your body may get used to it and you may feel off when you stop; and the gummy on the shelf may change status in weeks.
- **Thesis:** Everyone thinks CBN sleep gummies are the harmless, no-high option, but this new study shows the brain treats CBN like THC and gets used to it in days, which matters because millions take one nightly and US hemp rules are about to tighten.
- **Frame 0:** "Your sleep gummy may act like weed."
- **Key image:** A cosy bedroom at night, tiny person, one gummy on the nightstand; zoom into the brain where the gummy's key fits the exact same lock as a THC key.
- **Evidence:** Mouse study, short dosing, dose not compared to a gummy; no human dependence data. The law's effective dates have shifted (Nov 12 vs Dec 11) and CBN coverage is unclear: state as uncertain.
- **Risks:** Legal-date confusion; "mice not people" must be on screen; avoid implying gummies will definitely be banned.
- **Sources:** ScienceDaily 2026-10-24 https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261024221952.htm ; UConn Today 2026-10 https://today.uconn.edu/2026/10/common-herbal-sleep-aid-shows-signs-of-addictive-potential/ ; Nature World News 2026-10-26 https://www.natureworldnews.com/articles/73655/20261026/mice-developed-tolerance-popular-sleep-aid-cbn-within-three-days-federal-hemp-rules-tighten.htm ; Medical Daily (hemp law dates) https://www.medicaldaily.com/hemp-thc-gummies-drinks-federal-ban-december-11-478991 ; Saul Ewing https://www.saul.com/insights/alert/congress-enacts-hemp-thc-products-ban-what-new-federal-restrictions-mean-industry

## 3. 14,000 people injected a weight-loss drug that isn't approved, and the top complaint was hunger

- **News:** University of Pennsylvania study in *JAMA Network Open* (DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.38929; Sehgal, Guntuku, Ungar, Tronieri). Penn release 2026-10-15, ScienceDaily 2026-10-21. AI read ~150,000 Reddit posts from ~40,000 users; ~14,000 described taking gray-market retatrutide (Eli Lilly's triple-hormone drug, still in Phase 3, not approved).
- **Twist:** In trials, the common side effect is nausea. On Reddit, the most-reported symptom was increased appetite, the opposite of what the drug should do. One plausible reason the researchers name: "gray-market formulations vary", i.e. people may not be getting what the vial says.
- **Real person:** Jena Shaw Tronieri (Penn): "Some may be taking multiple experimental peptides, and we can't verify exactly what they're taking." Lyle Ungar: "We can't say that retatrutide is causing these symptoms."
- **So what:** Tens of thousands are buying a drug before approval from "research peptide" sellers to skip the price/wait of Wegovy/Zepbound; this is the first large look at what's happening to them, and you can't know what's in the vial.
- **Thesis:** Everyone's talking about the next miracle weight-loss jab, but this study of 14,000 people already injecting it from the gray market found their top complaint was hunger, which matters because you can't know what's in an unapproved vial.
- **Frame 0:** "14,000 people took a drug before approval."
- **Key image:** A dense city of tiny apartment windows, each with someone holding a little vial; a giant open laptop forum glows above them like a moon.
- **Evidence:** Self-reported, unverified, "hypothesis-generating" (authors). Causation not shown. Freshness: 12 days, at the edge. User counts vary between outlets (14,000 vs ~10,000).
- **Risks:** Must not frame as "retatrutide is dangerous". Uses AI as a tool (ok, not an AI story). Brand mention of Eli Lilly fine as context.
- **Sources:** MedicalXpress/Penn 2026-10-15 https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-ai-reddit-potential-side-effects.html ; ScienceDaily 2026-10-21 https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261020085018.htm ; EurekAlert https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1147934 ; Technology Networks https://www.technologynetworks.com/tn/news/researchers-turned-to-reddit-to-study-an-unapproved-glp-1-drugthe-findings-surprised-them-417472

## 4. Your coffee got 40 % more fattening, and sugar isn't why

- **News:** Tufts Friedman School study, *Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics*, online 2026-10-19 (DOI 10.1016/j.jand.2026.156908), release 2026-10-20. NHANES data, ~30,000 US adults, 2003-2018.
- **Twist:** Americans drink the same amount of coffee (~13 oz/day), but calories from coffee rose 43 -> 60 kcal/day (~40 %). Biggest source by 2018: non-dairy creamer (18.5 kcal) ahead of sugar (16) and milk (11); creamer grew about 2.4x. The "lighter" plant-y swap is the heavy one (saturated fat, calorie dense).
- **Real person:** Yongyi Pan (lead author, Tufts): non-dairy creamer "being added to coffee has now passed both sugar and dairy" as a source of energy from coffee.
- **So what:** Per-person averages include non-drinkers, so a daily drinker's real number is higher; the habit you never think about is the one that changed.
- **Thesis:** Everyone blames sugar for unhealthy coffee, but this new study shows creamer quietly overtook it, which matters because the swap people think is lighter is where the calories went.
- **Frame 0:** "Your coffee got 40% heavier. Not sugar."
- **Key image:** A giant mug as a cosy town square; tiny people pour in sugar cubes while a huge creamer bottle towers over the skyline like a water tower.
- **Evidence:** Solid dataset but ends 2018 (not current); self-reported recall; no health outcomes measured. Small absolute numbers (17 kcal).
- **Risks:** Low stakes; could feel like a "fun fact"; must not exaggerate health harm. Most shareable/light of the four.
- **Sources:** News-Medical 2026-10-24 https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261024/Non-dairy-creamer-drives-up-calorie-count-in-coffee-cups.aspx ; ScienceDaily 2026-10-22 https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261021231224.htm ; Newswise (Tufts) https://www.newswise.com/articles/americans-are-putting-more-additives-and-calories-in-their-coffee
