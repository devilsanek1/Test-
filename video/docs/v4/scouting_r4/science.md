## 1. Someone who looks unconscious may hear you: a bedside EEG let 1 in 3 "unresponsive" ICU patients steer a sound with their thoughts (Columbia, published 2026-10-22)

- **What happened, when, who.** Columbia University Irving Medical Center / NewYork-Presbyterian (lead: neurologist Jan Claassen). Paper in the journal *Brain*: "Clinically unresponsive patients with acute brain injury activate a brain-computer interface", DOI 10.1093/brain/awag210; online 2026-10-22 (first author Qi Shen, last author Jan Claassen); press release and coverage 2026-10-22/23. 41 patients who looked unresponsive after a severe brain injury (tested a few days after admission) wore the ordinary EEG cap already used in ICUs. Through headphones, a recorded voice asked them to raise or lower the pitch of a beeping tone by *trying* to open and close their hands; a bedside computer read the EEG in real time and moved the tone. After brief training, 13 of the 41 (32 %, "about 1 in 3") controlled the tone with thought alone (the paper also tested 8 healthy volunteers and 22 conscious ICU patients), including every patient already found (by a separate exam) to have "cognitive motor dissociation" (hidden consciousness).
- **What it does and doesn't mean.** It shows some people who seem to be in a coma are awake inside and can *act* through a non-invasive setup built on standard ICU EEG. It is NOT communication yet (tone control only, no yes/no, no feelings), 41 patients, one centre, a research tool with no date for routine use. Where AI comes in: the paper's abstract says hidden consciousness (CMD) "was detected by applying machine learning to EEG recorded while patients listened to motor commands", and the interface itself "analysed EEG in real time and modulated frequency and pitch of a tone" (the team's own program; the classifier is not named in the abstract). The same team's 2019 work used EEG and AI to find hidden consciousness in ~15 % of unresponsive patients (per the release). Say "a computer reads the brain waves", never "AI reads minds".
- **Sources.** Medical Xpress (Columbia release), 2026-10-22: https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-brain-interface-unresponsive-icu-patients.html ; EurekAlert (same release): https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1148573 ; News-Medical, 2026-10-23: https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261023/Brain-computer-interface-enables-unresponsive-ICU-patients-to-control-sounds.aspx (all three are the same Columbia release). Primary source checked: the paper, *Brain* advance article, online 2026-10-22: https://academic.oup.com/brain/advance-article/doi/10.1093/brain/awag210/8931697 (abstract: "13 clinically unresponsive patients (32%) achieved BCI control"). No mainstream outlet found in one search.
- **So-what for an ordinary person, and when.** Anyone whose relative is in intensive care after a brain injury, stroke or accident: "unresponsive" may not mean "not there", and the kit is already at the bedside (Claassen: "EEG is used in clinical practice every day in the ICU."). Not in hospitals yet; the team wants next to let patients signal pain, hunger, how they feel. No timeline.
- **Why it's hopeful.** It gives families a reason to keep talking to the person in the bed, and doctors a way to find who is still in there before decisions about care are made, with equipment hospitals already own.
- **Buzz.** Science-press wire pickup (Medical Xpress, EurekAlert, News-Medical); mainstream reach not confirmed yet. Covert consciousness is a long-running, very shareable theme ("locked in", "they could hear us").
- **Real person in their own words.** Jan Claassen (Columbia): "in principle, we could hopefully in the future give some of these patients a voice." / "Our brain-computer interface is only a first step" / "Patients with hidden consciousness or cognitive motor dissociation are unable to show any expression or communicate in any way". No patient or family member named (families gave anonymous feedback: they asked for a loved one's voice in the headphones).
- **Opening line (max 12 words).** "If someone you love was in a coma, could they hear you?"
- **The twist.** The proof isn't a word or a blink: it's a beep that goes up when they try to squeeze a hand they can't move. And the gear isn't a brain chip, it's the cap the ICU already uses.
- **One drawable key image.** A hospital bed, a still face, an EEG cap; above it a line of beeps (a little waveform) that climbs as a hand-shaped ghost closes, while a relative's hand holds the real, motionless one.
- **Evidence strength and doubts.** Peer-reviewed (*Brain*; abstract checked), but small (41), one centre, tone control only, success 13 of 41. Coverage so far is one press release plus the paper; buzz is the weak spot. The machine-learning link is real but sits in the detection step (keep it plain). Sensitive subject (families of comatose patients): never promise "they can all hear you".

## 2. A product manager in Spain, his AI helpers and NASA's open data: a possible new planet the official filter had set aside (Scientific American, 2026-10-14)

- **What happened, when, who.** Scientific American (Adam Kovac), 2026-10-14: Pavel Rabtsevich, a Spain-based product manager and space hobbyist, pointed several AI agents plus his own machine-learning filter at public data from NASA's planet-hunting satellite TESS (more than 126,000 stars). It steered him to star TIC 4206066, about 116 light-years away in Orion, which dims for about 2 hours every 3 days (he found 22 more dips in the archive). Best explanation: a rocky "super-Earth" about 1.4x Earth's width on a 3-day orbit (about 540 °C). TESS's own detection filter had flagged the signal in 2020 and rejected it for lack of evidence. NASA's TESS team will look again when the star comes back into view in November 2026; the star is now on TESS's high-cadence list.
- **What it does and doesn't mean.** A *candidate*, not a planet: unpublished (no preprint), other explanations not ruled out, and some 2018 dips coincide with TESS thruster firings. The pros were closing in too: per TESS team member Pablo Mercader Perez (MIT), the signal only became statistically detectable in January 2026, a deep-learning model from his team flagged it, and Michelle Kunimoto's LEO-Vetter picked it in early 2026, so it "would almost certainly" have reached the official list within months. So: never "an amateur beat NASA"; say "he got there first, with AI doing the grunt work, and NASA is checking in November." Where AI comes in: the AI agents "wrote the code, ran it and checked each other's work"; he set the question, plan and rules.
- **Sources.** Scientific American, 2026-10-14: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/amateur-astronomer-harnesses-ai-to-find-a-possible-hidden-exoplanet/ ; Adafruit blog, 2026-10-26 (pickup): https://blog.adafruit.com/2026/10/26/amateur-astronomer-harnesses-ai-to-find-a-possible-hidden-exoplanet ; primary check: NASA/IPAC ExoFOP target page for TIC 4206066, read 2026-10-28: https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/target.php?id=4206066 (0 TOIs, 0 community TOIs: not on the official candidate list yet, consistent with SciAm's "within the next few months").
- **So-what for an ordinary person, and when.** The data is free and the tools are now within reach of a hobbyist: anyone curious can do real discovery science from home. The verdict on this one: after TESS looks again in November 2026 (then months to confirm).
- **Why it's hopeful.** AI as a power tool for curious people, not a replacement: one person with a laptop added to humanity's map of the sky, and professionals welcomed it ("a great example of human-AI teamwork").
- **Buzz.** Scientific American feature; maker/tech blogs (Adafruit). Modest so far; the "amateur + AI finds planet" angle is very shareable, and November's re-check gives a natural follow-up.
- **Real person in their own words.** Pavel Rabtsevich: "Models agreeing with each other isn't evidence." / "the research itself was mine: the question, the plan, the rules, the computing." / "What I rely on is a signal anyone can measure, calculations anyone can rerun". Greg Laughlin (Yale): "a great example of human-AI teamwork" / "There's every reason to expect that this candidate super-Earth will pass further tests."
- **Opening line (max 12 words).** "Could you find a new planet from your kitchen table?"
- **The twist.** The AI didn't make the discovery for him: he made his AIs check each other, and he still says their agreement "isn't evidence". The signal NASA's filter had shelved in 2020 is now being re-checked by NASA itself.
- **One drawable key image.** (Staging, not a reported detail.) A man at a kitchen table with a laptop; on screen a flat line of starlight with a tiny regular dip; through the window, the same star in Orion with a little dot crossing it every 3 days, and a satellite turning its eye toward it.
- **Evidence strength and doubts.** One reputable outlet (SciAm, well reported, two named outside experts) plus the ExoFOP check (not yet listed); no paper; candidate only, could still be a false signal (thrusters, a background star). Pros were on its trail. Low stakes for daily life.

## 3. A cancer vaccine built from your own tumour: the first big (Phase 3) win for a personalised mRNA vaccine, in melanoma (full data at ESMO, Madrid, 2026-10-24)

- **What happened, when, who.** Moderna and Merck (MSD) with lead investigator Prof Georgina Long (Melanoma Institute Australia). Phase 3 trial INTerpath-001: 1,137 people whose high-risk melanoma (stage IIB-IV) had been cut out, at 157 sites worldwide; 757 got the personalised mRNA vaccine "intismeran" plus the standard immunotherapy (Keytruda), 380 got a placebo plus Keytruda. Headline already announced on 2026-08-19 (topline, no numbers); the numbers leaked on Thursday 2026-10-22, NBC published them 2026-10-23, and Long presented them on Saturday 2026-10-24 at ESMO 2026 (Presidential Symposium, Madrid); accepted by the New England Journal of Medicine. After two years (median follow-up 24.6 months), 82.2 % of the vaccine group were alive and cancer-free vs 70.0 % with Keytruda alone (42 % lower risk of the cancer returning or death; HR 0.58); cancer spreading to distant organs: 88.5 % vs 80.2 % free of it.
- **What it does and doesn't mean.** The first Phase 3 proof that a vaccine made for one person's own tumour helps: about 12 more people in every 100 still cancer-free at two years. It is NOT a cure, NOT a vaccine that prevents getting cancer (it is given after surgery, to stop the cancer coming back), and NOT approved anywhere: regulators come next and review "may take several years, depending on the country" (MIA). No survival benefit shown yet (too early); it takes a little over nine weeks to make each one, and 17 % of screened patients' tumour samples were too small to make one (NBC). Side effects: fever (52 % vs 8 %) and a sore arm (51 % vs 9.5 %), like a COVID jab; serious side effects 23.6 % vs 20.3 %. Melanoma only for now; trials running in lung, kidney and bladder cancer. Where AI comes in (only "next to it"): the tumour's DNA is read by sequencing and an automated computer pipeline picks up to 34 targets ("neoantigens") for the mRNA to encode; none of the sources in hand calls it AI, so say "a computer reads the tumour's DNA and picks the targets", never "AI-designed".
- **Sources.** NBC News (David Cox), 2026-10-23: https://www.nbcnews.com/health/cancer/personalized-mrna-vaccine-melanoma-cut-risk-cancers-return-late-stage-rcna605208 ; EMJ Reviews (numbers, side effects), 2026-10-24: https://www.emjreviews.com/oncology/news/new-personalised-mrna-therapy-improves-melanoma-outcomes-esmo-2026/ ; Melanoma Institute Australia release, 2026-10-23: https://melanoma.org.au/news/personalised-cancer-vaccine-cuts-risk-of-melanoma-recurrence/ ; Merck, 2026-10-13 (ESMO session, "up to 34 neoantigens"): https://www.merck.com/news/merck-showcases-first-time-pivotal-phase-3-results-from-oncology-pipeline-and-continued-innovative-potential-of-existing-portfolio-at-esmo-2026/ ; STAT, 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-27: https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/25/mrna-cancer-vaccine-merck-moderna-esmo-intismeran-next-applications/ , https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/27/cancer-vaccines-intismeran-autogene-esmo-results-delivery-access/ ; also Nature news (https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03417-0), Science news ("Milestone cancer vaccine success leaves hard questions unanswered"), US News 2026-10-28, ScienceAlert, Scimex expert reaction (not read in full: paywall or 403). "Automated algorithm": Wikipedia, Intismeran autogene.
- **So-what for an ordinary person, and when.** Melanoma is the deadly skin cancer; for people who have had a high-risk one removed, this could lower the chance it comes back. Not available yet (regulatory filings next; likely years, varies by country); the same "read your tumour, print a wanted poster" approach is being tested in lung, kidney and bladder cancer.
- **Why it's hopeful.** The idea people have waited decades for (a medicine written for one person's cancer) worked in a large, randomised, placebo-controlled trial, with COVID-style side effects, and the same mRNA "printer" can be pointed at other cancers.
- **Buzz.** The week's biggest health story: NBC, Nature, Science, STAT ("Cancer vaccines are finally here"), US News, ScienceAlert; Moderna's stock fell after the leak (NBC). Outside experts: "game changer" (Robert Vonderheide, Penn, AACR president-elect).
- **Real person in their own words.** Prof Georgina Long (led the trial; MIA): "This treatment is about mopping up the cancer cells we can't see." (NBC) / "Cancer can fool the immune system with a fake friendly handshake" / "We analyse a sample of each patient's tumour to read its unique neoantigen fingerprint" (MIA) / "absolutely superb" (as summarised by the search engine from the Nature/Science coverage; not read directly). No patient found in any source read (a named participant would need a dedicated search).
- **Opening line (max 12 words).** "What if your cancer could be turned into your vaccine?"
- **The twist.** The vaccine isn't against a germ: it's a "wanted poster" printed from your own tumour, so your immune system can hunt the cancer cells surgery couldn't see. And it's made with the same mRNA tech as the COVID jab.
- **One drawable key image.** A tumour cell with its own "fingerprint"; the fingerprint is copied onto a wanted poster; the poster goes up on a wall inside the body, and little guard cells (T cells) study it, then find and mop up the hidden cells; a 100-person grid: 70 lit vs 82 lit.
- **Evidence strength and doubts.** Strong: large randomised Phase 3, placebo-controlled, accepted by NEJM (one report: published next month), presented at ESMO, many reputable outlets. Doubts: a relative risk number (42 %) that is easy to oversell (use 82 vs 70 in 100); survival not shown yet; 2-year follow-up; cost and the 9-week custom build unknown at scale; outside expert (Shirin Bajaj, Mount Sinai): the gain over Keytruda is "relatively modest" (NBC); the AI link is thin (computational design, not "AI").
