- https://www.theopennotebook.com/2020/01/07/videogram-how-a-vox-video-explains-the-science-behind-the-first-photo-of-a-black-hole/
  (S: Vox's Joss Fong interviewed by The Open Notebook) - "something very fast-paced at the very top"; an abstract idea
  shown as the actual thing ("pretty abstract", so "I wanted to actually show people those telescopes"); camera moves
  "that weren't too crazy, to create some energy"; music changes every ~20 s, its starts and ends "are cues to
  viewers", never competing with the pitch of the voice; "charging forward and not realizing that the viewer is not
  with you".
- https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-english-online/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/
  (S: the transcript of Kurzgesagt's own video "How to Make a Kurzgesagt Video in 1200 Hours", YouTube uFk0mgljtns) -
  "When the script is ready, we begin sketching out the video. We try to think of visual metaphors, transitions between
  ideas, and decide which scenes need the most attention"; "find the right images to the words in the script to explain
  as clearly as possible, while also having interesting things to look at"; the music is "composed to the finished video
  ... to highlight the important parts"; sound effects "really add an additional layer".
- https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/ (T: Smith, T. J. 2012, "The Attentional Theory of Cinematic
  Continuity", Projections 6(1) 1-27, eye-tracking research; PDF https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/2/6679.pdf)
  - continuity holds when a cue moves the eye first ("off-screen sounds, ... onsets of motion, gaze cues, pointing
  gestures, departures from our field of view") and the expectation ("location on the screen, orientation and direction
  of action") is met "within a few frames"; match-action cuts are the hardest to notice ("a third of all match-action
  cuts were missed", Smith and Henderson 2008); a whip pan scatters the gaze ("uncoordinated gaze as viewers scurry to
  the right edge"); with nothing to follow, gaze returns to the screen centre (Mital et al. 2011); crossing the 180-degree
  line reverses the expected screen direction.
- https://nofilmschool.com/must-know-camera-movements (P: practitioner guide) - the push-in "is usually done at a pivotal
  moment"; the pull-out "can also reveal a space or information that serves the story"; tracking "can be motivated by
  the movement of the characters"; a boom "is not often motivated by movement, and should be used sparingly"; "If you
  can't justify the camera's movement, then don't do it."
- https://www.pulsecollege.com/animation-courses-storyboard/ (P: an animation school's storyboard course) - "too much
  camera movement is uncomfortable to watch so try to be sparing with them; use one to introduce the scene and after
  that each move should be designed to reveal another important aspect of the story"; "there's no point in cutting
  from one scene to another unless there's a reason".
- https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/grammar-of-the/9781351803519/ (T: Bowen, Grammar of the Shot, 4th ed., Routledge;
  its contents list the tip "Motivate Your Dolly-In and Dolly-Out Camera Moves"; text not read).
- NOT FOUND as an industry rule: "one camera move per shot" (searched; only an AI-video prompting guide states it,
  https://skills.sh/emily2040/seedance-2.0/seedance-camera). Pros state it as restraint plus motivation (above); ours is
  a house rule (DIRECTION rule 8: "no compound moves").
