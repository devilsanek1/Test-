- https://www.theopennotebook.com/2020/01/07/videogram-how-a-vox-video-explains-the-science-behind-the-first-photo-of-a-black-hole/
  (S: Vox's Joss Fong interviewed by The Open Notebook) - "something very fast-paced at the very top"; an abstract idea
  shown as the actual thing ("pretty abstract", so "I wanted to actually show people those telescopes"); camera moves
  "that weren't too crazy, to create some energy"; music changes every ~20 s, its starts and ends "are cues to
  viewers", never competing with the pitch of the voice; "charging forward and not realizing that the viewer is not
  with you".
- https://www.lingq.com/en/learn-english-online/courses/689474/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-120-4887128/
  (S: the transcript of Kurzgesagt's own video "How to Make a Kurzgesagt Video in 1200 Hours", YouTube uFk0mgljtns) -
  "When the script is ready, we begin sketching out the video. We try to think of visual metaphors, transitions between
  ideas, and decide which scenes need the most attention"; "find the right images to the words in the script to explain
  as clearly as possible, while also having interesting things to look at"; the music is "composed to the finished video
  ... to highlight the important parts"; sound effects "really add an additional layer".
- https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/ (T: Smith, T. J. 2012, "The Attentional Theory of Cinematic
  Continuity", Projections 6(1) 1-27, eye-tracking research; PDF https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/2/6679.pdf)
  - continuity holds when a cue moves the eye first ("off-screen sounds, ... onsets of motion, gaze cues, pointing
  gestures, departures from our field of view") and the expectation ("location on the screen, orientation and direction
  of action") is met "within a few frames"; match-action cuts are the hardest to notice ("a third of all match-action
  cuts were missed", Smith and Henderson 2008); a whip pan scatters the gaze ("uncoordinated gaze as viewers scurry to
  the right edge"); with nothing to follow, gaze returns to the screen centre (Mital et al. 2011); crossing the 180-degree
  line reverses the expected screen direction.
- https://nofilmschool.com/must-know-camera-movements (P: practitioner guide) - the push-in "is usually done at a pivotal
  moment"; the pull-out "can also reveal a space or information that serves the story"; tracking "can be motivated by
  the movement of the characters"; a boom "is not often motivated by movement, and should be used sparingly"; "If you
  can't justify the camera's movement, then don't do it."
- https://www.pulsecollege.com/animation-courses-storyboard/ (P: an animation school's storyboard course) - "too much
  camera movement is uncomfortable to watch so try to be sparing with them; use one to introduce the scene and after
  that each move should be designed to reveal another important aspect of the story"; "there's no point in cutting
  from one scene to another unless there's a reason".
- https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/grammar-of-the/9781351803519/ (T: Bowen, Grammar of the Shot, 4th ed., Routledge;
  its contents list the tip "Motivate Your Dolly-In and Dolly-Out Camera Moves"; text not read).
- https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-1-joss-fong (S: Joss Fong, a founding member of Vox's video team, interviewed)
  - "the perfect correspondence between what you're seeing in these animations and what you're hearing" helps viewers
  understand and remember; Vox chose to "lean into motion graphics and ... narration"; harder "when you're covering
  topics that are less literal and physical".
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132380/ (T: Brame 2016, "Effective Educational Videos", CBE-Life Sciences
  Education, a peer-reviewed review of Mayer's principles) - signaling: "the appearance of two or three key words ...,
  a change in color or contrast ..., or a symbol that draws attention to a region of a screen (e.g., an arrow)"; weeding:
  extra features "require the learner to judge whether he or she should be paying attention to them"; match modality:
  narrate a process while it is animated; "make these elements complementary rather than redundant".
- https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10192876/ (T: Trypke, Stebner and Wirth 2023, "Two types of redundancy in
  multimedia learning: a literature review", Frontiers in Psychology, 63 studies) - Mayer and Johnson 2008 showed
  "the benefits of embedding written keywords from a narrated text into visual displays"; Adesope and Nesbit 2012:
  "narrations accompanied by short written keywords fosters learning ... narrations accompanied by identical written
  text impaired learning"; text with narration helps most when it is short and for viewers with low prior knowledge.
- https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2020/03/19/seductive-details-inhibit-learning (T: the university's account of
  Sundar and Adesope 2020, Educational Psychology Review, a meta-analysis of 58 studies, 7,500+ learners) - learners with
  interesting but irrelevant details "performed lower"; worse when the detail sat next to relevant diagrams or stayed
  on screen; the advice: keep details that serve the objective.
- https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1131387/full (T: a 2023 Frontiers in Psychology article
  summarising Phillips 2000, "The Impact of Verbal Anchoring on Consumer Response to Image Ads", Journal of Advertising
  29(1) 15-24) - words that give a clue to a pictorial metaphor raise comprehension and liking; words that fully
  explain it raise comprehension but lower liking.
- https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices (S: TikTok's own ads help, updated June 2025) -
  "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness"; "Prioritize your hook
  in the first 6 seconds"; "Use captions or text overlays to provide context"; vertical 9:16, sound, the UI safe zone.
- https://www.facebook.com/business/news/insights/capturing-attention-feed-video-creative (S: Meta's Facebook IQ,
  2016-04-20, with Nielsen and Fors Marsh Group data) - "people spend, on average, 1.7 seconds with a piece of content
  on mobile compared to 2.5 seconds on desktop"; "it takes only 0.25 seconds of exposure for people to recall mobile
  feed content"; ads must "be understandable regardless of sound by using captions or other visual techniques".
  NOT on this page: the often-quoted "47 % of the value in the first 3 s" (only in secondary write-ups; unverified).
- https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5256470/ (T: Cutting 2016, "The evolution of pace in popular movies",
  Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications, 180 films 1930-2015) - contemporary films average 4.3 s a shot;
  "rapid changes in the visual field attract our eye movements and attention" and "motion also captures attention";
  at the end of the climax "increasing shot durations and decreasing motion".
- https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/pip-and-pop-non-spatial-auditory-signals-improve-spatial-visual-s/ (T: Van der
  Burg, Olivers, Bronkhorst and Theeuwes 2008, JEP: Human Perception and Performance) - a short sound synchronised with
  a visual change makes a hard-to-find object pop out, even though the sound says nothing about where it is.
- https://www.adamsebire.info/vertical-film-festival/9-16-tips-and-tricks/ (P: Adam Sebire, co-founding director of the
  Vertical Film Festival) - "Pans or rapid action moving across the frame can be discombobulating for audiences due
  to a reduced field of view"; "Look out for strong vertical features in the composition"; move a strong horizon
  above or below the middle; "keep important detail and especially text away from frame edges".
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_Ten_(film) (T: encyclopedia, on the Eameses' 1977 film) - one continuous
  camera from "an overhead view of a man and woman picnicking", out "at a rate of one power of ten per 10 seconds" and
  back in "at a rate of a power of ten per 2 seconds".
- NOT FOUND as an industry rule: "one camera move per shot" (searched; only an AI-video prompting guide states it,
  https://skills.sh/emily2040/seedance-2.0/seedance-camera). Pros state it as restraint plus motivation (above); ours is
  a house rule (DIRECTION rule 8: "no compound moves").
