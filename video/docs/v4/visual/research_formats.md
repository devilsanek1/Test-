## Working notes (read so far)

- A/V script: two columns, video and audio side by side; "Every row should represent one synchronized moment of the
  finished video"; "If narration drives your project, it's wise to write your audio first so you can match visuals to
  it later"; optional time-marker column at far left; a third column or comments for "timing, references, shot
  numbers"; "easy to scan, easy to annotate, and easy to sign off on" (Celtx, practitioner:
  https://blog.celtx.com/essential-av-script-template-for-better-video-scripts/).
- StudioBinder puts audio LEFT, visuals right (Celtx: video left): the side is house style. Abbreviations V.O., O.C.,
  SFX, VFX, MONTAGE; time per row from word counts or a timer; comments pinned to lines; a status colour per row
  (green approved, yellow in review) (practitioner: https://studiobinder.com/blog/av-script-template).
- Three-column scripts for short-form (commercials, PSAs, web content): time cues left (back-timed), video cues centre
  (camera framing, graphics, full screens, lower thirds), audio right (talent lines in caps, music, SFX stingers)
  (guide, open textbook: https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/City_College_of_San_Francisco/Video_Production_Handbook_I/03%3A_Scripting_and_Formats/3.03%3A_Multi-Column_Scripts).
- Documentary paper edit in two columns (Peter Thompson, director, 1989 template): visuals in caps on the left ("CU
  BOUNDARY OF ...", "PHOTO: ..."), narration on the right; "break up your speeches into a few sentences per cell"
  (practitioner/maker: https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3docedit_scriptmpl/docs2col.doc).
- Documentary radio edit first: "the editor tells the story by laying out all the audio first", then rhythm, music,
  SFX, B-roll, graphics; review with "timecode-specific comments" (practitioner: https://blog.frame.io/2023/10/16/premiere-pro-text-based-editing/).
- TV animation, script-driven vs board-driven; the in-between "scriptline": "story beats with some degree of specific
  jokes, dialogue, and shot suggestions sprinkled in"; "my job as a writer was to make the storyboard artists' job
  easier" (Spencer Rothbell, Clarence writer/story editor, practitioner: https://www.srothbell.com/blog/2020/6/24/advice-for-animation-writers).
- Foster's (Craig McCracken's production blog, via Animation Obsessive): index cards per act on a wall, "shape, sculpt
  and change these cards over and over till the gags, plot points and character motivations all fit and flow
  together"; script notes ask "are the emotional beats there? Is the story being told not just through dialog but
  through the visuals as well? Does the overall pacing feel right"; 1-3 revisions; "we record before storyboarding so
  the board artists can work off the actors' performances" (studio: https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/writing-fosters-home-for-imaginary).
- Coraline (Laika): voices recorded, then "story reels" of the boards "for timing of each shot ... they can't shoot
  'coverage' on a stop-motion animated film. It has to be right, to be planned and edited, before it begins" (maker,
  Neil Gaiman reporting Henry Selick: https://journal.neilgaiman.com/2006/12/on-leaks-and-buttons.html).
- Laika: the head of story manages "the flow of boards to editorial" (studio tooling account: https://www.foundry.com/news-awards/laika-picks-flix).
- Leica reel = story reel = animatic, made after the voices are recorded, boards arranged with the recorded track
  (weak: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leica_reel).
- Exposure sheet (x-sheet, dope sheet): one line per frame; a far-left column for the animator's notes on the action,
  a dialogue column split into phonetic parts on their frames, levels, background, a camera column (pans, trucks,
  field size); header with sequence, scene and scene name; the editor's bar sheets carry the sound breakdown (weak:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposure_sheet).
- Beat boards: a few key images, the story's turning points, "the concept art before the concept art", from
  thumbnails (practitioner: https://www.thegnomonworkshop.com/tutorials/creating-beatboards-for-pre-production;
  https://80.lv/articles/learning-to-create-beatbords-for-pre-production-in-226-minutes).
- Story artist's homework: "go through your script, identify what each line is trying to accomplish, and scribble
  that on the page"; Eric Elrod (Skydance head of story): every idea "competes for the small amount of running time";
  Michael Yates (Pixar) on a sequence with no point of view: "omnipresent - it feels like we're watching a theater
  play" (practitioner: https://storyfieldnotes.substack.com/p/the-wrong-way-to-make-a-storyboard).
- Prior lanes already cover boards, reels and AE timing (`docs/look/research/storyboard_*.md`, `animatic_*.md`); this
  lane covers the WRITTEN step before them.
- AHRQ shooting script (US agency guide, 2024): the script lets "everyone ... agree on exactly how the video should
  look and sound before any images are recorded"; for animation a 3rd column of "instructions for the editor of your
  animation, such as 'boxes in center will spin'"; "enough detail so that a script reviewer (and your director) can
  understand the planned visuals. If you want someone to move a certain way, say how. If someone exits the frame, say
  so"; "Left/right directions are written from the camera's point of view"; the audio side holds ONLY what is heard;
  "create many more boxes with one visual direction in each"; a header with title, version date and time (guide:
  https://ahrq.gov/sites/default/files/wysiwyg/research/publications/pubcomguide/Shooting-Script-How-to.pdf).
- Studio Pigeon's client process (explainer studio): the script is two columns, "VO Text | Picture Description", "the
  picture description of the best understanding of what will happen in parallel to the voice over"; then a black and
  white storyboard where "the movement of the camera and composition of the frames is planned"; key illustration
  (style) is a separate step (studio: https://studiopigeon.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Pigeon_Production_Process_2022.pdf).
- Boords explainer template: Scene | Audio/Voiceover | Visual, a word count and running time per scene at 130 WPM;
  "Describe what should appear on screen in clear, actionable terms - 'Character walks from left, stops at laptop' -
  not vague directions like 'show the product'. Include notes on transitions, text overlays, and timing"; "When your
  script is approved, create an explainer video storyboard" (practitioner: https://boords.com/video-script-template/explainer);
  script -> storyboard is one frame per scene with Label, Action, Dialogue, Sound fields (https://boords.com/docs/script-to-storyboard).
- Toon Boom Storyboard Pro default caption fields per panel: Dialogue, Action Notes, Slugging ("notes regarding the
  timing of dialogues and actions ... instructions on how to create the animatic"), Notes (scene heading, characters
  and props, ambient sounds, background and foreground actions, continuity) (guide/manual:
  https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-24/storyboard/caption/about-default-panel-caption.html).
- Anime e-konte: "Modern storyboards usually contain 5 columns": shot number, picture, written description, dialogue,
  timing; "storyboards are, first and foremost, instruments of communication" (practitioner/scholar:
  https://animetudes.com/2023/04/13/atom-storyboards/).
- Shot list core: Scene Number, Shot Number, Shot Description, Camera Movement, Shot Size and Type; a move inside a
  take written as its states ("low-med-high"); movement vocabulary Static, Pan, Tilt, Pedestal, Dolly, Truck, Arc,
  Crane, Zoom (practitioner: https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/shot-list-template/).
- Shooting script: on approval scenes get numbers, each shot inside a scene is numbered; pages locked, inserts as 45A,
  cuts kept as "OMITTED" (weak: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_script); 12, 12A, 12B; asterisks mark changed
  lines; the title page lists each revision with its date (practitioner: https://filmdaft.com/script-revision-colors-explained-blue-pages-a-pages-and-revision-marks/).
- Découpage (French film): "la division de chaque séquence du scénario en plusieurs plans ... valeur de plan, angle de
  prise de vue, mouvement éventuel. Pour certains films, un Story-board complète ou remplace le découpage" (guide:
  https://cafedesimages.fr/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/lexique-du-montage.pdf); "document écrit ... donnant toutes les
  indications techniques nécessaires au tournage" (guide: https://www.cinemaparlant.com/fichesactivites/ft_glossaire.pdf).
- Pixar, Derek Thompson (WALL-E story artist): reels let them "step through and feed the images to the director one by
  one" (a wall of boards makes you "get ahead of yourself"); "you've got to be able to sit as an audience member, with
  a clear objective and a blank slate, and understand what's going on" (studio: https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/exclusive-the-storyboards-of-walle/).
- Pixar, Christian Roman: story artists "study the script and map movement and camera angles"; "Every angle, every
  moment, how the lighting works, the things in the background - it isn't just random"; thumbnails first; the pitch,
  "acting out the scenes complete with dialogue"; notes, "make another version, and pitch it again"; then a reel "with
  rough music and voices" goes to layout (studio: https://bu.edu/bostonia/summer14/pixar/).
- Pixar, Josh Cooley: "the script is really a jumping off point"; Brad Bird's brief: "have the mouse fall in and almost
  get killed. Figure it out."; "storyboarding is story re-boarding" (32 redos of one scene) (studio talk, reported:
  https://siliconprairienews.com/?p=1324).
- Pixar Braintrust: "should have no authority. Our group could not tell the director what to do"; "You don't have to
  listen to anyone's notes, not even ours" (studio, Catmull reported: https://review.firstround.com/Spark-Creativity-with-These-Tips-from-Pixars-President).
- DreamWorks pitch: artists "perform to show how the rhythm and visuals will fit in the completed film" (museum with
  the studio's material: https://www.acmi.net.au/works/100626--shrek-storyboard-pitch/).
- TV board artist: "Each frame should represent a single step in the story ... draw the character looking, draw what
  they see, and then draw their reaction"; "Pauses between lines are often more important than the dialogue itself";
  thumbnails, pick the one "that best communicates the story point"; rough reviewed by the director; keep screen
  direction (practitioner, Annie winner: https://www.creativebloq.com/advice/storyboard-tips).
- Bar sheets: "Shuffle Off To Buffalo" (1933) "was planned down to the frame by two men - a director and a musician -
  before a single animation drawing had been done" (archive: https://animationresources.org/technique-musical-timing-rediscovered);
  the x-sheet "breaks down the action, dialogue, and sound frame-by-frame" (weak: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation).
- Giant Ant: "Once we go through a gate - like the style frame phase - the intention is to never go back through that
  gate. So we stack our process with several phases where the client can see the work in progress and make approvals"
  (studio, Communication Arts: https://commarts.com/features/giant-ant).
- Kurzgesagt: "deliberating over the best visual metaphors and ideas for each scene in the sketching phase"; the
  voiceover "provides the timing for the animation team" (studio: https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/).
- TED-Ed: after script revisions "animator Avi Ofer took over, creating a storyboard to show how he planned to bring
  the lesson to life with images. I recorded the narration, and once it was approved, Avi finished" (maker:
  https://katemessner.com/the-making-of-a-ted-ed-video/); Buzzco worked with the educator "to ascertain the accuracy of
  certain images and go further explaining the lesson than just illustrating his words" (studio: https://buzzco.nyc/sex-determination/).
- Vox, Joss Fong: "the perfect correspondence between what you're seeing ... and what you're hearing tends to boost
  comprehension and retention"; scenes that "walk through some data in a very physical way" (maker:
  https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-1-joss-fong). Vox's internal format: no first-hand source found (a teacher's
  two-column advice only: https://jea.org/digital-media/video-explainers-engage-readers/).
- Storyboards lay out "the sequence of events"; style frames carry "tone, mood, color palette, and even the texturing"
  (practitioner: https://boords.com/blog/what-are-style-frames-definition-and-examples).
- "Visual script" is not one fixed industry term: the same step is the A/V script's video column (corporate,
  explainer), the shooting script and shot list (live action), the découpage (French film), the scriptline (TV
  animation), the e-konte's written columns (anime), the story artist's thumbnails (features).
