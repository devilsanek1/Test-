# Video 4 visual concepts: the story-and-attention judge (2026-10-29)

**Status: DONE (2026-10-29).** This file judges the three concepts of workflow `v4-visual-script` (`BRIEF.md`) as the
viewer would meet them: a regular person scrolling on a phone, with the sound on or off. The three are A, the heat's
journey; B, the designed view; and C, from your chat to a home. Inputs:
- `concepts/A.md`, `B.md`, `C.md`;
- the take (`video/public/v4/voice_words.json`: 42.24 s, the first word at 0.13 s). Every time here is a word's start
  in it;
- the playbook's "Hook and pacing" and "Storyboard (G3) and flow" sections, plus the owner's words they point to
  ("Audience and goal", "Visual style");
- `research_craft.md` (C1-C12);
- `METHOD.md`, `THROUGHLINE.md`, and the line jobs in `script/script_g1.md`.

Scores run 1-10, and 8 means "ready for G3 with small fixes". Per `METHOD.md` step 2, where this judge and the craft
judge disagree, the story verdict leads.

## Ranking

1. **C (51).** It wins the two moments where a viewer decides: the "wait, what?" at 4-6 s, and the payoff. The lead
   is robust: move any one of its scores by a point and C still wins.
2. **A (48).** It has the cleanest flow: the heat leads every join, in one screen direction, along one road.
3. **B (47).** It has the richest pictures, and most of the grafts come from it.

A and B are a near tie. A tells the story better from shot to shot; B's single pictures are better.

## Why C wins

1. **It shows line 2's job on screen, inside the first 6 s.**
   - Every cold panel first pictured AI as living in the phone, or as a smart thermostat, at "AI to heat"
     (`script_g1.md`, line 2's job and the "actually" connector).
   - C shows the viewer that picture, their own phone, and corrects it in one move as the voice says "AI actually
     runs on giant computers" (4.28-5.94 s). The reply's typing dots become the three lights of a giant computer.
   - A and B push in on the machine instead: informative, but no surprise.
   - C's version is the opening's second jolt, and it lands where the playbook says to polish most ("the first 2 s
     and 5 s"). TikTok also says to "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds" (S,
     https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices).
   - The match from dots to lights is not a metaphor needing explanation: the voice names it in that same second.
     Words that cue a picture metaphor raise comprehension (Phillips 2000, via a Frontiers summary, T,
     https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1131387/full). After the match, everything is the real
     machine.
2. **It earns the core line.** The core line drives frame 0, the hook, the cover and the payoff (playbook, Hook and
   pacing).
   - In A and B, "your chatbot" arrives cold at 38.86 s, on a phone first seen in the last 3.8 s. It is the word all
     three cold viewers stopped on ("My chatbot? I'm in Ohio.", `panel_d3/`).
   - In C, the viewer saw their chat's answer come from that machine at 5.68 s. So at 38.86 s the reply that pops
     under the warming window is a callback, and the core line becomes one picture.
   - The only doubt left is "is MY chatbot in Helsinki?". That is the comment the orchestrator kept "your" for, and
     it gives the panel's own share line, "look, your chatbot is heating Finland".
3. **Its shape is one a viewer can feel.** The film goes in through your chat, out to the planet, down to one home,
   and back to your chat. "You" are at both ends: the main arc the playbook wants "visible across the whole film".
   - Vox's model film uses the viewer's own wrist as its "you" (our measurement, M,
     https://archive.org/details/youtube_-_UCLXo7UDZvByw2ixzpQCufnA_2016).
4. **It keeps what the other two do well:**
   - the same strong frame 0, in the voice's order: the computer draws on at "AI" (1.50), the window lights on
     "heat" (2.14), the wave of windows on "seventy thousand homes" (2.34-3.92);
   - the same scale shot and turn;
   - the literal mechanism;
   - 0 plain cuts;
   - the thread on screen ~98 % of the time;
   - one word in the picture (2027).
5. **Its weak spots, against the others, are small, and the grafts below close them without a new place or a new
   mover.** Against A's flow, the chat takes the lead from the heat for ~1.5 s. Against B's pictures, the steam, the
   rise and the dip are plainer.

## Grafts onto C (best first)

1. **From B: the computer opens on "how" (L9).**
   - On "how" (28.70), as the camera drops, the giant computer's side opens like a cutaway. Inside are its stacked
     machines and their pipes, empty and still. On "take" (31.14) the water runs in through them, past the three
     lights.
   - Why: L8-L9 is the film's one dip. At the plan line the panels said "A beat slow" and "I dipped a little here"
     (`script_g1.md`). A machine opening on the question is the freshest event any concept puts there. It also shows
     the mechanism inside the machine, not beside it.
   - Keep it clear of the no-pause join at 29.56.
2. **From B: the snow melts on the hot computer (L3).**
   - Snowflakes fall through the plan's missing roof. From "Those" (8.76) they melt as they land on the computer's
     top. On "really" (9.74) the melting turns to steam, and on "hot" (10.20) heat pours off as steam.
   - Why: it is the everyday sign of "hot", readable with the sound off at phone size. C's roofless plan already has
     the snow falling on it.
3. **From A and B: one meaning for steam.**
   - Steam appears only where heat is let go: first on "really hot" (L3), then the kettles, the rise and the curve.
     On "WANTS" (24.04) the steam is caught into the pipe, and from then on the heat is water in a pipe.
   - Drop C's curl of steam on frame 0's computer, and the one over the hot water at L10.
   - Why: with the sound off, steam means "thrown away" and the pipe means "kept", so "WANTS" reads as the one
     reversal. An unexplained signal makes viewers "judge whether he or she should be paying attention" to it (Brame
     2016, T, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132380/).
4. **From A: the pipe is frame 0's first stroke, and un-drawing it is the loop.**
   - The pen's first and boldest event is the pipe itself: one long stroke from the far left, where the computer will
     draw on at "AI", to the radiator under the one window. The block and the window follow right behind. The water
     then runs the same way from ~0.40.
   - At the end, while your hands leave the frame, the pipe un-draws from the window back to the source, so the next
     loop's first stroke picks up the gesture.
   - Why: one moving pen tip crossing the frame is the most visible opening event. The owner on v3's opening: "the
     initial drawing effect is barely visible". The viewer also learns the path before watching the heat travel it.
5. **From A and B: the rise (L6).**
   - As the camera rises with the steam, our own machine's plume spreads and thins into the sky (A). The heat we saw
     being made is the heat thrown away.
   - Helsinki's spot, where the mug was, holds the subject axis the whole way out, and frame 0's view passes through
     the frame and shrinks onto its coast (B). The viewer sees the map they learned, from above.
   - On "data centers" (19.94-20.38), boxes pop on across the land, more and more (B), before the nearest box's fans
     turn on "big fans" (20.94-21.18).
   - Why: the eye has one anchor through the big move. With no cue, the gaze falls back to the centre (Smith 2012,
     citing Mital et al. 2011, T, https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/). The push-through on "But"
     then goes where the eye already is, and "Normally" escalates from one box to many.
6. **From B and A: the plan reads as a plan (L8).**
   - On "plan" (26.18), the walls draw over once more: the drawing of a building that isn't there yet (B).
   - On "to use it" (26.52-27.00), the plan's own stretch of pipe, marked out the same way, leaves toward the city's
     pipe (A).
   - Why: C fills its plan with a row of running machines from ~7 s, so of the three it gives the strongest "it's
     already running" impression. The fact lock wants the plant read as a plan (FL 6.7).
7. **From B: two touches at the end.**
   - Pip is back on its frame-0 balcony in the end frame, for "find Pip" on the rewatch.
   - The un-build takes your hands first, as B's does, instead of a separate move lowering the phone. That frees time
     in the 0.92 s tail (41.32-42.24).

**Not grafted:**
- A's push-through into the machine at L1 -> L2: C's dive replaces it.
- B's extreme wide at frame 0: C's wide reads bigger on a phone.
- B's planned hall at L10: C's row already is "its computers".

**One G3 thumbnail variant (B's own fallback):** if the locked opening reads calm at 1x on a phone, start a touch
closer on the one window. Pull back as the wave overtakes the frame on "seventy thousand homes" (2.34-3.92): first the
one home you expected, then the reality. The framing must ease back to frame 0's by the tilt at ~4.0, and the
un-build must push back in so the loop still meets.

## C's risks to check in the cold read and at G3

- **Could the phone read as an ad?** Your phone is on screen at ~4.0-5.3 s and again at the end. It must read as your
  everyday chat, never a product shot: a drawn phone in gloved hands, no words, no logo, no app.
  - v3's shopping screen read as an ad (playbook, Audience and goal).
  - The payoff's "Your [product] could [benefit]" cadence already made one panel hear "the end of an advert"
    (`script_g1.md`).
  - So the cold panel asks "does this look like an ad?" across the first 6 s and the last 4 s, not only of frame 0.
- **Does the dive read with the sound off?** "Where does the chatbot's answer come from?" must come back as "a giant
  computer". If it reads as a trick, use C's own fallback: hold ~0.2 s on the screen of three dots, and let the hum
  take over from the typing tick.
- **Does the window's state read?**
  - The sequence: the window is lit in the promise (2.14 to ~5.2), dark when we come home at L7, lit on "homes"
    (37.48), and warmer on "heat an apartment" (39.98).
  - The cold read must take the dark window as "not yet", never as a mistake.
  - Fallback: B's continuous state, where the window stays lit throughout and the radiator and window warm in steps at
    L11 and L12.
- **Can L2's four pictures be read in 4.5 s?** Phone, dots, the computer close, its row: each must hold at least
  0.6 s at 1x on a phone.
- **The "you" appears twice (L2 and L12).** This is a named departure from the card, which has the "you" at L12 only.
  It is the owner's call, so if the writer keeps it, it goes in the visual script as an open question.

## The reasoning, criterion by criterion

### 1. The first 3 s

All three keep the card's opening:
- the street drawn on from frame 0;
- the warm water's run, with its ding at ~0.4 s;
- the window lighting on "heat" (2.14);
- the wave of windows across "seventy thousand homes" (2.34-3.92);
- the payoff in the captions by ~2.3 s.

**A 8.**
- Its first event is the boldest: the road drawn as one long stroke across the frame, before the world appears.
- The cause comes before the effect: the water leaves the machine at ~0.5 s.
- The wave runs "on past every edge".
- But the machine is small from frame 0 and only puffs on "AI" (1.50). So of the three, A makes "AI = this machine"
  weakest; A flags this itself.

**B 7.**
- It has the tightest match to the words: the far city lands on "Helsinki" (0.64) and the computer draws on at "AI"
  (1.50).
- Its wave is the biggest, since the far city sits on its own horizon.
- But it is an extreme wide, with the most to read in 0.6 s, so every opening effect is smaller on a phone ("a subtle
  effect counts as none"). B names the risk of a calm read itself.

**C 8.**
- It is B's build at a wide size, with a slow float. The computer draws on at "AI", and every key word gets its own
  event.
- It shares B's small oddity: the warm water runs from ~0.40, before its source draws on at 1.50.
- Its second jolt (4.3-5.7 s) falls outside this criterion; it counts under criteria 4 and 5.

### 2. Every line literal, sound off

**A 8.**
- Every line shows its real thing.
- The rise is literally the heat leaving.
- "to use it" gets the plan's own pipe.
- Weaker spots:
  - "AI" at frame 0.
  - "Those computers" and "just one of these" fall over a machine that was always alone.

**B 9.** It matches pictures to words most closely:
- the computer appears on "AI";
- the walls rise on "buildings";
- the snow melts into steam on "really hot";
- the boxes appear on "data centers";
- the machine opens on "how";
- rows of planned machines appear on "big new data center".

**C 9.**
- It is the most precise on plural and singular:
  - your chat is corrected on "actually";
  - the row lights up on "Those computers";
  - ours blinks alone on "just one";
  - windows light along the pipe on "people's homes".
- Weaker spots:
  - The dive is a match that the voice must anchor. It does, in the same second.
  - The window is dark at L7, with no word that explains it.

### 3. One subject at a time

**A 8.**
- One mover, the heat, leads every shot.
- The frames stay simple: a street, a machine, a curve, a cutaway.

**B 7.**
- Its home frame is the fullest picture in any concept: the cut-open street, the plan, the harbour, the far city.
- It is shown three times: at the start, mid-move (~19.9), and at the end with the whole path plus your hand, your
  phone and the bubble.
- L10 adds a hall of planned machines behind the subject.
- The build and the voice's order hold the eye, but it is the most to read at phone size.

**C 8.**
- One subject per beat, handed on in the voice's order: the city, your phone, the dots, the computer, the row, ours
  alone.
- Things to watch:
  - L2's phone, with the lit window still at the top of the frame;
  - L3's whole row blinking at once.

### 4. A flowing story

**A 9.**
- The heat leads every join:
  - it climbs the road (L1);
  - its plume carries the rise (L6);
  - "the only spot with no plume" cues the dive (L7);
  - the hot water leads the follow (L11);
  - the road re-draws for the loop.
- The flow keeps one screen direction throughout.
- The arc is visible from frame 0. This is the overview-and-ground toggle that Vox's Sam Ellis describes (S,
  https://www.storybench.org/?p=14818).

**B 8.**
- Every join has a cue just before it: the lights blink, the snow vanishes, the whoosh, the hush.
- Its pull-back from the mug through frame 0's view to the planet is the best single move in the three concepts.
- But it explores a picture more than it follows a mover. Nine of its twelve joins are pushes into or pulls out of
  one poster, six of them pushes in. That is the slideshow risk B names itself.

**C 8.**
- Its round trip has the clearest shape.
- L2's dive through the screen is the most energetic join in the three.
- But two things break the flow a little:
  - the heat hands the lead to your chat for ~1.5 s (L1 -> L2, while the thread is down to the lit window);
  - the window's state jumps at L7.

### 5. A memorable picture

**A 7.**
- Its own moments are clear: the road drawn first, the heat riding into the sky, the steam turning into the pipe. But
  none of them is the thing people send.
- Its best images are the shared ones: forty kettles with a mug on top, and the curve.

**B 8.**
- A city cut open like a page from a cross-section book.
- "From a mug of tea to the whole planet".
- The computer opening up.
- An end frame that explains the whole path in one picture: the screenshot people keep.

**C 9.**
- Your chat's typing dots turning into a giant computer's lights is new and everyday, and it changes how you see the
  dots next time.
- The reply popping under a warming window is the core line in one image, and it is the panel's own share line.

### 6. The ending and the loop

**A 8.**
- The road un-draws from the window back to the machine, and the pen draws it again: the most elegant loop gesture.
- But your phone is first seen only in the last 3.8 s.

**B 8.**
- The end frame IS frame 0's view, now readable end to end: the best reward for a rewatch. Pip is back on its
  balcony.
- But:
  - the phone also arrives cold;
  - the end frame is the busiest of the three;
  - the un-draw has only ~0.4 s (41.82-42.24).

**C 9.**
- The film's own question closes. The reply to the chat sent at 4.3 s pops on "chatbot" (38.86), under the window
  that warms on "heat an apartment" (39.98), in frame 0's view. Then everything un-builds into frame 0.
- It is tight: your hands leave and the un-build happens inside the 0.92 s tail (graft 7 buys the time).

**Where the craft judge may differ:** C's build risks are the swap frame on "giant", the 0.92 s tail, and the nested
rise. None of them is a story risk that a G3 watch at 1x can't settle, and the grafts add no place or mover.
