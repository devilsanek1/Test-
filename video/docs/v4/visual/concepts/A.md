# Visual concept A: THE HEAT'S JOURNEY (video 4)

**Status: DONE (2026-10-29).** One of three visual concepts of workflow `v4-visual-script` (`../BRIEF.md`), for the two
judges. The lens: the camera follows the heat itself (the thread: warm water, a drawn thing) from one giant computer to
one home along one continuous path, each line of the voice moving it one step further; the scale beat (the Earth's
curve) is where heat is thrown away everywhere else. Inputs: the locked take (`public/v4/voice_words.json`, 42.24 s,
first word 0.13 s), `script/script_g1.md` (each line's job), `THROUGHLINE.md` (the card), `research_craft.md`,
`research_formats.md`, the fact file (`video/docs/v5/research/factlock.md`, FL). Style-neutral: content, staging,
camera, timing, joins, words and sound only; how anything looks is the look's (G4).

## 1. The concept

**In three sentences.** One road, drawn by the pen at frame 0, runs from one giant computer at Helsinki's harbour,
through a pipe under a snowy street, into the radiator under one window, and the whole film travels it: the hook runs
the heat along it once as a promise, then every line of the voice moves the heat (and the camera with it) one step
further, from the machine that makes it to the home it warms. The heat has two possible roads, and the film shows both:
the usual one, where we ride it up off the Earth's curve and watch it vanish into the sky above every other data center
("Normally..."), and Helsinki's, where on "But" we dive back down and it turns into the pipe and travels home ("WANTS
that heat"). Along its road the heat only ever moves one way on screen (from the computer at the left to the home at
the right, then up), the camera never cuts, and the film ends on frame 0's window, so the loop closes the road.

**The heat's itinerary, one step per line:** promised (L1) -> born in a giant computer (L2) -> made (L3) -> measured
(L4, L5) -> let go, the usual road (L6) -> wanted (L7) -> planned (L8) -> waited for (L9) -> caught by water (L10) ->
carried under the streets (L11) -> home, with your chatbot (L12).

**Why it fits this story and its viewers.**
- **The story is a "how?"** ("How could AI heat seventy thousand homes?") and a journey answers it in the order things
  really happen: computer, water, pipe, radiator. Every beat is the real mechanism shown literally, so it reads with the
  sound off (playbook: literal beats clever; `research_craft.md` finding 5).
- **A moving thing is what a scrolling eye follows** ("motion also captures attention", Cutting 2016, T,
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5256470/), and a traveller asks "where does it go next?" on its own,
  which carries 42 s without a new place or a cut.
- **One road learned once carries the whole film** (a fixed layout, one screen direction: Smith 2012, T,
  https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/; finding 4): the viewer meets the road at frame 0, sees its
  start at L2, and recognises it when the heat finally travels it at L11.
- **The scale beat is not a side trip** but the heat's other fate: the turn on "But" is felt as a choice between two
  roads, which is the script's own spine (heat wasted -> heat wanted).
- **It is the overview-ground toggle pros use** (Vox's Sam Ellis, S, https://www.storybench.org/?p=14818; finding 9):
  the whole road (overview), the machine (ground), the Earth (the one scale peak), the machine again with home behind it
  (re-established), the cutaway (ground), the whole road again (the payoff).

**The world: ONE designed view, the home place** (the card's snowy Helsinki street at dusk with one warm window, staged
as the heat's road). Seen side-on and always from the same side, a street climbs from the harbour (screen left, low) up
to an apartment block with ONE big window (screen right, high, on the subject axis in the stage). The ground under the
street is cut open like a poster's cross-section, and in it runs the pipe. At the street's foot, on the harbour's edge,
stands the source: one giant computer (one tall machine with rows of small lights, cables and pipes at its foot and its
top), inside the new data center's plan (a building that exists only as its plan: footprint and walls marked out, never
built; FL 6.7). Behind: the street's other blocks, the city's roofs, the harbour, and across the water the far city on
its own horizon (the designed view, DIRECTION rule 7); the cathedral only as an optional far landmark (Uspenski, FL
6.10), never a beat. The heat's road is the street's leading line: the eye runs along it from the machine to the window.

**What this concept changes on the card's picture ideas** (the card's thread, home place, "you", Pip and carriers stay):
1. **One screen direction for the heat on its road, in every shot:** left (the machine) to right (the home), then up
   (its usual road, L6, goes straight up). So after "But" we land AT the source; the card's L8, where the water led the
   camera back to its source, is gone (research finding 4).
2. **L1 -> L2 is a push-through down the road** into the machine (A), not a long sideways glide (findings 3, 8).
3. **Frame 0's pen draws the road first,** and the loop un-draws it, so the opening's build tells the film's path.
4. **The rise is the heat's own road:** we ride its plume up and lose it; the curve shows the same loss everywhere, and
   Helsinki's spot is the one with no plume.
5. **The turn lands on the fork:** the machine, its steam rising as before, with the street (home) climbing behind it;
   on "WANTS" the steam turns into the pipe (home re-established and the choice shown in one frame).
6. **No "≈ 40" tag** (the forty kettles are counted; the caption says FORTY: finding 10); **the small box stays calm**
   (no change of state with no word: finding 5).
7. **The heat's two forms are its two roads:** steam is heat let go into the air; water in a pipe is heat kept.

## 3. The three moments a viewer remembers

1. **The city lighting up from one machine (0.5-3.9 s).** Warm water races up a snowy street from a machine at the
   harbour, one window lights, then the whole city's windows light in a wave that runs off the edges of the frame. It
   is the hook made physical: the expected one home, then reality overtaking it, in the voice's order.
2. **Riding the heat into the sky, and diving back on "But" (19.3-24.0 s).** We rise with the steam of forty kettles'
   worth of heat until it vanishes; from high over the Earth's curve every data center blows its heat away into the sky,
   and only Helsinki's little lit spot keeps its warmth; then we dive straight back into it, and the steam turns into
   the pipe. The film's one scale moment is also its turn.
3. **The heat coming home (31.1-39.4 s).** Cold water climbs through the giant computer and leaves it hot, runs under
   the snowy street with a tram crossing above, climbs into one radiator, and the window lights; we pull back to your
   phone as a chat bubble pops and that window warms one step more. The shareable last image: your chatbot, in
   someone's radiator. (Runner-up: forty kettles boiling round one computer with a mug of tea on top, the smile.)

## 4. Risks and how the concept handles them

**Clarity**
- **The machine is small at frame 0, so "AI" may not read as a computer until L2.** It sits where every line of the
  road leads, it is the only thing in the far ground that acts (steam, lights), the heat visibly leaves it, and it
  puffs on "AI"; 0.4 s after the hook the push-through lands on it, and "giant computers" names it. Fallback (the
  card's): keep it off-frame at frame 0 and first show it at L2's landing (the water then enters from the left edge).
- **The heat has two forms (steam, water) and could read as two things.** The swap happens on screen once, at the fork
  (L7, on "WANTS"), with the camera still; the voice calls both "that heat" (L6, L7, L11); steam always means heat let
  go, water in a pipe always means heat kept.
- **The steam bending into the pipe is a drawn act, not physics.** It is the film's one figurative move, made in the
  second its anchoring word is said ("WANTS": words that cue a picture metaphor raise comprehension, Phillips 2000 via
  a 2023 summary, T, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1131387/full), and the literal mechanism
  follows at L10 (water in pipes). G3 can test the literal alternative: on "WANTS" the city's pipe opens at the
  machine's top and the steam simply stops rising.
- **A lit window says "light", not "heat".** The radiator under the sill warms first (the heat arrives), then the
  window lights (someone's warm home); the captions say "heat" and "homes" on those frames.
- **"One of these computers" could be heard as one building.** On "one" the machine stands alone in cleared snow, the
  kettles ring the machine itself, and the plan's walls stay back at the frame's edges.
- **The cutaway must read as "the ground is cut open".** It is part of the home view from frame 0, so the viewer learns
  it once; the L9 drop only goes deeper into a cut they already know.
- **The curve's designed view could read as a cartoon planet.** Only the boxes' size is lifted (allowed for the scale
  landing, DIRECTION rule 7); the land, coast and dusk are drawn as real, and the nearest box's fans are the read.
- **The one upstream move (L2) could muddle the direction.** The warm water keeps running toward the home while the
  camera goes the other way, the push follows the street's leading lines into the source, and the heat itself never
  runs backward (finding 4).

**Pace**
- **15 s at the source (L2-L5)** could idle: one story event every 0.6-1.6 s (4.66 landing, 5.68, 6.92, 7.56, 8.76,
  9.74, 10.96, 12.54, 13.72, 15.06, 16.22, 17.76, 18.62), never two within 0.3 s (finding 6).
- **A journey means motion** (1 big, 1 mid, 4 small, 5 carriers in 42 s): the camera moves ~14 s in all and is at
  rest about two thirds of the time, locked on "WANTS" and from "chatbot" to the end; the small moves stay 5-10 %, and
  the in-then-out pair (L3, L4) stays small, each with its reason.
- **The no-pause join (L9 -> L10):** the drop starts in the pause before "So" on an ease-out, so it reads as the
  question's move and lands as "work?" ends; nothing new starts before "big" (29.96).
- **The rise's speed** (three levels in ~1.9 s, over the ~15 px a frame budget on its large masses): each level is a
  recognisable picture held ~0.6 s (Kurzgesagt's continuous zoom runs ~0.7 s a scale step, measured: M,
  https://archive.org/details/youtube_-_UCsXVk37bltHxD1rDPwtNM8Q_2018; finding 3), with the plume fixed on the axis as
  the eye's anchor; G3 passes it on a 1x phone watch.
- **The road is travelled twice (L1, L11).** L1 is the promise (wide, ~1.5 s, the whole city), L11 the explanation
  (closer, ~3 s, the street above, the box, the radiator), and L12 then adds you: the repeat is the promise kept.

**Hard to build in a 2D layered engine**
- **The rise to the curve (L6), the hardest shot:** the pull-back rig (`src/lib/pullback.tsx`: depth-graded planes,
  each scaled geometrically by one progress value) run vertically, the three levels as planes that shrink into the
  next and the curve as the last; the layers drawn ~500 px taller than the start frame (CINEMATIC_GUIDE S1). New: a
  vertical variant of the rig (about half a day).
- **The dive from the curve (L7), a ~30-50x change in 1 s:** the engine's `push-through` (zoom 7 into Helsinki's spot,
  then the incoming panel pulls back 7x out of its own point, the steam over the machine): ~49x, existing.
- **The follow (L11):** one diagonal strip ~2.5 frames wide with 3-4 parallax planes; the water's front a fill clipped
  to the pipe's path at the camera's speed (~1,600 px of travel in ~3.1 s: ~500-700 px/s, ~9-12 px a frame, inside the
  ~15 px budget); the road is short by design (a few blocks from the harbour to the window), so the follow never has
  to hurry.
- **Steam and water:** drawn shapes on paths (rise, swell, bend into the pipe's mouth, thin by shrinking and breaking
  up), never an opacity fade (DIRECTION rule 8); water a fill advancing along a path.
- **The wave of windows (L1):** a seeded field of windows, each lit at a delay set by its distance from the road.
- **Frame 0 and the loop:** per-element draw-on and fill-in (the frame-0 build) with the road's stroke first; the
