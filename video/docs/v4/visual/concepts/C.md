# Visual concept C: from your chat to a home (video 4)

**Status: DONE (2026-10-29).** One of three visual concepts of workflow `v4-visual-script` (`../BRIEF.md`).
Lens: FROM YOUR CHAT TO A HOME. Style-neutral: content, staging, camera, timing, joins, words on screen and sound cues
only; the look is G4's. Every time is from `video/public/v4/voice_words.json`.

## 1. The concept
**In three sentences.** The film is the trip from your chat to a home: frame 0 draws the hook on a snowy Helsinki
street seen from where you stand, and at 4.3 s your own phone comes up into that same frame and the camera dives into
your chatbot's "typing" dots, which become the three blinking lights of the giant computer that actually does the
work. From that one machine the camera only opens out (forty kettles, then the film's one big rise to the Earth's
curve, where data centers blow their heat away), until "But" dives back down into Helsinki and follows the heat its
plan would catch, in water in pipes, under the street and into one window. It ends on the frame it began on, now
with your phone in it: the reply to your chat pops, the window above you lights a step warmer, and the street
un-builds into frame 0, so the question sent at 4.3 s is answered at 38.9 s and the loop closes on "you".

**How the camera keeps finding your chat:** after the dive, your chatbot's three typing dots ARE the giant computer's
three lights, so every return to the machine finds them: planted on "AI" in frame 0, singled out on "just one" (L4),
pulsing as the question is asked (L9), passed by the hot water on its way out (L10); and the phone itself twice, at
the question (L2) and the answer (L12). One motif on the subject, never a second thread or a second subject.

**Why it fits this story and its viewers**
- **Line 2's "actually" corrects the picture every cold panel started from** (AI lives in my phone, or a smart
  thermostat: `script_g1.md`, the connectors table). C shows that picture first, the viewer's own phone, and
  corrects it in one move: the chat's dots are a giant computer's lights. The line's job, made physical, sound off.
- **The payoff's "your chatbot" gets set up instead of arriving cold.** It is the one word the panels stopped on
  ("My chatbot? I'm in Ohio.", `panel_d3/`), heard for the first time in the last line; the orchestrator kept it for
  the comments it draws. C plants your chat at 4.3 s and keeps its dots on the subject, so at 38.9 s "your chatbot"
  is a callback the picture has earned, and the doubt that's left is the argument the film wants ("why aren't we
  doing this here?").
- **It starts from the viewers' own situation** (the script's: they ask a chatbot things every day and never think
  where its heat goes) and ends on it. The chain needs no trade word: my chat, a giant computer, heat, wasted,
  wanted, water, pipes, a window.
- **One flowing story with a shape you can feel:** the camera goes in through your chat, out to the planet, back
  down to one home, back to your chat. Frame 0's view is the film's home frame (at 0, 4.3 and 38.9 s, the way
  Johnny Harris keeps coming back to one home frame: `research_craft.md`, M), and the loop is built in.

**Kept from the card:** the thread (the heat as warm water, on screen ~98 % of the time), the home place (the snowy
street with one warm window, its cut-open ground, its view down to the harbour and the new data center's plan), the
one "you" (POV only: your gloved hands and your phone), Pip hidden, the one comparison shot (L6), 0 plain cuts.
**Departures, named:** (1) L1 -> L2 finds your phone and dives through it to the giant computer, where the card
glides upstream along the pipe; (2) the "you" is on screen twice (L2 and L12), both times in frame 0's view, where
the card shows it once; (3) the giant computer's three lights blink in your chat's typing rhythm (a detail of the
subject, never a second thread); (4) no "≈ 40" tag (it repeats the caption) and no box on the pipe that changes the
water with no word to explain it (both research flags); (5) L7 lands at the plan, so L8 never travels against the
flow (research flag); (6) L12's pull back lands on frame 0's own view, so the payoff frame is the loop frame.

## 2. Beat by beat, timed to the voice
Times are the take's (`voice_words.json`). A beat runs from its line's first word to the next line's first word
(where the join's midpoint sits); "on 'word' (t)" is that word's start, and the picture leads it by ~0.1 s. Left and
right are the camera's. **One flow direction in every shot:** the water runs from the giant computer (screen left, or
below) to the home (screen right, then up). The camera may travel any way; the water never runs back.

**The home frame (F0).** Your view from the snowy street at dusk, at eye level. On the right of the axis, the
apartment block with ONE big window, its wall cut open under the sill to show a radiator. Across the lower stage the
ground is cut open under the snow, with ONE big pipe in it running from the left to the block and up the cut-open wall
into the radiator. On the left the street runs down to the harbour; at its end stands the new data center's plan
(walls only marked out, never built, no roof) around one giant computer. Across the water, the city. Frame 0 draws
F0; L2 and L12 add your gloved hands and phone at the bottom; the loop un-builds back into it.

**The state rule.** Frame 0 shows the promise (what Helsinki wants, working). When the film comes back to Helsinki at
L7 it shows the street as it is now: the plan not running, the one window and the street's windows along the pipe
dark (the far city's lit), until the heat arrives at L11.

## 3. The three moments a viewer would remember
1. **The dive into your chat (4.3-7.8 s).** Your own phone comes up into the street, the reply's typing dots pulse,
   the camera dives into them, and they turn out to be the blinking lights of a giant computer. It sticks because it
   starts from the viewer's everyday object and ends on a small "wait, that's where it lives?", the second jolt inside
   the first 6 s, right after frame 0's lighting city (the playbook polishes the first 2, 5 and 10 s most).
2. **From forty kettles to the Earth's curve (15.1-22.9 s).** Forty kettles boil round one machine, then the camera
   rises with all that steam until every data center on the planet is blowing its heat away and only Helsinki's
   lights stay. The film's one scale moment, and it makes "normally" feel huge just before "But".
3. **The reply under the warm window (38.5-42.2 s).** The film's first frame comes back with your phone in it, the
   reply to the chat you sent at 4.3 s pops, the window above you lights a step warmer, and the street un-builds into
   frame 0. The payoff lands on the viewer's own phone, the question gets its answer, and the loop restarts on the
   same window.

Frame 0's lighting city is the hook that earns all three; it stays as the card has it.

## 4. Risks and how the concept handles them
**Clarity**
- **The dive could read as a trick instead of "your chat runs on that".** The dots never move or resize across the
  hand-over (B), they blink in the lights' rhythm, the typing tick turns into the computer's hum, and the voice says
  "actually runs on giant computers" over it; the pull back then shows the machine whole among its kind. G3's
  sound-off question: "where does the chatbot's answer come from?" (it must be "a giant computer"). Fallback: hold
  ~0.2 s on the screen filled by the three dots before the computer forms round them.
- **The phone could read as an ad or an app demo** (v3: a shopping screen as the first image read as an ad). It is
  never frame 0 (the hook comes first); no app name, logo or words in the bubbles; gloved hands in a snowy street at
  dusk; ~1.3 s on screen before the dive; over it, a friend's correction, never a pitch. The cold read asks "does
  this look like an ad?".
- **"My chatbot? I'm in Ohio."** From 4.3 s the picture puts your chat in front of Helsinki's plan. The voice stays
  general there ("AI actually runs on giant computers") and the giant computer is the kind, unnamed; the planting
  makes the payoff's "your" expected, and the doubt that's left is the comment the line was kept for. The cold panel
  tests the "you" (playbook: "test 'you' claims with a viewer panel").
- **The plan reads before "plan" is said:** from L2 the giant computer stands inside walls only marked out, and a
  viewer may think "a building being made" until L8. That read is right (it isn't built); at L8 the walls are seen
  whole beside real roofs and the 2027 tag pins on (the card's open item 9: the plan reads as a plan at a glance).
- **The window's state jumps** (lit in the promise at 2.1-5.2 s, dark at L7, lit at L11, warmer at L12): the state
  rule above; the reset happens off screen, during the journey. G3's cold read checks no one sees it as a mistake.
- **L7's steam dropping into the pipe is a gesture, not the mechanism:** it lands on "WANTS" (the word explains it),
  and L10 then shows the real mechanism, water in pipes; the two agree (the heat goes into the pipe either way).
- **Two things in L2's frame** (your phone, the lit window above it): the phone is the only mover; the window is the
  still home spot.
- **The cozy share** (DIRECTION rule 7: >= 50 %): about 25 s happen at the plan's site by the harbour. It must be
  composed as part of the home place (the street's buildings framing it, the lit hill behind, snow and steam), never an
  open lot; otherwise only the street frames (~13 s) count as cozy. W1 and G3 compose it (the card shares this risk).

**Pace**
- **L2 carries four pictures in ~4.5 s** (the phone, the dots, the computer close, its row): each holds >= 0.6 s; the
  push and the hand-over are one gesture; the pull back is at rest by ~7.9, before the pause ends.
- **The long runs never idle:** every beat above lands an event every ~1-1.5 s on a stressed word, none within 0.3 s
  of another; the camera rests at the turn ("WANTS") and from the payoff's landing (research findings 6, 12).
- **L9 -> L10 has no pause:** the drop lands on the cold water as "work?" ends, and the next event waits for "take".
- **Many moves** (in, out, up, down, along): each follows its subject (the dots, the steam, the water) and has its
  one-line reason; one big move (L6); no whips, no compound moves.
- **The tail is only 0.92 s** for the un-build: quick, yet it must read; G3 times it.

**Build (a 2D layered engine)**
- **The dive:** the phone layer scales past the frame's edges while the dots layer holds; when the screen fills the
  frame, its flat field swaps for the computer's front (the same field, so the swap hides), then the pull back. Both
  device types exist (`push-through`, `matchcut`); the swap frame is built and checked at 1x. About 5x in ~0.9 s
  is ~2.9 % a frame, just inside the zoom budget at the subject (~3 %).
- **The rise (L6), the hardest piece, as on the card:** nested drawings (the plan, the city, the coast, the curve),
  each holding the next at the right spot, swapped as they leave; ~0.5 s a step may pass the zoom budget at the
  subject (a WARN, judged at 1x); it needs the multi-key camera (`G3_ANIMATIC.md` §7, tier 2). The push-through back
  down into Helsinki plays a shorter nest in reverse.
- **The water's runs** (frame 0, L10, L11): an animated stroke along a turning path, one shared component.
- **The follow (L11):** one long drawing of the street over its cut-open ground, with the turn up the block's wall;
  parallax on the street; the water held on the axis.
- **The pull back out of the window (L12):** anchored on the window's spot; the hands layer enters from the bottom by
  parallax. **Forty boiling kettles:** forty instances of one steam loop, kept at a supporting weight so the mover
  budget holds (they act as one subject). **The un-build:** frame 0's draw-on played backwards.

**Facts** (`video/docs/v5/research/factlock.md`, FL)
- **The plan is a plan** (FL 6.7): walls only marked out at every appearance, never a finished building; the 2027 tag
  pinned to the plan; no name, no real site.
- **"Your chatbot runs here"** (the dive) is the core line's own generalization (FL's comment-drawing line, "true
  enough at full size"); no tenant is signed (FL 6.7), so no pipe or line ever runs from your phone to anything, and
  "could soon" carries the payoff.
- **The giant computer** is a generic AI machine with no logo; forty kettles is FL 2.2's arithmetic (a 3 kW kettle,
  named in the post's text); its height is drawn, never stated.
- **The steam:** a liquid-cooled machine doesn't steam by itself; here its heat meets the snowy air through the plan's
