# Video 4, visual concept B: THE DESIGNED VIEW

**Status: DONE (2026-10-29).** Concept B of workflow `v4-visual-script` (`../BRIEF.md`), written blind to A and C. The
lens: one poster-like designed view of Helsinki that the camera explores with push-ins, cutaways and one big
pull-back, and returns to at the end (the loop). The clock is the take (`video/public/v4/voice_words.json`: 42.24 s,
the first word at 0.13 s): every time below is a word's start in it, never the card's estimates. Style-neutral:
content, staging, camera, timing, joins, words and sound; how anything looks is G4's. Read: `../research_craft.md`
(findings C1-C12), `../research_formats.md`, `../METHOD.md`, `../../script/script_g1.md` (each line's job),
`../../THROUGHLINE.md` (the card), the playbook's Hook and pacing and Storyboard sections, `DIRECTION.md`, `CRAFT.md`
§a and §f.5, `LAYOUT.md`, `CINEMATIC_GUIDE.md`; FL = `video/docs/v5/research/factlock.md`.

## 1. The concept

**In one sentence:** the whole film is ONE picture of Helsinki, cut open like a page of a cross-section book, that
draws itself, lets the camera into its parts in the voice's order, shrinks once to a warm spot on the planet, and ends
on itself again, now readable end to end.

**In three sentences.** Frame 0 draws one designed view: a snowy street at dusk with the ground below it cut open so
one big pipe shows, an apartment block with ONE window, the harbour, the far city on its own horizon, and at the
street's harbour end the marked-out plan of the new data center, where one giant computer will stand. Every line of
the voice is a part of that picture seen closer (the computer, its heat, forty kettles, the plan, the computer opened
up, the pipe, the radiator), entered and left by one continuous camera, and the one big move is a pull-back from a mug
of tea through the whole picture out to the Earth's curve, where Helsinki is one warm spot among data centers blowing
their heat into the sky. On "But" the camera dives back in, and the film ends where it began: the same view with the
heat's whole path running through it and your phone in front of it, un-drawing into frame 0 for the loop.

**Why it fits this story.** The story is a hidden path (a giant computer, a pipe under the street, a radiator in a
home), and a cutaway is the one picture that shows a hidden path inside its own place, with no diagram and no new
place: "Cutaway illustrations ... facilitate a good understanding of spatial ordering" (Diepstraten et al. 2003,
quoted on Wikipedia's "Cutaway drawing" [guide, weak]); underground workings have been drawn that way since Agricola's
*De Re Metallica* (1556, same page). One picture is also the card's one home place taken literally: the viewer learns
the map once (the plan on the left, the home on the right; the heat always runs left to right, then up: `LAYOUT.md`
§3, C4) and every close is entered from a view that showed where it is (C9), the map-and-ground toggle that Vox's Sam
Ellis says "seems to really help people understand the story" [studio, in `research_craft.md`]; Johnny Harris returns
every cutaway to one home frame [ours, measured, same file]. The one comparison keeps that logic: the world is the same
picture seen from far away (Kurzgesagt's title is one zoom through ~7 nested scales [ours, measured]; the Eameses'
*Powers of Ten* one continuous camera out and back [textbook, same file]).

**Why it fits its viewers.** A regular person scrolling gets a big, bold event from frame 0 (a whole city drawing
itself, cut open, then lighting up), a question the picture asks on its own ("what's under that street?") and a
payoff they can read at a glance in one frame. The owner said of the posters' designed view: "unique camera angle and
composition of objects is fascinating" (playbook, Visual style), and loves the pull-back crane (`CINEMATIC_GUIDE.md`
S5). The loop pays a rewatch twice: the frame-0 picture that was a puzzle is now readable from the computer to the
window, and Pip waits on the same balcony to be found (cross-section books do this: Biesty's *Man-of-War* has "the
challenge of spotting a stowaway", Wikipedia "Stephen Biesty" [guide, weak]).

### The view (what frame 0 draws, top to bottom)
- **The sky**, one calm field through the caption band.
- **The far city across the harbour**, just under the band, lifted onto its own horizon (the designed view's far
  stage, `CRAFT.md` §f.5), full of windows; optionally the Uspenski Cathedral on its rock as a far landmark only
  (never a beat, never named, never Helsinki Cathedral: FL 6.10).
- **The harbour**, a band of water that hides where the far stage meets the near world.
- **The near street**, running left to right across the frame: at its left end by the harbour, the new data center's
  plan, at first only a footprint marked on the snow (the giant computer stands in it from "AI"); on the subject axis,
  the apartment block with the ONE window (the home spot) in the read zone; more blocks to the right, cut by the edge.
- **Under the street, the ground cut open like a cross-section:** snow, street, earth and one big pipe from the plan to
  the block; the cut turns up the block's wall in a narrow strip, so the pipe can be followed to the radiator under the
  one window.
- **The bottom:** the cut earth, a mass cut by the bottom edge.

The near world keeps one camera (the street's top recedes behind the cut edge) and the far stage holds still in every
move. The only other designed frame is the scale landing (the Earth's curve): two designed frames, as §f.5 allows.

### Key frames
1. **Frame 0 (0.00-0.60):** the view draws itself, the block and its one window first, then the cut ground and its pipe.
2. **The claim (2.14-3.92):** the warm water reaches the one window, which lights; then the city's windows, in a wave.
3. **The one big move (18.95-21.18):** from a mug of tea, pull back through the whole view to the Earth's curve.
4. **The turn (23.46-24.04):** the dive back into Helsinki's spot; the computer's steam bends down into the pipe.
5. **The answer (28.70-33.98):** the giant computer opens like a cutaway; cool water in at its top, hot out at its foot.
6. **The payoff (38.48-40.40):** frame 0's view with the heat's whole path in it, your phone in front, a chat bubble.
7. **The loop (41.82-42.24):** the view un-draws to frame 0's first strokes.

5. **The steam first appears on "really hot" (L3), not at frame 0,** so steam always means heat leaving, and "WANTS"
   is where it is caught.
6. **No "≈ 40" tag (C10); the small box changes nothing in the water (C5):** it is where the plan's pipe joins the
   city's.
7. **The plan is a footprint at frame 0;** its walls rise on "buildings" (L2).
8. **The end is frame 0's view itself with your hand and phone in front of it** (the card: the street at the window's
   foot); the loop un-draws that same view.

## 3. The three moments a viewer would remember
1. **A whole city draws itself, cut open, and lights up (0-3.9 s):** warm water runs under the snow, one window
   lights, then the whole city in a wave.
2. **From a mug of tea to the whole planet (17.8-23.5 s):** "That's a lot of tea", then the camera pulls back through
   all of Helsinki until it is one warm spot on the Earth among data centers blowing their heat into the sky, and
   dives back in on "But".
3. **The same picture at the end, readable at last (38.5-42.2 s):** the heat's whole path from the giant computer to
   the window in one frame, your phone in front with its chat bubble; then it un-draws and starts again.

## 4. Risks, and how the concept handles them

**Clarity**
- **A full picture at frame 0: where does the eye go?** The build follows the voice, one event at a time, and the eye
  rides the water's path (left to right, then up); the near street and its one window are big in the lower half, the
  far city small and calm (tier C). Check: freeze at 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0 s at phone size with the sound off; by 3.0 a
  stranger says "a computer's heat runs under the street into the homes" (C7).
- **Does the cut ground read as "under the street"?** It is the textbook cutaway (underground workings, 1556 on); the
  snowy street with a tram and footprints sits right on the pipe, and the water's run makes the pipe a pipe. Check:
  the sound-off cold read names it.
- **The plan in a world that is itself being drawn ("is the building real?").** The plan never finishes, while
  everything else is finished by ~0.60; the giant computer is the only finished thing inside it; its three moments sit
  on their words ("buildings", "plan", "big") with the 2027 tag; G4 must keep the two apart at a glance (the card's
  open item 9). Check: the cold read at S8 says "it isn't built yet". Fallback: the plan stays a marked footprint
  until "plan" (L8).
- **A poster explored by a camera can read as a slideshow over a still.** Every frame keeps its life (snow, a ferry, a
  tram, people in windows), the thread always runs, and holds get the parallax float by depth, never a flat card
  (playbook); each move has its one-line reason (C1).
- **The steam bending into the pipe on "WANTS" is a picture of the line, not the mechanism.** The voice anchors it in
  that second (C5, Phillips 2000), and the real mechanism comes 7 s later (S10).
- **Where does the cool water come from (S9-S10)?** From the left, upstream, unexplained; no cold panel asked. If G3's
  cold read asks, a return pipe under the hot one (the true loop, still and calm) answers it.
- **The pull-back's steps at ~0.5 s each** (mug, computer, the view, the coast, the curve): one of them is frame 0's
  view, already learned; Helsinki holds the pivot spot, so the eye never searches; the landing holds 1.8 s.
- **Two subjects at the payoff (the bubble, the window):** one at a time in the voice's order, 1.5 s apart.
- **The "you" in front of a designed view (whose hand?):** POV only, as the card; it reads "you, here; the apartment,
  there". Fallback: the card's frame (the street at the window's foot, your hand in front) with the window kept at
  frame 0's spot and size for the loop.

**Pace**
- **A locked frame 0, when a calm, pretty opening lost ~80 % by 3 s.** The build is the motion: something new every
  few frames, each effect bold, long and with its own sound, the wave the size of the frame. If G3's 1x phone watch
  reads it calm, the fallback is a story move: start ~1.2x on the block, pull back on "seventy thousand homes" as the
  wave overtakes the frame, and push back in under the un-draw so the loop still meets.
- **19.3 s at one place (S1-S5) must never idle:** an event every ~1-2 s (in each block), a size change at every join
  (XW, W, C, M, INSERT), and four new things (the walls, the steam, the kettles, the mug).
- **Short shots (S5 1.50 s, S9 1.22 s):** each is one continuous camera with its neighbours (C), so the eye never
  re-orients; the average stays 3.52 s.
- **In-and-out moves (S5 in, S6 far out; S7 in, S8 and S9 in):** each has its reason (lean in for the joke, context,
  the turn, the subject); S5's push is small.
- **S11's follow turns up the block's wall:** one follow of one moving subject, but if G3 reads the turn as a second
  move, the shot splits there as S11A on "into" (36.80), joined by C; the average would fall to 3.25 s, so L4 and L5
  then share one shot (S4, the mug popping in its frame, no push) to bring it back to 3.52 s.

**Hard in a 2D layered engine**
- **The nested pull-back and the dive back (S6, S7):** each scale is a layer with the next drawn inside it at matched
  scale, the G3 "push-through (scale-through)" device (`G3_ANIMATIC.md` §4) and Kurzgesagt's measured title zoom. The
  dive is the film's fastest move (~10x in ~1.0 s, over the ~3 %-a-frame zoom budget): flat masses, through steps the
  viewer just saw on the way out; G3 watches it at 1x. Fallback: land on frame 0's view (one step less) and let S8's
  push do the rest. The pull-back itself is ~3 % a frame, inside the budget.
- **Two horizons (the far stage):** allowed in the establishing view and the scale landing only (CRAFT §f.5); every
  close is on the near street (one camera); no move trucks along the far stage's junction (S11 runs under the street,
  the far stage out of frame). The float on S1 and S12 moves the near world most and the far stage not at all: the
  multiplane camera's own principle, artwork moved "at various speeds ... the further away from the camera, the slower
  the speed", the tool Disney used for the wide "establishing shot of Pinocchio's village" (Wikipedia, "Multiplane
  camera" [guide, weak]).
- **The section faces** (the cut street, the strip up the block's wall) are flat frontal planes, easy in 2D; the
  street's top recedes behind the cut edge (one camera).
- **The computer opening (S9):** a casing layer that swings or slides away over a drawn interior layer.
- **The water's runs:** a path with a moving front, the engine's draw-on.
- **The window wave (S1):** hundreds of windows as a seeded field (`CINEMATIC_GUIDE.md` Scale cues 3), the wave a
  timed sweep across it.
- **The planned hall (S10):** a receding array of simple repeated shapes behind the opened machine.
- **Your hand (S12):** the nearest layer, revealed by the pull-back (it moves fastest, as a multiplane foreground does).

**Facts**
- **The plan is a plan (FL 6.7):** the building is never finished, even at frame 0 and the end, where the picture
  shows what Helsinki "wants" and "could soon" do, in the voice's words; the 2027 tag sits on the plan; the plan's own
  stretch of pipe stays as the plan is, up to the joint with the city's real pipes.
- **The curve could say "only Helsinki keeps its heat",** which is false (Stockholm, Odense, Paris, Espoo: FL 8.2,
  8.12). It is generic land with no coast or city a viewer can name and generic boxes (the card); Helsinki's spot is
  the one the camera came from, not the only one, and one or two far boxes may send up no plume. "Normally" is the
  voice's own generalization (FL 7.1; the script's comment line).
- **Steam off a machine and plumes off the boxes** are pictures of heat meeting cold air (the card's thread); real
  cooling towers make plumes, and the voice never says steam.
- **The giant computer:** generic, no logo, no stated height (FL 2.2); forty kettles is FL 2.2's arithmetic (a 3 kW
  kettle, in the post's text).
- **The small box** is the heat pump (FL 6.3): unlabelled, unnamed, changing nothing on screen (C5). The one stream
  of water from the computer to the radiator is really two loops joined there; the script's "that heat" keeps it true.
- **Water in pipes, never loose in the machine; the heat into a radiator, never a tap** (the card's truth list).
- **The far landmark (optional):** Uspenski, never Helsinki Cathedral (FL 6.10), never a beat.
- **"Next year" is 2027** on the tag (FL 6.7; a G8 re-check from 1 January 2027). No company name or logo anywhere.
