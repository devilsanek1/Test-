## Findings (most severe first)

**1. MAJOR · S7-S8 (into S9-S10) · the heat is caught before the plan exists, by a mechanism the facts don't support.**
- **Problem:** On "WANTS" (24.04) the steam "bends down and pours into the pipe's mouth". On "heat." (24.94) it "turns
  to warm water there, and waits", and S8's thread is "the caught heat, warm water waiting in the pipe's mouth".
  Heard with the next line ("The city has a plan to use it, starting next year"), the picture says the heat is already
  being caught and only its use waits for 2027. In fact nothing is caught today: there is no building, no tenant and
  no site (FL 6.7). Steam also can't be caught back out of the air. The real catch is water through the machine (FL
  6.3), and S10 shows it at the same pipe mouth, so with the sound off the film catches one heat twice, two ways. The
  playbook rules this out ("the real mechanism, never a metaphor the facts don't support"), and the writer's own note
  concedes it (line 188). It also causes the thread's ~2.6 s gap in S9 (line 184).
- **Fix:** Show the want without a catch, and name it as a departure from the card's L7 picture. On "WANTS" the steam
  keeps rising (the heat is still let go: this is now). Meanwhile the city's empty, marked-out stretch of pipe draws
  itself out from the city's pipe toward the computer's foot: Helsinki reaching for that heat, the signed plan to
  connect (FL 6.1). S8 keeps its walls on "plan" and 2027 on "next"; its "use" redraw, 0.34 s after the walls, moves
  here. The rising steam stays the thread through S8-S9, which closes the gap. The heat is first caught on "take"
  (31.14): as S10's water runs down through the machine, its steam thins and stops. That is the card's catch, moved to
  the real mechanism and to the word that says it.

**2. MAJOR · S1, S11, S12 · the heat never reads as heat at the homes.**
- **Problem:** The only thing that changes at a home is light: "the window lights" (2.14, 37.48), the 70,000 shown as
  windows lighting (2.34-3.92), "one step warmer" (40.40). A window lighting up is how pictures say "power on". The
  water's warmth is shown by colour alone, and colour is absent from G3's grey board (CLAUDE.md; `G3_ANIMATIC.md` §2).
  The radiator is too small to read in the wide. The film's drawn heat signs, melt and steam, appear in S3-S7 only
  (line 14). So with the sound off the hook can read "the machine sends something under the street and the lights come
  on": AI powering lights, the very picture this film corrects. The playbook asks for the hook made physical "through
  the real mechanism".
- **Fix:** Give the heat one drawn sign that works in grey and at phone size, the film's own from S3: melting snow.
  The snow over the pipe melts in a line just behind the warm water's front wherever the water first runs:
  - in S1 from "know" (0.46): a long, bold event that reaches the block on "heat" (2.14) as the window lights (snow
    really does melt over district-heating lines);
  - in S10-S11 from "water" (33.02), along the plan's stretch, widening past the box. That widening is the boost the
    box is there for (FL 6.3); today the box does nothing you can see ("the water doesn't change").
  The lit windows then stay what they are, homes, and the melt says heat.

**3. MAJOR · S7-S12 (open question 2) · the street goes dark on "But" with no word, and says Helsinki's homes have no
heat now.**
- **Problem:** From the dive on, the windows along the pipe are "unlit (now)", and they light only as S11's water
  reaches them. The writer lists this as a change with no word (line 188), which METHOD's sound-off check forbids
  ("nothing changes state without a word that explains it"). A viewer will also take it literally, and it is untrue:
  those homes are heated today by the city's pipes (FL 6.1), and the plan would add about a tenth of the city's heat
  (FL 6.6). A street at dusk with every window dark reads as a blackout, not as "not yet". The code also contradicts
  itself: "the far city stays lit", but lit by what, if lit means this heat?
- **Fix:** Close open question 2 the other way. The windows stay lit in every shot after frame 0's ripple, so nothing
  in the street changes on "But". "Not yet" stays on the plan, where it is true and already drawn: its marked-out
  walls, its empty stretch, 2027, and, with findings 1-2, no melt over that stretch and the steam still rising until
  S10. S11's arrival becomes the hot water's front and its melt reaching each riser and the radiator. S12's "one step
  warmer" is explained by its own words ("heat an apartment").

**4. MAJOR · S12 (open question 1) · nothing links your chat to the heat, so the core line shows as two unrelated
pops.**
- **Problem:** "A chat bubble pops on your phone" on "chatbot" (38.86), then "the one window lights one step warmer"
  on "apartment" (40.40), with nothing drawn between them. With the sound off it is a phone, then a window. The core
  line drives the payoff (playbook, Hook and pacing), and "your chatbot" is the word all three of draft 3's cold
  viewers stopped on (`panel_d3/`; the story judge's case for concept C).
- **Fix:** Draw the chain with what the film has already planted. As your bubble pops, the giant computer's small
  lights blink back (the cue already used at 3.92 and 27.96). A pulse of warm water then leaves its foot and runs the
  pipe, with its melt, to the radiator, arriving on "apartment" as the window warms: your chat, the computer, the heat
  and the home in one picture. This settles open question 1 at no cost: the "you" appears once, at the end (as on the
  card), and concept C's riskier phone dive at S2 isn't needed. Keep your phone clear of the pipe's run (for example
  bottom right, beside the block), with its bubble inside the key-read box, above y 1480 (`LAYOUT.md` §1).

**5. MINOR · S6 (and S8) · the scale shot's key reads land at the move's peak and at the frame's edge.**
- **Problem:** "Data centers pop on across the land" on "data" (19.94). With the guide's S5 ease on S6's move
  (18.84-21.18; cubic-bezier 0.55, 0.03, 0.50, 1.0), the peak falls at 20.07 s. So the boxes pop at ~2x the mean
  speed, only 41 % of the way out, onto a frame still rushing through its scale steps. The fans on "big" (20.94) sit on
  "the nearest box (bottom left)". The card wanted the fans to read at phone size, to settle "fans" as machines. Bottom
  left is off the axis where a scale shot keeps its at most 2 points of interest (x 380-700), and it is toward the
  bottom UI zone where the platform's caption text sits (y > 1520). S8's 2027 tag, "pinned to the plan's corner", has
  the same risk: the plan meets the street's roofs at the right edge, under the right rail (x > 950, y 900-1500).
- **Fix:**
  - No pop at the peak: the boxes stand in the land from the coast step and ride in with it, so they are on screen
    while "data centers" is said.
  - Land the near box with its turning fans just below Helsinki's spot, inside x 380-700 and above y 1480.
  - Pin the 2027 tag to the plan's front wall, inside x 380-700 and y 600-1480.

**6. MINOR · S11 · the follow is two moves.**
- **Problem:** "FOLLOW ... while the street slides past, then up the block's wall to the radiator and the window" is a
  truck that turns into a rise. By METHOD's camera rule that is two moves ("ONE move per shot ... a second move is the
  next shot, joined by C"). It also needs two camera keys, which SketchReel's one camera segment per panel can't play
  without the tier-2 multi-key camera (`G3_ANIMATIC.md` §7, effort M). It is the one shot that breaks the craft judge's
  "no new camera tooling" case for concept B.
- **Fix:** Let the truck land on "into" (36.80), framed so the block's cut wall, the radiator and the window are already
  in frame. The water then climbs inside the resting frame on "people's" (37.04). Or split the rise off as S11A, joined
  by C.
