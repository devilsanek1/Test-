# Video 4, visual script: the cold read (2026-10-29)

**Who:** a film director who has never seen this project. I read only `VISUAL_SCRIPT.md` (v1, 23:20 UTC), once, top
to bottom, then wrote this without looking back. Status: DONE.

**In short:** the story reads, and it is one strong idea: a cut-open picture of Helsinki, explored by one
continuous camera, with one trip out to the planet. I could storyboard it **mostly**. Missing: a layout key frame
with the caption band, a line of scale, the in-betweens of the trip out, start times for moves that begin in a
pause (two places contradict), and what the water and the steam do from the turn to the end.

## 1. The film as I picture it, shot by shot

A vertical phone film, about 42 s, one narrator, every word in the captions. I found no cuts: it plays as one
continuous camera moving through a single cut-open picture of Helsinki, with one trip out to the planet in the middle.
It ends on the picture it opened with, so it loops.

**S1, ~0-4 s, the street draws itself.** A near-blank page and a pen tip on the left. The pen sweeps one long line:
a pipe, running right under a snowy street and then up inside the wall of a block of flats to a radiator under one
window. The rest sketches in fast behind it: the block, the street, the ground below cut open like a page from a
cross-section book, a harbour on the left, the sky. Warm water sets off along the pipe from the left, close behind the
pen. A far city lands across the harbour. On "AI" a big computer cabinet draws itself in at the left, exactly where the
water comes from. The water reaches the radiator and the window lights; then windows light one after another, along
the street, across the water, all through the far city. The camera is locked, wide, the whole time. I hear the pen's
scratch, a phone-like ding, a low hum as the computer appears, a click as the window lights, then a ripple of small
plinks as the city lights up.

**S2, ~4-9 s, upstream.** The computer's lights blink and the camera pushes in toward it, against the water, which
keeps running right and out of frame. We land on the computer: tall, stacked, blinking. On "buildings" wall outlines
shoot up round it: a building only marked out, no roof, snow falling inside. A whoosh, then a soft sweep.

**S3, ~9-11 s, hot.** Snowflakes land on its top and melt. The camera pushes closer, onto its upper half and the air
above. On "really" the melt starts to steam; on "hot" steam pours off it into the cold air. A sizzle, then a rising
hiss.

**S4, ~11-18 s, forty kettles.** The camera eases back to the whole computer standing alone on the snowy floor of its
outline building, steaming harder on "makes". On "FORTY" forty kettles pop in round its base in rows; on "boiling" they
all boil, lids rattling, their steam joining its own. A run of clicks, then a rolling boil. (For its first four seconds
this shot is one steaming object; the kettles land late.)

**S5, ~18-19 s, the joke.** A push in to the computer's top in all that steam. On "tea" one mug pops onto it, its own
little curl of steam. A clink.

**S6, ~19-23 s, the planet.** The camera pulls back and up in one long rising move: the mug, the computer and the
kettles shrink, the street view flies past, then the coast, and we land high above the Earth's curve at dusk, Helsinki
a small lit spot exactly where the mug was. Plain boxy data centers pop up across the land; the nearest one spins its
roof fans on "big"; on "blow" plumes rise off all of them. A rising whoosh, a fan whirr, wind; then the wind dies to a
hush.

**S7, ~23-26 s, the turn.** On "But" we dive straight back into Helsinki's spot and come out at the computer by the
harbour. Things have changed: the kettles and the mug are gone, the pipe at its foot is empty, the street's windows are
dark. On "WANTS" the computer's steam bends over and pours down into the pipe's mouth at its foot, turns into warm
water there, and sits. The music steps up; a soft swirl.

**S8, ~26-28 s, the plan.** A slow push onto the outline building. Its walls re-trace themselves; the stretch of pipe
from the computer toward the city re-traces itself too, as an outline, empty. A paper tag swings on, pinned to a
corner: 2027. A paper rustle, a paper flick.

**S9, ~28-30 s, how?** A push in to the computer's top; a pipe comes in from the left with cool water waiting in it.
On "how" the computer's side opens like a cutaway: stacked machines inside, an empty pipe running down past each one.
A soft clunk.

**S10, ~30-34 s, the mechanism.** Behind the open computer the outline hall grows: rows of other giant computers, all
only marked out, running off the frame. Cool water runs in at the top and down past every machine; the camera tilts
down after it; at the bottom it comes out hot into the pipe under the snow. The sound of running water, brighter as it
warms.

**S11, ~34-38 s, home.** The camera follows the hot water's front along under the street: footprints and a tram above,
dark windows. It passes a small box where the plan's pipe joins the city's; two windows above light as it goes by; it
climbs the block's wall into the radiator under our window, which warms, and on "homes" the window lights. The flow,
small clicks, a radiator tick, a click.

**S12, ~38-42 s, you.** The camera pulls back out of the window, fast, all the way to the opening view, and my own
gloved hand holding my phone comes into the bottom of the frame: I am standing here. On "chatbot" an empty chat bubble
pops on the phone; on "apartment" the window glows one notch warmer; then only the snow and the far life move. The
music lifts, a soft pop, a warm low note. On the last word the picture un-draws itself in reverse (hand and phone
first, the pipe last, the pen backing along it to where it started) and the film loops into its own first stroke and
the ding.

Somewhere on balconies and at a ferry rail hides "Pip"; I don't know what Pip is.

## 2. Where I could not picture it, or pictured two things

1. **The home place in a tall frame.** Every shot is a piece of one view, and I had to invent its layout. I pictured
   two: (a) the harbour at the left end, the computer on its shore, the far city on a horizon beyond the water, upper
   left; (b) the harbour as a band behind the whole street, the far city a skyline over its roofs. Nor do I know where
   the block sits across the width, how much height the cut ground takes, or where the caption band is.
2. **"Under the caption band."** Walls, steam, plumes and the hall all "top out" or "thin" under the band. Until the
   checks at the very end ("the caption band stays clear") I read it both ways: they stop below the band, or they pass
   behind it. Now I take it as below; say so where it is used. And since all of them must stop below it, the band's
   height decides every composition, and the script never gives it.
3. **The giant computer's size.** It has to be big in the wide (big enough for a 2x push to reach a medium) and a
   building's walls rise round it, so I pictured a fairy-tale computer as tall as the block; but it is also one of many
   in a hall (S10), stands among kettles in rows, and wears a mug that must read on a phone, so I also pictured a
   realistic cabinet. One line of scale (computer : block : kettle : mug) would settle it.
4. **S1's build.** "Water chasing the pen" puts the pen still on the pipe at ~0.5 s, yet the far city lands at ~0.6 s
   and the block, street and sky "follow" the pipe. Does one pen draw everything, and in what order, or do some things
   land by themselves? And what exactly is on the very first frame: a blank page and a pen tip, or the plan's footprint
   already there? (That is the frame a scrolling thumb sees.)
5. **"Only marked out."** A drawn convention (outlines, a blueprint) or real marks on the site (stakes, string, lines in
   the snow)? "The walls redraw themselves once" (S8): re-traced by the pen, or a flicker? Also, the line that teaches
   "buildings called data centers" (S2) shows a building that isn't there; the first solid data center I see is a box
   on the planet in S6. I read S2's outline as "under construction" at best.
6. **The world changes during the trip to the planet (the biggest one).** In S1-S2 the computer's warm water runs into
   homes; in S3-S5 the same computer throws its heat into the air as steam; after the trip its pipe is empty and the
   street's windows are dark. I pictured two films: (a) S1-S2 is the future and the rest is now; (b) one present in
   which the computer both heats homes and wastes heat, and then the system breaks. Nothing on screen marks the switch
   (it happens off screen, during S6), and in S4's view of the whole computer I didn't know whether warm water still
   leaves its foot. The dark street also reads as "Helsinki's homes are cold right now", which I doubt is meant; the far
   city staying lit makes the street look singled out.
7. **Forty kettles at eye level.** "Round its foot" (a ring) or "5 rows of 8" (a grid)? At eye level the back rows hide
   behind the front one; I pictured one clear front row and a crowd of lids behind it.
8. **S6: from a flat cut-open page to a round planet.** The opening view is side-on and cut open like a diagram; the end
   of the pull-out looks along the land to a curved horizon from high up. I couldn't picture the in-between: does the
   cut-open page shrink like a card into the lit spot, or does the camera rise over a solid city while the cut closes?
   The boxes must be cartoon-big at that height, which is fine, but "one or two far boxes send none" told me nothing as
   a viewer.
9. **S9's inlet pipe.** A pipe "coming in from the left" at the top, with cool water in it, appears here for the first
   time. Is it in the opening picture? Where does the cool water come from? To me it looked new.
10. **S10: the waiting water and the steam.** The steam caught on "WANTS" sits at the foot as warm water; now hot water
    comes out of the foot. Do they join and move off together? And does the computer stop steaming once the water
    carries the heat? From S9 on the script doesn't say, so I don't know whether it steams in the final picture. (I'd
    want the steam to die as the water starts: a sound-off proof of the mechanism.)
11. **S12: where am I standing?** The last view is a cut-open diagram, yet my gloved hand is in it, so I am physically
    there: on the street, looking at a city with an x-ray cut, or holding a page of the book? And the hand and phone sit
    at the bottom edge, which in this view is the cut ground with the pipe (and maybe the captions): I pictured the phone
    hiding part of the pipe's run, the one thing the end frame should show end to end.
12. **"Frame 0" means two things:** the literal first frame (almost empty) and the finished opening view ("frame 0's
    view again"). Twice I had to stop and work out which.
13. **Sound with no picture.** The ding on "know" is called a phone sound, but no phone is on screen until S12; I heard
    it as a notification (maybe the point: you check your own phone) or as a tick for the water starting. It is the
    only plant for "your chatbot". Music is named only where it steps up ("But") and lifts ("So"): is there music from
    the start, and what is under S1-S6?
14. **Small ones.** What Pip is and how big (I can't hide what I can't draw). "No one comes for it" (the mug, S5): I
    didn't know what is being ruled out.

## 3. Camera moves and joins that were unclear

1. **The join letters** (C, A, B, F) are never defined in the script; I worked out C = one continuous camera, A = a
   push-through, B = a match across the loop, F = a plain cut. "No cuts: one continuous camera" is never said outright;
   I got it from "F 0". One line at the top would save every new reader that work.
2. **Moves that start "from the pause (x-y)"** don't say when inside the pause they start; for the animatic I need a
   time or a frame. "Into the pause" (S1, S11) has the same gap.
3. **Two places contradict.** S6 is "locked into the pause (22.88-23.46)", but the join's push-through runs "from the
   start of the pause" and S7 calls itself "the dive's second half", from "But": either the planet holds for half a
   second (a held breath) or the dive starts the moment "outside" ends; I pictured both. Same at S11-S12: S11 is "at
   rest into the pause (37.76-38.48)", S12 pulls back "from the pause", and S12's fallback ("start it as 'homes.' ends",
   37.76) implies the default starts later.
4. **S2's push "in, upstream"** toward a computer at the left end of the frame is a push plus a move left (or a pan):
   name the direction, and where the computer sits when it lands (centred by S4; already centred at S2's landing?).
5. **S6's move.** "Eye level rising to high" is a crane back and up, but to see land, boxes and the curve the lens has
   to tilt down somewhere: when? Holding Helsinki at the mug's screen spot also puts the computer's top at that spot as
   the street view passes, not where frame 0 had it, so "frame 0's view passes through" can't be exact unless the
   subject axis is where the computer stood. And the boxes pop on "data" and the nearest spins on "big" while the camera
   is still racing out (it lands on "fans"): where is the camera at "data", still over the coast? Do the boxes pop up
   as we pass over them?
6. **S7's dive** runs back through the scale steps in half a second to a second and lands on "the plan and the street's
   start", a framing we haven't seen. I could picture it, but its fallback (land on the whole opening view and let S8's
   push do the rest) reads safer.
7. **Rhythm, S3-S9.** In, out, in, far out, then in, in, in. Every move has its reason, but four reversals round one
   object in ten seconds may feel like pumping, and S4's first four seconds (one steaming object; the kettles at ~15 s)
   are the softest stretch of the film.
8. **S11-S12** is called a push-through (A) but is a pull out through the window, and on the way the free camera
   becomes my eyes. Say where it ends: the opening view's height ("a little above the street"), or a standing person's
   eye on the street, which would change the picture.
9. **"The subject axis"** and "the home spot": I assumed the subject axis is the frame's centre line; the mug-to-Helsinki
   pivot depends on where it really is.

## 4. The moment I'd remember

On "WANTS", the steam bending over and pouring down into the pipe: one gesture that says the line and turns the story,
readable with the sound off. Runners-up: the mug becoming Helsinki's lit spot on the way out to the planet (the
cleverest join), and forty kettles rattling their lids (the one laugh).

## 5. Could I storyboard it from this alone?

**Mostly.** The order, the beats on words, the events, the sound cues and most of the moves are there; I could board
S1-S5 and S7-S11 now, on my own layout guesses. What's missing:
1. One layout key frame of the home place in the 9:16 frame with the caption band drawn in (the places and sizes of
   the block, the window, the footprint, the harbour, the far city, the cut ground and the pipe).
2. One line of scale: computer, block, kettle, mug, hall.
3. The S6 in-betweens (page to planet: what the cut does, when the lens tilts, what the coast step looks like) and the
   camera's place at each of its word events.
4. A start time for every move that begins in a pause, and the S6-S7 and S11-S12 contradictions settled.
5. The water and the steam from S7 to S12: what the waiting water does in S10, whether the steam stops, whether the
   inlet pipe is in the opening picture.
6. The S12 point of view: where I stand, and where the phone sits against the pipe and the captions.
7. What Pip is, and one line defining the join letters.

**From cold, on the two open questions.** Phone once or twice: the end works without a plant, but the only plant now is
a sound (the ding). Windows unlit until the heat arrives: I read the dark street as "Helsinki is cold now", a wrong
read; the "stay lit" option avoids it, but then S11 needs its own visible event as the heat arrives.
