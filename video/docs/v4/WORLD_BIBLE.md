**In 5 lines**
1. **What:** the film's world in text: 8 sets (all but the planet are one place, a snowy Helsinki street by the harbour at night), one light rig for that place and one for the planet, the palette, one colour-script line per shot, the tiers per shot and the people. G3 takes the tiers, the rigs as shapes, the planes and the density key; G4 takes the palette and the rigs in colour.
2. **The key:** T3's low key as a night. A dark indigo sky with the captions' flat band at L* 31, a pale lilac glow on the horizon (L* 64) behind one dark belt (the block, the giant computer, the far city), mid-dark snow (L* 50 far, 31 at the frame's foot); one story light, the one window (honey, L* 95); one cooler source, the computer's small lights (cyan, L* 80).

## 0. The world in one screen

- **One place, one look.** A snowy Helsinki street by the harbour, at night in winter, seen side-on from where a person
  stands. Every shot but S6 happens in it, above the snow or (once, at the block's wall) through a soft painted cut.
  The planet (S6) is the one scale shot, in the same key.
- **One colour key** (playbook PROPER LIGHT; DIRECTION 6): one cold family, the night sky's own (indigo -> blue-violet
  -> lilac, h 260-310), every colour tone-on-tone in its mass; ONE warm accent, the heat (honey and amber, h 64-84), at
  the beat's focal. Nothing else in the film is warm but quietly (the other homes' windows, a muted amber +15 L* in their
  facades, no bloom; a burgundy tote at L* 25).
- **The value plan** (ORGANIC §8's T3, translated to a night; `organic/composition.md` §5): a dark sky top (L* 26) and the
  caption band flat at 31 (`TYPE_GUIDE.md`'s night band: warm-white words at 7.96:1); the sky lightening to a pale lilac
  glow on the horizon (64): the city's light on the low snow cloud; one dark belt standing against that glow (the block
  23, the computer 16, the far city 48 converging on the air); the snow a mid field that mirrors the sky, light far (50,
  it mirrors the glow) and dark near (31, it mirrors the zenith); the one window the hardest step (95 on 23). About 60-70 %
  of a home-view frame sits under L* 40 (ref 02, the owner's night model, 83 %; scene 1's T3 at dusk 45 %). As ORGANIC
  §8 decided for T3: lifted toward T1 only if the G4 clip reads gloomy at 400 px beside refs 02 and 03 (the band stays at
  31 for the words; the lift goes into the snow and the glow).
- **Painted = real, chalk = planned.** Everything that exists is painted, at its plane's dose (DIRECTION 4; `ORGANIC.md` §5).
  The plan (the marked-out walls, the plan's stretch of pipe, the hall of planned machines) is drawn in pale chalk lines
  by the brush cursor: a plan IS a drawing (the card's "pale pencil plan", `THROUGHLINE.md` §1.7; thin lines only on
  things that are lines, DIRECTION 4). When the voice explains the plan working (from "water", S10), the snow between
  the stretch's chalk lines melts: the plan shown working, never built.
- **The heat's signs, readable with no colour** (v3: "the heat's sign"): the melt (a dark wet strip in the light snow),
  steam puffing from the manhole, the frost clearing on the one window, steam where heat is let go (a light mass on the
  dark night). With colour each carries the honey: the melt's wet strip shows the lit windows above as a warm sheen, the
  steam is warm at its root. No glow over the pipe (v3's delegated call 7; DIRECTION 5: a glow only at a source).
