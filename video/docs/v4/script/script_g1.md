## The story in 3 sentences

Helsinki wants AI to heat seventy thousand homes, because AI runs on giant computers in buildings called data centers,
those computers get really hot (just one of them makes as much heat as forty kettles, boiling non-stop), and in most
data centers big fans just blow that heat outside. Helsinki wants that heat instead: a new data center there would
cool its computers with water, and pipes under the streets would carry that warm water into people's homes. Nothing is
built yet, but Helsinki started heating homes with a data center under a cathedral back in 2010, so from 2027 your
chatbot could heat an apartment in Helsinki.

- **Throughline question:** "How could AI heat seventy thousand homes in Helsinki, and what does my chatbot have to do
  with it?" Line 1 opens it; lines 2-5 answer the first "how?" (giant computers that get really hot) and sharpen it
  (forty kettles); line 6 says what usually happens (the fans blow it outside); line 7 turns it; lines 8-10 answer how
  Helsinki would catch it; lines 11-12 test it (not built yet, but done before, under a cathedral); line 13 closes it.
- **The turn (line 7): "But Helsinki WANTS that heat."** Everything before it is heat being wasted; everything after it
  is heat being kept.
- **The twist (line 12):** Helsinki has already done this, in 2010, from a data center under a cathedral (it sat in a
  wartime shelter cut into the rock, FL 6.10; the voice and the picture leave the shelter out, since a data center in a
  bunker that heats homes is video 5's twist). It changes "nice plan" into "they know it works", and it is the film's
  weirdest picture.
- **The one joke (line 5): "That's a lot of tea."** Forty kettles boiling non-stop: that's a lot of tea. One step, kind,
  literal; the owner's "directionally okay" tea, said simply. It also hands the next line its contrast: all that heat,
  and most data centers just blow it outside.
- **Ad or news?** News: a friend's "did you know" about a city (what's happening), then why it works and why it
  matters (the heat, the waste, the homes, your chatbot). No product, no company, no ask, no price.
- **Cold retell (the writer's own, of draft 2, in a regular viewer's words, for the panel and the critic to beat):**
  Helsinki wants to heat seventy thousand homes with heat from AI. AI runs on huge computers in data centers, and they
  get really hot: just one of them makes as much heat as forty kettles, which is a lot of tea, and most data centers just blow
  that heat outside with fans. Helsinki wants it: a new data center there would cool its computers with water, and the
  warm water goes through pipes under the streets into people's homes. It isn't built yet, but Helsinki already did
  this in 2010 with a data center under a cathedral, so from 2027 your chatbot could heat an apartment there.
