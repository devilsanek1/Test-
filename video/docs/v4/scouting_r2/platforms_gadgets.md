## 1. The $1,999 foldable iPhone that cracks in hours, and the $19 sticker Apple planned in advance
- **News:** The iPhone Duo, Apple's first foldable, went on sale Fri 23 Oct 2026 at $1,999. On Mon 26 Oct Bloomberg (Mark Gurman) reported a growing number of owners whose inner screen cracked along the crease after hours or days. Bloomberg verified "more than half a dozen" cases. Apple said it is "aware of an issue" on "a small number of units" and that owners should contact AppleCare for a replacement. Earlier, on 2 Oct, Apple's Kate Bergeron said the inner screen's top layer is "replaceable... you can peel off that layer and get a new one", for $19 with AppleCare+.
- **Twist:** A folding screen isn't really glass you can fold. It's a stack of thin, bendy layers, so it is built to wear out. Apple priced the swap for the top layer ahead of launch. The early cracks look like a separate problem, which reports put on the hinge rather than the panel.
- **Real person:** Surya Mangi (Allen, Texas): his inner screen cracked after about 30 hours (Bloomberg, via Yahoo Finance). Jonathan King: the inner screen is "now unusable."
- **So what:** Foldables are going mainstream. If you pay $2,000 for one, its screen is the part that wears, and your warranty and stock of replacements decide how long it lasts, not how careful you are.
- **Sources:** Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance, 26 Oct 2026, https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/apple-foldable-iphone-duo-draws-171331167.html ; ARY News, https://arynews.tv/iphone-duo-launch-hit-by-reports-of-broken-displays-and-hinge-problems ; HotHardware, 2 Oct 2026, https://hothardware.com/news/apple-19-fix-iphone-duo-screen ; TechRepublic, https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-iphone-duo-crease-foldable-display/
- **Risks:** We don't know how many phones are affected. Apple says "small number", so we must not call it widespread. The hinge cause is unconfirmed (I couldn't find the iFixit teardown directly). It risks feeling like a gadget review.

## 2. Your phone now asks your bank "is that really you calling?" and hangs up on the scammer
- **News:** On 14 Oct 2026 Google announced that Android's "verified financial calls" feature has blocked more than 3 million spoofed calls pretending to be Banco do Brasil and Itaú Unibanco since it launched in June. The feature first appeared 12 May 2026 with Revolut, Itaú and Nubank. It works like this: when a call appears to come from your bank, Android asks your signed-in bank app whether the bank is really calling, and if the answer is no, the phone ends the call. Banks can also flag numbers they never call out from. From early December, apps can no longer switch on call forwarding in the background (a trick scammers use to steal one-time codes).
- **Twist:** Caller ID can be faked, and that is the whole scam. So the fix doesn't check the number at all. It asks a second channel, the bank's own app, which a scammer can't fake.
- **Real person:** No named person. Google's blog post is bylined by Eugene Liderman (Director, Android Security & Privacy) but quotes nobody. Brazil's bank federation FEBRABAN reported a 195.7% rise in bank-impersonation scams over 12 months.
- **So what:** "Your bank is calling about suspicious activity" is the most common way people get emptied. For people with a participating bank, that call may now never ring. Everyone else can learn the same rule: hang up and call the number on your card.
- **Sources:** Google blog, 12 May 2026, https://blog.google/security/whats-new-in-android-security-privacy-2026/ ; The Next Web, Oct 2026, https://thenextweb.com/news/google-android-scam-protections-gemini ; Let's Money (BR), https://letsmoney.com.br/noticias/android-chamadas-verificadas-bb-itau/ ; Canaltech (BR), https://canaltech.com.br/apps/celulares-android-vao-bloquear-automaticamente-ligacoes-falsas-de-bancos/
- **Risks:** It's 13 days old. It's mostly Brazil so far and only works with participating banks. The 3M figure is Google's own number, reported second-hand. It could read as an ad for Google.

## 3. Smart glasses that recorded people without being asked, and why the EU says a tiny light isn't enough
- **News:** In mid-October owners of Ray-Ban Meta glasses reported, starting on Reddit r/MetaGlasses and then in Android Authority, Gizmodo (14 Oct) and Malwarebytes (20 Oct), that after a firmware update their glasses recorded conversations without the "Hey Meta" wake word, even with that option off. Meta said: "This issue has been resolved." On 13 Oct the European Data Protection Board said smart glasses must give clear warnings when recording, such as a bright LED or a sound, and that a phone notification isn't enough. Italy's Garante backed that on 14 Oct. Norway is preparing a proposal to ban AI glasses in schools and hospitals, and a major cruise line has banned them.
- **Twist:** The person being recorded isn't the customer. The only thing protecting them is a small white LED they may not notice, and in this bug it was the wearer who didn't know either.
- **Real person:** None named. The Reddit owner is anonymous, and Meta's statement is corporate.
- **So what:** You can be recorded and transcribed in a café or classroom by glasses that look normal, without the wearer meaning to. Europe's answer is to make recording obvious, not to ban it.
- **Sources:** Gizmodo, 14 Oct 2026, https://gizmodo.com/metas-smart-glasses-recording-without-consent-2000826018 ; Malwarebytes, 20 Oct 2026, https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/10/meta-says-it-fixed-smart-glasses-unprompted-eavesdropping ; Al Jazeera, 13 Oct 2026, https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/13/eu-watchdog-urges-smart-glasses-to-clearly-signal-when ; RTÉ, 13 Oct 2026, https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/1013/1595264-eu-smart-glasses-privacy/
- **Risks:** The story is 13 days old. Nobody has given a cause or a count of affected glasses, and we have no named person. We must not claim Meta kept or misused the audio.

## 4. One company's terms of service slowed a war's internet for a week
- **News:** For more than a week in mid-October 2026, Starlink and the military Starshield service were slowed in Ukraine. Ukraine's defence ministry said on 24 Oct that speeds were fully restored. On X that same Saturday, Elon Musk said SpaceX had restricted the service: "Ukraine military violated Starlink terms of service and used it for offensive drone strikes to kill people." Commanders reported lost drones and disrupted missions. Who ordered the slowdown is disputed: Zelenskyy pointed to Washington, Trump said he didn't make the call, and Rubio and Hegseth denied any administration involvement.
- **Twist:** The internet a whole army relies on runs under a consumer-style terms-of-service agreement, and a single setting can change it.
- **Real person:** Musk (quote above); Zelenskyy: "The speeds are now where they need to be."
- **Sources:** NPR, 24 Oct 2026, https://www.npr.org/2026/10/24/nx-s1-6015482/ukraine-starlink-working-after-disruptions ; PBS NewsHour, 24 Oct 2026, https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/ukraines-starlink-satellite-speeds-restored-after-a-week-of-front-line-disruption ; CNBC, 23 Oct 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/ukraine-says-starlink-restrictions-remain-despite-trump-assurances.html ; ABC News, https://abcnews.com/International/ukraine-starlink-satellite-access-fully-back-after-weeklong/story?id=137538401
- **Risks:** It's politically charged (Musk, Trump, war) and close to v2's military theme. It's far from viewers' wallets, and the facts about who decided are disputed. I'd rank it last.
