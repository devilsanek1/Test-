## 1. The promise, and the storytelling rules that shape the PICTURE
- **Promise:** "You've heard about it. Now actually understand it." Wanted reactions: "Wait, seriously?" / "Ohhh" / "now I
  get why that's a big deal" / "I need to send this to someone" (CB §1). Identity: a smart friend, edited like a great
  short documentary; "a premium hybrid of cinematic animation and precise explanatory motion graphics" (CB §3, §20).
- **Animation explains, it doesn't decorate:** it "does some of the explaining" with diagrams, comparisons, real examples
  and cinematic transitions, and "should replace unnecessary narration" (CB §20). Mistake 5 is "making the narrator carry
  the story" (CB Part 8); standard 9: "can animation make the insight more obvious?" A shot that only illustrates the
  sentence, or prints the narration's words on an object, is not explaining.
- **Something worth knowing every 3-5 s** (CB §13): the picture moves the understanding on at that rate too: a new fact,
  a turn, a consequence made visible. No beat re-uses an earlier frame without new information.
- **One story, not a list** (PB Script; CB §12): every shot follows from the last (joinable by "but / so / because"),
  building to a turn and the payoff; the understanding shift BEFORE -> "wait, really?" -> "oh, now I get it" (CB §10).
- **Literal beats clever** (PB Storyboard): recognisable things (a laptop that looks like a laptop, a building of
  servers, a plain labelled chart); **no physical metaphor the facts don't support**: show the real mechanism (PB; CG
  "The opening"). Every shot must read with the sound off (PB).
- **The payoff:** stated in words on frame 0, landed at the end; target 30-45 s and end close to the payoff (PB Hook).
- **Fair and true:** nonpartisan, no manufactured villains, never ridicule people affected (CB §17-19); scale shown as fact
  must be true or signposted (CS §1.5). Engagement only through subtle, defensible hooks: an open loop the payoff closes,
  "wait, seriously?" facts, a loop-friendly last line; never needy (CB "Engagement layer"). v1's saves 0.4 % / shares
  0.7 %: give people a picture worth sending (PB Performance log).
- **This film (fixed):** voice take 2, `public/v2/voice.wav`, 39.08 s, words `public/v2/voice_words.json` (lead 0.12 s).
  Anchors: "She quit Google." 0.12 · "contract" 2.60 · "for life." 4.84-5.32 · "You'd think" 6.12 · "But Google's staff"
  8.36 · "scared to speak out" 10.04-11.24 · "So why is she talking?" 13.24 (the open loop) · "military" 18.92 · "In April"
  20.52 · "any lawful government purpose" 24.04-25.16 · "can't veto" 26.36 · "But Google says" 28.20 · "of any kind" 32.52
  · "She says she can't" 33.08 · "talking about facts is not criticising" 36.44-39.08 (the callback; loop to frame 0).
  Frame-0 words (locked): **SHE SAYS: NO CRITICIZING GOOGLE. FOR LIFE.** + "Source: BBC News, 21 Oct 2026" (CL §1).
- **This film's truth rules on screen** (CL §2): "SHE SAYS" full size wherever the clause appears; "for life" never in
  quote marks; no count, no "wave", never "silenced/gagged", no legal claim; no war, weapons, Hegseth or Trump on frame 0,
  the cover or the hook; no Google logo near Maven or strikes, never imply Gemini picks targets; whenever "any lawful
  government purpose" is shown, the oversight clause is shown in full; BBC quotes exact, with the source line; she and
  Kirsch are generic figures, never traced from BBC photos; "Google" hand-lettered, never its logo; Google's reply gets
  its own beat with equal visual weight, and nothing implies Google confirmed or denied her clause.

## 2. The hook: frame 0, 3 s, 5 s, 10 s (the most polished part of the film)
- **Evidence:** v1's calm, pretty opening lost ~80 % of viewers by 3 s (first 45 min); at 24 h retention was ~76 % at 2 s,
  ~58 % at 5 s, ~43 % at 10 s, ~33 % at 15 s, then nearly flat: the leak is the first 15 s (PB Hook, Performance log).
  So polish the first 2 s and 5 s most, then the first 10 s; late quirks are acceptable (PB Hook).
- **Frame 0** (PB Hook; CG "The opening", measured on the owner's reference): the opening scene as bare ink lines, flat
  colour flooding in shape by shape within ~0.3 s; the payoff in words (the core line) on screen; never a title card,
  never a still frame. Both states must read: ink-only and fills-only (SB §3).
- **0-3 s:** voice from the first frames (ours at 0.12 s); **our own positive phone sound in the first second** (pop,
  ding, sent-swoosh family, `audio/tools/make_phone_sfx.py`; never Apple's or Instagram's) (PB Music and sound). The
  hook's claim made physical and escalating: **the expected thing shown, then reality overtaking it** (the reference: neat
  markers for 200,000 marchers, then a crowd pours past them on "half a million"; the keyword highlighted in the caption
  at that moment). Reality overtakes through the TRUE cause, each unit of cause one visible tick of the effect (CG
  "Show the real mechanism"). A new element every 3-6 frames (of 30 fps); one continuous opening shot, no cut until the
  idea has landed; camera only a slow ~5 % pull-out that gathers speed; the big scale move belongs later (CG). Nothing
  scale before 3 s unless the move IS the claim; never a silent scale opener (CS §5).
- **5 s ("for life", 5.32 s) and 10 s ("scared to speak out", 10.04 s):** the escalation must land by 5 s and the second
  jolt by 10 s: "a plain line needs the frame-0 image, the first-second sound and the second line to bring the jolt"
  (PB Taste signals). "Personal framings natural, never over the top" (PB Hook).
- **The loop:** the last frame returns to the bare-line frame 0 (with its caption), mid-motion, so the rewatch is seamless
  (PB Hook; MS §8 T-UNBUILD + LOOP: fills shrink, ink retracts, the opening's first motion starts 3-4 frames before the cut).

## 3. Visual style
- **Hand-drawn ink, flat colour, like reference 1; v1 is the look** (PB Visual style; CG "Why"). Full-bleed 9:16 world,
  the frame a window into it, no card (BR "Cinematic direction" 1). Base = "bright, warm, legible, charming editorial
  illustration with snappy pacing"; eye-level immersive framing most of the time (BR "Balance note").
- **Ink** (SB §3, §11): warm dark `#2B1B0A`-family, never black or coloured; weight by depth ~4.8 / 3.4 / 2.2 / 1.6 px at
  1080; round caps, tapered ends; loose architectural line: corners overshoot 3-15 px, structural lines run past, long lines
  broken into 2-4 segments, 0.3-1° off-axis; 20-40 % of contours left open (the fill finishes the shape). Lines VERY subtle
  and only where they carry form: never a uniform cartoon outline around every silhouette, never outlines on shadows,
  sky, ground or far things (PB Texture; PAL "Texture and lines"; CS 3b.7).
- **Fills** (SB §4): flat single colours; no gradients, blur, glow, haze, bloom or opacity stacking (CG "Not for us"); each
  fill misregistered 2-5 px off its ink (one offset per object); glass and windows 40-70 % partially filled; shade = a flat
  polygon one step darker; one light direction.
- **People** (SB §6; LD §4): back view or rear 3/4 always: head in the hair colour, neck, tombstone torso, one accessory
  each, neighbours never share hair and coat colour; extras smaller and in mid colours; faces only in a protagonist's one
  face beat; real people as generic figures, never real staff's likeness (CS §5 Respect); Arcane gives environments only,
  never faces (PB; CG "Not for us").
- **Palette = a family of tools, not a cage** (PB; PAL "The house palette family"; 9 palettes in `palettes.json`). One main
  palette per film, a sister palette at most for one deliberate shift (e.g. the scale beat). **DNA:** background soft and
  light (L* >= 75, C <= 20) over 50-80 % of the frame, made of many light fills, never one empty slab (CS 3b.6); soft mids;
  ink L* <= 25 at >= 7:1; **one hero** (C >= 30, 5-15 %, on the subject or the story's object), **one tinted dark anchor**
  (L* <= 40, ~5 %), **at most one hot pop** (C >= 50, 1-3 %, only "look here"); colours keep their jobs all film;
  dressing never takes the hero or the pop (LD §2). The playbook's "Cream & Cobalt for a serious story" was the dropped
  G4's pick: taste (a clean, calm page for serious news; a cold sister only for the scale beat), not a lock.
- **Warm, never sickly** (PB; CS 3b.6): no beige, muddy tan, olive-brown lines or grey; warmth = peach creams, terracotta,
  red-brown lines, a golden lamp pool, warm shadows, a saturated dusk blue outside. Shadows are colour (red-brown, plum,
  toward blue on cool surfaces), never grey; >= 10 % of the opposite temperature in every frame; saturation as confetti
  (many patches < 1 % of the frame in many hue families), never as fields; whites tinted; darks warm and rare.
- **Texture:** the film must work flat. Optional halftone as the very last step, <= 6 % tone, on a few chosen elements
  only (never the background, people, small or fast things, captions); locked to the world, never boiling (PB; PAL; CS 3b.7).
- **Edges / paper:** the owner liked `paint-subtle` (white paper, colour painted on, ink running past it) (ED §0, status),
  but **never at the top of the frame or beside the caption band** (PB: read as a screen recording). The caption block
  always sits inside the colour; keep rects for the heroes and Pip (ED §0, §5.1). A final-stage pass, never a fix.
- **Captions** (PB Captions; CS §5; CG notation): burned in (platform auto-captions off); Patrick Hand
  (`public/fonts/CaptionHand.woff2`), max 2 lines, centred; **pop in place** (pages, lines and highlight boxes), never a
  clipped roll-in or slot reveal; one peach-type highlight box per line at most, leading its word by ~0.1 s (SB §10; MS
  §4, §7); in screen space, never on a camera layer, never clipped by a wiper, never shrinking; a change at least every
  1.5 s; block **y 317-556, x 95-945 on ONE flat calm field** (sky or a flat facade fill, L* >= 70-75): no ink, windows,
  wires, roof tops, heads or props there (CG Rules 2; CS 3b.2; LD §8). Dark ink on light; cream text on a dark sky (BR 2).
- **Phone safe zones:** key reads in x 120-880, y 290-1250; the bottom ~35 % and the right rail (x > 780 from y 840) are
  platform UI: masses may run under them, figures, labels and the pop never (CS §5; LD §8).

## 4. Aliveness and density; cozy and scale
- **"Every scene must FEEL ALIVE: that is the key, not an object count"** (PB Visual style; N3 G5: "way too empty ...
  those feel a lot more alive"). A dense, specific world, people near and far, layered depth, a recurring living character,
  ambient life that never stops. Big flat empty fields and an object alone on a blank wall fail even in a draft.
- **Fullness first, motion second** (LD §0): the world's density makes a frame full even when little moves (the riverside
  "doesn't feel empty at all" at ~1 event/s). Judge the PAUSED frame at phone size beside the bar first; if a still looks
  empty, motion won't fix it. Fix in order: the world (things, layers, thin lines), then people (still ones count), then
  idle motion. Never put motion on a void to liven it up. Checks: no empty patch over ~200 px outside the caption band and
  the one quiet field; the biggest flat region <= 20 % apart from the quiet field; the quiet field is ONE band at a frame
  edge (sky, water, floor), still patterned.
- **What fills a frame** (LD §0-3): many small things, each size step down holding more; stacked by overlap (a new surface
  every few dozen px down a column); several colours in every small patch, repeats varied (each house, awning, coat); thin
  lines are the cheapest fullness (wires, poles, antennas, rails, mullions; never in the caption band); specific dressing (a
  pharmacy cross, a clock, a cart sign), 8+ items per shot from the setting's inventory (LD §3); at least 2 elements cut by
  a frame edge, the world going on past it. Per frame (LD §1): a wide holds 12+ props, 3-4 planes, 10 to 300+ people; an
  interior 8+, 2-3 planes, 2+ people (a silhouette in a window counts); an insert 5+ cut by the edges and a row of backs.
- **Hierarchy keeps the read** (LD §2): the subject keeps the strongest contrast, the hero colour and a calm 30-60 px ring;
  dressing one value step off its ground; ambient motion small, slow, low-contrast, off the subject's path.
- **Information lives in the world** (LD §7): a chart, number or quote on a real object in a real place with people
  looking at it (a noticeboard on a street wall, a billboard, an A-frame, a shop window, a phone in a hand), straight-on,
  level and big; never a card on a blank wall. v1 did it everywhere (boards, billboards, sun, crowds of backs).
- **Two alive profiles, one per shot** (LD §0, §6): **BUSY** (explainer beats, the opening): flat <= 40 %, objects >= 105
  /Mpx, detail >= 34 %; >= 5 medium events/s per film, >= 3.5 per shot, no gap over 1 s, static run <= 0.4 s; always on:
  weather, smoke or steam, clouds >= 10 px/s, a window switching every 0.6-1.5 s, people walking and bobbing, Pip's idle,
  one small loop per shot; a big burst (10+ items in 4 frames) every 6-10 s on a number or hero word, then <= 0.5 s of
  settling, never stillness (MS §6, §9). **DENSE-CALM** (cozy holds): flat <= 30 %, detail >= 33 %, objects >= 120;
  movers >= 2 at all times, empty <= 5 %, steady (cv <= 0.3, or one slow swell per 6-7 s), passing sideways at real
  speeds, half the people at rest, nothing coming at the viewer, one quiet ambient element always on (CS 3b.4). Secondary
  motion: the world reacts within 0.1-0.3 s (birds lift, heads turn, Pip reacts) (LD §6). One breath beat per film,
  <= 2.5 s, never frozen (LD §8). Meter: `scripts/qa/density.py` (+ `--cozy`), a warning signal, never the verdict (LD §9).
- **Cozy = held, not shut** (PB; CS §1, 3b): walls on 2-3 sides, one opening the subject faces, the threat visible but
  outside; enclosing mass 35-70 % of the frame, >= 40 % of the perimeter closed, the far end closed; doses NOOK, LANE,
  BALCONY (35-55° down, a lid of canopy and facades), RIVERSIDE (level across water) (CS 3b.2). One low lamp, warm pool
  one side, cool window the other; abundance at the edges, a calm centre that is never empty; >= 1 sign of care (steaming
  mug, plant, blanket); rounded inside, hard outside (CS §3). Cozy >= 50 % of the runtime outside chart inserts (CS §1.3).
  **Coziness belongs to the people affected, never to the threat; break it once, at the turn**; on the heaviest line strip
  the comfort (no cute props near real harm) (CS §1.5, §3 Shape).
- **Scale, occasionally, and it must feel GREAT** (PB; CS §1.3): 1-2 moments (v2's plan: 1), 1.5-4 s each, <= 20 % of the
  runtime, never two scale wides in a row, a beat of agency within ~5 s of a small-making beat (CS §1.4). The 11 cues and
  checklist (CG "Scale cues"): nothing ends inside the frame | 5-7 planes, each 2.2-3x smaller | the far band packed,
  detail peaking just under the horizon | flat atmospheric steps (0 / 12 / 22 / 40 / 65 % toward the sky, ink full -> none)
  | feet 2-3 figure heights below the horizon, then the drop | one huge-yet-far landmark rising 1.1-1.4 F over the horizon
  | a hero cloud >= 1.0 W behind it | huge means slow (clouds 0-6 px/s) | tiny anchors, lines leading up | 3 tiers of
  life, never 1 s still (birds, a pennant, steam), none inside the city mass. Daylight colours only; no night-black, haze,
  god rays, airships or VFX (CG "Not for us"). Respect: camera at or below a person's eye level; no glow, rim or hero
  colour on the thing under critique; a high angle only on a crowd (CS §5).

## 5. Motion and camera
- **60 fps** for production renders ("smoother and more crispy", PB Motion); everything animates by time (seconds), so
  ambient motion moves every frame; drawn poses may stay stepped (12-24 drawings/s) while the camera glides; deterministic
  seeds (PL "Frame rate"; LD §6).
- **No fades, ever:** every change is a pop, scale, wipe, slide or draw-on; nothing animates opacity (MS §0; MAS "No fades").
- **Build grammar** (MS §3, §6, §10): strokes draw on in 0.10-0.20 s, starting ~1 frame (of 30) apart; fills land 0.13-0.27 s
  after their outline (or fill-first, lines over flat silhouettes); rises 0.2-0.33 s, out-expo, from behind a front mass;
  counting builds staggered ~0.10-0.17 s, texture builds 1 frame, the big number a burst; captions and highlights pop.
- **Sync** (MS §7): pictures lead the voice ~0.1 s (an element starts ~0.10 s before the word that names it; a big build
  starts 0.25-0.40 s early so it completes ON the word); hand-drawn marks and character beats land on the word (±0.05 s);
  one emphasis per caption line, one big visual beat per sentence; ~2 medium visual events per spoken word.
- **A reference move gives the SHAPE, never the speed** (PB Motion): copy path, framing, reveal order and easing shape, then
  retime for the reel (usually faster). Detailed layers <= ~15 px/frame at the render rate (strobe limit; readability at
  1x is the test) (PL "Frame rate").
- **Camera dose** (CG Rules 3, 5; CS §1.4, 3b.5): ONE big move per video (S1 rise-over or S5 pull-back crane, never both);
  other story moves push 3-8 % on a line; cozy holds locked or a slow 2D float (8-15 px/s, legs turning back within ±12
  px), **never a zoom in a hold** (a 1-4 px/s push reads frozen); S3/S4 breathers a 1.5-2 % pull-out. Physical camera
  (crane, dolly, truck, tilt), ease in, land over the last third; no roll, no compound moves, no cut mid-move.
- **The S5 pull-back crane (the owner's favourite, "it has pretty much everything")** (PB; CG S5): the film's one big reveal,
  from the small world to the vast system: dolly back + crane up + a ~70-80° arc round two small backs on ONE ease,
  cubic-bezier(0.55, 0.03, 0.50, 1.0); the figures are the pivot; suspense scheduled by progress (a bright slot in their
  eyeline, a dormer hiding the landmark, a foreground wipe at peak speed, a light burst before the landmark lands); ~3.3 s
  default at 60 fps (156 f move + 42 f hold; 2-5 s range), landing hold >= 0.5 s; the end frame runs the scale cues.
- **Transitions for a full-bleed world** (BR 4; MS §8; CS K9): a camera move that carries one scene into the next, a
  match-cut (the sun, the horizon, a shared flat shape zoomed through), a foreground wipe, a push through a window; the
  un-build to bare lines for the loop. The card flip is retired. They live in voice pauses >= 0.3 s with their midpoint on
  the new sentence's first word (MS §7.4); reference 1 changes scene every 3.4-6.4 s (MS §2).
- **Cuts on the beat** (PB Hook: "cuts locked to the beat"): bend the bed so cuts land on beats (`scripts/qa/beatsync.py
  --fit-tempo`, `audio/tools/beatlock.py` half-beat) (SP v5). Every frame >= 8/10, no dead frames (PB).

## 6. Pip, the channel's living character (in EVERY shot, like reference 1's cat)
- **Role:** the recurring living character, one in every shot; reference 1 had the cat (and the sun), v1 had Pip (and the
  sun) (LD §5; PB Visual style); the profile identity is Pip (avatar `deliverables/profile/pip_power_button.png`, PB). The
  dropped v2 pass used "her cat, in her home only": not the house rule.
- **Jobs** (LD §5; MS §5): (1) perch on or beside the subject and watch it (`sit-back`, right or middle third); (2) point
  the eye: the head turns to the next subject a few frames before the voice names it; (3) react on a stressed word (ear
  flick, hop, `trunk-up`, `ears-cover`, `shrug`), at most one big reaction per shot, never on the subject's own beat; (4)
  idle always (blink every ~2.6-5 s, ears, tail, trunk sway, breath ~3.2 s); (5) carry continuity: hop or fall across
  every cut and the loop, as the cat did across every transition; (6) sleep or wait in the calm beat; (7) in a scale wide,
  a simple >= 40 px silhouette beside the tiny figures, as a scale anchor.
- **Tone:** reactions fit a serious story (worried, `ears-cover`, watching), never a cute gag near real harm or ridicule
  of anyone affected (CS §3 Shape; CB §17).
- **Design rules** (MAS §1-5; SB §9): an original small periwinkle elephant (`#8D90CC`, body plan: baby proportions, big
  round ears, head tuft, teddy-bear soles; no PHP lettering, no logo copy); its colour is reserved for Pip only; in each
  film a colourway that stays its own hue, ΔE76 >= 20 from every ground it sits on, never the hero or the pop colour (LD
  §5; colours in `src/lib/mascot/colors.ts`). Ink like the cat: ~3.4 px tapered, outline open at the head top and base;
  fill misregistered 3 px right / 2 px down; 3 back wrinkles + 2 haunch arcs. Size: scale 0.9-1.0 (~86-96 px sitting) in
  wides and mediums, 1.8-2x in close-ups, allowed 0.75-1.35; never in the caption band or the right rail, never over the
  subject. Built with `Elephant` / `ElephantTimeline` / `hop` / `popIn` / `drawIn` / `unbuild` (`src/lib/mascot`); poses
  `sit-back`, `sit-front` (trunk-dangle gag), `stand-side`, `walk-side`, `peek`, `trunk-up`, `hold`, `ears-cover`, `shrug`,
  `sleep`; enters by draw-on or pop, leaves by un-build, never a fade.

## 7. Sound and music
- **The voice is the show** and always on top (PB; SP §7.1). Both references sell scale with picture and voice alone (no
  music, no SFX): sound stays discreet (SP §1). The content must still work listened to without watching (CB §21).
- **Music: cinematic but subtle, especially at the start**; the voice and the hook carry the opening (PB). v1's approved
  bed: felt-piano pulse and motif, quiet low strings, air; the hook "air only"; music under the voice median ~27 dB, never
  closer than ~12 dB; a -14 dB side-chain duck; <= 2 soft accents, no trailer drums, orchestral swells or booms; a stop in
  the pulse is itself the effect (SP v5, §3, §7.6). Avoid dramatic or overly emotional music (CB §21).
- **SFX** (PB; SP §7): MORE than v1, especially our own positive phone sounds (pop, notification ding, message-sent swoosh),
  one in the first second; each lands on a visual hit (pictures lead, sound follows the visual onset), never on the middle
  of a key word; one sound per idea; as small as the gesture (a label pops `pop_high`, a building lands `pop_low`, a line
  draws a soft pencil); story beats get their special sound once, callbacks 3 dB softer; SFX >= 8 dB under the voice
  (`mix.py --sfx-min-gap`); ambiences (`city_bed`, `air_bed`) only under a real vista, 20-25 dB under. Cozy holds: room
  tone and weather behind glass 18-24 dB under, soft close foley; the one breach opens the filter, then near-silence (CS §3).
- **Mix and master:** -14 LUFS integrated, -1 dBTP (v1 -1.2), 48 kHz; music, SFX tails and dynamics circular so the loop
  seam is clean; check once on phone speakers and once on earbuds (PB; SP §4, §6). Kit: `audio/sfx/`, `audio/mix.py`,
  `audio/cues.json` (re-derive every cue from the FINAL beats, never a storyboard) (`WRAPUP.md` Sound).

## 8. Process lessons for a direct-to-final production
- **A rejected cut restarts clean:** no sketch gate, the finished film directly, full creative freedom inside these rules;
  never patch or reuse the rejected cut (PB Process; N2 2026-10-27). Locked means dropped: prompts never read the archive.
- **v3 G5, "way too empty ... the quality and smoothness is not there"** (N3; LD §0, §9; CS 3b.5): flat 61 %, detail 16 %,
  63 objects/Mpx (v1: 32 % / 45 % / 141); nobody in 9 of 14 shots; 31 % of its moving pixels sat on void; centred 1.4 %
  push-ins moved ~2 px/s at the edge and read frozen; a 540x960 render blurred the dressing. Lessons: fullness first,
  people in every shot, a float not a push, the owner's cut at full 1080x1920.
- **v3 G3:** "what does the AI mountain signify?", "why is the data center consuming the buildings?" -> literal shots, the
  real mechanism, a sound-off cold read before the owner sees anything (N3; PB Storyboard).
- **The rejected v2 cut (the owner gave no reasons; this is OUR read of its frames, not the owner's words):** it PASSED the
  meter (6.5 events/s, flat 19 %, detail 46 %, `density.py` on `deliverables/v2/archive/v2_g5_blocking.mp4`) and was still
  rejected, so the meter is never the bar. What it was: **tableaux**: one room seen four times, an office, a top-down desk
  four times; people seated FACING the camera with faces and scribble speech bubbles; a document card whose typed lines
  repeat the narration for ~10 s, so the text explained and the animation did not; **stillness of idea** (ambient motes
  ticked, but the middle third showed the same desk frame across four lines); **off the house look**: uniform cartoon
  outlines, a saturated violet/cobalt wash over whole sets instead of a light page with a 5-15 % hero, almost no v1
  architecture, streets, sky band, sun or crowds of backs; **the living character** was a cat confined to her home, not
  Pip; **the one scale beat** was a static top-down building with none of the great-scale cues. Fix for this production:
  a WORLD (exteriors and interiors that connect), information in the world, people from behind, Pip everywhere, a
  mechanism that moves, each beat a new picture.
- **Assembler lessons** (G5B §8-9): one kit with a consistency contract (one model each for her, Pip, every hero prop, the
  sets), shown at EVERY size it is used, fixed before lanes start; life the eye cannot see is not life (read per-frame
  movers, not the mean); life crossing lettering misreads ("Googlz"); a cropped figure loses its shape; scribbled speech
  reads as an empty form; people need a visible "who"; lanes add a zoom "breathe" to holds by habit (forbid it in every
  brief); the last shot's builder owns the loop seam (no solid blocks, no near-empty frames); do the BARE sound-off cold
  read at phone size on the first full assembly; fold fixes back by pixel diff.
- **Without a sketch gate, decide the shape on paper and prove it in pixels:** competing treatments judged against this

  LD "Approved bar").
- **Cost discipline that still applies** (PL "What video 1 actually cost", anti-waste 2-5): small checkpointed tasks,
  commit after each agent, ~4 agents at once, lane renders at 0.33-0.5 scale, ~8 images per session, one combined review
  round per shot (max 6 findings) then fix and stop; no review loops on things that will still change.
- **Delivery** (PB Process; PK): the owner gets ONE film, labelled with its stage, as a download (previews may play muted);
  1080x1920 at 60 fps, H.264 High, AAC 48 kHz, ~10-12 Mbps or CRF 14, +faststart (a 1440x2560 master for Shorts); cover
  from the hook frame; AI label on; captions burned in; cite the BBC source in the caption and pinned comment.

## 9. Acceptance checklist: a finished film must answer YES to every question
1. Frame 0: the opening scene as bare ink lines, colour flooding in by ~0.3 s, the core-line words and source on screen?
2. Voice from the first frames, and our own positive phone sound inside the first second?
3. Does the opening show the expected thing, then reality overtaking it by the "for life" beat (~5.3 s), through the true
   mechanism, with a second jolt by ~10 s?
4. Is the opening one continuous, never-still shot (something new every few frames) until the hook's idea has landed?
5. Sound off, at phone size: can a cold viewer say what every shot shows and retell the story as a story?
6. Does the animation explain (a mechanism, a comparison, a real example) rather than illustrate or print the narration?
7. Does the picture add something worth knowing every 3-5 s, with no beat re-using a frame without new information?
8. Beside v1 and reference 1, is it unmistakably the house look: loose warm ink (open, overshooting, tapered), flat
   misregistered fills, no gradients, glow, blur, opacity or uniform cartoon outlines?
9. Are people seen from behind, anonymous and varied, with real people as generic figures and at most one face beat?
10. One palette (+ at most one sister shift) with a light soft page, a hero at 5-15 % on the story's object, a ~5 % anchor
    and at most one 1-3 % pop on "look here"; dressing never in the hero or pop colour?
11. Warm, never sickly: no beige, muddy tan, olive or grey; coloured shadows; >= 10 % opposite temperature per frame?
12. Paused at phone size beside the bar, is every frame full: no hole over ~200 px outside the caption band and the one
    quiet field, no flat region over 20 %, nothing alone on a blank wall?
13. Does every shot have people (still ones count) and specific, varied dressing, with >= 2 things cut by the frame edge?
14. Is each shot BUSY or DENSE-CALM and does it meet its row (BUSY >= 5 events/s film-wide, >= 3.5 per shot, no gap over
    1 s; cozy: >= 2 movers always, a locked or 8-15 px/s floating camera)?
15. Is there never a second with nothing visible moving, and no life crossing lettering or the caption band?
16. Is Pip in every shot, in its own colourway, 86-130 px, perched in the right or middle third, out of the caption band,
    the right rail and the subject?
17. Does Pip watch, lead the eye before the voice, react at most once per shot, idle always, hop across cuts and the loop,
    and stay serious near the military beat?
18. Does every number, quote or clause live on a real object in a real place with people looking at it, straight-on and big?
19. Truth on screen: "SHE SAYS" full size; "for life" never quoted; exact BBC quotes with the source line; the oversight
    clause with "any lawful government purpose"; Google's reply at equal visual weight; no logos; no war imagery in the hook?
20. Respect: camera at or below people's eye level, no glow or hero colour on the thing under critique, coziness given to
    the people affected and broken at most once?
21. Do cozy holds make >= 50 % of the non-insert runtime, enclosed (mass 35-70 %, one opening, a sign of care), with no
    zoom in any hold?
22. Is there one scale moment (two at most), 1.5-4 s, that feels GREAT on all the scale cues (the world keeps going,
    5-7 planes, a huge-yet-far landmark, a huge slow cloud, tiny anchors, 3 tiers of life)?
23. Is there exactly one big camera move (S5 or S1), its shape from the reference, retimed to 2-5 s, landing on >= 0.5 s
    of hold, with no roll, compound move or cut mid-move?
24. 60 fps, everything animated by time, no opacity fade anywhere, motion readable at 1x and strobe-free?
25. Do pictures lead the voice by ~0.1 s, marks and Pip's beats land on the word, and transitions sit in pauses with
    their midpoint on the new sentence?
26. Is every transition a full-bleed device (camera carry, match-cut, foreground wipe, push-through), with cuts on beats?
27. Captions: burned in, popping in place, <= 2 lines, <= 1 highlight per line, on one flat field in y 317-556 with
    nothing drawn there, changing at least every 1.5 s?
28. Are all key reads inside x 120-880, y 290-1250, clear of the bottom UI and the right rail?
29. If edges or texture are used: paint-subtle, never at the top or beside the captions; halftone <= 6 % on a few elements;
    does the film still work flat?
30. Does the last frame return to the bare-line frame 0 mid-motion, with no solid block or empty frame at the seam, and
    does the last line flow back into the first?
31. Is the music subtle (most of all in the opening), >= 12 dB under the voice at every word, with <= 2 soft accents?
32. Does every SFX sit on a visual hit, >= 8 dB under the voice, one sound per idea?
33. Mix -14 LUFS / -1 dBTP, A/V in sync, a clean loop seam, checked on phone speakers and earbuds?
34. Does the film end close to the payoff (39 s of voice plus the loop's un-build), every frame >= 8/10, no dead frames?
35. Is there one image a viewer would screenshot or send ("wait, seriously?")?
36. Rendered at full 1080x1920 60 fps, gate-checked (duration, sync, captions in the band), delivered as a download with
    its stage named, and nothing reused from the dropped first production?
