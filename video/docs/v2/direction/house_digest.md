## 1. The promise, and the storytelling rules that shape the PICTURE
- **Promise:** "You've heard about it. Now actually understand it." Wanted: "Wait, seriously?" / "now I get why that's a
  big deal" / "I need to send this to someone" (CB §1). A smart friend edited like a great short documentary; "a premium
  hybrid of cinematic animation and precise explanatory motion graphics" (CB §3, §20).
- **Animation explains, never decorates:** it does some of the explaining (diagrams, comparisons, real examples, cinematic
  transitions) and replaces narration (CB §20); "making the narrator carry the story" is mistake 5 (CB Part 8). A shot that
  only illustrates the sentence, or prints the narration's words on an object, explains nothing.
- **Something worth knowing every 3-5 s** (CB §13): the picture moves understanding on at that rate (a fact, a turn, a
  consequence made visible); no beat re-uses a frame without new information.
- **One story, not a list** (PB Script; CB §10, §12): each shot follows from the last (but / so / because) to a turn and the
  payoff. **Literal beats clever:** recognisable things, no metaphor the facts don't support, show the real mechanism; every
  shot reads with the sound off (PB Storyboard; CG "The opening").
- **Payoff** stated in words on frame 0 and landed at the end; 30-45 s, ending close to the payoff (PB Hook).
- **Fair and true:** nonpartisan, no manufactured villains, never ridicule people affected (CB §17-19). Engagement only via
  subtle, defensible hooks (an open loop, quotable "wait, seriously?" facts, a loop-friendly last line), never needy (CB
  "Engagement layer"); v1's saves 0.4 % / shares 0.7 %: give people a picture worth sending (PB Performance log).
- **This film (fixed):** voice take 2 `public/v2/voice.wav`, 39.08 s, words `public/v2/voice_words.json` (lead 0.12 s).
  Anchors (s): "She quit Google." 0.12 · "for life." 4.84-5.32 · "You'd think" 6.12 · "But Google's staff" 8.36 · "scared to
  speak out" 10.04 · "So why is she talking?" 13.24 (the open loop) · "military" 18.92 · "In April" 20.52 · "any lawful
  government purpose" 24.04 · "can't veto" 26.36 · "But Google says" 28.20 · "of any kind" 32.52 · "talking about facts is
  not criticising" 36.44-39.08 (the callback, then loop). Frame-0 words, locked: **SHE SAYS: NO CRITICIZING GOOGLE. FOR
  LIFE.** + "Source: BBC News, 21 Oct 2026" (CL §1).
- **Truth on screen** (CL §2): "SHE SAYS" full size wherever the clause appears; "for life" never in quote marks; no count
  or "wave", never "silenced/gagged", no legal claim; no war, weapons, Hegseth or Trump on frame 0, cover or hook; no Google
  logo near Maven or strikes; "any lawful government purpose" always with the full oversight clause; BBC quotes exact with
  the source line; she is a generic figure, never traced; "Google" hand-lettered, never its logo; Google's reply its own
  beat at equal visual weight, never confirming or denying her clause.

## 2. The hook: frame 0, 3 s, 5 s, 10 s (polished most)
- **Evidence:** v1's calm, pretty opening lost ~80 % by 3 s (first 45 min); at 24 h ~76 % stayed at 2 s, ~58 % at 5 s,
  ~43 % at 10 s, ~33 % at 15 s, then nearly flat: the leak is the first 15 s. Polish the first 2 s and 5 s most, then
  10 s; late quirks are fine (PB Hook, Performance log; owner 2026-10-25).
- **Frame 0** (PB Hook; CG "The opening", measured on the owner's reference): the opening scene as bare ink lines, flat
  colour flooding in shape by shape within ~0.3 s, the core line in words on screen; never a title card or a still frame.
  Ink-only and fills-only states must both read (SB §3).
- **0-3 s:** voice from the first frames (ours: 0.12 s); **our own positive phone sound in the first second** (pop, ding,
  sent-swoosh family, `audio/tools/make_phone_sfx.py`, never Apple's or Instagram's) (PB Music and sound). The claim made
  physical and escalating: **the expected thing, then reality overtaking it** (reference: markers for 200,000 marchers,
  then a crowd pours past them on "half a million", the keyword highlighted then), through the TRUE cause, one unit of
  cause = one visible tick of the effect (CG). Something new every 3-6 frames (of 30 fps); one continuous shot until the
  idea lands; a slow ~5 % pull-out only (the big move comes later); no scale before 3 s unless the move IS the claim (CS §5).
- **5 s and 10 s:** the escalation lands on "for life" (5.32 s), the second jolt on "scared to speak out" (~10 s): "a plain
  line needs the frame-0 image, the first-second sound and the second line to bring the jolt" (PB Taste signals).
- **The loop:** the last frame returns to the bare-line frame 0 with its caption, mid-motion (PB Hook; MS §8 T-UNBUILD:
  fills shrink, ink retracts, the opening's first motion starts 3-4 frames before the cut).

## 3. Visual style
- **Hand-drawn ink, flat colour, like reference 1; v1 is the look** (PB Visual style; CG "Why"). A full-bleed 9:16 world,
  the frame a window into it (BR 1); base = "bright, warm, legible, charming editorial illustration with snappy pacing",
  eye-level immersive framing most of the time (BR "Balance note").
- **Ink** (SB §3, §11): warm dark `#2B1B0A` family, never black; weight by depth ~4.8 / 3.4 / 2.2 / 1.6 px at 1080; round
  caps, tapered ends; loose architectural line: corners overshoot 3-15 px, structural lines run past, long lines broken,
  0.3-1° off-axis; 20-40 % of contours open. Lines VERY subtle, only where they carry form: never a uniform cartoon outline
  round every silhouette, never on shadows, sky, ground or far things (PB Texture; PAL "Texture and lines"; CS 3b.7).
- **Fills** (SB §4): flat; no gradient, blur, glow, haze, bloom or opacity (CG "Not for us"); misregistered 2-5 px off the
  ink (one offset per object); windows 40-70 % partly filled; shade = a flat polygon one step darker; one light direction.
- **People small and simple, like reference 1** (PB Visual style, owner 2026-10-27: "don't use big models of people"):
  figures that live in the world (crowds, small full figures, backs of heads, simple round heads), **never big character
  models or close-up faces** (this overrides LD §4's protagonist face beat). Back view or rear 3/4: head in the hair
  colour, tombstone torso, one accessory each, neighbours never sharing hair and coat (SB §6; LD §4); real people generic,
  never real staff's likeness (CS §5); Arcane gives environments only (PB; CG).
- **Palette = a family of tools** (PB; PAL; 9 palettes in `palettes.json`): one per film, a sister palette at most for one
  deliberate shift. **DNA:** a soft light page (L* >= 75, C <= 20) over 50-80 % of the frame, made of many light fills,
  never one slab (CS 3b.6); ink L* <= 25 at >= 7:1; **one hero** (C >= 30, 5-15 %, on the subject or story object), **one
  tinted anchor** (L* <= 40, ~5 %), **at most one pop** (C >= 50, 1-3 %, "look here" only); colours keep their jobs all
  film; dressing never takes the hero or the pop (LD §2). PB's "Cream & Cobalt for a serious story" was the dropped G4's
  pick: read it as taste (a clean, calm page for serious news, a cold sister only for the scale beat), not a lock.
- **Warm, never sickly** (PB; CS 3b.6): no beige, muddy tan, olive-brown lines or grey; warmth = peach creams, terracotta,
  red-brown lines, a golden lamp pool, a saturated dusk blue outside. Shadows are colour (red-brown, plum, bluish on cool
  surfaces); >= 10 % opposite temperature per frame; saturation as confetti (patches < 1 %, many hues), never fields;
  whites tinted, darks warm and rare.
- **Texture and edges, last and optional; the film works flat:** halftone <= 6 % tone on a few elements only (never
  background, people, small or fast things, captions), locked, never boiling (PB; PAL; CS 3b.7). `paint-subtle` edges are
  liked (ED §0) but **never at the top or beside the caption band** (PB: it read as a screen recording); keep rects for
  the heroes and Pip (ED §5.1).
- **Captions** (PB Captions; CS §5; CG notation): burned in (auto-captions off); Patrick Hand
  (`public/fonts/CaptionHand.woff2`), <= 2 lines, centred; **pop in place** (pages, lines, highlights), never a clipped
  roll-in; <= 1 peach highlight per line, leading its word by ~0.1 s (SB §10; MS §7); screen space, never on a camera
  layer, clipped by a wiper or shrinking; a change at least every 1.5 s; block **y 317-556, x 95-945 on ONE flat calm
  field** (sky or a flat facade, L* >= 70-75) with no ink, windows, wires, roofs, heads or props in it (CG Rules 2; CS 3b.2;
  LD §8). Dark ink on light; cream text on a dark sky (BR 2).
- **Phone safe zones:** key reads in x 120-880, y 290-1250; the bottom ~35 % and the right rail (x > 780 from y 840) are
  platform UI: masses may run under them, figures, labels and the pop never (CS §5; LD §8).

## 4. Aliveness and density; cozy and scale
- **"Every scene must FEEL ALIVE: that is the key, not an object count"** (PB; N3 G5: "way too empty ... those feel a lot
  more alive"): a dense specific world, people near and far, layered depth, the living character, ambient life that never
  stops. A big flat empty field or an object alone on a blank wall fails, even in a draft.
- **Fullness first, motion second** (LD §0): density makes a frame full even when little moves (the riverside "doesn't feel
  empty at all" at ~1 event/s). Judge the PAUSED frame at phone size beside the bar first; motion never fixes an empty
  still. Fix in order: the world (things, layers, thin lines), people (still ones count), then idle motion; never motion on
  a void. Checks: no empty patch over ~200 px outside the caption band and the one quiet field (ONE band at a frame edge:
  sky, water, floor, still patterned); the biggest other flat region <= 20 %.
- **What fills** (LD §0-3): many small things, stacked by overlap; several colours per small patch, repeats varied; thin
  lines (wires, poles, antennas, rails; never in the caption band); specific dressing, 8+ items per shot; >= 2 things cut
  by a frame edge. Per frame (LD §1): a wide 12+ props, 3-4 planes, 10-300+ people; an interior 8+, 2+ people (a window
  silhouette counts); an insert 5+ props and a row of backs. The subject keeps the strongest contrast, the hero colour and
  a calm 30-60 px ring; ambient motion small, slow, low-contrast, off its path (LD §2, §6).
- **Information lives in the world** (LD §7): a chart, number or quote on a real object in a real place with people
  looking (a street noticeboard, a billboard, an A-frame, a shop window, a phone in a hand), straight-on, level, big; never
  a card on a blank wall. v1 did this everywhere.
- **Two profiles, one per shot** (LD §0, §6; MS §6, §9): **BUSY** (explainer beats, the opening): flat <= 40 %, objects
  >= 105/Mpx, detail >= 34 %; >= 5 medium events/s per film, >= 3.5 per shot, no gap over 1 s; always on: weather or steam,
  clouds, a window switching every 0.6-1.5 s, people walking, Pip's idle, one small loop per shot; a burst (10+ items in 4
  frames) every 6-10 s on a number or hero word. **DENSE-CALM** (cozy holds): flat <= 30 %, detail >= 33 %, objects >= 120;
  >= 2 movers always, crossing sideways at real speeds, half the people at rest, nothing coming at us, one quiet ambient
  element on (CS 3b.4). The world reacts within 0.1-0.3 s; one breath beat per film, <= 2.5 s, never frozen (LD §6, §8).
  Meter: `scripts/qa/density.py` (+ `--cozy`), a warning signal, never the verdict (LD §9).
- **Cozy = held, not shut** (PB; CS §1, §3, 3b): walls on 2-3 sides, one opening the subject faces, the threat visible but
  outside; enclosing mass 35-70 % of the frame, >= 40 % of the perimeter closed; doses NOOK, LANE, BALCONY (35-55° down
  under a lid of canopy and facades), RIVERSIDE (level across water). One low lamp, warm pool one side, cool window the
  other; abundance at the edges, a calm never-empty centre; >= 1 sign of care; rounded inside, hard outside. Cozy >= 50 %
  of the runtime outside chart inserts (CS §1.3). **Coziness belongs to the people affected, never the threat; break it
  once, at the turn**; on the heaviest line strip the comfort, no cute props near real harm (CS §1.5, §3).
- **Scale, occasionally, and it must feel GREAT** (PB; CS §1.3-1.4): 1-2 moments (v2's plan: 1), 1.5-4 s, <= 20 % of the
  runtime, never two scale wides in a row, a beat of agency within ~5 s. The cues (CG "Scale cues"): nothing ends inside
  the frame | 5-7 planes, each 2.2-3x smaller | the far band packed, detail peaking under the horizon | flat atmospheric
  steps (0 / 12 / 22 / 40 / 65 % toward the sky, ink full -> none) | feet 2-3 figure heights under the horizon, then the drop
  | one huge-yet-far landmark 1.1-1.4 F over the horizon | a hero cloud >= 1 W behind it | huge = slow (clouds 0-6 px/s) |
  tiny anchors, lines leading up | 3 tiers of life, never 1 s still, none inside the city. Daylight colours; no night-black,
  haze, god rays, airships or VFX (CG "Not for us"). Respect: camera at or below a person's eye level; no glow, rim or hero
  colour on the thing under critique; a high angle only on a crowd (CS §5).

## 5. Motion and camera
- **60 fps** ("smoother and more crispy", PB Motion); animate by time, ambient motion every frame; drawn poses may step
  (12-24 drawings/s) while the camera glides; deterministic seeds (PL "Frame rate"; LD §6).
- **No fades, ever:** pops, scales, wipes, slides, draw-ons; nothing animates opacity (MS §0; MAS "No fades").
- **Build grammar** (MS §3, §6, §10): strokes draw on in 0.10-0.20 s, ~1 frame (of 30) apart; fills land 0.13-0.27 s after
  their outline (or fill-first); rises 0.2-0.33 s out-expo from behind a front mass; counting builds ~0.10-0.17 s apart,
  texture builds 1 frame, the big number a burst.
- **Sync** (MS §7): pictures lead the voice ~0.1 s; a big build starts 0.25-0.40 s early to complete ON the word; marks
  and character beats land on the word (±0.05 s); one emphasis per caption line, one big visual beat per sentence; ~2
  medium visual events per spoken word.
- **A reference gives the SHAPE, never the speed** (PB Motion): copy path, framing, reveal order and easing shape, then
  retime (usually faster). Detailed layers <= ~15 px/frame at the render rate; readability at 1x is the test (PL).
- **Camera dose** (CG Rules 3, 5; CS §1.4, 3b.5): ONE big move per video (S1 rise-over or S5 pull-back crane, never both);
  other story moves push 3-8 % on a line; cozy holds locked or a 2D float (8-15 px/s, legs turning back within ±12 px),
  **never a zoom in a hold** (1-4 px/s reads frozen); breathers a 1.5-2 % pull-out. Physical camera (crane, dolly, truck,
  tilt), ease in, land over the last third; no roll, no compound move, no cut mid-move.
- **S5, the pull-back crane (the owner's favourite: "it has pretty much everything")** (PB; CG S5): the film's one big
  reveal, small world -> vast system: dolly back + crane up + a ~70-80° arc round two small backs on ONE ease, the figures
  the pivot; suspense by progress (a slot in their eyeline, a mass hiding the landmark, a foreground wipe at peak speed, a
  light burst before the landmark lands); ~3.3 s (2-5 s) with a >= 0.5 s landing hold; the end frame runs the scale cues.
- **Transitions** (BR 4; MS §8; CS K9): a camera move carrying one scene into the next, a match-cut (sun, horizon, a shared
  flat shape zoomed through), a foreground wipe, a push through a window; the un-build for the loop; no card flip. They sit
  in voice pauses >= 0.3 s, midpoint on the new sentence's first word (MS §7); reference 1 cuts every 3.4-6.4 s (MS §2).
- **Cuts on the beat; every frame >= 8/10, no dead frames** (PB Hook): bend the bed so cuts land on beats
  (`scripts/qa/beatsync.py --fit-tempo`, `audio/tools/beatlock.py`) (SP v5).

## 6. Pip, the channel's living character (in EVERY shot, like reference 1's cat)
- **Role:** one recurring living character in every shot: reference 1 had the cat (and the sun), v1 had Pip (and the sun)
  (LD §5; PB); Pip is also the profile identity (`deliverables/profile/pip_power_button.png`). The dropped v2 pass used
  "her cat, in her home only": not the house rule.
- **Jobs** (LD §5; MS §5): (1) perch on or beside the subject and watch (`sit-back`, right or middle third); (2) lead the
  eye: the head turns to the next subject a few frames before the voice names it; (3) react on a stressed word, at most one
  big reaction per shot, never on the subject's own beat; (4) idle always (blink ~2.6-5 s, ears, tail, trunk, breath); (5)
  carry continuity: hop or fall across every cut and the loop, as the cat did; (6) wait or sleep in the calm beat; (7) in a
  scale wide, a simple >= 40 px silhouette beside the tiny figures as a scale anchor.
- **Tone:** worried, `ears-cover`, watching; never a cute gag near real harm or at anyone affected (CS §3; CB §17).
- **Design** (MAS §1-5; SB §9): an original small periwinkle elephant (`#8D90CC`; big round ears, head tuft, teddy-bear
  soles; no PHP lettering or logo copy), its hue reserved for Pip; per film a colourway that stays its own hue, ΔE76 >= 20
  from every ground, never the hero or the pop (LD §5; `src/lib/mascot/colors.ts`). Ink like the cat (~3.4 px tapered, open
  at the head top and base), fill offset 3 px right / 2 px down. Scale 0.9-1.0 (~86-96 px) in wides, 1.8-2x in close-ups;
  never in the caption band, the right rail or over the subject. `src/lib/mascot` (`Elephant`, `ElephantTimeline`, `hop`,
  `drawIn`, `unbuild`; poses `sit-back`, `sit-front`, `stand-side`, `walk-side`, `peek`, `trunk-up`, `hold`,
  `ears-cover`, `shrug`, `sleep`): enters by draw-on or pop, leaves by un-build.

## 7. Sound and music
- **The voice is the show**, always on top; both references sell scale with picture and voice alone (SP §1, §7.1); the
  film must still work heard without watching (CB §21). **Music: cinematic but subtle, especially at the start** (PB).
  v1's approved bed: felt-piano pulse and motif, quiet low strings, air; the hook "air only"; under the voice median ~27 dB,
  never under ~12 dB; a -14 dB duck; <= 2 soft accents, no trailer drums, swells or booms; a stop in the pulse is itself
  the effect (SP v5, §3, §7). Never dramatic or overly emotional (CB §21).
- **SFX** (PB; SP §7): MORE than v1, especially our own positive phone sounds, one in the first second; each on a visual
  hit (following the picture's onset), never mid key word; one sound per idea, as small as the gesture (`pop_high` a label,
  `pop_low` a landing, a soft pencil a line); a beat's special sound once, callbacks 3 dB softer; >= 8 dB under the voice;
  ambiences only under a real vista, 20-25 dB under. Cozy holds: room tone and weather behind glass 18-24 dB under, soft
  foley; the one breach opens the filter, then near-silence (CS §3).
- **Mix:** -14 LUFS, -1 dBTP, 48 kHz; circular music, tails and dynamics for a clean loop seam; check on phone speakers and
  earbuds (PB; SP §4, §6). Cues from the FINAL beats (`WRAPUP.md` Sound; `audio/sfx/`, `audio/mix.py`, `audio/cues.json`).

## 8. Process lessons for a direct-to-final production
- **A rejected cut restarts clean:** no sketch, the finished film directly, full creative freedom inside these rules; never
  patch or reuse the rejected cut; prompts never read the archive (PB Process; N2 2026-10-27).
- **v3 G5, "way too empty ... the quality and smoothness is not there"** (N3; LD §0, §9; CS 3b.5): flat 61 %, detail 16 %,
  63 objects/Mpx (v1 32 % / 45 % / 141); nobody in 9 of 14 shots; 31 % of moving pixels on void; 1.4 % push-ins at ~2 px/s
  read frozen; a 540x960 render blurred the dressing. So: fullness first, people in every shot, float not push, full size.
  v3 G3 ("what does the AI mountain signify?"): literal shots, the real mechanism, a sound-off cold read first (N3; PB).
- **The rejected v2 cut (no reasons given at the time; OUR read of its frames):** it PASSED the meter (6.5 events/s, flat 19 %,
  detail 46 % on `deliverables/v2/archive/v2_g5_blocking.mp4`) and was still rejected: the meter is never the bar. It was
  **tableaux**: one room seen four times, an office, a top-down desk four times; big people seated FACING the camera with
  faces and scribble speech bubbles (since confirmed by the owner: "don't use big models of people", N2 2026-10-27); a document whose typed lines repeated the narration for ~10 s (text explained, animation
  did not); **stillness of idea**: motes ticked, but the middle third held one desk frame across four lines; **off the
  house look**: uniform cartoon outlines, a saturated violet/cobalt wash over whole sets instead of a light page with a
  5-15 % hero, almost none of v1's architecture, streets, sky band, sun or crowds of backs; **the living character** a cat
  shut in her home, not Pip; **the scale beat** a static top-down building with none of the cues. So: a connected WORLD,
  information in it, people from behind, Pip everywhere, a mechanism that moves, a new picture every beat.
- **Assembly lessons** (G5B §8-9): one kit with a consistency contract (one model each for her, Pip, every hero prop and
  set) shown at EVERY size used, fixed before lanes start; life the eye cannot see is not life (read per-frame movers);
  life crossing lettering misreads ("Googlz"); cropped figures lose their shape; scribbled speech reads as an empty form;
  lanes add a zoom "breathe" to holds by habit (forbid it); the last shot's builder owns the loop seam; a BARE sound-off
  cold read at phone size on the first full assembly.
- **No sketch gate, so decide the shape on paper and prove it in pixels:** competing treatments judged against this digest
  and v1 (PL "Creative exploration"; NO LIMIT ON TOKENS for creative work, PB), then a hero-shot pilot at final density
  and full size judged beside the bar BEFORE the full build (the v3 G5b pilot pattern, LD "Approved bar").
- **Still true** (PL anti-waste 2-5): small checkpointed tasks, commit after each agent, ~4 agents at once, lane renders
  at 0.33-0.5 scale, ~8 images a session, one combined review per shot (max 6 findings), then fix and stop.
- **Delivery** (PB Process; PK): ONE film, its stage named, sent as a download; 1080x1920 60 fps, H.264 High, AAC 48 kHz,
  CRF ~14, +faststart (a 1440x2560 master for Shorts); cover from the hook frame; AI label on; BBC source cited in the post.

## 9. Acceptance checklist: a finished film answers YES to every question
1. Frame 0: the opening scene as bare ink lines, colour flooding in by ~0.3 s, the core-line words and source on screen?
2. Voice from the first frames, and our own positive phone sound inside the first second?
3. The expected thing, then reality overtaking it by "for life" (~5.3 s) through the true mechanism, a second jolt by ~10 s?
4. Is the opening one continuous, never-still shot (something new every few frames) until the hook's idea has landed?
5. Sound off at phone size, can a cold viewer say what every shot shows and retell the story as a story?
6. Does the animation explain (a mechanism, a comparison, a real example), never just illustrate or print the narration?
7. Does the picture add something worth knowing every 3-5 s, with no beat re-using a frame without new information?
8. Beside v1 and reference 1, the house look: loose warm open ink, flat misregistered fills, no gradient, glow, blur, fade?
9. People small, simple and from behind, like reference 1: no big character model, no close-up face, no cartoon outlines?
10. One palette (+ one sister shift at most): light soft page, hero 5-15 % on the story object, ~5 % anchor, <= 1 pop?
11. Warm, never sickly: no beige, muddy tan, olive or grey; coloured shadows; >= 10 % opposite temperature per frame?
12. Paused at phone size beside the bar: no hole over ~200 px outside the caption band and quiet field, no flat field > 20 %?
13. Does every shot have people (still ones count), specific varied dressing, and >= 2 things cut by the frame edge?
14. Is each shot BUSY or DENSE-CALM and does it meet its row (BUSY >= 3.5 events/s, no gap > 1 s; cozy >= 2 movers)?
15. Never a second with nothing visibly moving; no life crossing lettering or the caption band?
16. Is Pip in every shot, in its own colourway, ~86-130 px, out of the caption band, the right rail and the subject?
17. Does Pip watch, lead the eye before the voice, react once at most per shot, hop across cuts and stay serious?
18. Does every number, quote or clause live on a real object in a real place, with people looking, straight-on and big?
