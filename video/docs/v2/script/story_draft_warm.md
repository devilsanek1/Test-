- **Core line (locked):** She quit Google, and says her contract bans criticizing it, for life.
- **Frame-0 text:** SHE SAYS: NO CRITICIZING GOOGLE. FOR LIFE. + "Source: BBC News, 21 Oct 2026"

  Never cut Google's line or the callback.

## The story in one breath

**Throughline (one question):** she says her contract bans her from criticizing Google for life, so why is she talking?

**Situation:** you've had a job where you bit your tongue and thought "once I leave, I'll say what I really think".
Quitting is supposed to set you free, so "for life" stops you cold: they can DO that?

**Chain:** She quit Google and says her contract bans her from criticizing it for life. You'd think that's one odd
contract, **but** Google staff told the BBC they're scared to speak out because of theirs. **So** why is she talking?
**Because** Sofia didn't agree with where Google's military work was heading, **but** in April Google signed a deal
letting the Pentagon use its AI on classified work for "any lawful government purpose", with no veto, **so** in
October she quit. **But** Google says staff can raise concerns safely and it bans retaliation. **But** whoever you
believe, she still says she can't criticize Google, and in her words, "talking about facts is not criticising."

**Turn (line 8):** her own answer. As she says "facts", the pages we've just seen (the deal, the clause, no veto,
Google's own reply) stack up beside her contract, and the viewer gets it: everything they heard was the kind of thing she
says she's allowed to say. Shown, never narrated. That's why no opinion line of hers is in the script ("ashamed",
"watched"): any of them would let a viewer answer "but that IS criticism" and take the edge off the turn.

**Payoff:** the locked callback, word for word, said warm and plain with no mic drop. It closes the question from line 3,
keeps the lifetime ban hers ("she says"), and the frame melts back to the bare-ink contract of frame 0 for the loop.

**Retell test (what a viewer would tell a friend):** "This Google AI engineer quit, and she says her contract bans her
from criticizing Google for life. Other staff told the BBC they're scared to speak out too. She didn't like where Google's
military work was going, like this deal letting the Pentagon use its AI for any lawful purpose, so she quit. Google says
there's no retaliation. And she's talking anyway because, she says, talking about facts isn't criticizing."

## Why it reads as one story

- **One question carries it.** Line 1 raises "they can DO that?", line 2 answers it (it isn't just her), and that
  answer raises the real question in line 3: so why is SHE talking? Everything after is her story, and the last line
  is her answer.
- **Every pair joins with a real link:** but, so, because, but, so, but, but. No line could move without breaking the
  one after it.
- **One room holds the film.** Her contract never leaves the desk (its orange line runs the whole time), so each new
  page (the deal, Google's statement) lands next to it, and the payoff is just those pages stacking up.
- **The turn is shown, not said:** her words on "facts", the fact pages beside the contract, then the loop to frame 0.

## Taken from the judges

- **From one_room (the base):** the one lamp-lit desk as the whole world, the scale moment through the laptop (the wall
  of CLASSIFIED doors), "You'd guess it's one odd contract, but...", Google's statement in the same size and the same
  lamp light, the fact pages stacking on the payoff, and no opinion quotes, so the turn holds.
- **From mystery:** the throughline question said out loud early, and the locked order (not alone by ~4 s, then her,
  the deal, Google, her answer).
- **From two_contracts:** the heavy official stamp on the deal against the cheerful chime on her contract; Google's page
  the same size as hers; 6.1 / 6.3 on screen at the deal and 6.2 spoken in Google's beat.
- **Fixes:**
  - She is never said to have objected *because* of the April deal (she objected to the direction; "But in April"
    puts the deal after it).
  - "Ashamed" and "watched" are both out, and there are no walls closing in.
  - No lit windows across the street: one staffer from behind, typing and deleting, labelled.
  - No "her talking is a risk" in our voice. The fear stays the staff's, attributed.
  - No re-asked question at ~30 s, since the locked callback does that job. No narrator "it was all facts".
  - No "this month", no "just", no "last evening".
  - 6.3 is credited to 9to5Google, not put on the BBC card.
  - The orange line is never on anyone else's page and never stops.
  - The name caption arrives with her (line 4), not on a crowded frame 0.

**Not taken:** "whoever you believe" as a spoken line (the "Google says / she says" pair does that job), the clue or
detective framing, the 2018 history, the kitchen table.

**Optional insert (doesn't fit under 115 words):** "About six hundred staff had asked the CEO to say no, and Google
signed within a day." (2.7, 2.8; never "hours later", never "600 engineers"). It costs 17 words, so it only goes in
if the merge frees that many elsewhere.

## Open checks

1. **ElevenLabs:** line 2 opens by pointing back at the hook ("that's"). `ELEVENLABS.md` warns that a line that refers
   back can make v4 say the hook twice, so check the first take for a doubled hook.
2. **Sound-off cold read (G3):** line 2's staffer must read as "scared to post", never as Google deleting posts (N2).
   Line 5's wall of doors must read as "classified" from its label.
3. **The full clause (2.3)** puts "autonomous weapons (including target selection)" on screen at ~19 s. It's required,
   and it's well after the hook. Keep it as small hand-lettered text on the page, with no imagery.
4. **Staging:** the desk is a generic drawn set. It is never captioned as her home, and the narration never claims a
   scene happened.
5. **Pinned comment** for "is that legal?" is ready in CORE_LINE s2.
6. **Pacing:** the throughline question lands at ~12 s, not ~4 s. The hook's "they can DO that?" carries 0-12 s, and
   line 2 answers it at ~6 s. If the cold-read panel finds 4-12 s slow, tighten line 2 before moving the question.
