# Video 2, G1 script: "Why is she talking?"

Status: G1 final draft, 2026-10-26. Not committed and not yet seen by the owner. It starts from D1 ("warm"), which all
four cold readers ranked #1 (story 7/10). It applies the flow and standards fixes and takes the best of D2 and D3 (see
"What changed from the drafts" at the end). Facts: `../research/factlock.md` (FL). Locked brief: `../CORE_LINE.md`.

- **Core line (locked, G0.5):** "She quit Google, and says her contract bans criticizing it, for life."
- **Frame-0 on-screen text:** SHE SAYS: NO CRITICIZING GOOGLE. FOR LIFE. Below it, small: "Source: BBC News, 21 Oct 2026".
- **Throughline question:** she says her contract bans her from criticizing Google for life, so why is she talking?
- **Word count (spoken):** 113 (`check_paste.py`). No spoken numbers. Dates are said as months only.
- **Estimated length at 165 wpm:** 41.1 s of speech. The three … beats, the gaps between lines and a 0.3 s lead add
  about 2 s, for **about 43 s**. For comparison, v3's 114-word take ran 40.4-42.5 s on ElevenLabs v4, so expect 41-43 s.
  If a take runs past 45 s, trim "and told the BBC" from line 4 to "and says" first. Never cut Google's line or the callback.
- **First-second sound:** our own cheerful two-note "signed!" chime at 0.0-0.3 s as her signature lands on her job contract.
  This is the one light touch: a happy e-signature sound on a lifetime clause. Then the room goes quiet for "for life".
- **Palette:** 3 Cream & Cobalt. Cobalt is used only on her, never on "Google". Orange is used only on FOR LIFE and her
  highlighted line. For the scale moment in line 5 the palette shifts to its sister, 6 Dusk.
- **Paste file:** `script_elevenlabs.md` (`check_paste.py`: OK, paste-safe).

## The story in 3 sentences

She quit Google and says her contract bans her from criticizing it for life, and Google staff told the BBC they're scared
to speak out because of their contracts, so why is she talking? She worked on Google's AI and disagreed with where its
military work was heading, which in April meant a deal letting the Pentagon use its AI for "any lawful government
purpose" with no Google veto, though Google says it follows its AI principles and bans retaliation "of any kind". She
still says she can't criticize Google, but in her words, "talking about facts is not criticising."

- **Situation:** you've had a job where you bit your tongue and thought "once I leave, I'll say what I really think".
  Quitting should set you free, so "for life" stops you cold: they can DO that?
- **The chain:** for life, **but** it's not one odd contract, **so** why is she talking? **Because** she disagreed with
  where Google's military work was heading, **which in April meant** the Pentagon deal. **But** Google says it follows
  its principles and bans retaliation. **But** she says she can't criticize Google, **but** in her words, facts aren't
  criticism.
- **The question gets a first answer straight away (line 4: her reason) and its full answer at the end (line 7: how she
  squares it with her contract).** The cold readers said D1's question "went cold" for 30 s, so it now pays off in two
  steps. Both halves mirror her own sentence to the BBC (5.4): "because I believe it's important for these things to be
  known and I also think that talking about facts is not criticising".
- **The turn (line 7):** her own answer changes how everything before it reads. The deal, the clause and the missing veto
  are all on the record, so the video itself is the way she talks without breaking her contract, as she sees it. It is
  shown, never narrated: the narrator never says "it was all facts". That's why none of her opinion lines are in it
  ("ashamed", "watched"). Any one of them would let a viewer say "but that IS criticism" and take the edge off the turn.
- **The one light touch:** the "signed!" chime on a lifetime clause (frame 0). No other jokes.

## Plain voice-over (for captions and timing)

She quit Google. And she says her contract bans her from criticizing it… for life. You'd think that's one odd contract…
but Google staff told the BBC they're scared to speak out, because of their contracts. So why is she talking? She worked
on Google's AI, and told the BBC she disagreed with where its military work was heading. In April, that meant a deal
letting the Pentagon use its AI for "any lawful government purpose"… and Google can't veto how it's used. But Google says
it follows its AI principles, and bans retaliation "of any kind." She says she can't criticize Google. But in her words,
"talking about facts is not criticising."

Captions keep the BBC's spelling inside her quote ("criticising") and US spelling everywhere else. The paste file drops
the quote marks, because v4 can slip into a "character" voice for quoted text and the narrator always reads the quotes. It
also spells the last word "criticizing": the audio is identical, and `check_take.py` matches the take against an American
transcript.

## Google's side

- **Its own beat (line 6, about 29-34 s), spoken:** "But Google says it follows its AI principles, and bans retaliation
  'of any kind.'" This is Google's one statement to the BBC (6.1 + 6.2). The "but" does two jobs: "AI principles"
  answers the deal (line 5), and "retaliation" answers the staff's fear (line 2). CORE_LINE puts this beat at ~22-28 s.
  It lands about 6 s later because the deal has to come first for "its AI principles" to answer anything. It still sits
  where CORE_LINE asks: after the fear and before the payoff.
- **Equal visual weight:** the page is the same size as her contract, in the same lamp light, and hand-lettered GOOGLE SAYS
  (never the logo). Her orange line holds still for the beat. The full statement is on screen, with the spoken parts large.
- **On the deal (line 5):** the deal's own limit, the full oversight clause (2.3), is on screen whenever "any lawful
  government purpose" is. Google's "proud to support national security" (6.3) is a pinned slip in line 6, credited
  9to5Google, 28 Apr 2026.
- **Delivery:** the same warmth as every other line, with no CAPS, no irony and no "either way" or "still" waving it off.
- **What we never imply:** that Google confirmed or denied her clause (its statement doesn't mention it), that it
  retaliated against anyone, or that its AI picks targets. In line 7, Google's page stays beside the "facts" stack, not
  in it, so its words never read as her facts.
- **Pinned-comment answer, ready for "is that legal?":** "We can't say. We haven't seen her contract, and Google's
  statement doesn't mention it. This is her account to the BBC."

## What changed from the drafts

- **The question no longer goes cold.** All four readers lost D1's "So why is SHE talking?" during the backstory. Now line
  4 answers it straight away (her reason), and the callback finishes the answer, which keeps D3's satisfying
  question-and-answer without D3's asked-and-answered line.
- **"No veto" is now "Google can't veto how it's used".** All four readers asked "veto over what?".
- **She is introduced by her job, out loud,** ("She worked on Google's AI"), and her name is only on the caption. Two
  readers asked "who is Sofia?".
- **The fake links are gone.** "So in October, she quit" (a false cause, and a repeat of the hook) is cut. D1's "But in
  April" (really "and then") becomes "In April, that meant", which ties the deal to her objection without saying she
  quit over it. That also avoids D2's "Like the deal", which readers heard as one item on a list.
- **Google's line opens on its AI principles,** so its "but" answers the line before it (the reviewers' fix for the 6-7
  jump).
- **CAPS are down to three:** CONTRACT, SHE and FACTS. Readers called SCARED, STAFF and VETO shouting, and CAN on Google's
  line invited an ironic read.
- **Pictures:** the staffer typing and deleting a post (a behaviour nobody reported) is replaced by the NAME WITHHELD
  badge, which was reported. The "facts" stack leaves Google's page out. The folder pile replaces D3's climbing arrow,
  which read as data we don't have.
- **Paste-file note:** line 2 follows the one-tag opening block and starts "You'd think that's one odd contract…". It is
  a full sentence with no ellipsis and no correction, unlike v3's "Well, AI DATA CENTERS are." If a take repeats the
  hook anyway, `check_take.py` will flag it and `clean_take.py` can cut it.
