# Video 2, G1 story draft (tight editor): "Why is she talking?"

Draft, 2026-10-26. Not committed and not owner-reviewed. One writer's draft of the winning spine (`one_room`) with the
judges' grafts. Facts: `../research/factlock.md` (FL). Locked inputs: `../CORE_LINE.md`.

- **Core line (locked):** "She quit Google, and says her contract bans criticizing it, for life."
- **Frame-0 text:** SHE SAYS: NO CRITICIZING GOOGLE. FOR LIFE. + "Source: BBC News, 21 Oct 2026"
- **Spoken words:** 111 (checked with `check_paste.py`: paste-safe). No numbers spoken.
- **Length:** about 39 s of speech at the cued paces, about **41 s** with the beats, line gaps and a 0.5 s tail.
- **First-second sound:** our own cheerful two-note "signed!" chime as her signature lands (0.0-0.3 s). It's the one
  light touch: a happy e-signature sound on a lifetime clause. It's never used on Google's deal; that page gets a heavy stamp.

## The story

- **Throughline question:** she says she can't criticize Google, for life. So why is she talking? Line 1 makes the ban
  real, line 2 says it isn't only her, line 3 opens the question ("still told the BBC"), lines 4-5 are what she's talking
  about and Google's answer, and line 7 answers it.
- **Situation:** you've had a job where you bit your tongue and thought "once I leave, I'll say what I think". Quitting
  is supposed to set you free, so "for life" stops you: they can DO that?
- **The turn (line 7):** her own words, "talking about facts is not criticising". Everything the viewer just heard was
  facts: the deal's terms, the missing veto, what staff told the BBC, and Google's reply. So the video itself shows how she
  talks without, in her view, breaking that line. It's shown, not narrated: the pages stack up beside her contract. No
  opinion quotes anywhere ("ashamed", "watched"), so nobody can answer "but that IS criticism".
- **Payoff:** the locked callback, said warm and plain, then the loop to the bare-ink frame 0.
- **Retell test:** "A Google AI engineer quit over Google's military work, and she says her contract bans her from
  criticizing Google for life. Other staff told the BBC they're scared to speak out, and Google says it bans retaliation,
  but she talked anyway, because in her words, talking about facts isn't criticizing."

words, "talking about facts is not criticising."

## What changed from the spine, and why (the judges' notes)

- **Grafted from `mystery`:** the contradiction line, "But Sofia still told the BBC…" (line 3), which opens the
  throughline question right after the fear beat.
- **Kept from `one_room`:** "You'd think that's just her contract, but…" (line 2, the "not alone" seed at ~5 s); one
  lamp-lit room; no opinion quotes; the scale moment through the laptop; the payoff shown as pages stacking.
- **Grafted from `two_contracts`:** a heavy stamp on Google's deal (the chime stays hers); Google's page drawn the same
  size as her contract; 6.1 on screen at Google's beat, 6.2 spoken.
- **Cut:** the "watched" quote (an opinion that hints at surveillance, and a second fear beat); "so her talking is a
  risk" (a claim in our voice); the beat-7 re-ask (folded into "Either way", 2 words); "this month"; the lit windows
  across the street (they don't read with the sound off; replaced by plain pages with blank names); "objected because
  in April" (it ran backwards; now "growing military work, like the deal").
- **Scale moment:** a hall of locked server cabinets stamped CLASSIFIED instead of a wall of doors. Classified
  networks are servers, so it reads literally with the sound off.
