# COMPOSITION from the owner's reels: every idea they show, and which work in 9:16 (2026-10-30)

The owner (2026-10-30): "After we are done with this I want you to do another research and distill of the composition
shots to use / Take a look at this folder I collected some initial references but do the deeper research". Earlier, on
our list of differences: "8 agreed this is an important point about scene composition"; on still v6: "our scene is very
busy" (playbook SCENE COMPOSITION).

**What this is.** The REELS part of that research. It covers the five screen recordings the owner collected
(`docs/look/refs_composition/`), watched frame by frame at 4 fps. It catalogues every composition idea they show: its
name, what it is, the example used, and how it is drawn or framed, with measured positions (§2-3). Each idea is judged for
our vertical frame: 1080 x 1920, the caption band y 290-600, one home place, one subject, a person near the camera at
~130-220 px and one story light (§3-4). It also records the reels' own teaching device (§5) and the examples scored with
our composition check (§6). **Rank:** research notes, the lowest rank on DIRECTION's ladder. Nothing here is a rule until
the distill integrates it into `LAYOUT.md`, `CINEMATIC_GUIDE.md` or the playbook. We learn principles: no frame of the
reels is traced, copied or used as an input to a picture of ours, the creators are not named, and no living artist is a
style target. Paths are from `video/`.

**Conventions.** Positions are fractions of the reel's picture (x from the left, y from the top), read by eye on a 10 %
grid (+-0.03). A time like "02 10.0 s" means reel 02, 10.0 s into the recording. "At full width" means the share of the
picture's width x 1080: that is the size in our frame of a thing the owner sees on his phone with a landscape reel at full
width (DIRECTION rule 7 converts the same way). Our zones are from `LAYOUT.md` §1. R1-R12 are the rules of
`organic/composition.md` §4; R7's focal box is x 380-700, y 860-1200.

## 0. Bottom line

- **Five recordings hold six reels and 30 named ideas.** The hand-drawn sheet (reel 03) alone shows 18 of them; the
  README's first look missed its "tunnel" and a second, nested "golden section". On top of those, the examples make 11
  moves they never name (§3 E). Five ideas recur across reels:
  - rule of thirds (4 reels);
  - golden spiral or golden ratio (4);
  - frame within a frame (3, plus the two title shots seen through train windows);
  - symmetry (2, plus the same two titles);
  - leading lines, one-point perspective and foreground (2 each).
- **The reels teach armatures, not value plans.** Every example has a line structure (a grid, a spiral, converging
  lines, an inner frame) that points at one place. We scored them the way we scored the owner's references
  (`organic/composition.md` §1). These photos are much busier than the owner's painted models:
  - 1-14 value calls each (median 6), against 1-2 for the painted models;
  - 17-83 % quiet (median 51 %), against 67-91 %.

  More telling: in 12 of the 20 examples with one subject, neither the strongest value call nor the strongest colour
  call sits on the subject the lines point at. A sunlit patch on a path wins instead, or a white car, or a bright corner
  of sky. **The lines lead, but the light decides.** A device only works when the value plan agrees with it (R1-R4): a
  leading line has to end at the hardest value step and the story light, not just at the subject. The two calmest
  examples, a stairwell's lit gap with one man in it and a row of windows with one man in one window, are the closest to
  "big quiet areas and one place to look" (§6).
- **One small human figure is the reels' constant.** 11 of the 23 photo examples put one person at the device's focal
  point, mostly seen from behind or in silhouette (8 of the 11). At full phone width, 8 of the 12 measured figures are
  150-226 px tall (median 178). Three far anchors are 92-97 px; one mid shot is 447. That is the size DIRECTION rule 7 asks for
  (~130-220 px): new evidence for DEPTH AND SCALE FROM A HUMAN VIEWPOINT.
- **The read-zone transfer.** At full width a 16:9 reel picture is 1080 x 610 px, almost exactly the shape of our read
  zone (y 600-1250, 1080 x 650). Place the picture there, with our sky and caption band above it and our near ground
  below it (R5's two quiet masses). Then 16 of the 21 focal points land inside R7's focal box or within 40 px of it. So
  most of these landscape compositions become ours just by opening the frame up and down at the same width. The ones
  that don't are built on width: a figure on the far left third, side-by-side balance, an X from the corners.
- **Our layout already is a golden-section grid.** In 1080 x 1920 the phi lines fall at:
  - x 413 / 667, which is our subject axis (x 380-700);
  - y 733, close to our default horizon (y 768);
  - y 1187, the floor of R7's focal box (y 1200).

  The rule of thirds fits worse. Its intersections (x 360 / 720, y 640 / 1280) sit at the caption band's foot and below
  the read zone. The golden triangle's two eyes (around (820, 461) and (260, 1459)) sit in the band and by the bottom UI.
- **Best fits for our films** (verdicts in §4):
  - frame within a frame, a lit door, gate or window with the figure in it. This is v6's door already, and it sets up a
    push-through join;
  - leading lines and one-point perspective running up the tall ground to the figure, ending at a closed vista;
  - scale: a small figure under a tall structure;
  - level change: two levels joined by steps;
  - the L-shape, which is our cozy asymmetric frame;
  - the S-curve: a canal winding up the frame;
  - a pattern with one break: a facade's windows with one lit window;
  - negative space as a quiet, patterned sky field;
  - foreground as a small hint at the bottom edge;
  - unbalanced (R8's counterweight);
  - symmetry once (a landmark mirrored in still water).

  **Weak for us:** balance (two equal calls); double diagonal and the spiral seen from above (top-down angles); the
  golden triangle and the thirds' intersections as placement grids; any focus or motion blur (our look has none:
  DIRECTION rule 5).
- **The reels' own device is "the line first, then the world under it."** A line on black holds 0.25-1.5 s, the shot
  appears under the line, the line fades, and the shot holds 0.5-2 s: 2.25-3.25 s per idea. That is our opening's
  draw-on (DIRECTION rule 9), used once per idea. Reel 03's red armature over a grey value thumbnail is a ready-made way
  to show a G3 panel's composition to the owner (§5).

## 1. Method

- **Frames.** `ffmpeg -i REEL.mp4 -vf fps=4 -q:v 2 rNN/f_%04d.jpg` on each 540 px recording gives 381 frames in
  `/tmp/comp/reels/`. Time = (frame - 1) / 4 s (+-0.13 s). Each reel was watched as montages of its picture (all frames)
  and as native-size frames with a 10 % grid.
- **The picture inside the phone screen** (rows that are bright in most frames):
  - 01 and 02: 540 x 406 (4:3), y 328-733;
  - 04: 540 x 305 (16:9), y 379-683;
  - 05: 540 x 337 (~16:10), y 363-699;
  - 03: full-screen 9:16 phone footage of a drawing tablet, the app's UI over it.

  The UI was ignored. Each reel's like-button pokes into the picture's lower right; it was masked from what stays white
  across all of that reel's black diagram cards and inpainted for the numbers. A camera maker's burned-in watermark in
  01 and 05 was masked the same way. Each recording ends on the phone's control centre (the owner's swipe).
- **Sizes.** A figure's height runs head to feet (+-0.02 of the picture's height). At full width, the picture's height
  is 812 px (4:3), 610 px (16:9) or 674 px (16:10).
- **The numbers in §6.** These come from `organic/composition_study.py`'s `measure_one`, imported and run with no file
  written. It works at 400 px wide with the same squints, notan and call thresholds as `organic/composition.md` §1. We
  ran it on one clean frame per photo example (23 examples + 01's title). Subject boxes are by eye. Reel 03's drawings
  and 04b's last shot (cut off by the recording) were not scored.
- **Limits.** Photos with focus blur and grain are not paintings, so a call count only approximates what pulls at a
  squint. Each moving shot was scored on one frame. These are landscape pictures, judged for a 9:16 frame by geometry
  (§4), not by test renders.

## 4. What works in a vertical frame

**Count:** of the 41 ideas and moves, 26 work as they are (**yes**), 12 work with a change (**adapt**) and 3 are **weak**:
balance, double diagonal and the golden triangle as a placement grid. Thirteen of the 30 named ideas need a change or
are weak, because they were drawn for a landscape frame:
- grids that place the subject (A1, A3, A4, A5);
- lines or masses that reach the frame's corners or sides (A5, B4, B5, B7, B11, B12, B13, D3, D6).

**The transfer, in one move: open the frame up and down at the same width.** A landscape reel picture at full width
(1080 x 610 for 16:9, 1080 x 812 for 4:3) becomes our read zone. Above it go the sky and the caption band (y 0-600: R5's
first quiet mass, flat in the band); below it goes the near ground (y ~1250-1920: R5's second quiet mass, which may run
under the bottom UI). With one camera, a taller frame at the same width shows exactly this: more sky and more ground.
Placed this way:

The five outside are width-built: a figure on the far left third (negative space, scale), a spiral's eye in the upper
left, a subject at the right edge, a crowd at the foot. In 9:16 each moves to the axis and uses the height instead. The
negative space goes above the figure (the sky); the tower rises past the top edge behind a figure on the axis.

**A shortlist for our films** (one home place, one subject, a thread object, a person near the camera, a story light),
each with its 9:16 recipe:

1. **Frame within a frame + the lit opening (C1, E2).** A door, gate, window or arch, 270-460 px wide (the subject box),
   its inside the frame's brightest area, the figure dark in it at y 860-1200, the surround a mid or dark mass. It sets
   up a push-through join (A). v6's lit doorway is one already; the composition lane's T1 is its value plan.
2. **Leading lines or one point, up the tall ground, to a closed vista (B1, B2, E7).** Path edges, kerbs, a canal's
   coping or a pier's rails rise from the bottom corners at ~60° and meet at the subject (x 480-640, y 950-1150). The
   sight line ends on one object, and the lines end at the light (§0: the lines lead, the light decides).
3. **Scale (D2, E1).** One figure, 150-220 px, low near the axis (feet above y 1250); the tall thing rises past the top
   edge, its top in the gutter beside the band (x > 945 or < 95) or cut by the frame. This is LAYOUT's K6/K7.
4. **Level change (C3).** Two levels (y ~700-950 and ~1150-1450) joined by steps; the subject on one, life on the other.
   Only the tall frame can hold both.
5. **The L-shape and C-shape (B10, B9).** One tall side mass cut by the top edge and flat through the band, the ground
   line, and a curve wrapping the subject. These are our cozy recipes already (LAYOUT §2, §3).
6. **The S-curve (B8).** A canal or path winding from the bottom edge to the subject: a slower way into the depth than
   B1, for a calm beat.
7. **A pattern with one break (D7).** The facade's windows as texture inside one mass (tone-on-tone, R2, R11), one window
   lit with a person in it: the subject, or the thread object's home.
8. **Negative space as a quiet sky (D1).** The big field above the figure is the sky with the caption band in it,
   patterned (R5), the figure small at its foot. This is the frame the owner's "big quiet areas" asks for.
9. **Foreground as a hint (C2, E5).** One or two real shapes at the bottom edge, inside the near shade mass, simpler and
   lower in contrast. Never half the frame, never blurred.
10. **Unbalanced (D4).** One big soft counterweight (the hero cloud in the top-right gutter, a tree cut by the edge)
    against the small loud subject (R8).
11. **Symmetry, once (A7).** A landmark on the axis (x 540), mirrored in still water below it: a stillness beat.
12. **Phi placement (A2).** Already our grid: the subject on x 413 or 667, the horizon near y 733, the focal above y 1187.

**What fights our rules** (none of these come in):
- focus and motion blur (E3, E5; DIRECTION rule 5);
- titles over the shots (01, 04a, 04b; the playbook's "no title over the captions": the core line goes on the cover);
- the busy photographic texture: the scores show the photos are 2-14 calls busy, so we take their armatures, not their
  density (R2, R3);
- top-down angles for anything but one K10 shot;
- a big near layer (05's terrace) or a big blurred near head (04b);
- the opacity cross-fade the reels use between their line and their shot (DIRECTION rule 8: no opacity fades; §5).

## 5. The reels' own device: the line first, then the world under it

All five reels teach the same way:
1. an abstract line on black (the idea);
2. the shot appears under the line, which turns out to trace the shot's own structure exactly;
3. the line fades and the shot holds.

Reel 03 does it live: a pen draws the red armature over a grey value thumbnail. Timing across the reels: the line card
0.25-1.5 s, the line over the shot 0.5-1.25 s, the shot alone 0.5-2.0 s, so 2.25-3.25 s per idea. 02 holds its drawing
longer than its picture; 05 the reverse. The line styles:
- red lines with yellow bold capitals (01);
- white hairlines with a small lowercase serif label (02);
- red marker over grey (03);
- thin white lines with a small sans label (04a, 05);
- dark-red lines with gold capitals (04b).

What it means for us (proposals, not rules):

- **It is our opening's move, used once per idea.** DIRECTION rule 9 draws the subject's line on in the clean pen and
  floods colour in under it. The reels prove that "line first, then the world" reads at phone size in 0.5-1.5 s. Their
  shortest line card is 0.25 s, and only because the line stays over the shot for another 1 s; the owner found ~0.3 s
  invisible (2026-10-28). Ours would draw on and flood or wipe in, never cross-fade (rule 8).
- **For the G3 board:** reel 03's panel pairs, an abstract armature above and a grey value thumbnail with the armature
  traced over it, are a ready-made way to show the owner a panel's composition (`STORYBOARD_STYLE.md` decides the
  board's drawing). It is also the notan of R1, drawn.
- **For a film:** a draw-on is allowed "where the drawing is the beat (a chart line in step with the voice)" (DIRECTION
  rule 8). A mechanism drawn as a line first, with the world appearing under it, is that kind of beat; it is a story
  choice for W1, not a look rule.

## 7. Notes

- **What we take:** the armatures (where the lines lead and where the subject sits), the read-zone transfer, the
  figure's size and pose, and the "line first" device, all as principles.
- **What we don't take:** any reel's footage, layout, place, motif, palette or type style (the yellow, gold and red
  title types, the serif hairline labels), and the creators' handles (visible in the app's UI; not named here or on the
  sheet).
- **Names and families.** The ideas are named as the reels name them. Several of the hand-drawn sheet's panels are
  classical landscape-painting armatures: Edgar Payne's composition types (*Composition of Outdoor Painting*, 1941;
  the summary cited in `organic/composition.md` §8) include the S or compound curve, the cross, the circle, the
  triangle, radiating lines, the tunnel, the steelyard (the sheet's "unbalanced") and the balance scale. The deeper,
  sourced research on composition is the distill's other parts; this file covers the reels.
- **Rerun** (scratch only; nothing here is a repo tool):
  - frames: `ffmpeg -v error -i docs/look/refs_composition/NN_*.mp4 -vf fps=4 -q:v 2 /tmp/comp/reels/rNN/f_%04d.jpg`;
  - the numbers: import `docs/look/organic/composition_study.py` (with `sys.dont_write_bytecode = True`), register each
    clean picture in `INPUTS` and `SUBJECT`, and call `measure_one(name, save_maps=False)`;
  - the sheet: the frames listed on each of its tiles, cropped to the picture box (§1).
