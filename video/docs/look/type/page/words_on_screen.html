Words on Screen

How the words in our films could look, sit and move: 14 stills on video 4's real words at their real times. The research is distilled into a guide with 10 recipes; six picks for you are below.

"there is so much more we can do to make it look premium, more interesting, dynamic and more engaging"

  • The handevery spoken word, light and airy
    Gaegu Light, about 1.2x our captions now. The nearest free match to your cat reel's lettering.
  • The brushWANTS · 70,000 · heat
    Nanum Brush Script, for the 3-5 words the voice leans on, the big numbers and the cover. A brush, not a pen, like the new painted look.
  • PlacementSet into the open sky or wall by the subject, never a box. Each group moves a little (80-220 px) so the frame stays alive while the eye never hunts.
  • MotionEvery spoken word, 2-4 at a time, in the voice's order. Words pop in place or build word by word with the voice, and are never clipped. One landing word may swell or be painted on.

2How do we mark the word the voice leans on?

Recommended: both. The brush for the few landing words and numbers, the colour for everyday stress.

3May a landing word be painted on, stroke by stroke?

About 0.2 s, led by a brush tip, the way frame 0 paints the scene. For a few frames you see part of a letter.A. Yes, for landing words and the lettered phrase only. B. No, every word pops in whole.

Recommended: A, shown first in the G4 look clip so you can judge it moving.

4Where do the words sit?

A. Follow the open space by the subject: above it by default, beside it in close shots, moving a little between groups (four moments of one shot above).B. One fixed band, only the line stagger varies.

Recommended: A.

5Which cover title?

Recommended: A, the same hand as the film so the grid reads as one series.

6Is the size contrast enough?

Three steps: everyday 116 px, hook and payoff 136 px, brush 184-300 px.A. Keep it. B. Bigger everyday words (fewer words per line, more two-line groups).
