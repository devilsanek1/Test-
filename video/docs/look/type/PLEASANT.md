# The words on screen: PLEASANT to look at (iteration, 2026-10-30)

**The owner, verbatim (2026-10-30, on still 02; their crop `refs/owner_note_02_blood_message.jpg`):** "One immediate
note / The text should be pleasant to look at like it shouldn't look like a blood message on the wall / Reevaluate".
The playbook rule it became (`video/docs/OWNER_PLAYBOOK.md`, Visual style, "The words are PLEASANT to look at"): soft,
warm and calm, never menacing; no dark red or blood-like colours, no rough, scratchy or dripping letters, no shouting
capitals; a stressed word stands out by size, weight, light or a gentle warm colour; judge every word as a regular
viewer would at a glance. The owner's words win over the guide.

ITERATION: one agent, no critic, no panel; stills only. **Status: DONE (2026-10-30), for the owner's pick.** The
recommendation: THE LIT HAND (section 4); the options: stills 15-17; every still that used the brush or the burnt colour

helps; the owner's rule removes all five (the colour, the rough brush, the capitals; size and light carry the stress).

## 2. Research (only what decides the answer)

Strength: **[S1]** a study, a standard or a platform's own spec · **[S2]** a studio's or designer's own account ·
**[S3]** a practitioner's advice or a press summary · **[REF]** the owner's references, measured.

- **P1. Sharp angles read as a threat; people prefer curves.** Over 140 pairs of objects and 140 pairs of meaningless
  patterns, people preferred the curved version; the authors propose that sharp contours signal threat. [S1] Bar & Neta
  2006, *Psychological Science* 17(8): 645-648, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01759.x (free PDF:
  https://moshebar.org/_files/ugd/a77db7_5598c791a95a4436960eb2fe671ca1e5.pdf). **For us:** W-A-N's pointed vertices
  and the brush's spiky tails are the threat shape; the gentle stress is round: a rounded hand with round stroke ends.
- **P2. Rounder typefaces are liked more and read more easily; round faces match "sweet", angular ones "bitter, salty,
  sour".** 12 typefaces, n = 101 and 99: angularity correlated with disliking (r = -.79) and roundness with legibility
  (r = .73). [S1] Velasco, Woods, Hyndman & Spence 2015, *i-Perception* 6(4),
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4934647 . **For us:** the hand's own Bold (Gaegu 700: round ends, an even
  stroke) over any brush or angular script.
- **P3. Brightness drives pleasure; saturation drives arousal and dominance.** Standardised: Pleasure = .69 B + .22 S,
  Arousal = -.31 B + .60 S, Dominance = -.76 B + .32 S (Munsell chips). [S1] Valdez & Mehrabian 1994, *J Exp Psychol
  Gen* 123(4): 394-409, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/ . **For us:** the burnt word (L* 36, C* 52) was the
  least pleasant, most dominant colour in a pastel frame; a light, softly saturated warm (honey #FFD58C: L* 87, C* 42)
  is the most pleasant. On light skies a light colour cannot carry a word, so there the stress keeps the running ink and
  gets light around it.
- **P4. Red is read as danger without thinking.** Two Stroop-type tasks with colours matched for lightness and chroma: a
  strong red-danger link (words and symbols), a weaker green-safety one. [S1] Pravossoudovitch, Cury, Young & Elliot
  2014, *Ergonomics* 57(4): 503-510, https://doi.org/10.1080/00140139.2014.889220 . **For us:** no red family in the
  words (burnt sat at hue 50°, with rust and dried blood at 40-50°); the accent moves to the windows' honey light (hue
  82°; the night frame's lit windows measure #F7C16F, hue 78°), away from red.
- **P5. Capitals read as shouting.** The internet's etiquette guideline: "Use mixed case. UPPER CASE LOOKS AS IF YOU'RE
  SHOUTING." [S1, a standards body's informational guideline] RFC 1855 (IETF, 1995),
  https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1855 . **For us:** the take writes a stressed word in capitals as a direction to the
  voice ("WANTS"); the picture sets it in lowercase, as spoken.
- **P6. Horror's own lettering is still 02's features.** "Horror type works by breaking the rules of clean lettering to
  trigger unease"; its sub-styles are "dripping/blood (liquid runs off the letters)" and "jagged/scratched (claw marks,
  torn edges, distressed slashes)", with a blood-red accent. [S3] https://madegooddesigns.com/horror-fonts/ . **For
  us:** anything that runs, tapers into tails or scratches reads as that genre at a glance: clean, round, even strokes only.
- **P7. Warm, low light reads as cosy and calms.** A "cozy" lighting ambience (2700 K at 120 lx, with orange accent
  light) raised pleasure and lowered arousal and skin conductance in anxious older adults, against a neutral one
  (n = 38). [S1] Kuijsters, Redi, de Ruyter & Heynderickx 2015, *PLoS ONE*, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0132732 .
  **For us:** on night skies the stressed word is LIT, not painted: the windows' honey light with a soft golden glow,
  the film's own story light.
- **P8. The owner's references stress by size and light, never by a dark colour.** Ref 1's landing word "day" is the
  same off-white hand, only bigger and set lower right; ref 2's brush title is white on a photo; both are lowercase or
  mixed case on a calm field. [REF] `refs/README.md` and its images. **For us:** the stress is the same hand, bigger and
  bolder, in light.

## 4. Recommendation: THE LIT HAND (dusk C, night B, number B)

**The stressed word, the landing word and the big number are the hand itself (Gaegu), in its Bold (700), bigger
(1.35-1.4x the running size; the number ~2.15x), in lowercase, and LIT:** on night skies in the windows' honey light
#FFD58C with a soft golden glow; on light skies in the running ink (now a deep plum #3A2B48) with a gentle peach-gold
halo behind it. The brush (Nanum Brush Script) and the burnt colour are retired; nothing is red or brown; nothing
tapers, runs or scratches.

Why:
- **One look, one family:** every word on screen is the hand (Regular for running words, Bold for the stress), as ref 1
  stresses "day" by size in its own hand (P8). The brush was the second family; the gentler faces (E, F) would be a new
  one, and each read as a second speaker on the phone.
- **Every cue the owner's rule asks for and nothing it bans:** size and weight carry the stress (A already stands out),
  light carries the warmth (P3, P7), round even strokes (P1, P2, P6), lowercase (P5), no red (P4).
- **The story light:** the accent is the film's warm light (video 4: the heat, the lit windows), so the stressed word
  reads as part of the lit city, never as paint on its wall; the glow is the same idea as the halo the running words
  already have, warmed.
- **The light sky:** a light warm colour cannot carry a word on a light sky (honey on the dusk band measures 1.2:1), and
  every dark warm colour is a brown or a red (P3, P4); so the stress keeps the ink and the warmth comes as light around
  it (C), the same gesture as the night's glow. B (no halo) is the fallback if the halo reads as a smudge to the owner.
- **The running ink on light skies becomes plum:** softer than umber, in the sky's own hue (the dusk sky and the plum are both at
  hue 311°), still 8.0:1 (the house's 7:1 holds); umber was brown-black, the heaviest thing in the frame.

**The entrance (the write-on goes):** the brush write-on (still 14's question) is retired with the brush: it showed
part-letters behind a dry, dragged edge, the closest thing to a word being daubed on, and a reveal that cuts letters
is what the owner already ruled out. The stressed word now pops whole in place like every word, and its light BLOOMS:
the glow (or halo) rises 0 -> full in 8 frames with the swell (0.96 -> 1.00 in 8 frames, ease-out), so it arrives as a
warm light coming on, never as paint going on. Still 14 shows it mid-bloom.
