# FRAME_CARD: the questions every idea and key frame is planned on

Fill it in words before a frame is built, and again when it is judged. Every answer is the artist's own choice;
nothing here is a placement or a number (owner, 2026-10-31: "A number is never a target"). The tools for each answer
are in `DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md`. A card that leaves an answer blank leaves a piece for the owner to point at.

## 1. The moment and the feeling (answer these first; everything else serves them)
- **What happens** at this moment of the story, in one plain sentence?
- **What should the viewer FEEL here,** in a few everyday words (e.g. "the quiet thrill of stepping out", "cozy and safe",
  "a small shock")? Why this feeling, at this point of the film's arc, after the frame before it?
- **What should they understand** in one second, with the sound off?

## 2. The idea
- What is the one picture that says it, invented for this story? Why would someone stop scrolling for it?
- Does it belong to this film's one world, concept and thread?

## 3. Life and action
- Who is there, and what are they doing? Where is the story's action: is it where the eye will be, not pushed to the
  back by the layout?
- What else lives in this world (the place going on around them, its everyday life), and does it support the feeling
  or fight it?

## 4. The eye
- Where does the eye land first, then second, then third? What leads it there (light, contrast, a line, a colour accent,
  a figure, motion)?
- Is there one place to look, and is everything else quieter than it?
- **Which composition devices carry this feeling?** Pick them from the library, the owner's own reel ideas first
  (`COMPOSITION_SHOTS.md`: leading lines, a vanishing point, a frame within a frame, negative space, a foreground layer
  ...), name them by their code, and say why each serves THIS feeling; then invent the frame (a device is a tool, never
  a placement).

## 5. Space and scale
- Where does the camera stand (a person's viewpoint, high, low)? How far are we from her, from the world?
- How is distance felt (near, middle and far layers, overlap, air), and how is scale felt (a person against something big)?

## 6. Light, value and colour
- What time and what light? Where does the story light come from, and what does it touch?
- How light or dark, how contrasty, and how warm or cool, and WHY for this feeling (each scene lands differently: an airy
  morning, a warm intimate night, a stark moment)?
- What is the colour key, and the one accent?

## 7. Shapes, edges and paint
- What shape language carries the feeling (soft and round, tall and sharp, heavy and wide)?
- What is crisp (the subject), what is painterly (the far world), what is painted by hand (sky, greenery, surfaces)?

## 8. Words
- What do the captions say here, and do they sit in their band, readable? Is any word in the picture, and does it read at
  phone size (or is it cut)?

## 9. Motion and sound
- What moves first, what is alive (hair, cloth, water, light), and how does the camera move, if at all?
- What do we hear? Is there a moment where a sound meets the picture, and why does it earn one?

## 10. The hand-off
- What carries the eye and the story into the next frame (a shape, a motion, a colour, the thread)?

## 11. The test (before and after building)
- Name the feeling the frame gives in three words, looking at it at phone size. Does it match section 1?
- Is it worth pausing on? Is it one whole picture, not a layout with things placed in it?
- Mistakes only: captions readable and in their band, the app's buttons and bars clear of anything that must read,
  nothing important cut by an edge, no banding.
