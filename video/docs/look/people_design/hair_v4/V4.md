## 1. The method (what replaced v2/v3's generator)
1. **Each cut is DRAWN, per view, as a few authored pieces** (`cut_*.ts`): cubic Béziers in head units with few
   deliberate anchors (notation in `bez.ts`: corner / smooth / broken nodes), layered like a cut-out rig: **back**
   (behind the head and body; in profile over the coat), **cap** (rigid on the skull: the hairline, the parting, the crown
   with volume), **front** (bangs, curtains, face-framing lengths, a swoop), **0-2 accent strands** (open curves drawn as
   tapered ribbons). Plus ONE authored shadow shape and ONE highlight per view (each piece clips its copy), and 1-3 thin
   flow lines. Two tiny helpers are notation only (`draw.ts`: `scallop` turns hand-placed notches into curl-clump arcs,
   `join` smooths hand-placed points); every point is placed by hand.
2. **Drawn large on the real heads**: the design desk (`Dev.tsx`, a head-unit grid and the rig drawn over the figure) and
   a fast preview loop (esbuild + static SVG + Playwright, ~0.7 s a look), then LOOKED at beside the references and
   redrawn (5-8 rounds per cut).
3. **The 3/4 and profile drawings of a piece have the same anchors, landmark by landmark**, so a head turn MORPHS the
   designed shapes by the head's yaw (Live2D's keyforms; `morphOf`: yaw -38 -> -69), never pops them; the back view is its
   own drawing (yaw -150, a slight lost profile).
4. **The rig** (`rig.ts`): the cap glued to the head; each hanging piece skinned to 1-3 chains of 4-6 bones (a wide fall
   blends its two nearest chains); each anchor's weights by where it projects along its chain (a C1 partition of unity,
   the top share glued to the head); the anchor and its two handles take ONE blended affine transform, so a smooth node
   stays smooth in any pose (no kinks by construction); a guard eases a folded shape back toward rest.
5. **The springs** (`sim.ts`): Verlet at 240 Hz, the root pinned to the head, deterministic from t = 0 with a settled
   pre-roll, cached per motion. Per joint: **strength** (the bone back to its animated absolute direction, like gravity on
   a pendulum, so a sideways load tilts the chain as a whole), **shape** (the bone's rest angle relative to its parent),
   **elasticity** (a weak pull to the animated pose), **damping relative to the parent** (a steady walk is not braked),
   **air** (drag toward the wind's velocity; a walk is its own headwind), **gravity** (a bounded turn back to plumb when the
   head tilts), **bend limits**; a change of DRAWING (the yaw morph) is felt at 42 % (Spine's inertia), the head's own
   motion in full. Per cut (`Cut.spring`): long hair heavier and slower, short hair stiff, curls springy but holding.

## 3. The shading: soft (the pick) and crisp
Both from the same shapes (`hair.ts`): crisp = flat lit fill, the shadow and highlight as hard flat shapes; soft = one
linear ramp across the whole hair in head units from the key (upper left, +8 L*) to the far lower side (toward the shade
tone), the shadow and highlight soft-edged (blur 0.03 / 0.018 u). Both: 1-3 thin flow lines in the deep tone; the RIM
(`render.tsx` Fig4): a band inside the piece where the FIGURE's silhouette faces the key (the piece minus the whole
figure shifted away from the light; never on an edge against the face or another lock), 0.03 u, the key high on the left
so it lives on the crown and thins down the sides. **Pick: soft** (`stills/STILLS.md`: rounder, the gloss reads as gloss,
crisp reads as the flat v3 look). A short-hair `fade` (the skin showing through toward the hairline) is a ramp region.

## 4. The API for the assembler
```ts
import {hair, earsOf, viewOfYaw, specOf} from './hair_v4/hair';
import {CUTS4} from './hair_v4/cuts';
// after the head: const H = headV2(...) (or the new face's head, any HeadFrame with P/yaw/rot/s)
const view = viewOfYaw(H.hf.yaw);            // the head's own drawing: 'q' | 'p' | 'b'
if (earsOf(cut, view)) parts.push(...ear(...)); // the cut leaves the ears showing
parts.push(...hair(cut, view, H.hf, motion, {tone, shading: 'soft', px, skin: skinTone.lit}));
// motion?: {t, key, head: (t) => HeadFrame, wind?: (t) => [vx, vy]} (sim.ts MotionState); omit it for a still head
// (the hanging pieces settle under gravity for the head's tilt).
```
Parts are graphic/part.ts Parts plus optional `paint` (gradient), blurred `regions` and a `rimBand` (part4.ts): Fig4
(`render.tsx`) draws all of it; the old Figure draws them flat. Names start `hair-`; z: back 18 (46 in profile for long
hair), tucked lengths 52.3, cap 54.2, fronts 54.6-55, a layer over the shoulder 61; from behind 76-77.6. The test
figures (`people4.ts`) show the whole assembly on the v3 bodies; the motions (`motion4.ts`) show how a scene animates
the head and the air.

## 5. Bugs found by looking (and the fix)
- Gravity as a force summed bone by bone and flung long hair to its limits: now a bounded plumb turn of each bone.
- A drawing change (the yaw morph) moved chains half a head-width at once and they whipped: felt at 42 % only.
- A walk and a gust did nothing visible: the drag was 10x too strong, every pose folded and the anti-fold guard returned
  the rest drawing (a silent failure); then damping on absolute velocity braked a steady walk (the hair lagged a head
  behind); then relative-only springs let a sideways load curl the chain. Fixed: realistic drag, damping relative to the
  parent, an absolute-direction strength term (+ a weak elasticity).
- In profile the long back hair was drawn behind the coat, so a walk showed nothing: in profile it lies over the coat
  (z 46), behind the neck.
- Design: the far side boxing her face; the layer hidden behind the sleeve; a profile bang drawn as a slab; a helmet seam
  from behind; the lob's thin swoop and a skin tooth behind its ear; spiky crop tips; the flow reading as a bob; the
  ponytail's tail vanishing into the cap from behind. All redrawn (STILLS.md).
