# Hair, proportions, stature: the research (iteration 2, 2026-10-30)

**Status: done (job 1 of 2).** The research behind `RECIPE.md` (next to this file). It adds only what changes the
recipe; the earlier study stays in force where nothing here contradicts it (`../design_research.md` §3 proportions,
§7 hair, §10 poses; `../fashion.md` §2.4 and §5 hair).

Brief: `BRIEF.md`. The owner's references: `../refs_hair_2026-10-30/` (five images and their README). Our current
her: `baseline_her.png` (direction C with the LASH face, three views).

**Strength marks:** **S** = a study or a studio's own technical account; **T** = a textbook or a pro artist's course;
**P** = a practitioner's advice (a stylist, a barber, a magazine, a tutorial). Sources were read in full where they
opened (curl through the proxy, or WebFetch); where only an abstract or a search summary opened, the finding says so.

**Units:** H = a figure's height, crown to sole (1000 units in the engine). u = the skull, crown to chin (the head's
unit in `hair.ts`; an adult skull is about 22-23 cm, so 1 cm is about 0.045 u). px = pixels in the 1080 x 1920 frame.

| 1 | **Cuts now trade precision for movement:** "The strongest hair right now feels designed, but not over-designed"; the new bob keeps "a strong perimeter" and disrupts "the interior", "creating softness, separation and movement"; "waves, curls, bends, cowlicks, and irregularities can all become part of the haircut". | Marie Claire, fall 2026 haircuts (Adam Federico of R+Co; Christine Bellemare), https://www.marieclaire.com/beauty/hair/best-fall-2026-haircuts/ (P); Coveteur, fall 2026 (the ripped bob's "softly separated, piece-y ends"; the lob "between the collarbone and shoulders"), https://coveteur.com/fall-2026-haircut-trends (P) | Every cut is a clear overall silhouette (the perimeter) broken at its edges into separated, tapered pieces. | 2 | **Women in New York and Los Angeles now:** the soft swing bob (chin to collarbone, "a slight bend, a tucked side, a deep part"), the lazy lob (skims the shoulders), long layers with curtain or "bombshell" bangs (long, opening around the eyes and cheekbones), love layers (from below the chin, narrowing to the ends), the butterfly cut, the evolved shag, the bixie and the razor crop; air-dried natural texture over blow-outs. | Bangstyle, "The 5 haircuts everyone in New York wants right now", https://www.bangstyle.com/posts/trending-nyc-haircuts-6092 (P); Coveteur (above) (P); Marie Claire (above) (P); Fashion Magazine 2026 trends, via a search summary only: the butterfly cut as "a softer, more refined take on the wolf cut", https://fashionmagazine.com/beauty-grooming/womens-mens-haircut-trends-2026/ (P, weak) | The women's half of the library (W1-W7 in `RECIPE.md`). | 4 | **Textured hair leads:** natural curls and coils worn big; the curly modern shag "lifts volume at the roots while maintaining bounce", works on 3A-4C, "volume that moves, not volume that sits"; the textured pixie and the TWA; knotless braids; the curly mullet ("shorter at the front and dramatic in the back"). | StyleRave, "Textured hair trends 2026", https://www.stylerave.com/textured-hair-trends-2026/ (P); Coveteur's "shaggy curls" (above) (P) | Four of the twelve cuts are curly or coily, each a set of defined clumps with bounce, never a smooth ball. |
| 5 | **Hair gathers into clumps at several scales:** "adjacent hair strands tend to form clusters at multiple scales due to static attraction"; in motion the clusters split into sub-wisps and merge back, by their relative speed and direction. | Kim and Neumann, "Interactive multiresolution hair modeling and editing", SIGGRAPH 2002, abstract at https://history.siggraph.org/?p=118452 (S); Bertails, Kim, Cani, Neumann, "Adaptive wisp tree", SCA 2003, https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA03.207-213 (S, abstract) | The big-medium-small lock hierarchy is the physics, not only a drawing habit. At rest the locks merge into the mass; in wind, a walk or a turn they split: more locks and gaps show. |
| 6 | **Stylized studios design hair coarse to fine and keep that structure in motion:** Disney's groomers work "by first defining a small number of large clumps that roughly define the general shape of the groom, and then subdividing the clumps to add detail", and keep the hierarchy through simulation to hold "appealing shapes and silhouettes ... while maintaining the organic motion". On Tangled, loose springs keep "the hair generally together without forcing it to move as a single mass. The hair can still break apart from the volume into smaller pieces". | Kaur, Simmons, Whited, "Hierarchical controls for art-directed hair at Disney", SIGGRAPH Talks 2018, https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/175/asset/a13-kaur.pdf (S); Ward, Simmons, Milne, Yosumi, Zhao, "Simulating Rapunzel's hair in Disney's Tangled", SIGGRAPH Talks 2010, https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/56/asset/rapzHairSim.pdf (S) | The design order: the base mass first (the cut's identity), then one big lock, two or three medium, one to three small, then 0-4 wisps. The mass carries who she is; the locks carry the life. |
| 7 | **Gravity against stiffness:** a single hair bends under its own weight over a length of about 5 cm, ℓ = (A/λg)^1/3 (hair 79 µm thick, Young's modulus about 4 GPa); shorter hair holds its own shape, longer hangs. A ponytail leaves its tie at a "launch" angle of about 17 degrees, is swelled by the hairs' random curliness (its "body"), and narrows toward the tips under its weight. | Goldstein, Warren, Ball, "Shape of a ponytail and the statistical physics of hair fiber bundles", Physical Review Letters 108, 078101 (2012), https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.0371 (S); University of Cambridge news, https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/rapunzel-leonardo-and-the-physics-of-the-ponytail (S, summary) | In head units ℓ is about 0.2 u. Hair shorter than ~0.2 u stands or sticks out (a crop's top, a shag's crown layers, baby hairs: the flyaways). Longer hair rises off the scalp 15-20 degrees and turns down within ~0.2-0.3 u. Wavy and curly hair swells the mass. |
| 8 | **Every strand is a helix:** hair is an inextensible elastic rod with natural curvature and twist; modelling each strand as a chain of helices ("super-helices") predicts real hair motion across hair types. Curl is measurable: hair shape falls into eight groups by curve diameter, curl index and the number of waves. | Bertails, Audoly, Cani, Querleux, Leroy, Lévêque, "Super-helices for predicting the dynamics of natural hair", SIGGRAPH 2006, https://history.siggraph.org/?p=113612 (S); De La Mettrie et al., "Shape variability and classification of human hair: a worldwide approach", Human Biology 79(3), 2007, https://digitalcommons.wayne.edu/humbiol/vol79/iss3/1/ (S, abstract) | A lock's spine is drawn as a helix seen from the side: a soft S (straight), an S-wave (wavy), a chain of loops (curly), a lobed cloud (coily). The weight below stretches the helix most at the root, so waves are looser at the top and tighter at the ends (our inference from 7 and 8). |
| 9 | **Curls bounce; they never unwind:** Merida's curls first looked wrong as plain springs; Pixar added a "core spring" that "resists unwinding and maintains curl shape", lets curls stretch and spring back ("adding bounce during a walk cycle by allowing hair to stretch"); 1,500 sculpted curves grew 111,700 hairs. | Iben, Meyer, Petrovic, Soares, Anderson, Witkin, "Artistic simulation of curly hair", Pixar / SCA 2013, https://graphics.pixar.com/library/CurlyHairA (S); patent US9449417, https://patents.google.com/patent/US9449417 (S); Inside Science on Brave, https://insidescience.org/news/brave-features-hair-raising-animations (P) | A curly lock stretches 5-10 % on the step and recovers; it keeps its loops in every state. Never draw wind pulling a curl straight. |
| 10 | **A ponytail swings sideways when the head bobs:** the hanging tail is unstable to sideways motion under the head's up-and-down bob; a 25 cm tail's natural frequency is about 1.4 Hz, near a running cadence. | Keller, "Ponytail motion", SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 70(7), 2010, https://fermatslibrary.com/s/ponytail-motion (S; abstract and introduction read, plus a Scientific American summary) | In a walk the head bobs twice a stride and the ponytail swings side to side about once a stride; long loose ends sway the same way. A still of a walk shows the tail off to one side. |
| 11 | **Wind is everywhere, and it is shadowed:** Disney ran wind fields "in almost every shot" of Moana, "from subtle keep-alive to turbulent storms", with "wind shadowing" (the head and body shield part of the hair); "stiction" lets clumps stick, then break apart. Wet hair clumps into fewer, sharper clumps. | Thyng, Evart, Jones, McAdams, "The art and technology of hair simulation in Disney's Moana", SIGGRAPH Talks 2017, https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2017-Talks-Thyng_The-Art-and-Technology-of-Hair-Simulation-in-Disneys-Moana.pdf (S) | A breeze lifts the downwind outer locks and their tips most; the upwind side lies flat against the head; the hair in the head's lee moves least. Every head in a shot shares the scene's wind. In motion, "calm" still has a keep-alive. |
| 12 | **Follow-through and overlap:** loose parts such as hair keep moving after the body stops and move at different rates (Disney's principle). Animators delay hair tips about 2-4 frames behind the head at 24 fps (capes 3-6), each level of a chain 2-4 frames after its parent, and settle with eases. | Thomas and Johnston, "The Illusion of Life" (1981), the principle "follow through and overlapping action", https://archive.org/details/illusionoflifedi0000thom (T); Rebusfarm, "Follow through and overlapping action", https://rebusfarm.net/blog/follow-through-and-overlapping-action-the-animation-principle-that-prevents-stiff-motion (P); Brian Lemay, https://brianlemay.com/Pages/animationschool/animation/lipsyncbook/followthrough.html (P) | At our 60 fps: ends 5-10 frames behind the head, neighbouring locks 2-5 frames apart, a settle of one or two overshoots (`RECIPE.md` (c)). |
| 13 | **Hair has two highlights, across the strands:** the cuticle's tilted scales shift the white surface highlight "6 to 10 degrees toward the root" and the coloured, internally reflected one the other way; the coloured one is "not present in black hair". | Marschner, Jensen, Cammarano, Worley, Hanrahan, "Light scattering from human hair fibers", SIGGRAPH 2003, https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~ago820/cs395/Papers/W02_newRendering/Marschner_2003Hair.pdf (S) | The sheen is a band across each lock, at right angles to its flow, broken where locks overlap and repeated on each wave crest. Light hair may carry a second, warmer band a little toward the tips; black hair takes one low, cool band only. |
| 14 | **Draw volumes, not strands:** "design them as volumes, not strands"; a lock is "like a ribbon with some thickness"; connect the shadow shapes; "stray strands ... soften and break up the edge"; "a good balance of simple areas and complex areas"; taper each stroke from the root to the tip; vary the highlights. | Stan Prokopenko, "How to draw hair", https://www.proko.com/how-to-draw-hair/ (T) | The hair's inner divisions are slivers of shade between locks, never drawn lines; one to four wisps at most; one quiet area for every busy one. |
| 15 | **Pleasant proportions and real heights:** legs about 5 % longer than average are rated most attractive, much longer worse (already our P2); differences in stature are mostly differences in leg length, while the head stays nearly constant (about one eighth of the height at 25); US adults aged 20-39 average 162.7 cm (women; 5th-95th percentile 151-173) and 176.0 cm (men; 164-188). | Sorokowski and Pawlowski, Evolution and Human Behavior 29 (2008), https://stafforini.com/works/sorokowski-2008-adaptive-preferences-leg/ (S, abstract); Bogin and Varela-Silva, "Leg length, body proportion, and health: a review with a note on beauty", IJERPH 7 (2010), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2872302/ (S); Fryar et al., "Anthropometric reference data for children and adults: United States, 2015-2018", NCHS Vital Health Stat 3(46), tables 9 and 11, https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/series/sr_03/sr03-046-508.pdf (S) | A height sheet from 150 to 190 cm covers the 5th-percentile woman to the 95th-percentile man. The head changes little (±4 %) and the legs carry the height, so a 150 cm woman is ~7.2 heads and a 190 cm man ~8.5. |
| 16 | **Relaxed and healthy is not upright-rigid:** the craniovertebral angle (the line from the base of the neck at the back, C7, to the ear, measured from the horizontal) averaged 53.7 ± 5.1 degrees in 72 healthy adults aged 22 ± 2 standing "in a self-selected comfortable upright posture" (below ~48-50 counts as forward head posture); the pelvis normally tilts forward about 13 ± 6 degrees; open, expansive postures read more attractive ("an open, inviting kind of dominance"). | Yong, Lee, Lee, "Correlation between head posture and proprioceptive function in the cervical region", J. Phys. Ther. Sci. 28(3), 2016, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4842453/ (S); "Clinical measures of pelvic tilt in physical therapy", IJSPT 2021, https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/27978 (S, review); Vacharkulksemsuk et al., "Dominant, open nonverbal displays are attractive at zero-acquaintance", PNAS 113 (2016), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4839399 (S; one successful replication) | Our her's neck is a vertical block at about 72 degrees (measured below): lean it to 55-60, give the back its S, let the arms hang off the body. Never hunched, never ramrod. |
