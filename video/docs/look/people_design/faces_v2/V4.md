## 1. What was asked, and the read of the gap
The orchestrator's verdict on the faces_v2 sheets: the directions are right, the drawing is not at the references'
level (blocky profile shapes, straight segments where curves belong, a helmet of placeholder hair). The bar: the
blue-cliff woman (ref 05): delicate, soft, cute, appealing. The owner's newest note (the ref 03 bob beside our v3, no
words; `../hair_v4/BRIEF.md`) adds "her face: delicate, appealing, alive; ours: a blank face", and asks for one "open
eyes, looking away, never at us" variant beside LASH.

**Why the old drawing read blocky (the method, not the numbers):** every face was a ROUNDED POLYGON (`graphic/geo.ts`
rpath: straight edges cut back at each corner by a radius). Where the radius is smaller than half an edge, a straight
run stays between two arcs and the curvature jumps from zero to a fixed value; a face drawn that way is a chain of
straights and circle pieces, so the nose, the lips and the jaw read as cut cardboard however good the landmarks are.

## 2. The method (v4)
1. **A pen, like an illustrator's** (`pen.ts`): each contour is a few authored NODES, each a point, a tangent
   direction and two handle lengths, joined by cubic Beziers. A smooth node keeps both handles on one line, so every
   join is tangent-continuous by construction; a corner node (only the lips' meeting) gives the out-handle its own
   angle. Long handles make the flat runs, short ones the tight turns (the nose's tip, the chin, the lips), so the
   curvature varies along each line the way a drawn line does. This is how a vector pen tool works: smooth anchor
   points with direction handles, corner points where a path changes direction [T: Adobe's own Pen tool
   documentation, https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/using/drawing-pen-tools.html].
2. **The cranium kept exactly**: each view's crown-and-back is the current code's own outline (the same rounded
   polygon, extracted as segments by `rpolySegs`), so every hair shape made for the current heads still fits; the new
   face joins it at two points with the old tangents (profile: (-0.155, -0.44) heading -18.9 degrees and (0.415,
   0.2) heading 137; the 3/4 and the lost profile likewise, `faces.ts` craniumP / craniumQ / craniumL). The head
   frame is unchanged: head units, crown -0.5, chin 0.5, the face toward screen-left.
3. **Three drawn views, like a cut-out rig's turnaround**: the profile (yaw -90), the 3/4 (yaw -36) and the lost
   profile (yaw -138), each authored on its own (`faces.ts` DESIGNS); a turn swaps the drawing at the switch points
   (`head.ts` viewOfYaw: |yaw| < 63 the 3/4, < 114 the profile, else the lost profile; mirrored for screen-right), as
   cut-out tools rig characters from drawn pieces with image swapping [T: Toon Boom Harmony's own documentation,
   https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-22/premium/cut-out-animation/about-cut-out-animation.html].
4. **Soft modelling inside a crisp two-tone**: the lit plane is a gentle gradient (warmer and lighter toward the key
   and the face's front, about 5 L* across), ONE crisp terminator to the shade plane, a warm tone on the cheek's apple
   (a radial fade, never a dot), the lips a rosy tone fading toward the mouth's corner (never a line, never lipstick),
   the nose's one comma-shaped crease, the ear one shape with one notch, the jaw's cast shadow a crescent on the neck
   (DIRECTION rule 5: colour moving gently inside a plane, the step between planes kept crisp; the playbook's "few
   shapes lit warm on one side with soft gradients"). `soft: false` gives the two flat tones back.
5. **Looked at, beside the references, at 1400 px a skull and at film size, and redrawn** (the log below): about a
   dozen rounds on the profiles alone.

## 3. The API (for the assembler)
```ts
import {head, placeHead, viewOfYaw} from './people_v4/head';
import {body} from './people_v4/body';
// the body first: parts in the figure's units (H = 1000, the crown near y 0, the sole at y 1000) + where the head goes
const B = body('f', 'average', 'stand', 'tee', {yaw: -58, headYaw: 22, tones, neck: true});
const {view, mirror} = viewOfYaw(B.head.yaw);                       // the drawn view for the head's yaw
const H = head('f', view, {skin, hair, px: skullPx, eyes: 'lash'}); // {headFrame, faceParts} in head units
const parts = [...B.parts, ...placeHead(H, {c: B.head.c, s: B.head.s, rot: B.head.rot, mirror}), ...hairParts];
// draw: paint.ts partsSvg(parts, uid) -> one SVG string (gradients included); the figure is then scaled by h / 1000
```
- `head(sex, view, o)`: `sex` 'f' | 'm'; `view` 'p' | 'q' | 'l'; `o.skin`, `o.hair` (Tone); `o.eyes` 'lash' (default)
  or 'open' (the variant); `o.brow` (default: on for him and for open eyes, off for her lash face); `o.px` the
  skull's size on screen for the detail ladder (under 12 px: the contour and the shade plane only; 12-18: + the 3/4
  nose's shade and the jaw's shadow; 18-40: + the lash, the brow, the ear as one shape, the cheek and lip tones, the
  nose's crease; 40 and up: + the ear's notch); `o.soft` (default true).
  Returns `headFrame` {units 'head', crown -0.5, chin 0.5, view, yaw, `cranium` (the kept crown and back: the hair's
  fit), `outline` (the whole skin outline), `lm` {faceX, nape, jawAngle, earC, eyeY}} and `faceParts`.
- **Part names and z** (the hair goes round them): neck 50, head 52 (its regions: the shade plane, the cheek, the lips,
  the nose), ear 53.2, eyes 53.3, brow 53.4, lash and lid 53.5. A hair cut puts its back mass under 50 (under 40 when
  the body faces front, so it hangs behind the shoulders) and its cap over 53.5; on a tucked cut the ear (53.2) stays
  in front of the hair's back and under the cap.
- **The neck rule**: in profile the head's own designed neck (the throat's line, his Adam's apple) is used and the
  body's is off (`neck: false`); in the 3/4 and the lost profile the body draws the neck (it follows the body's yaw)
  and the head's `neck` part is dropped (see `assemble.ts` figureV4, the reference assembly).
- `body(sex, build, pose, outfit, o)`: `build` 'slim' | 'average' | 'athletic' | 'soft' (the recipe's ratios held,
  the widths moved); `pose` 'stand' (the relaxed upright stance: the weight on one leg, an arm off the body) | 'walk';
  `outfit` 'tee' (a fitted tee, slim trousers, low shoes: the composition check; the wardrobe builds next); `o.yaw`,
  `o.headYaw`, `o.tones` {skin, top, bottom, shoe}, `o.neck`. Returns `parts`, `skel` (stance.ts SkelV2), `cam`, and
  `head` {c, s, rot, yaw} for placeHead.
- Rebuild the stills from `video/`: `node scripts/render-frames.mjs PvHer <dir> 0 --entry=src/entry-look-people-v4.tsx
  --concurrency=1` (also PvHim, PvBodies); the reference crops: `python3
  docs/look/people_design/faces_v2/stills_v4/refs_crops_v4.py`.

## 5. The bodies (RECIPE section 3, built on the current skeleton)
The skeleton is hair_v2's (`stance.ts` skelV2 + standV2 with the UPRIGHT neck); the projection is the rig's
(`graphic/rig.ts`); every outline is new: the limbs are tubes along a smooth axis with AUTHORED radius profiles (her
leg: the thigh's full top, its taper, the knee's narrowing, the calf's swell high on the shin, a long slim taper to a
27-unit ankle; the arm: the deltoid, the upper arm, the elbow, the forearm's swell below it, a 24-unit wrist), the
torso rings at the recipe's widths and depths (the shoulders' slope 19 degrees for her, 13 for him), and each
silhouette is ONE smooth curve (centripetal Catmull-Rom through the projected silhouette, as cubics). The terminator
is the rig's, drawn as a smooth curve; a soft gradient inside the lit plane.

## 7. My look (the brief's question)
- **Her: cute and appealing? Yes, at our flat style's dose.** In profile the small scooped nose with the lifted round
  tip, the soft lips, the small round chin on the face line and the short soft jaw under a closed, flicked lash read
  delicate and pretty, the nearest of all our versions to ref 05's feeling; the 3/4 reads open and gentle (a soft V
  face, the nose a small shadow); never a doll, never sexualised. She is less "alive" than ref 03's girl with the lash
  closed: that is what the open-eye variant is for, the owner's pick.
- **Him: manly and friendly? Yes.** The brow shelf, the deep bridge, the straight nose with a defined tip, the square
  chin and the jaw's crisp corner against a thick neck with a soft Adam's apple read manly; the calm straight brow and
  the short relaxed lash keep him friendly, never a brute.
- **Against now**: the gap is plain in every panel: now's long straight nose, sunken lips, receding chin and long jaw
  shelf (her) and his brow-less, cornerless face are gone, and every line is a drawn curve.
- **At film size** (200 px): the faces are the contour, as planned; the profiles keep a cute nose (her) and a jaw
  against the neck (him); the bodies read feminine and manly at a glance. Nothing odd seen at 1:1.

- **The owner's calls**: (1) the LASH or the open-eye variant for her (01, row 1 and 2); (2) brows: v4 draws a soft
  brow on him by default and on the open-eye variant (PEOPLE_DESIGN 5.3's never-list says "no brows drawn as arcs";

  keeps no brow; `o.brow` switches either way.
- **The stand-in hair** is not a cut proposal; the stills will read better with the hair round's cuts on these heads.
- **The 3/4 is drawn at one yaw (-36)**: a turn swaps views at the switch points (section 2.3); in-between yaws are not
  interpolated (a cut-out turnaround, never a morph); a 3/4 at -55 to -60 would be a fourth drawing if a film needs it.
- **Hands** are simple mittens (one smooth shape) and the tee is the only outfit: the wardrobe and the hand poses are
  the next build (wear.ts's garments can be redrawn on these smooth tubes the same way).
- **Motion is not tested** (stills only, as the brief says); the face parts are per view and rigid with the head, so
  they ride the head's rig as one piece.
