# Calm city water, drawn premium: mirror, Fresnel, slices, glitter, ripples, wakes (research, 2026-10-28)

The water researcher's lane for the owner's note on video 2's G4 look clip v5 (`deliverables/v2/v2r_G4_look_v5.mp4`):
"I don't like the water too much, leafs are great" ... "It's just looking way too cheap, not clean or premium quality or
refined. Just lazy and cheap. I mean that about the water." v5's water: a flat mint band with box-shaped beige
reflection patches, scattered white dashes and four-point star sparkles. The playbook's rule (DEPTH AND SEPARATION:
"water gets the same clean, premium, refined drawing as everything else ... designed ... as one shared component") and
CRAFT's "Failures the eye catches first" (cheap water) come from that note; this page is the research behind the
redesign: how refined flat illustration, print and animation draw calm water, the optics that make it read true, rules
with numbers, and how to build one shared `lib/water.tsx` that S1, S2, S3, S4, S5 and S8 all feed.

**Rank:** research notes, the lowest rank (owner's words > DIRECTION > CRAFT > PIPELINE_V2 > gate specs > look docs >
these notes). Nothing here is a rule until the integrator moves it into `CRAFT.md` or `src/lib`. **The one decided
exception** (this job's brief): the water may reflect the shot's own sky preset (`lib/sky.tsx`, toned toward the water,
dithered the same way), because that is the sky's own gradient seen in the water; nothing else in the water gradates
(no blur, no glow, no other gradient). **Tags:** VERIFIED = computed or measured here (Fresnel, the S1 colours read
from `lib/sky`, the render costs, the bench frames). REPORTED = from a web search summary: page fetches were blocked
in this sandbox (jamesgurney.substack.com and www.montana.edu did not resolve), so book and blog content is as the
search summaries quote it. DERIVED = geometry worked out from the cited optics for our level cameras. PROPOSAL = our
own numbers, not yet rendered as a design. S1's camera: horizon y 965, focal 1400 px, the eye 1.45 m over the water
(the diagnosis lane's `src/v2r/water_study/geometry.ts`, `S1W_CAM`).

## 0. Bottom line

**Why v5 reads cheap, in one line:** it is a fill, not water. Real water is a MIRROR of its own shot, seen through a
Fresnel veil, cut by ripples; v5 mirrors nothing (its mint, L* 75-80 at h ~185, sits 11-15 L* under, and at the
opposite temperature of, the peach-to-cream sky it should mirror), and its marks (boxes, dashes, stars) are stamps
that follow no geometry.

**The six rules that matter most** (all 14 in §3):
1. **Mirror the shot** (R1): every reflection hangs straight down from its own waterline, y' = 2 y_m - y; the sky
   mirrors about the horizon (S1: y' = 1930 - y, so S1's water shows the sky's warm lowest band, never the mint);
   reflections show the scene from below and lose what stands set back.
2. **Fresnel** (R2): the far water mirrors, the near water shows its body: S1's reflectance 0.53 at the far edge, 0.26
   at our edge; reflections lose about half their strength toward the viewer, in 3-4 steps.
3. **Tone like a shadow, never mix in RGB** (R3-R4): lights a little darker (-3 to -8 L*), darks lighter (+8 to +18),
   hue and warm chroma kept; an RGB or `atmos` mix of S1's warm sky into the teal gives khaki or beige (VERIFIED).
4. **Simplify, then slice** (R5-R6): masses, verticals and lights survive, lines and small parts do not; ripples cut
   reflections into level slices spaced by perspective (S1: 2.6 px far, 7.7 at y 1215, 13 near) with a gentle per-row
   sway; open water stays a calm field.
5. **Light = vertical columns; the sun = one narrow glitter column of level slivers** (R7-R8): a lit door draws a
   column as wide as itself; the sun's path is ~7x longer than wide in S1, densest at the sun's mirror point (y 1158),
   made of lens-shaped slivers (6-40 px x 1.5-4 px, 12-24 alive, 0.25-0.7 s each), never four-point stars.
6. **Calm motion** (R12): the masses, the sky ramp, the contact lines stay put; only slices sway (+-1.5-3 px far, +-4-7
   near, 2.5-5 s), the phase travels toward us at 3-26 px/s (real wave speeds, foreshortened), glitter shimmers in its
   column, wakes follow their movers as a clean, very flat Kelvin V (2.5 deg per arm at S1's duck).

**Build:** one spec-driven component, `lib/water.tsx`: (1) a WATER RAMP, the shot's sky mirrored and toned per row
(one dithered gradient over the water's box: the allowed exception), (2) reflections from a LIST of simplified sources
per shot (masses, verticals, lights; each with its own waterline), sliced into perspective rows and batched per
colour, (3) light columns, (4) the glitter column, (5) contact lines and bank shades in the rig's water tint, (6) a few
ripple lines, (7) wakes. Measured on S1 (§4): the list + ramp + dither add ~20-40 ms per frame to S1's ~665 (the
ramp alone is within the bench's noise); re-rendering the set flipped costs +180-240 ms and drags every line and the
hero colour into the water; 24 sliced copies cost +930-1290 ms and crashed the page in 8 of 8 fresh browsers; an
`feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` wobble costs +250 ms and smears every edge (rejected).

## 2. Cheap vs premium (the list)
| cheap tell (v5 or common) | premium (what the rules below build) |
|---|---|
| a flat band whose colour has nothing to do with its sky | the shot's own sky mirrored (y' = 2 hz - y) and toned by Fresnel: warm where it mirrors the horizon, the body's teal near us |
| box-shaped reflection patches (square ends, one per object) | each object's reflection under its own base, simplified, cut into level slices with round or tapered ends |
| scattered white dashes all over the water | 6-14 ripple lines, level and lens-tapered, on perspective rows, placed where they counterchange with a reflection edge |
| four-point star sparkles ("magic" emoji, not light) | ONE glitter column under the sun of horizontal lens slivers, densest at the sun's mirror point |
| the same contrast from the far bank to our feet | Fresnel: the far water mirrors, the near third shows the body; reflection strength halves toward the viewer |
| a stamp of the object's colour, any length | a mirror about the waterline: set-back things lose their base, undersides show, verticals run straight down |
| a full-detail copy (letters, mullions, outlines, the hero's colour) | masses, verticals and lights only; no lines; the subject's reflection calmed to tier C |
| pure white highlights (#FFFFFF) and white dashes | warm white L* 95-97, only in the sun's (or a lamp's) column |
| reflections toned by opacity or an RGB / `atmos` mix (khaki, beige, grey) | OKLCH toning: L* toward the water's, hue and warm chroma kept, a light crossing |
| a hard seam where bank meets water | a thin dark contact band (the wet stone / waterline), then the bank's own reflection; the bank's shade on the water |
| uniform stripes across the whole water ("barcode") | slices only inside reflections; open water a calm ramp; rows spaced by perspective |
| two random white strokes behind a duck | a Kelvin V at 19.47 deg in the plane, foreshortened (2.5 deg per arm at S1's duck), tapered, aging into dashes |
| jitter, popping sparkles, fast scrolling dashes | still masses; slow sway; shimmer only in the glitter column |
| the water pasted under the scene, unlit | the same rig: sun-side warmth, the glitter where the sun is, boats' and quays' shade in the rig's water tint |

Precedents: woodblock printers carried the gradation into river and sky (Hiroshige's bokashi, partly wiped pigment;
Ashmolean), and the hastily printed later editions are the ones that "simplified gradations into flat bands" (UMMA,
REPORTED): a flat water band is the cheap edition. Hasui's evening canal adds "flecks of yellow ... to the bluish
green to capture the reflections of the light" (Yokohama Museum of Art, REPORTED): the glitter is small, warm flecks
on a calm field. Hockney called water "a formal problem ... because it can be anything" and moved between flat planes
and line systems (Phillips; Geist; REPORTED): water is a designed language, decided once, not a texture.

## 3. The rules, with numbers
**R1. The water is a mirror of its own shot.** (Ruskin: the reflection is the objects above the water reversed
beneath it, and you see of them whatever you could see from where you stand; Gurney, Water Reflections Part 3: a
reflection is not simply a flipped copy, its forms run to the scene's own vanishing points; McNee: the reflected scene
is seen "from an angle of view as far below the surface as your eyes are above"; all REPORTED.)
- Level camera (DERIVED, all our sets): a point at (x, y) of a thing whose waterline (the water surface right under
  it, at its depth) is y_m shows in the water at (x, 2 y_m - y). The sky and the sun (at infinity) mirror about the
  horizon: y' = 2 hz - y (S1: 1930 - y). Vertical lines stay vertical; reflections hang straight down, whatever the
  light direction (unlike shadows).
- A straight quay receding on screen (y_m = a0 + s x) mirrors everything on its face with ONE affine map:
  `matrix(1 2s 0 -1 0 2a0)` (x' = x, y' = 2 a0 + 2 s x - y). S2's and S3's receding banks need no per-column work.
- From below: undersides show (a soffit, eaves, the arch's intrados: a bridge's arch and its reflection close into an
  ellipse; a hull's chine), tops of horizontal surfaces never do (a pier shows its underside; Painting With
  Watercolors). A thing set back from the water loses its lower part behind the bank's reflection (an Adobe forum
  answer: posts behind a bank show "only the upper few inches"); the farther back, the less of it.
- S1 (VERIFIED): the strip mirrors the sky at y 820-630 only, the warm horizon and the light band, never the mint
  caption field; in front of the wing it holds the ground storey's lowest ~1.2-2 m; v5 shows none of it (§1). The
  mirror is exact for a level camera: "compressing" more of the sky into the water (to bring the mint down) is a
  declared art cheat, not the default.

**R2. Fresnel: the far water mirrors, the near water shows its body.** (Fresnel; Schlick; 3drender's glossary;
Gurney, Transparency of Water: looking steeply down you see into the water, looking across you see reflections.)
- Reflectance of calm water by the view's depression angle (exact Fresnel, n 1.333, unpolarised; VERIFIED): 1 deg 0.90,
  3 deg 0.72, 5 deg 0.58, 6 deg 0.53, 8 deg 0.43, 10 deg 0.35, 12 deg 0.29, 15 deg 0.21, 20 deg 0.13, 30 deg 0.06,
  >= 45 deg 0.02-0.03. Schlick's approximation reads ~10 % high near grazing (0.58 at 6 deg).
- S1: the far edge (y 1110, 5.9 deg) 0.53; y 1215 (10.1 deg) 0.34; our edge (y 1290, 13.1 deg) 0.26: the far water
  is twice the mirror of the near.
- In flat colour: 3-4 strength steps across the water, following the perspective rows: reflections at full design
  strength along the far bank, ~half in the near third, where the body (CM.waterDeep's teal) shows most. A steep view
  (S3's sliver under our hull, a view down from a bridge) is mostly body, little mirror.

**R3. Values: lights a little darker, darks lighter, mids hold.** (Gurney, Paint Here "Water Reflections": the light
tones of the scene appear a little darker in the reflection; a WetCanvas summary of his rule: dark colours reflect
slightly lighter, light ones slightly darker, medium ones nearly the same; another painter: in very clear still water
the values are nearly identical, sediment (a city canal) increases the shift; Watercolour Workout: dark objects reflect
lighter, light objects darker, and water softens what it mirrors; all REPORTED.)
- Numbers (PROPOSAL, from R2's weights): lights -3 to -8 L* (the mirrored horizon L* 88.8 -> ~83-86; the lit lobby L* 85
  -> 80-83), darks +8 to +18 L* (the boat's slate hull 52 -> 59-67; the anchor plum 17 -> 35-53), mids within +-3
  (brickLight 74 -> 74-76): every reflection moves toward the water's own L* by k = 0.3 at the far bank, 0.6 near.
- Nothing in the water is lighter than the sky it mirrors except the glitter (R8) and the light columns' cores (R7).

**R4. Colour: tone in OKLCH, keep the hue and the warm chroma; never mix in RGB.** The house NEVER list (beige, muddy
tan, olive, grey) is where a naive mix lands:
- VERIFIED on S1: the physically weighted mix of the mirrored sky into the teal body in linear light (R 0.53) gives
  #CDC6B2 (L* 79.9, C* 10.9, h 95: khaki); `lib/depth` `atmos` toward the teal gives #D5C0AD (C* 13, h 70) and #C8BAAF
  (C* 8): beige; the bench's opacity overlay over the mirrored sky reads beige-grey (`2026-10-28_water_bench.jpg`, row
  "W-sky"). The sky lane met the same trap (a 2-stop mint -> peach crosses khaki #DCDBC5) and solved it the painters'
  way; the water inherits that answer.
- The toning (PROPOSAL; a `reflectTone(src, k, water)` in OKLCH or lib/light's CIELCh): L* toward the water's own L*
  by k (R3); hue kept, warm hues may turn 5-10 deg toward rose and cool ones toward violet (the rig's shade family,
  CRAFT §d.3); warm chroma >= 0.9x and never under 18 at h 40-95 below L* 86 (there it reads beige; lighter, a low
  chroma reads as cream); near-neutral sources (C* < 8:
  glass, limestone, a pale band) take the water's hue at C* 8-10; the olive band (h 88-118 at C* < 30) never entered.
  Test vectors, k 0.3 / 0.6 toward CM.water (VERIFIED arithmetic): horizon #FBD9BB -> #F1CFB4 / #E7C4AC; lamp #F7CF86
  -> #F2C784 / #ECBF82; brickLight #E3A88C -> #E5AA92 / #E7AD98; slate hull #6F7C91 -> #858FA2 / #9CA2B4; anchor
  #36223A -> #5F4B62 / #8B788D; glass #CDE1DF -> #C1D8D7 / #B8CFCE.
- The water's own ramp (the mirrored sky, R1) crosses from warm to cool where the sky does, through a LIGHT, low-chroma
  band (L* >= 86, C* <= 8: the mirrored light band), never through a darker grey; the warm rows are toned in value
  only (-3 to -6 L*), the cool near rows go toward the teal body (hue, chroma and value). S1 test vector (PROPOSAL):
  y 1110 #E9CBB0 (L* 83.5, C* 18.5, h 70) ... y 1215-1250 #E9DBD0-#E2DED6 (L* 88-89, C* 4-8) ... y 1300 #A6C6BA
  (L* 77.3, C* 13.3, h 169). Untoned, the mirrored sunset reads as paving or sand (bench row "W-rampDither"): the
  warmth needs the contact band (R10), the reflections and the ripples to read as water, and a far water that must
  not melt into the far towpath (paving #EFD8C2, L* 88): CRAFT §f.3's separation applies at the far edge.

**R5. Simplify: what survives the mirror.** (Gurney, Water Reflections Part 2: even a light wind's wavelets break up
a reflection and dissolve its horizontal lines while the verticals stay intact; masts stay crisp while gunwales blur;
his 2016 post: thin horizontal lines disappear; 2012: the elongation grows with the contrast; REPORTED.)
- Keep (PROPOSAL, S1 scale): masses >= ~20 px tall and ~12 px wide in the reflection, verticals >= 3 px wide (lamp
  standards, posts, figures, bollards, masts), every light. Drop: anything under ~6 px (mullions, sills, letters, rails,
  ropes), every line (tier C: no outline or hairline in a reflection). One value per reflected object (its lit and
  shade merged), two at most for a big mass; a sign is a block, never mirrored letters. One plane simpler than the
  thing itself (`planeLod(plane + 1)`).
- The subject's reflection is environment, never a second subject: her teal (the hero colour lives on her only) goes
  through R4 at k >= 0.6 and stays at C* <= 25, a calm, broken silhouette that grounds her. The bench's plain mirror
  shows the failure: a crisp upside-down teal coat right under her (`W-mirror`).

**R6. Ripples cut reflections into level slices spaced by perspective; verticals survive.**
- Rows at equal real spacing, projected (DERIVED): dy = (y - hz)^2 dz / (f H). S1 (f 1400, H 1.45 m), dz 0.25 m: 2.6
  px at the far edge (y 1110), 4.2 at 1150, 7.7 at 1215, 13 at 1290 (VERIFIED arithmetic): ~5x thicker near.
- A slice is a flat band with round or tapered ends (never square); between slices a gap of the water's own ramp
  colour, 1-2 px far -> 2-4 px near (counterchange), but ONLY inside reflections: open water stays a calm field (the
  bench's full-width stripes read as a barcode, as v3's canal did: row "W-rampDitherList").
- Per-row sway (DERIVED: a sideways tilt g shifts a far reflection by ~g (y - hz)): +-1.5-3 px far -> +-4-7 px near
  (tilts of 0.5-1.2 deg), neighbouring rows varying smoothly, so a vertical becomes a gentle zigzag, never a sawtooth;
  a post's reflection stays one wavy vertical.
- The reflection is crispest at its axis and breaks up with distance from it: its last 25-35 % fragments into separate
  dashes with growing gaps (Gurney 2018: the reflection's bottom edge breaks into horizontal fragments; REPORTED); a
  rippled reflection may run 10-30 % longer than its source (PROPOSAL; Gurney 2018: reflected trees stretch downward).

**R7. Lights become vertical columns with parallel sides.** (Ruskin via Gurney: motion in water stretches reflections
into vertical streaks; a 1 deg tilt toward or away from the eye moves the image 2 deg up or down (phys-l); Kuchel
2026: the light pillar keeps a constant image width, its sides parallel, because the widening patch of contributing
facets is compressed as 1/distance; REPORTED.)
- Every lit thing over the water (lit doors and windows, lamps when on, a bright sky gap) draws a column straight down,
  as wide as the light (+-15 %), as long as the slopes allow: +-2-3 deg tilts give +-4-6 deg, ~+-100-150 px at f 1400
  around the light's mirror point: in S1 the whole strip.
- Built from R6's rows: one sliver per row under the light, lengths +-20 %, a few rows missing, densest and lightest at
  the light's mirror point, thinning toward the ends; colour by R3-R4 (the lobby's gold -> L* 80-83 at C* >= 35).
- S1: the lit lobby doors (x 540-720) -> a gold column ~150-180 px wide through the strip; the far lamps when lit (S8)
  -> 8-14 px columns; a dark pane (W after 0.40) -> no column (part of the facade's mass).

**R8. The sun's glitter path: one narrow column of level slivers.** (Shaw, Glitter patterns, OPN 1999: for a high
source the angular length is ~4x the maximum wave slope, and "the width-to-length ratio is given by the sine of the
source elevation angle", narrowest when the elevation is twice the maximum slope; NOAA: calm water gives only a mirror
image, gentle waves a narrow path, steeper waves a wider one; a later study (Cleveland State, "Glitter and glints on
water"): most glitter comes from capillary wavelets; REPORTED.)
- Straight under the sun; densest and brightest at the sun's mirror point y = 2 hz - y_sun (S1: SUN0 at y 772 ->
  y 1158, mid-water); it runs from the far water to our edge, thinning toward both ends.
- Width (DERIVED for a level camera, effective max slope a): half-width ~ a (y - hz) + f a sin e, e the sun's elevation
  (S1: 7.85 deg, sin e 0.14: the path ~7x longer than wide). A calm canal (a 3-4.6 deg): 35-54 px wide at y 1110,
  40-62 at 1158, 47-71 at 1215, 56-85 at 1300: a slowly widening column, never a fan (v5: 80 -> 160 px over 56 px).
- Slivers, not stars: level lens shapes (pointed ends); length 6-12 px far -> 20-40 px near, height 1.5-2 -> 3-4 px
  (aspect 6-12:1); 12-24 alive at once (one phenomenon, one field); each lives 0.25-0.7 s, growing in and shrinking out
  along its length (no fade); placed blue-noise in (x, y, t), denser and longer at the mirror point (a Gaussian in y,
  sigma ~0.3 of the column); warm white L* 95-97, never #FFFFFF (McNee: no pure white for sunset highlights). Under them
  a still, flat gold bed: the sun's reflected colour (L* +6 over the water round it), the brightest field in the water.
  At phone size (360 px) the column reads as a shimmer; near slivers >= 20 px long (CRAFT §f.6's floor).
- Flat precedents: Tuts+'s flat lake scatters glints made from a small shape cut in half along a horizontal line (a
  flat-bottomed sliver); Hasui's yellow flecks (§2).

**R9. Ripple lines: few, level, tapered, placed for counterchange.**
- 6-14 in S1's strip (1080 x ~150 px), never a scatter: each a lens tapered at both ends, on a perspective row; length
  ~25-40 px far -> 60-120 px near (with the px per metre: 100 at the far edge, 224 near), 1-1.5 px thick -> 2-3 px.
- A light line (the mirrored sky's colour, +6-10 L*) across a dark reflection, a darker line (the body, the rig's water
  tint, -6-10 L*) across the light sky; most of them at a reflection's edge, where they explain its breaks.
- They drift with the water (R12) or hold and breathe (length +-15 % over 2-4 s).

**R10. The bank: a contact band, then the bank's own reflection, and its shade.** (Painting With Watercolors: a darker
line "where the hull meets the water ... some of this color to bleed up as well as down"; REPORTED.)
- Where a quay, step or hull meets the water: a thin dark band, the wet stone or the waterline (2-3 px far -> 4-6 px
  near), in the deepest value of that thing's own reflection (its shade, toned by R4), never a grey contact strip on
  water (LIGHT.md: they grey the teal); right under it, the reflection of the quay face: the darkest band in the water,
  its own height mirrored (S1's far coping, 15 px, as the diagnosis' `reflTop` has it).
- A bank between the sun and the water shades it: a flat band of the rig's water tint (cast strength,
  `TINT_CANAL.water`) along the bank, 8-20 px, parallel to it; reflections run through it one step darker (multiply);
  no glitter inside it.
- In S1 the contact band is also the separation the warm far water needs from the warm far towpath (R4).

**R11. Wakes: a clean V at the Kelvin angle, foreshortened.** (Kelvin 1887: in deep water the arms sit at arcsin(1/3) =
19.47 deg from the path at any speed, a 39 deg wedge filled with curved transverse waves; fast hulls show narrower
wakes (Rabaud and Moisy 2013), while the outer edge holds the Kelvin angle (Darmon, Benzaquen and Raphael); a duck and
a cargo ship make the same V at different scales; REPORTED.)
- On screen at row y (DERIVED): tan(arm) = k tan 19.47 deg with k = (y - hz) / f: S1's duck (y 1136, k 0.12) 2.5 deg
  per arm, the arms 17 px apart 200 px behind it; y 1300, 4.8 deg; a mover heading into or out of the picture opens
  wide (56-71 deg per arm in S1): S2's passing boat.
- Drawing: two tapered light lines from the chest (the crests' sky reflection, +8-12 L*), 2-3 px -> 1 px, 3-6 body
  lengths long (S1's 62 px duck: 190-370 px), 1-2 short transverse arcs inside the V near the bird, a small bow
  crescent; the tails break into 2-3 dashes and shorten as they age (never fade); the V lies on the water plane, so it
  sits on R6's rows.
- Speed: a mallard cruises ~0.5 m/s (a popular summary, weak) -> ~60 px/s at S1's duck; slower reads calmer. A still
  duck gets no V, at most 1-2 slow rings from its paddling (ellipses with ry/rx = k, growing ~25-35 px/s, thinning).

**R12. Motion: slow, small, and most of the water still.**
- Still (riding only the plane's parallax float): the sky ramp, every reflection's position (only its slices sway),
  the contact bands, the bank shades, the glitter's gold bed.
- Sway: per row, offset = A(y) sin(2 pi t / T - phi(y)), A +-1.5-3 px far -> +-4-7 px near, T 2.5-5 s; the phase travels
  toward the viewer at real wave speeds foreshortened (DERIVED, S1): 0.25-0.5 m/s -> 2.6-5 px/s at the far edge,
  8-15 px/s at y 1215, 13-26 px/s at y 1290. A slice's peak sideways speed 2 pi A / T ~ 5-15 px/s.
- Ripple lines drift with the current or the breeze at 0.1-0.25 m/s -> 10-25 px/s far, 20-55 px/s near (S1); for
  scale, the slowest free ripple on clean water travels 0.23 m/s (the capillary-gravity minimum; REPORTED), so much
  faster than ~0.3 m/s reads as a scroll, not as water. Glitter lives 0.25-0.7 s; wakes ride their movers.
- Tier C (CRAFT §b): the water's motion together <= ~10 % of the frame's motion energy; nothing enters in a join's
  0.4 s (the shimmer keeps its pace); a pure function of t (seeded sin, `@remotion/noise`, lib/rng), never
  Math.random or a clock; at rest with the float on every map-critical frame.

**R13. Evening and morning: one component, the shot's own sky and rig.** (Yoshida printed the same blocks in new
colours for morning, forenoon, afternoon, mist, evening and night (Mia; Toledo; MOA; REPORTED): the geometry stays, the
colour comes from the time of day.)
- Evening (S1-S4, S8): a low sun -> a long, narrow glitter column (width/length 0.1-0.2); the far water warm (the
  horizon mirrored), the near water teal; warm columns under lit doors, windows and (S8) lamps; an evening breeze ->
  a few more ripple lines.
- Morning (S5, April): a higher sun -> a wider, shorter column (width/length = sin e: 0.34 at 20 deg, 0.5 at 30 deg),
  smaller, whiter slivers; the water mirrors a paler, cooler sky; morning calm -> longer, more intact reflections,
  fewer ripple lines; no warm columns.

**R14. Far water: bands and flecks, never one flat band.**
- When R6's rows would be under ~2 px (S5's Potomac: y 801-824, 23 px tall, far): no slices. 2-3 flat bands (the far
  shore's reflection as a darker band right under it, the mirrored sky ramp, the near shore's contact band); glitter as
  single short flecks (2-6 px) in the sun's column; current as 2-4 long, thin tone-on-tone lines; motion only in the
  flecks. The bokashi rule (§2) holds at every size: even a 23 px river carries its sky's gradient.

A single `<use>` of a group costs about what A costs (Chrome builds a shadow copy of the referenced subtree; not
measured separately). A canvas or WebGL detour (`@remotion/three`, a 2D water shader that flips a screen texture and
offsets it with noise, as Godot's 2D water shaders do) would rasterise the vector edge and is an owner decision.
Strip slicing is how 16-bit consoles faked it (a background drawn upside down with a per-scanline scroll, "squished" by
skipping lines: the Konami-style mirror raster effect; SNES HDMA sine offsets; REPORTED): in SVG the same idea is
cheap only when the slices are our own shapes (D), never clipped copies of the set (B).

## 5. The recommendation: `lib/water.tsx`, one shared component
**5.1 The spec** (PROPOSAL; each shot keeps its numbers in one exported const, as S3's `S3_WATER` already does):
```ts
type WaterSpec = {
  region: Pt[];                 // the visible water (screen px at camera rest), tucked under its banks
  horizon: number; f: number;   // the shot's level camera (groundCam): hz, focal px
  eye: number;                  // the eye's height over the WATER (m): z(y), rows, px/m
  sky: SkySpec;                 // the shot's own lib/sky preset (mirrored: R1, R2, R4)
  water: string; body: string;  // the water's mid colour (R3's target L*) and its deep body (near rows, ripples)
  tint: string;                 // the rig's water tint (contact bands, bank shades, boats' shade)
  sun?: {x: number; y: number; slope?: number};  // the glitter column (R8); slope = a (deg), calm 3-4.6
  sources: Src[] | ((t: number) => Src[]);       // what stands above (R5): masses, verticals; dynamic for movers
  lights?: Light[] | ((t: number) => Light[]);   // lit doors, windows, lamps (R7)
  banks?: Bank[];               // quay / step / hull edges: contact band + optional shade (R10)
  wakes?: Wake[] | ((t: number) => Wake[]);      // ducks, boats (R11)
  ripples?: {count: number; seed: string; drift: number};                 // R9
  motion?: {amp: [number, number]; period: [number, number]; seed: string}; // R6 / R12
  lod?: 'near' | 'far';         // far: R14
  t0?: number; joins?: number[]; // births and the join windows (CRAFT §f.6)
};
type Src = {kind: 'mass' | 'vertical'; x0: number; x1: number; top: number; base: number;
            axis: number | [number, number, number, number]; color: string; tier?: 'subject' | 'environment'};
```
**5.2 Layers, bottom to top** (raw SVG like `lib/sky` and `lib/light`: computed colours, never re-tinted by the pen's
tier pull; inside the shot's water plane group, so the parallax float carries it): (1) the water ramp (E2; R1, R2, R4);
(2) reflection masses and verticals as row slices (R5, R6), batched per colour; (3) light columns (R7); (4) the bank
shades in one isolated multiply group (R10); (5) contact bands (R10); (6) ripple lines (R9); (7) the glitter's gold bed
and slivers (R8); (8) wakes and rings (R11). Rows, colours and the ramp are cached by value (a spec rebuilt every render
costs nothing); per frame only the sway, the glitter's live set, the ripple drift and the movers are computed.
**5.3 Colour math in one place:** `reflectTone(src, k, spec)` (R3-R4) and `waterRamp(spec)` (the mirrored sky toned per
row, its crossing kept light), both OKLCH with lib/sky's helpers, both under a `waterProblems` guard.

**5.5 QA:** `waterProblems(spec)` before a render (no reflection or ramp colour in the beige band (h 55-95, C* < 18,
L* < 86), the olive band (h 88-118 at C* < 30) or grey (C* < 4 under L* 85); far rows at least as reflective as near ones; the glitter column

joins); `scripts/qa/check_banding.py` on the ramp's open water after the master encode; `scripts/qa/check_depth.py`

byte-identical in two processes); the cost (at most +50 ms per frame on S1).
**5.6 Open decisions** (for the integrator or the owner, not applied quietly):
- CRAFT §f.6's glints ("20-36 px sparkles or level dashes, 4-6 twinkling at once") and `lib/particles`' glints preset

  (<= ~16) has to treat the column as one field.
