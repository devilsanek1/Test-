# Coziness: what makes a drawn space feel cozy, as rules for our shots (research lane, 2026-10-25)

For v2 (DeepMind staff resigning over military AI, afraid to speak out) and v3 (laptop prices: desk nook at night, data
center outside). Colours follow `../PALETTE.md`. Tags: **VERIFIED** = primary source or several independent sources agree;
**REPORTED** = one secondary source or a search summary; **UNCERTAIN** = contested or our inference; *(proposal)* =
our numbers, to test at G4. **Caveat:** page fetches were blocked here (DNS / proxy 403), so every claim comes from
search-result summaries.

## 1. The model: safety + abundance + softness, with the threat kept outside

- **Project Horseshoe 2017** ("Coziness in Games: An Exploration of Safety, Softness, and Satisfied Needs", T. Short et
  al.): coziness is how strongly a work evokes the fantasy of **safety** (no danger or risk, nothing forced),
  **abundance** (needs met, nothing pressing, so attention turns to belonging) and **softness** (gentle sensory signals,
  slower tempo, intimacy, a manageable scope). [VERIFIED, S1-S3]
- **Small spaces feel safe because you can check them at a glance**, but only by choice: a small space you can't leave
  becomes a trap, and vast unknown space cancels safety. A campfire makes a cozy zone inside a forest. [REPORTED, S1]
- **The threat outside makes it stronger, as long as it stays outside.** Cold rain on a window makes a reading nook
  warmer, but a storm coming in through a broken window ends the coziness (D. Cook's write-up). [REPORTED, S2]
  Bachelard: winter besieging a house deepens its intimacy, and he dwells on Rilke's lamp in a hut window. [REPORTED, S4]
- **Prospect-refuge** (Appleton): people prefer to see out (prospect) while protected (refuge), which is a nook with a
  window. [VERIFIED as a theory, S5; how strongly it holds is UNCERTAIN]
- **Coziness is a device, not a genre** (Krzywinska et al. 2025), so it can sit inside a serious explainer. [VERIFIED, S6]

## 2. The owner's references, in words

- **Sense of enclosure:** a narrow cobbled mews in overcast light. Facades about as tall as the street is wide hold the
  path, ivy softens the walls, wet cobbles lead in, and a tall tower **closes the end of the view**. Enclosure is the
  degree to which vertical elements define a space [VERIFIED, S7]. This is already v3's composition, with the tower in the data center's place.
- **Arcane collage:** top left, two small back-view silhouettes look over a city of tiny **warm** windows under a vast
  **cool** night (coziness and scale at once). Top right, a lone tower fires a cold beam: the data center's role.

## 3. Light and palette

- **Warm, low and dim reads cozy; bright, cool and even reads clinical:** a lower colour temperature raised perceived
  cosiness and more luminance lowered it (the Vogels line; a shop study agrees). [REPORTED, S8-S9] Danish *hygge* light
  is several small warm sources, not one overhead light [REPORTED, S10]. Wiking's "about 1,800 K" comes only via blogs [UNCERTAIN, S11].
- **At night, the lamp is orange-yellow and the window is the cool side** (Gurney, *Color and Light*). [REPORTED, S12]
- **Rules:**
  1. **The temperature contrast does the work, not darkness or saturation.** The room stays a light warm page (Sage
     Study `bg #EEF0E3`) with the lamp pool as a soft mid (`hl #F2D49B`). The cold lives inside the window in Dusk tones
     (`#BAC3CE`, `#8A9AA8`), with Dusk's anchor `#1F2433` on the silhouettes outside (about 5 %). In the nook shot the window is
     the only Dusk area, at most about 20 % of the frame *(proposal)*. In the scale pull-back Dusk takes the whole frame (PALETTE.md allows this pairing).
  2. **Light pools are flat and stepped:** a shade trapezoid widening about 3x, an ellipse pool 0.5-0.7 W (the
     `v1/style_bible_ref2.md` section 8 baseline) and at most 2 stepped rings (CINE `<Glow>`). No gradients, no blur.
     The pool lights the subject's **hands and face**, as Shizuku's desk lamp does in *Whisper of the Heart* (S16).
  3. **Low, few sources:** one lamp plus at most one secondary source (a screen or a reflection), no ceiling light
     *(proposal)*. The corners step down one palette step (`ground #D7E0C8`), never to a dark.
  4. **The lamp is not the pop.** The single hot pop (1-3 %) is kept for the "look here" beat, e.g. v3's price tag.
     Use Sage's amber or Dusk's coral, never both. The hero colour goes on the story's object (laptop or data center).
  5. **A warm reflection of the lamp in the night glass** *(proposal)* sets up the scale shot's one warm window.

## 4. Softness, abundance and object density

- **Curves read safer than angles:** people prefer curved versions of the same objects (Bar & Neta 2006, replicated
  across cultures). The explanation that angles signal threat is contested. [VERIFIED effect / UNCERTAIN cause, S13]
- **Lived-in clutter makes Ghibli rooms feel inhabited** (mismatched plates, books, herbs, pans), and food and the
  hearth carry care, as with Calcifer's breakfast in *Howl's Moving Castle*. [REPORTED, S14-S15]
- **Rules:**
  1. **Two shape languages.** Inside, everything is rounded (lamp, mug, cushions, book corners, the window's corners).
     Outside, the threat is rectilinear and hard (grids, server rows, a sharp roofline). Reverse this only at the breach.
  2. **Abundance at the edges, an empty centre:** props cluster on the shelf, sill and desk edge, about 15-25 % of the
     frame and 6-12 props, each readable at 60 px or more *(proposal)*. They are line drawings (PALETTE.md), so the subject stays the solid mass.
  3. **Show at least one sign of care:** a steaming mug, a blanket on the chair, a plant, a sleeping companion (S1).
  4. The halftone (at most variant B) goes on **one** cozy surface (the blanket or the shade), as the last step only.

## 5. Framing: walls that hold you, a window to see out

- **Enclosure in 9:16:** the portrait frame gives side walls for free. Put the subject between two vertical masses (the
  shelf; the window frame), on the desk, under a soft lid (a shelf top or the shade) below the caption band (y 300-560).
- **Always leave a visible exit:** the window is the prospect, the nook the refuge (S1, S5). Without one, the room reads
  as confinement, which v2 can use on purpose. **Close the vista with the threat** (seen through the window, like the
  mews tower), not beside the subject.
- **A lit window seen from outside means loneliness:** MoMA reads Hopper's *Night Windows* (1928) as access to
  strangers' lives against urban loneliness. [VERIFIED, S17] Inside = safe, outside = small and alone. Use this in the
  scale pull-out through the glass: our room becomes **one** warm rectangle in a cold grid.
- **Don't light every window:** Kinkade's all-glowing cottages are the textbook case of cozy tipping into kitsch, "so
  insistently cozy that it seems sinister". [REPORTED, S18] Light one warm window; the rest stay dark or cold.

## 6. Motion and time

- **Ma:** Miyazaki clapped and called the silence between the claps "ma". Non-stop action is just busyness, and constant
  tension numbs the viewer. [VERIFIED, S19] The *Totoro* bus stop: rain, waiting, near stillness, and drops drumming
  on an umbrella (an enclosure you can hold). [REPORTED, S20]
- **Rules:** inside motion is small, slow and looping (steam, breathing, a page). Outside, the weather is steady (rain
  streaks, snow, frost creeping in at the glass corners). The camera pushes gently, 3-8 % (CINE), and never shakes.
  **No flicker inside until the breach.** Use 1-2 still holds of 0.5-1.0 s after the heaviest line *(proposal)*.

## 7. Sound

- Natural sounds shift the body toward "rest and digest", most for stressed listeners (Gould van Praag 2017) [VERIFIED;
  for rain, UNCERTAIN, S21]. Room tone grounds every other sound [REPORTED, S22]. Cozy beds put crackle in front, rain
  on the glass behind, and thunder muffled. [REPORTED, S23]
- **Rules** (inside the playbook's mix: voice on top, -14 LUFS, music subtle at the start):
  1. **The bed:** room tone plus rain **behind glass** (low-passed), about 18-24 dB under the voice and ducked on
     speech *(proposal)*. It can stand in for the music at the opening.
  2. **Soft close foley on cozy actions** (a mug set down, a page, a pencil, a lamp click): sounds that feel good.
  3. **The breach in sound:** open the filter (harsh, present rain), add a gust, then near-silence for the ma hold.

## 8. Coziness next to a serious topic, without making it trivial

- **The trap has a name: the "cosy catastrophe"** (Aldiss, 1973). It was an insult for disaster stories in which the
  hero has a nice time while everyone else dies. [VERIFIED, S24]
- **Working models:** *In This Corner of the World* carries a war through domestic routine. The harm happens mostly
  offscreen and we see its impact, and critics say nothing is sugar-coated. [REPORTED, S25] *Spiritfarer* is sold as "a
  cozy management game about dying". [VERIFIED, S26]
- **Rules:**
  1. **The coziness belongs to the people affected**, never to the threat. The threat is never cute, rounded or warm.
  2. **The warm room is the stake:** make what is at risk specific and ordinary (your desk; a person's quiet life).
  3. **Keep the threat visible in the cozy shot** (through the window). Hiding it makes the warmth complacent.
  4. **Break the coziness once, at the story's turn:** cold light floods in, a pane cracks or the lamp gutters. Never twice.
  5. **On the serious line, strip the comfort:** stop the loops, drop the bed, hold the ma; no cute props near real harm.

## 9. Applying it

- **v3 desk nook:** the refuge sits between the shelf and the frosty window, and the lamp lights the hands and the
  laptop. Through the window, the data center closes the view as a hard cold grid of status lights. The breach (prices
  reach the room) comes at the turn. In the scale pull-back, the room shrinks to one warm window as the tower rises (0.7 H or more) in Dusk. The
  viewer feels small but still has a home in the frame. Shizuku's desk reportedly inspired the Lofi Girl mascot (S16).
- **v2 (resignations):** the cozy enclosure is the person's world (a kitchen table at night, a letter, a mug). "Afraid
  to speak out" is enclosure turned into confinement: blinds down, walls closer, a smaller lamp pool, no exit. Speaking
  out is the window opening. The institution is the cold, rectilinear outside, at scale. Never draw identifiable real staff.
