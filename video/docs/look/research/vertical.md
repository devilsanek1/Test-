# Vertical (9:16) scale and coziness: geometry, phone readability, platform UI, timing, respect (research lane, 2026-10-25)

Feeds `../COZY_AND_SCALE.md`; sisters `enclosure.md` (walls, H:W, Maertens), `coziness.md` (the feeling); colour per `../PALETTE.md`.
Tags: **VERIFIED** = our measurement / arithmetic, or several sources agree; **REPORTED** = one secondary source or a search
summary; **UNCERTAIN** = contested, vendor-only or unconfirmed. WebFetch failed (DNS) on every page, so web claims come from
search-result text. Conventions: 1080 x 1920, `persp1` f 1000, captions block y 317-556 / x 95-945, 6.1" phone at 34 cm.

## 0. The rules in brief
1. **Key reads live on the "stage": x 120-880, y 290-1250.** The bottom 35 % (y > 1250) and the right rail (x > 780, y > 840:
   the widest reported column) are covered by platform UI. Big masses may run under the UI; tiny figures, contact points and labels never do (§2).
2. **Stack depth vertically to match the UI:** foreground mass at the bottom (under the UI, dark anchor), the read in the
   middle (y 600-1250), the colossus / sky field at the top through the caption band as a flat fill (§1).
3. **The tiny observer's floor is 40 px tall; aim for 60-150 px (0.03-0.08 H)**, as a fill-only silhouette, on the centre
   axis (x 0.35-0.65 W), as the only high-contrast mark in the stage (§3).
4. **Eye-height scale shots: horizon at 0.52-0.59 H** (top edge ≥ 45° up, yet a ~115 px figure's feet stay above the UI);
   a low camera (0.3 m, the head breaking the horizon) allows up to 0.64 H (§1).
5. **A big scale move lasts 2.5-4 s, lands, holds 0.8-1.5 s with one small secondary motion**, and never runs over a gap
   in VO or captions longer than ~1.5 s. Peak-scale time in total stays at or under ~25 % of the runtime (§5).
6. **Never open on a silent scale shot:** frame 0 carries the payoff words; a scale opener must itself be the claim (§5).
7. **On serious topics, the camera stands with the person (eye level), looks up at the institution, and never hero-lights
   it.** Scale shown as fact must be true or signposted as a metaphor; an agency or human beat follows each small-making beat (§7).

## 1. What 9:16 does to scale (VERIFIED, arithmetic unless tagged)
- **Height is native, breadth is scarce:** per unit of width, 9:16 holds 3.16x the height of 16:9 (1.78 / 0.5625).
  With f 1000 the frame sees 56.7° across and 87.7° up and down. Towers, walls, shafts and one figure suit it; Arcane's
  wide vistas (collage A1) must become depth stacks (rows receding UP the frame), not breadth.
- **Looking up without tilting:** lowering the horizon in a level camera (a shift lens) keeps the verticals parallel, which
  suits our flat style and the enclosure walls. Tilting up makes the verticals converge (keystone); it is caused by tilt,
  not by the lens (photography sources, VERIFIED). Use a 3-point tilt for at most one peak shot per video.
- **Horizon height → how far up the frame looks** (f 1000; elevation of the top edge / of the caption band's bottom y 556):
  0.40 H → 37.5° / 12.0°; 0.50 H → 43.8° / 22.0°; 0.52 H → 45.0° / 23.9°; 0.62 H → 50.0° / 32.4°; 0.70 H → 53.3° / 38.2°.
  Maertens' 45° (see `enclosure.md` §4) = "too close to take in as a whole". So **feel-small = a colossus cut by the top edge
  with the horizon at y ≥ 0.52 H (top edge ≥ 45° up)**; **cozy = walls topping out at 27-45°**, flat where they cross the captions.
- **Feet of a figure standing on the ground:** feet y = horizon + figure_px x camH / 1.7 m. With an eye-height camera (1.6 m):
  75 px figure → horizon ≤ 0.61 H; 115 px → ≤ 0.59 H; 150 px → ≤ 0.58 H to keep the feet above y 1250. With a 0.3 m camera
  all three fit at a horizon ≤ 0.64 H. The style bible's hook recipe (horizon 0.62 H) needs the low camera.
- **The vertical layer map** (our proposal, built from §2): y 0-290 top UI → framing only (fronds, wires; no paper edge,
  playbook); y 290-600 captions on a flat field (sky or the colossus as one fill); y 600-1250 the read (the tiny figure, the
  colossus's base or door, the window in the nook); y 1250-1920 foreground mass cut by the bottom edge (a desk edge, a ledge,
  a big back-view figure: these read by mass under the UI). Big near figures may be cut by the bottom; tiny far ones may not.
- **Cozy + scale in one frame:** the nook's window is a 9:16 frame-within-the-frame placed in the stage; its mullions are the
  dark anchor; the data center appears through it, then the pull-back carries the window out past the frame edges.
- **Vertical viewing:** vertical ads raised engagement through fluency, no phone turning (Mulier et al. 2021, REPORTED);
  a reported experiment found no immersion difference between vertical and horizontal (UNCERTAIN, no primary source).

## 3. Readability at phone size
- **Scale of a pixel (VERIFIED, arithmetic):** on a 6.1" phone 1 video px = 0.060 mm = 0.61 arcmin at 34 cm (mean phone
  distances 36.2 cm for texts, 32.2 cm for the web: Bababekova et al. 2011, VERIFIED). A 5.4" phone: 0.54 arcmin; 6.7": 0.67.
- **Captions:** x-height 45 px = 2.7 mm = 1.9 M vs 1.1 M for texts (same study): 1.7x. Never shrink them at peaks.
- **Smallest figure (VERIFIED arithmetic on REPORTED criteria):** the eye resolves ~1 arcmin, so one line pair ≈ 3.3 px.
  Johnson's criteria (50 % thresholds, from military imaging): recognise a class at 4 line pairs, identify at 6.4, across the
  critical dimension (≈ 0.5 x a standing person's height). So "a person" reads from ~25 px tall and "a person doing what"
  from ~40 px; double that for compression and motion → **60-80 px for reliable reads**. Applying it to drawings: UNCERTAIN.
- **Below ~80 px draw the figure as one fill-only silhouette** (no interior lines; 2 px ink ≈ 1.2 arcmin is at the limit),
  like `WindowCloseup`'s LOD. Our halftone (6-7 px pitch) survived a 3.5 Mbps Reels-like encode (`../PALETTE.md`, VERIFIED).
- **Gist is instant, details take fixations:** a scene's gist is caught in ~100 ms; one fixation lasts ~275-300 ms (Greene &
  Oliva 2009; review, REPORTED). The "huge thing, tiny person" contrast reads at once; finding the tiny figure takes 2-3
  fixations (~0.6-0.9 s) if it is salient. Hence the 0.8-1.5 s landing hold and the single high-contrast mark.
- **Centre column:** gaze favours the screen centre whatever the task (Ioannidou et al. 2016); a centred subject in vertical
  video gave longer fixations (RealEye); educational TikToks drew "stable" gaze on 1-2 areas (n = 16) (all REPORTED). So **at
  most 2 points of interest per scale shot: the caption and the tiny figure (or one lit detail)**, both on the centre axis.

## 4. Scale compositions for 9:16 (proposals; numbers from §1-3 and `../../v1/style_bible_ref2.md` §8)
- **Tower / colossus:** cut by the top edge, ≥ 0.7 H of mass in frame, base or door inside y 600-1250, ratio colossus :
  observer ≥ 10:1; above the caption bottom it is a flat fill (`quietAbove`).
- **Wall of windows / canyon:** walls cut by the top AND both side edges (in 9:16 the sides are already tight), the vista
  closed by one object on the centre axis (`enclosure.md`); the observer at the canyon's floor line inside the stage.
- **Vista from a height (A1, re-stacked):** horizon 0.35-0.45 H, rows climbing the frame, the observer's back cut by the bottom.
- **Light shaft (A6):** core 0.10-0.14 W on the centre axis, pool and figure in the stage, its top fading below the captions.

## 5. Timing: how long a scale moment can last
| finding | number | tag |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok: state the proposition in the first 3 s; land the hook in the first 6 s | 3 s / 6 s | REPORTED (TikTok ads help) |
| Our v1: a calm, pretty opening frame lost ~80 % of viewers by 3 s | n = 70 viewers | VERIFIED (playbook) |
| Ref 2: big pull-back 3-3.5 s; per-shot zoom 1.0-2.5 s; a caption event every 1.13 s | measured | VERIFIED (style bible) |
| Establishing shots are held 2-5 s by convention | 2-5 s | REPORTED (blogs) |
| Static shots over 3-5 s may hurt retention; a pattern interrupt every 3-5 s | 3-5 s | UNCERTAIN (vendor blogs) |
| Faster pacing reduced sustained engagement (7 edits of a 20 s clip, N = 242) | — | REPORTED (conference paper) |
| Powers of Ten: out at 1 decade of scale per 10 s, back in at 1 decade per 2 s | 10 s / 2 s | VERIFIED (several) |
- **Rules:** one big move per video, 2.5-4 s, eased in and out; land and hold 0.8-1.5 s with one small motion (a window
  lighting, the observer's head turning); keep VO and captions running through the move (a caption change at least every
  1.5 s); 2-3 peak moments of 1.5-4 s each, ≤ ~25 % of the runtime; a still peak frame holds 1.5-3 s.
- **Rate of scale change:** short form can't afford Powers of Ten's documentary 10 s per decade; use its inward pace, about
  1 decade per 1.5-2 s. The v3 pull-back (a person at 1.7 m → a campus hundreds of metres across ≈ 2 decades) fits 3-4 s.
- **Not before 3 s** unless frame 0 already carries the payoff words and the move IS the claim.

## 6. What references and short-form creators do (REPORTED unless tagged)
- **Pull-back as a punchline:** TikTok's "insecurity vs Earth zoom-out" format (2022; Know Your Meme) and the 2025 AI "Earth
  zoom" effect, mostly outward as it "reads as more dramatic" (PixVerse, vendor). Lesson: intimate close-up, then pull back.
- **The industry's own scale language is map overlays:** Zuckerberg showed Meta's planned clusters over Manhattan (2025);
  Epoch AI measures campuses in Manhattan blocks and Central Parks (Hyperion / Holly Ridge ≈ 4x Central Park by 2030).
  Viewers know these units; they also tell us the honest shape: data centers are wide campuses, not towers.
- **Arcane's camera is physical:** no move that would not make sense in live action; calm, linear camera in Piltover,
  handheld in Zaun (3dvf, REPORTED). For us: crane, dolly and pull-back moves only, no impossible spins; calm and linear
  for institutions. Back views facing the vista in 7 of 12 stills (style bible measurement, VERIFIED).
- **Zack D. Films (25M+ subs):** fast 3D, curiosity gap, smooth camera (freelancer listings, UNCERTAIN). Kurzgesagt / TED-Ed: no source (gap).

## 7. Using scale respectfully on serious topics (v2: DeepMind staff vs military contracts; v3: AI and prices)
- **Power is relational:** height reads as power fastest when a power contrast is present in the frame (Schubert 2005;
  Lakens et al. 2011, VERIFIED). Put the person and the institution in the same frame rather than a lone tower.
- **Low angles flatter what they look up at:** product ratings were best from low angles and worst from high angles, but
  only for low-motivation viewers (Meyers-Levy & Peracchio 1992; one failed replication; REPORTED). Scrollers are
  low-motivation. So: **no golden rim, glow or hero colour on the thing under critique** (the military contract, the data
  center); it stays a flat, cool, fill-only mass; light and the pop belong to the people.
- **Dignity:** never look DOWN on the small person; the camera stays at or below their eye level: small, but we stand with them.
- **Truthful scale:** never distort visual information; label illustrations (SPJ Code, VERIFIED). The v3 "tower-tall" data
  center is a metaphor: say the true unit in VO or caption (campus area in Central Parks), or show the stack as a stack.
- **Small self, then agency:** awe shrinks the self and raised prosocial behaviour (Piff et al. 2015, one minute looking up
  at tall trees, VERIFIED). But threat without efficacy drives defensive avoidance (EPPM, VERIFIED), and 39 % avoid news
  because it hurts their mood (Reuters DNR 2025; 16 % "powerless" in 2022; REPORTED). Follow every small-making beat
  within ~5 s with a human voice, a fact of what is being done, or what the viewer can do [proposal].
- **Anonymity (v2):** generic back-view figures, no likeness of real staff (animated-documentary practice, REPORTED): scale + anonymity.
- **No spectacle, no harm:** no weapons or explosions as awe; no flashes over 3 per second covering > 25 % of the screen
  (Ofcom / WCAG 2.3.1, VERIFIED); no rapid zoom-in-out or large parallax swings (vestibular triggers, REPORTED).
- **Dose:** 1-2 peak scale moments in a serious video, not 3; the cozy frame is the place we return to.

## 8. Colour in scale shots (per `../PALETTE.md`)
- **The field stays light:** background / sky L* ≥ 75, C ≤ 20 even at night or at a peak; night comes from the palette
  (Sage Study → Dusk) and lit windows, not from dark fields. **Conflict to resolve:** style bible §3.2-4 night sky `#588077`
  is L* 50.5 and Arcane frames have 9-49 % median lightness; PALETTE.md (owner, 2026-10-25) wins unless the owner says otherwise.
- **The colossus is a soft mid**, fill-only, 1-2 haze steps from the background, so it can cover 30-60 % of the frame.
- **The tiny observer is the "look here" mark:** the one pop (C ≥ 50; under 1 % of the frame here) or a dark-anchor
  silhouette (L* ≤ 40) against the light field (ΔL* ≥ 35): the only high-contrast mark in the stage.
- **The dark anchor (~5 %) is the near frame** (window mullions, desk edge, ledge) at the bottom and sides, under the UI.
- **The hero (5-15 %) stays on the story's object** (v3: the laptop / lamp pool; v2: the people), never on the institution.
- **Captions:** ink on the light field, ≥ 7:1 (DNA), so no white-caption switch is needed.
