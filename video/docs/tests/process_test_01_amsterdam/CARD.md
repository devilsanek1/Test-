# CARD: the hero frame, "The paper half" (process test 01, Amsterdam; `video/docs/look/FRAME_CARD.md`)

The moment and the concept: `CORE.md`; the world and the look in words: `VISION.md`; the picked idea: `IDEAS.md` 1.
Filled before the build, in words; §11 is answered again on the built frame.

## 1. The moment and the feeling
- **What happens:** at dusk, on the little brick bridge by her house, she has just asked her phone "When was my street
  built?"; the canal under the bridge has stopped mirroring the street and shows its paper twin, and right under her
  the old pages are waking, glowing, and their writing is rising to her phone.
- **What the viewer should feel:** a hushed "whoa", the held breath of seeing something you know (a bridge and its
  reflection) turn quietly impossible: the city remembers, and it is answering her. Then a pull to stay and lean in:
  what is it saying? Warm and full of wonder, the feeling of a candle in a dark room, never eerie or ghostly.
  **Why here:** it is the hook, a second or two into the film, right after frame 0's ordinary evening bridge and the
  question dropping in; it has to stop a thumb with one gentle impossible thing, and its warmth has to promise that
  the answer will be personal.
- **Understood in one second, sound off:** the water under this Amsterdam street holds the street's past in old
  paper and handwriting, and that paper is lighting up and answering her.

## 2. The idea
- **The one picture:** from a small boat low on her canal we face her humped brick bridge. The arch and its
  "reflection" close into one ring, the most familiar Amsterdam picture there is, with one thing wrong: the lower half
  is old paper. Bound ledgers stand spine-out where the arch stones should mirror, long written sheets lie in courses
  for the bridge's face, and through the ring the canal's houses run on above the water while their paper twins hang
  below it into the dusk. Under her, inside the ring, the pages glow like paper lanterns, a few sheets peel up off the
  water, and a winding stream of light with loose loops of script climbs to the phone she holds out over the rail.
- **Why stop scrolling:** a double take in a second (that reflection is paper), then the light pulls the eye up the
  stream to a person, and the dark twin running on through the ring asks for a second look.
- **This film's world, concept and thread:** yes: the canal that remembers (the twin is the film's device), her
  canal, her bridge, her question as the small warm light; it opens what the later frames pay off (her own house
  answering, then the long twin still dark).

## 3. Life and action
- **Who and what:** her, alone on the crown of the bridge, leaning on the near railing on her forearms, phone held out
  over the water, head bowed toward the light, looking down at it, not at us; her bike leaning on the rail beside her.
  The story's action (the waking pages, the stream, her phone and lit face) sits in the middle of the frame, inside and
  above the ring, exactly where the eye goes; nothing of the story sits at the back.
- **The world going on:** a real Jordaan evening, quiet: lit windows up and down the canal (a kitchen, a reading
  lamp), bikes chained to the bridge railing, a houseboat moored at the right quay with pots on its roof and one warm
  window, an Amsterdam lamp at the bridge's left end, the late-October elms, a church tower faint down the canal. All
  of it low in contrast and small, so it says "a lived-in street, an ordinary evening" and makes the light feel more
  special, never competing with it.

## 4. The eye
- **First** the glowing pages in the ring (the brightest, warmest thing and the hardest value step, framed twice: the
  ring, then the dark around it); **second** up the winding stream to her phone and her gold-lit face, her head clear
  against the pale low sky behind the bridge; **third** the ring as a whole (brick above, paper below) and the dark
  twin running away through it; then the caption, its "answer" in the same gold.
- **What leads it:** the light (one gold source), the ring (a frame within a frame), the stream (a line from the
  source to her), the one colour accent (gold only on the pages, the stream, the phone and the lit word), the dark bow
  of our boat pointing into the ring from the bottom edge.
- **One place to look:** yes; the lamp and the houseboat window are smaller, dimmer, cooler warm lights, one each
  side, and the street is one quiet mid-dark mass.
- **Checked against the composition library after imagining it** (the ideas step did this; kept): C1 the frame within
  a frame as a ring (B13), E2 the lit opening, N7 figure and ground (her head against the pale low sky; the ring's light
  in the dark), E1 one small figure where the lines meet, E8 one colour accent, E6 atmospheric layers (the far canal
  paler through the ring), C2/E5 a near hint (our bow), D4 one lamp and one counterweight instead of a matched pair;
  E7 (a closed vista) left out on purpose: the dark through the ring stays open (the twin runs on).

## 5. Space and scale
- **The camera:** where a person sits in a small boat, eye about a metre above the water, some twenty metres from the
  bridge, straight on, verticals upright (a calm, frontal, almost symmetric view for the hush; the symmetry broken by
  her just off the crown, the stream's curve, the lamp on one side and the houseboat on the other).
- **Distance:** near (our bow, cut by the bottom, darkest and softest; the near water), middle (the bridge, her, the
  lamp, the bikes, the houseboat), far (through and above the bridge: the canal running on, two rows of gables
  converging, a smaller bridge, the tower in the haze, the low sky), each plane paler, softer and simpler with distance.
- **Scale:** a small person against a big round shape and a long street; the ring feels like a door, she feels like
  someone standing at the top of it.

## 6. Light, value and colour
- **Time and light:** blue hour after an autumn sunset. The story light is the waking pages (and the stream and her
  phone): candle-gold from below, real falloff; it lights the vault's underside like a lantern in a cave, the arch's
  stones and the bridge's face around the stream, the paper twin around it, and her chin, face, hands and coat front.
  The sky gives a cool violet fill from above and a soft rose afterglow down the canal behind her (it rims the roofs
  and lifts her silhouette); the lamp, the windows and the houseboat are warm but a clear step under the story light.
- **How light or dark, and why:** a low-key frame with one warm heart, because "hushed" is a candle in the dark: the
  sky a calm mid dusk blue that holds warm-white words, the street a mid-dark cool mass, real darks under the arch's
  sides, in the water and in the twin (painted, alive, never black), the brightest light only at the source. Contrast
  is strongest at the source and along the stream, gentle everywhere else.
- **Colour key:** the dusk's blue-violet (indigo at the top, a periwinkle-slate behind the words, a dusty rose low
  down the canal); brick, stone, water and paper all pushed into it (a cool plum brick, violet-sepia paper in the
  shade). **The one accent:** candle-gold, on the pages, the stream, her phone and the lit word, and nowhere else at
  that strength (Autumn Ember's lamp gold against its saturated dusk blue).

## 7. Shapes, edges and paint
- **Shape language:** soft and round for the wonder (the ring, the hump of the bridge, the bell and neck gables, the
  elms' crowns, the curled sheets), held by the upright rhythm of the narrow houses so it never goes soft and mushy.
- **Crisp:** her, the lit pages and the stream near the focal, the railing beside her. **Painterly:** the sky, the
  far canal, the twin's far parts, the water; far planes lose their edges and keep a few broad marks. **Painted by
  hand:** the sky and its clouds, the brick and the stone, the houses, the trees, the paper twin, the water; one
  painted language, the paper of the film over it all, no outlines anywhere.

## 8. Words
- **The caption:** "now / **answer** / your questions" (the core line's last group; the lit hand on "answer", in the
  same gold as the pages), set as the landing-word lockup in the caption band over the calm sky, warm white on dusk.
- **Words in the picture:** none. The handwriting on the pages is a texture of soft marks, never letters, dates or
  names (CORE: we have no real record to show).

## 9. Motion and sound
- **What moves first:** the question falls from her phone as a small warm light; where it lands the reflection turns to
  paper from the centre out and lights; sheets peel up; the stream climbs and reaches the phone. Alive: ripples rings
  out from the source, the loops of script unwinding, her hair lifted a little by the rising warm air, the lit
  windows steady, a slow parallax float (the bow and the bridge against the far canal). No camera move beyond the float.
- **Sound:** the canal's hush, a soft "plip" as the question lands, a paper rustle as the sheets peel, and one warm
  rising chime exactly when the pages light (the hook's one sync: the picture's event, earned); the voice over it.

## 10. The hand-off
- The stream reaches her phone and lights her face; the gold carries the eye up into the next frame (her own house's
  twin answering her), the thread (her question going out and coming back as light) continuing.

## 11. The test (before the build: what it must give)
- **In three words at phone size:** "a hushed, glowing doorway" (it is answering her).
- **Worth pausing on:** the twin's paper, the lit pages and the street's small lives must reward a second look.
- **Mistakes only:** the caption in its band and readable (warm white and the honey word at 4.5:1 or more on the
  bare sky); nothing that must read under the app's bars (the source above the bottom bar, her clear of the rail
  zone); no paper edge at the top; no ink lines; no banding in the sky.
