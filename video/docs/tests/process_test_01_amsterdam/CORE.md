# CORE: the core line and the moment (process test 01, Amsterdam)

**Source:** Capgemini client story "Amsterdam brings 750 years of history to life with AI" (published 2025-12-17,
updated 2026-03-09): https://www.capgemini.com/news/client-stories/amsterdam-brings-750-years-of-history-to-life-with-ai/
Read in full through curl (WebFetch cannot resolve the host here). It is the vendor's own marketing: two searches found
no page from the archive itself, so every fact below is the vendor's account. About 10 months old: not fresh news,
fine for this test.

## Key facts (the source's own words in quotes)
- Amsterdam's city archive built a chatbot with Capgemini and Microsoft, "Chat with History", that "makes 750 years of
  the city's archival history accessible".
- The archive is "fifty kilometers of paper archives": "millions of documents, maps, letters, and registers", "over
  five centuries of urban history".
- Hard to use until now: "Old handwriting, outdated Dutch, and limited search options".
- How: the papers are "digitized, transcribed, and translated from old Dutch to modern Dutch", then "searched and
  interpreted using generative AI".
- Anyone asks in their own words and language: from "What games did children play in the 17th century?" to "When was
  my street built?"
- In the papers: the lives of "residents, artists, merchants, and migrants".
- So far: "By the end of 2025, about two percent of the archive will be digitized."
- A real person's words: "What has been in the dark for centuries can finally come to light" (Pauline van de Heuvel,
  archivist). And: "Archivists and historians remain indispensable."

## What we must not claim
- That all 50 km can be asked already: about 2 % by the end of 2025. The hook may generalise (the playbook allows it);
  the film says the 2 % before it ends.
- "750 years of papers": the title says 750, the text "over five centuries". Say "centuries".
- That it speaks aloud or shows old maps and photos (the page lists "speech interaction" and "old maps and photos" as
  coming later; it names an avatar technology but never shows one in use), that it is never wrong, or that it replaces
  the archivists.
- A real street's date, a real person's record, a quote from the past: we have none, so any on screen would be invented.
- 50 km is the archive's length of paper, never a stack or a height.
- Never a commercial: no company names; the product's name at most once, plainly.

## THE CORE LINE
**"Amsterdam's 50 kilometres of old papers now answer your questions."**
Why it matters, in one line: lives written in handwriting almost nobody could read (who built your street, what
children played) now reach anyone, in their own language.
If the owner wants options: "You can now ask Amsterdam's old papers when your street was built." · the archivist's
own words: "What has been in the dark for centuries can finally come to light."

## THE FILM (the concept)
**The canal remembers.** One Amsterdam canal street at dusk, warm and lived in. Its water does not mirror today's
street: it holds the city's paper twin, the same houses made of old handwritten pages, dark and unreadable, running on
down the canal out of sight. A question drops into the water as a small warm light; where it lands, the old writing
wakes, untangles and rises as plain words (the archivist's "in the dark for centuries ... come to light").
**The arc of feeling:** a hushed wonder, then the frustration of a past nobody can read, the spark when it reads, the
warmth of your own house answering you, and awe at the end: the twin runs on, almost all still dark (about 2 % so
far); the city has barely started talking.
**Through-line:** the thread is her question, a small light going out and coming back; the home place is her canal,
her bridge, her house; the one "you" is a woman who lives here, home by bike, asking "When was my street built?" (the
source's own example).

## THE MOMENT (the hook's frame, the one that shows the most of this world)
- **What happens:** on the little bridge by her house at dusk, she has just asked her phone a question. Below her, the
  canal's reflection has become the street's paper twin, and right under the bridge its old handwriting is lighting
  up, warm, rising toward her.
- **Understood in a second, sound off:** the water under this Amsterdam street holds its past in old writing, and it
  is answering her.
- **Felt:** a hushed "whoa": the city remembers, and it is talking to her. Then the pull to stay: what is it saying?
  Warm and full of wonder, never ghostly.
- **The caption:** "now answer your questions" (the core line's last group, after "Amsterdam's 50 kilometres" and "of
  old papers"; "answer" is the one lit word). The picture alone must say "old papers".
- **Open for the ideas step:** the picture itself (camera, composition, light, the people). Two traps: the handwriting
  is a texture, never words to read (words in the picture few and big), and the paper twin is painted in paper and ink
  tones, never drawn in ink lines (no ink lines in the build).
