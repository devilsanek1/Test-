# VISION: "The canal remembers" (process test 01, Amsterdam; the art director's note)

Every frame of this film is a view of this one world, judged against this note; a frame that belongs to another film
is out, however good. The facts and what we must never claim: `CORE.md`.

- **The concept.** Amsterdam's canals don't mirror today's street: they hold its paper twin, the same houses built of
  old handwritten pages, dark and unreadable, running on down the water out of sight. A question dropped into the
  water is a small warm light; where it lands the pages wake, glow like paper lanterns, and their writing lifts off
  them and rises, untangling, to whoever asked.
- **The world.** One real canal street, lived in, at dusk: narrow brick houses shoulder to shoulder with step and neck
  gables, white window frames, hoist beams, elms, bikes chained to a little humped brick bridge with an iron railing,
  houseboats with plants on deck, a few windows lit (someone cooking, a cat on a sill). Above the waterline, today.
  Below it, the twin: the same street made of ledgers, letters, registers and maps, cream and sepia, its handwriting a
  texture nobody can read. The kit is drawn from real photos of a real Jordaan canal (the world kit's step).
- **The arc of feeling.** A hushed wonder (the water isn't a reflection) -> the frustration of a past nobody can read
  (the twin dark, its writing tangled) -> the spark (her question lands, the pages light) -> the warmth of her own house
  answering her (her house's twin lights up) -> awe (we pull back: the twin runs on for kilometres, almost all of it
  still dark; about 2 % so far: the city has barely started talking).
- **The thread.** Her question: a small warm light going down into the water and coming back up as the answer. The
  home place: her canal, her bridge, her house beside it. The one "you": a woman who lives here, home by bike, asking
  "When was my street built?" (the source's own example).
- **The look in words.** Our one look (clean, refined hand-drawn colour painted in one language, organic shapes, no
  outlines). Dusk blue-violet is the one colour key and the street sits quiet and cool inside it; the one hot accent is
  the gold of the waking pages, and her phone wears the same gold (the question and the answer are one light). The one
  story light is the lit pages: it lights the water, the bridge from below and her face, and everything else steps back.
  The twin is painted in paper and ink TONES (cream, sepia, umber), never drawn in ink lines; the lit paper glows like a
  lantern with its writing showing through as soft marks. Real darks in the water and under the bridge; the sky one
  gentle dusk gradient that holds the words. Warm and full of wonder, never ghostly: the glow is candle-gold, the
  shapes soft and round, the street alive around her. Palette: Autumn Ember's family (golden lamp light against a
  saturated dusk blue), pushed toward dusk.
- **Never on screen:** a readable date, a record or a quote from the past (we have none), a ghost, ink lines, a company.
