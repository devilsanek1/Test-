## THE THREE RULES ABOVE ALL (owner, 2026-10-30)
1. **Every frame is worth pausing on.** Judged at a glance beside video 1 (`docs/look/ref_v1_governments_frame.png`)
   and the owner's references, before any numeric check.
2. **Readable imaginative ideas.** Each beat gets an imaginative picture a viewer reads in a second (video 1's
   government buildings with browser windows, its power-button sun): never a dry diagram, never an abstraction that
   needs explaining.
3. **Frames planned and created WHOLE, from the get-go.** Each frame is designed as one complete picture (its idea, its
   world, its light, its people, its words) before it is built or animated. Never a frame built thin and filled later
   "for the sake of density": density comes from the imagination and the world, never from a filling pass.

**ONE FILM, NEVER A HODGE-PODGE:** one imaginative concept for the whole film above the beats (its world, its central
device, the thread's journey, the arc of feeling); ideas come and are picked as whole sequences, never a collage of the
best single ideas; one art director owns the vision and approves every frame against the whole; every frame is a view of
one shared world (one place, light, palette, cast, type); every frame is evaluated IN the board, and the whole board is
read in order after every round (PIPELINE_V2). — ("another possible problem is if every agent bulid a scene in
isolation, the whole thing comes up like a complete mess of hudge pudge ideas - is that a risk here? Anything in the
process that ensures this doesn't happen and the whole video and the narrative idea is clear?", 2026-10-30; v3's lesson:
"too busy, too incoherent; no story ... just a bunch of shots put together".)

**THE FRAME LOOP (every built frame, before the owner sees it):** build it whole -> a fresh evaluator judges the FEELING
first (does it give the beat's feeling and idea in a second, worth pausing on, beside video 1 and the references) ->
THERE (move on), ALMOST (adjust exactly what holds it back, at most 2 rounds) or NOT THERE (rework from the idea, a new
approach, at most once; then the owner gets the options); encoded in every workflow (PIPELINE_V2, "THE FRAME LOOP"). —
("make sure we have the loops setup correctly where the agents bulid the proposed scenes and we evaluate and perhaps
adjust it if its almost there or rework completely if it doesn't achieve the feeling we need - is that something you
would already do or we should explicitly setup?", 2026-10-30.)

## Script and voice
- **A script is a coherent STORY, not a collection of true sentences about the topic.**
  - One throughline question, a situation the viewer is in, and every line following from the one before (joinable by
    "but", "so" or "because", never "and also"), building to a turn and a payoff.
  - Spine first, then lines. A list of facts to prove is input, never the structure.
  - Test: someone who hears it once can retell it as a story.
  — ("just a collection of sentences", v3 G1, 2026-10-26).
- **ONE SUBJECT, GONE DEEP (the Big Bend rule).** One thing in one place carries the whole film, and every line digs
  deeper into that same thing (what it is, how it works, why, the twist, what it means for you). Other places or
  examples appear only as ONE comparison or scale shot that serves the subject, never as stops on a tour. The Big Bend
  reel: one tower, one city, one question (why does it curve back down?); every line deepens it (its height, its name,
  its year, the twist that it is the longest building, its curved lifts); the scale comes from one lineup beside other
  towers, not from visiting them. Our v5 draft 1 broke it: orbit, a mountain in Italy, the sea off Shanghai, Stockholm,
  then Helsinki in 43 s, one line each and nothing deepened (a list wearing a story's clothes). **Test, at G0 and G1:**
  name the film's one thing in 3 words; count the places (one home place, at most one comparison shot); every line
  must be about the one thing. This tightens CRAFT §a's "at most 3 places". **No side quests, and only the information
  the story needs:** a beat that doesn't carry the one story toward its payoff goes, however true, weird or fun (v4's
  2010 data center under a cathedral, a "twist" since G0: "the sidequest that doesn't really add much to the story").
  Test every beat at G0 and G1: cut it in your head; if the viewer still gets the story and the payoff, it stays cut.
  A sentence may grow when the words make it more natural or clearer; the film never carries more facts than its
  story needs. — ("I what I don't like about the current story is we are too wide and unfocused again like we talked about a bunch of topics all within 30 seconds - we started with space then mountain then  Stockholm then Helsinki / Compare it with big bend story and create a new rule for storytelling", v5 G1, 2026-10-29;
  "you are again packing more information than needed in the short update - its actually okay to keep the text
  longer if needed, but you are having again too much of a sidequest - like specifically this 'Computers have heated
  Helsinki before. In twenty ten, a small data center under a cathedral started heating homes.' is the sidequest that
  doesn't really add much to the story", v4 G1, 2026-10-29).
- **The first line sounds like a friend starting a story, and still pulls: curious, never pushy. "Did you know that
  ..." is the owner's model opener.** The Big Bend reel: "Did you know that New York once got a proposal to build this
  giant 2,000-foot tall bending skyscraper? It's called the Big Bend." A friend's question with a surprising fact in it
  (a place, a thing, a number), no sell (unlike v3's "Buying a laptop soon?"); the curiosity comes from the picture and
  the one odd detail. The core line itself can open as "Did you know that ..." ("instantly more interesting").
  — ("BTW I really like the script too in the video / See how it's using natural language it's
  first hook sentence is extremely natural like a conversation yet captures the attention and curiosity without being
  too pushy and the script has a rhythm we discussed before"; then "Oh I also meant that a video has an opening as "did you know that .." like that what I meant by great opening"; "Even with did you know the core line is instantly more interesting", 2026-10-29; saved: the study's
  `refs/bigbend_reel/`).
- **CLEAR TO A REGULAR PERSON ON ONE HEARING: every sentence is understood the first time, at feed speed, by regular
  people worldwide (many learned English at school; they read the captions as it plays and can't rewind), never only
  by us.** Ask of every sentence the owner's question, "is that sentence good and will be received well by regular
  people?", and write it as news you'd pass to a friend (v4's line 1, "Did you know Helsinki wants AI to heat seventy
  thousand homes?": "great thats what i would like to tell my friends"). How (the principles, their research and the
  failures of v1-v5: `docs/retro/2026-10-29_script_clarity/PRINCIPLES.md`):
  - **Everyday names, one per thing:** call each thing what regular people call it and keep that one name all film;
    no trade words, and no common word in its trade sense ("the chips", "a cabinet of them": to most people chips are
    food). A term the story needs is described once, plainly, where it first appears ("buildings called data
    centers"). Rounding a thing to its everyday form is the licence "generalizing is fine" gives (a 72-chip rack is
    "one stack of these computers").
  - **Introduce, then point:** "the X", "those X", "that hot" only for what the script already said; every "it",
    "they", "this" or "that" points at ONE obvious thing in the line before, or say the noun again ("But Helsinki
    WANTS it": the heat, the air or the sea? -> "But Helsinki wants that heat."). A short line keeps its noun (Rhythm,
    below).
  - **The plain thought, as the typical case:** say who does what, where, in physical words; never a compressed or
    clever hint at a deeper thought, and no vague scope ("almost everywhere": say what happens, e.g. "in a typical
    data center, here is what happens"). Test: write "What I mean is: ___" under the line; if that is clearer, it
    becomes the line.
  - **One new idea per sentence, hooked onto the last:** each sentence starts from what the listener just heard and
    ends on its new part; the joins are said and true ("but", "so", "because"); the line right after the hook and its
    payoff gives the plain base (what makes the claim possible) at once, so the viewer wonders about the story
    ("how?"), never about a word ("which chips?"). The beat order: PRINCIPLES.md, "How a 40-second script is structured".
  - **Literal words for a worldwide ear:** no idioms, slang or wordplay to decode ("first dibs", "by the mountain").
  - **The one joke is the plain situation, said kindly:** one step, in simple, gentle, everyday words, never weird,
    cheeky or scandalous ("nobody's even ..."); after one hearing a viewer smiles AND can say the point, or it goes.
  - **Numbers and comparisons by ear:** one number per sentence, round, tied to an everyday thing; a comparison says
    what is alike ("as much heat as forty kettles", never only "like forty kettles").
  - **A comment line is clear first:** people argue with what they understood, and swipe past what they didn't
    (Audience and goal: comments are a massive plus).
  - **Checked, never assumed:** `scripts/voice/check_clarity.py` OK at G1 (no error, every warning fixed or answered
    in the script doc's "Checks"; OK never means clear), then the G1 critic's cold viewer panel (`docs/PIPELINE_V2.md`,
    G1): three regular people hear the narration once, sentence by sentence, and every line that snags one of them
    (heard wrong, missed, re-linked to an earlier line, an unsure or swiping thumb) is rewritten. A readability score
    proves nothing ("The chips get that hot." scores as the easiest line), and neither does our own ear: we know what
    we meant.
  Ambiguous or weird loses the biggest audience, the one we write for. — ("so what i don;t like about these scripts we
  write lately is we are being too ambiguous and weird at times"; "We are talking about reaching the biggest audience
  and getting the engagement - but we would lose people on this sentence. Like we need to ask ourselves - is that
  sentence good and will be received well by regular people? Chips is a word not a lot of people would understand";
  "this is directionally okay, but still weird we need a simpler less scandalous language"; "I think you are having
  some deeper thought here and you choose to be obscure about it. So i think you want to say that in a typical
  datacenter here is what happens. When you say "almost everywhere" - this is very ambigous"; "When you say IT - what
  exactly is it - heat or air or sea?", v4 G1, 2026-10-29; earlier, "Who even knows WordPress now?", v1, October 2026.)
- **Sound like a real person talking, never like AI (the creative brief's RULE #1): check every sentence for
  AI-sounding wording;** the everyday words it asks for are the clarity rule's, above. — (v1, 2026-10-24).
- **Human connectors, placed TACTICALLY: a few spoken words a person telling a friend would use, at the moments that
  need a hand:** "To put that into perspective," before a comparison or a big number; "Actually," before the part that
  surprises; "So," at a consequence; "Now," when the story moves on; and the El Niño reference's (`docs/voice/refs/
  2026-10-29_el_nino/`): "So how does it work?" (the viewer's own question said aloud, then answered at length),
  "Or in other words," (the plain restatement right after a term's definition), "Normally," (the usual case before
  the change), "The result is that" (the consequence). A few in a film (about two to four), never on
  every line, each one true to the sentence it opens. They make the voice a person's and tell the listener what kind
  of sentence comes next (listeners follow and recall better with such markers: Chaudron and Richards 1985,
  `docs/retro/2026-10-29_script_clarity/research_ear.md` finding 11). They are not RULE #1's stock reveal phrases
  ("Here's the thing", "The catch?", "Turns out", "Let that sink in"), which promise drama and name nothing and stay
  banned (a question answered at length and "in other words" before a plain restatement are connectors; "The
  result? X." and "In other words, [zinger]" stay RULE #1's tells). — ("i would add more human / conversation like sentences TACTICALLY like before this 'Just one of them makes
  as much heat as FORTY kettles, boiling non-stop.' i want to say something like 'to put it into perspective' or
  something like this. or something like 'Actually' / 'so' that just adds a bit more human to it, figure out what to
  add", v4 G1, 2026-10-29; the El Niño voice line, "it sounds directionally good to me", 2026-10-29).
- **Rhythm: never a monotone run of long sentences; put a short one (5 words or fewer, e.g. "Let's find out." "So how
  does it work?") at the turns, after at most three long ones, three only when each is one flowing spoken thought

- **Scripts come with detailed emotional / delivery cues; the owner always gets the full script text with any take.**

## Music and sound
- **Sync picture to sound ON PURPOSE.** Cuts, camera moves and key actions land on the sound's beats, hits and the
  voice's stresses where that adds energy or meaning, and NOT everywhere: we already do it "decently okay", now it is a
  researched, named practice with a rule for where it fits: `docs/look/SOUND_SYNC.md` (distilled from the owner's two
  reels, `docs/look/refs_sound_sync/`). In short: spend it, don't sprinkle it (a count of three sounded changes in the
  hook, rest while the voice explains, ONE release at the payoff in a written voice pause); every hit lands on the first
  visible frame of one change of the beat's subject, never before it, and is heard; nothing on continuous motion,
  background life, captions or the music's beats. —
  ("the research distill and practice of syncing the video and shots with with sounds ... We already do this and I think
  decently okay we just need to be more intentional and educated about this effect and where it make sense to use",
  2026-10-30.)
- **Music: cinematic but subtle, especially at the start.** The voice and the hook carry the opening: the hook's sounds
  sit on a quiet floor (air, no bed); the bed enters after the hook as a small step and rises once, at the payoff, in a
  written voice pause (`docs/look/SOUND_SYNC.md` S1, S6, S7; both of the owner's sound reels open on near silence). —
  (v1; 2026-10-30).
- **Mix to -14 LUFS / -1 dBTP, voice always on top**; SFX land on visual hits: on the hit's first visible frame or up to
  2 frames after it, never before, and heard (in a voice gap, or the loudest thing in its band): `docs/look/SOUND_SYNC.md`

- **More sound effects, especially ones people link with something good on their phone** (like pop, notification ding,

## Hook and pacing
- **Every video has ONE core line, picked before the script (by default on the topic screen: each topic comes with its proposed core line, so the owner picks both at once; a separate core-line round only on request):** the single sentence at the core
  of the message with the biggest emotional reaction. It drives frame 0, the hook, the cover and the payoff; every line
  serves it. From the pick we draft its THROUGH-LINE CARD (one thread object, one home place, one "you":
  `docs/look/CRAFT.md` §a); the script (G1) completes it with a carrier for every join, taken from the script's own
  joins, and the owner approves the card with the script. The thread may also leave for a FAIRNESS beat the owner approves
  (the other side's equal-weight reply, a place the subject must never be): declare it on the card. — (v3, 2026-10-25; card:
  v3 retro, owner yes, 2026-10-27; fairness exception: v2, owner yes, 2026-10-27; picked with the topic by default: lean early stages, v4, 2026-10-28).
- **The first 3 s / first scene must be a banger ("something is about to happen"), and every opening effect must be
  BIG enough to stop a thumb:** judged as a viewer scrolling a feed on a phone, each effect (the draw-on, the hook's
  mechanism, the ticks) is unmistakable on first watch: bold, large on screen, long enough to register, with its own
  sound; a subtle effect counts as none. — (owner, v3, 2026-10-28: "initial animation effects like visual aspect should
  be better and more effective at capturing people attention ... the initial drawing effect is barely visible or
  noticeable and really don't impact or amazes me", of a 0.3 s pale draw-on and 30 px memory sticks.)
  - frame 0 is the scene being made, drawn on in the clean pen: the subject's outline (with the supporting lines)
    first, then colour floods in over it (no outline stays: Texture and lines, 2026-10-29), as a visible event (bold
    line at full contrast, a moving pen tip, long enough to
    read: ~0.3 s was invisible), never a pale wash (the lined films drew every line first);
  - voice from the first frames (the first word at ~0.12 s, the locked voice file's lead; never a silent lead);
  - the hook's claim made physical and escalating: the expected thing shown, then reality overtaking it at ~2 s,
    through the real mechanism;
  - something new every few frames from the story's own build (the subject and its mechanism), never from extra
    supporting life; never still, in one continuous opening shot;
  - the film loops back to frame 0.
  Details: `docs/look/CINEMATIC_GUIDE.md`, "The opening"; `docs/look/DIRECTION.md` rule 9. — (owner's reference, v3,
  2026-10-26; the clean pen and one focus, v3 G5b, 2026-10-27).
- **State the payoff in words within the first ~2 s, in the voice's order; polish the first 2 s and 5 s most, then the
  first 10 s.** A calm, pretty opening lost ~80 % of viewers by 3 s; late quirks are acceptable. The captions ARE the
  words on screen: frame 0 shows the first spoken line, and when the voice opens with a question the payoff is the next
  caption (by ~2 s). Never a title over the captions (no `frame0` block): it answers before the question is asked and
  repeats the spoken line; the core line as a title belongs on the cover. — (v1 IG insights, 2026-10-25; v3,
  2026-10-28: "why do we have the text repeat itself?", then "Why are we having this out of order weirdly? Should we
  just show buying ..?").
- **The hook is a concrete, surprising, TRUE claim with stakes**, verified against the primary source before it's
  locked; for a small news item, research the bigger story and lead with its strongest true framing. — (v2, 2026-10-25).
- **Personal framings must feel natural, not over the top.** Never cast the viewer as a loser in a contest they don't
  feel; test "you" claims with a viewer panel. — (v3 G2, 2026-10-25).
- **Target 30-45 s and end close to the payoff** (viewers left once the story felt resolved, ~40 s). — (v1 insights).
- **Every frame at least 8/10, no dead frames, joins on the beat** (the voice's beat: each join's midpoint on the next
