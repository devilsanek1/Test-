Video 4 Ideas

Four whole films drawn for your Helsinki voice, each from its own idea. Every frame went through the review loop: a fresh reviewer named the feeling it gives, then up to two rounds of fixes. Three judges then read them as films.

The pick: B, The Open Machine

A warm crack glows in the snow of a Helsinki quay. We follow it into the AI machine, door by door, find the one stretch of pipe still missing, watch it drawn in for next year, and follow the warm line home to her window and your phone.

  • It reads with the sound off. It's the only one of the four the judges could follow frame by frame without the voice.
  • The idea is the fact. The pipes under the snow are real and the computers' heat is real; the plan is the missing piece, drawn in pencil. So "a plan, starting next year" stays true in every frame.
  • One thread: the warm seam in the snow carries your eye from the first frame to the last.

What it still needs: a heart. One woman we follow through the whole film (D's warmth, told in B's world), plus three thin frames reworked: the tea, the plan and the door.

Weakest: the frost doesn't read before frame 8, so the idea needs explaining. It plays as calm views more than events.

Where the judges split

Only on A. The art director ranked it first for the world it could become; the viewer and the story editor ranked it last because its frost idea needs decoding. A painted world can be made rich later; an idea a viewer can't read can't be fixed later. So B is the base, and the art director's notes become its to-do list: every frame one whole picture, top to bottom, in a real place.

Before the judges, my own read leaned to C for its clean thread. Their case for B is stronger for this voice: B is the one you can follow with the sound off.

Your calls

  1. The base: B as recommended, or another film.
  2. Her: one woman through the whole film (she walks home on the quay, fans herself in the hot hall, sips her tea before the 40 kettles, raises the same mug in her warm window at the end).
  3. The sky for the words: the pale field above every B frame becomes the night's own sky when painted.
  4. The plan: drawn as a soft pencil sketch over the empty lot, never an ink line or a wireframe.
  5. Anything you love from the others (C's tea cosy, D's socks, A's frost drawing). It comes in only if it fits B's world, retold in its terms.
