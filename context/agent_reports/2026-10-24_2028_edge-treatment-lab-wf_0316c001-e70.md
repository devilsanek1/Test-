## build

The edge module, the lab and all the review outputs are done. All six variants hold a fixed edge while the camera moves, never flicker, and never cut into the caption block. My pick for "cleaner, more polished, premium" is `trim` (my own variant), with `panel` second. Nothing is switched on in the film yet: the house default is still `clean`.

**Variants** (every one has an `amount`: 0 = off, 1 = as designed, about 1.6 max; plus a `seed` for a differently cut edge)

| variant | what it does | cost per full-res frame |
|---|---|---|
| `clean` | Control, no treatment. | 108–117 ms |
| `panel` | Closest to the first video. Colour sits in a hand-cut panel 30–45 px in from the edge, slightly off-square, and the drawing's lines carry on into a white margin. At 2–3 corners one edge line overshoots the corner by 22–50 px; the other side stays open. | 133–155 ms (+25–35%) |
| `vignette` | Finished centre, sketched edges. Colour sits in a loose hand-drawn shape that hugs the sides and bites into the corners unevenly, deepest at the bottom; line art runs out to the screen edge. | 144–155 ms (+30%) |
| `construction` | Full-bleed colour plus sparse construction lines at the edges: perspective guides, a horizon guide on both sides, tick marks at the thirds and halves, two crop-mark corners. | 110–114 ms (about free) |
| `rough-tb` | Hand-cut paper edge at the top and bottom only; the sides stay fully immersive. | 142–164 ms (+20–50%) |
| `trim` (my proposal) | A hand-trimmed print. Slim 16–24 px side margins (the only edges a viewer sees in the app), deeper top and bottom margins that sit under the TikTok/Reels UI, small scissor-snipped corners, no added strokes. The sun and mascot keep their colour over the margin, like the first video's sun and cat. | 138–175 ms (+28–50%) |

Costs were measured at 1080×1920, concurrency 2, on the shared machine at load 2–5, so treat them as rough. Rendering the scene twice costs +20–50% per frame rather than double, because page capture and encoding are only paid once.

**How it works:** a screen-space wrapper (`EdgeTreatment`) renders the scene twice: a line-art copy on white paper underneath, and the full-colour copy on top cut to a fixed hand-drawn shape. The shapes don't depend on time, so the edge stays fixed while the camera moves. A probe with the outline drawn in magenta at 0, 2.5 and 4.97 s found every outline pixel within 1 px of its position at 0 s, during the street's push and pan and the monument's crane. A shape check (5 cut variants × 5 amounts × 40 seeds) found no case where a shape cuts into the caption block, and captions render above the treatment.

**Turning it on (not done; Film belongs to the core lane):** wrap Film's scene layers and overlays, not the captions, in `<FilmEdges>…</FilmEdges>`, and do the same around `Standalone`'s two scene blocks. You can then switch variants from the render command without code changes: `--props='{"edges":"trim"}'` or `'{"edges":{"variant":"panel","amount":0.8}}'`. Details are in `docs/EDGES.md`.

**Limitations**
- **Colour in the margin:** the core `Sun`, the cine `BigSun` (disc and glow rings) and the mascot ignore the line-art mode, so they keep their colour over the margin. That's intended. `margin="strict"` turns them white too.
- **Sky detection:** the sky is turned white by matching its colour. Scenes must use the theme/TOD sky colours or list theirs in `skyFills`.
- **Top and bottom margins:** these sit under the platform UI, so `rough-tb` and `trim`'s deeper margins mostly show in clean exports and paused frames.
- **Night:** at night the white margin is the brightest thing in the frame. For long night stretches a lower `amount` per scene may be better.
- **`construction`:** it needs each scene's vanishing point (`vp`). With the default it is only right for standard eye-level scenes.
- **Bug fixed along the way:** an early `rough-tb` shape cut a sliver off the left side. It's fixed, and its stills and clips were re-rendered.

**The three options:**
- **Clean** is the control. The picture runs edge to edge with no paper and nothing drawn on top. It is the cheapest to render and the safe fallback for very dark or very fast moments, but it has none of the first video's hand-drawn charm.
- **Trim** is the quiet one. A thin strip of paper, cut by hand with scissors, runs down each side (about 30 px on the left, 24 on the right) and a hairline runs along the top. The drawing's own lines cross the strips and run off the screen. The bottom is open, so the ground and the people in front stay in full colour all the way off the frame. There is one sharp scissor snip at the top-right corner and two thin ruled ink lines crossing at the top-left. It reads as a hand-trimmed window rather than a frame, and it is the most polished and premium of the three.
- **Panel** is closest to the first video. Colour stops at a hand-cut edge (wider on the left, about 40 px; narrow on the right, away from the app's buttons). The line drawing carries on into the white and visibly stops there, and ruled ink marks the two top corners. Near the bottom the left edge is snipped away at an angle, so the lower part of the picture runs to the edge in full colour. It has the most charm and looks a little more like a sketch on paper. Setting it slightly lighter (0.8) suits phones best.

**Fixes from the judges' notes, in both trim and panel:**
- **Seam:** the thin grey line along the cut is gone.
- **Banners:** their peach colour no longer leaks into the white margin.
- **Sun glow:** the large glow rings no longer spill into the white. Only the sun itself and the mascot cross the edge in colour.

**Checks:**
- **Steadiness:** in the clips the cut stays within 2 px in 95-98% of measurements while the camera moves. Comparing the cut's outline at three points in the move, panel's did not shift at all and trim's moved by at most one pixel.
- **Captions:** across 800 test shapes, the paper never reaches the caption area and no ink stroke enters it.
