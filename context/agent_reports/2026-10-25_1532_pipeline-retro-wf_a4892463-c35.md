The research is in /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Test-/59905704-1101-50eb-baa8-b5e4e9d5d2d2/scratchpad/retro/research.md. I used 6 web searches. Most sources are studio and agency process pages and are cited at the end of the file; claims from a single secondary source are marked. I did not render anything or edit any repo files.

**What studios do.** Kurzgesagt, TED-Ed, motion-design agencies and Pixar story reels all follow the same order: script, then voice, then rough pictures timed to the voice (the animatic), then layout, blocking (key poses), polish, sound and final. Each stage gets a sign-off, and what came before it is then frozen. Agencies allow about 2-3 revision rounds per stage and none on approved voice except technical errors. The animatic gets the most iterations because it is the first point where story and timing can be judged, and it is still cheap to change. A change costs about as much as the work already built on top of what changes. Short-form creators treat the first 3 s as a separate, swappable piece: 3-5 opening variants on the same body, compared by how many viewers leave by second 3.

**How our run broke this (from git):**
- **Late first viewing:** the owner first saw a playable film 18.7 h after the start.
- **Built before script lock:** engine, kit, mascot, sound and voice versions 1-2 were made for a 78 s script that became 55 s.
- **Polish before story:** the edge lab, the shared kit and the quality-check tooling were built before any scene existed.
- **No animatic:** the storyboard was 815 lines of text.
- **Story problems surfaced during polish:** the S08 peak and S10 readability issues showed up in per-scene scoring and shipped at 7 and 7.5.
- **Hook last:** the opening was reworked after the film was nearly done.

**Recommended gates.** At each gate the owner gets a playable 360x640 video with voice and captions.

| Gate | What the owner judges |
|---|---|
| G0 Angle | text only: topic, audience, length |
| G1 Radio cut | final-provider voice over plain paper with captions, plus 3 opening-line variants |
| G2 Voice lock | final take, which sets all timings |
| G3 Sketch animatic (new) | whole film as line sketches timed to the voice, rough camera, every transition labelled, 2 opening-visual variants |
| G4 Look | 1-3 finished stills plus a 3-5 s motion test, run alongside G3; for the second video it's a 15-minute "same house style?" check |
| G5 Blocking cut | real sets, final framing and camera, key poses, no paper edges or texture |
| G6 Polish cut | first point where per-scene AI critic scoring runs |
| G7 Sound | effects and mix |
| G8 Final | full-resolution render and checks |

Target: playable cuts at about hours 1, 3, 8 and 14 instead of 18.7.

**Rules:**
- Nothing is built for a later stage before its gate is approved.
- Every stage covers the whole film before any scene goes deeper.
- A change after a gate means reopening that gate openly, with the cost stated; the owner decides.
- No critic panels or pixel-level checks before G6.
- Precise numbers (camera speeds, cut matching) come at G5 or later.
- The opening goes first at every gate and is built as a swappable segment.
- One video per gate replaces the 30-minute status image strips.

**Remotion side.** One low-res preview render of the whole film with audio, a "draft" switch that turns off edges, paper and noise, and a gate, version, scene and timecode label on every pre-final cut. The only new tool to build is a SketchReel composition that draws the G3 animatic from a JSON file, so a G3 revision means editing data, not code.
